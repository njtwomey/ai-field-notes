<Definition>
A threshold model for an ordinal label $y \in \set{1, \dots, K}$ cuts a score $s = \wvec^\top\xvec$ at thresholds
$\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{K-1}$ and predicts class $k$ when $\theta_{k-1} < s \le \theta_k$. Fitting the thresholds
with the order imposed is the problem

$$
\min_{\thetavec} L(\thetavec) \quad \text{subject to} \quad g_k(\thetavec) = \theta_k - \theta_{k+1} \le 0, \qquad k = 1, \dots, K - 2,
$$

where $L$ is a <NoteLink to="cross-entropy-and-perplexity">negative log-likelihood</NoteLink> or a threshold loss.
Its <Gloss name="kkt" /> multipliers $\mu_k \ge 0$ satisfy $\mu_k(\theta_k - \theta_{k+1}) = 0$. A constraint is active, $\theta_k = \theta_{k+1}$, only when the data
push the two thresholds to cross, and $\mu_k$ is the size of that push <Cite id="kuhn1951" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

### The setup

Every example $i$ has a score $s_i = \wvec^\top\xvec_i$ on one direction $\wvec$. Hold $\wvec$ fixed. This is the
threshold step of a fit that alternates between $\wvec$ and $\thetavec$, and it is also the threshold part of a joint
fit: the ordering constraints do not involve $\wvec$, so the joint <Gloss name="kkt" /> conditions add
$\nabla_{\wvec} L = \zeros$ and leave the threshold conditions below unchanged.

The cumulative logit model, with $\sigma(z) = 1/(1 + e^{-z})$, sets

$$
P(y \le k \mid \xvec) = \sigma(\theta_k - s), \qquad P(y = k \mid \xvec) = \sigma(\theta_k - s) - \sigma(\theta_{k-1} - s),
$$

with $\theta_0 = -\infty$ and $\theta_K = +\infty$ <Cite id="mccullagh1980" />. The class probabilities are
non-negative for every score exactly when $\theta_1 \le \dots \le \theta_{K-1}$. The order of the classes is carried by
the order of the thresholds. See <NoteLink to="cumulative-link-model" /> for the model itself.

Some fits require strict gaps, $\theta_{k+1} - \theta_k \ge \epsilon$ for a margin $\epsilon > 0$. The constraint is
then $g_k(\thetavec) = \theta_k - \theta_{k+1} + \epsilon \le 0$. Every result below holds with $\epsilon$ added.

### When the order holds without a constraint

**The cumulative logit likelihood with every class observed.** Suppose $\theta_{k-1} \ge \theta_k$ and some example
$i$ has $y_i = k$. Its probability is $\sigma(\theta_k - s_i) - \sigma(\theta_{k-1} - s_i) \le 0$, because $\sigma$
is increasing. Its log-probability is undefined, so the negative log-likelihood is $+\infty$ there. As the gap
$\theta_k - \theta_{k-1}$ shrinks to zero from above, the same probability tends to zero and the negative
log-likelihood tends to $+\infty$. The loss is therefore a barrier on the ordered region. Any minimiser lies strictly
inside it, every ordering constraint is inactive, and every multiplier is zero.

**Per-threshold fits that share the score and see all the data.** Fit each $\theta_k$ alone by logistic regression of
the binary target $\indicator[y_i \le k]$ on the offset $\theta_k - s_i$. The loss for threshold $k$ is

$$
L_k(\theta_k) = -\sum_i \Big(\indicator[y_i \le k]\log\sigma(\theta_k - s_i) + \indicator[y_i > k]\log\sigma(s_i - \theta_k)\Big).
$$

Its derivative is $\sum_i \sigma(\theta_k - s_i) - N_{\le k}$, where $N_{\le k}$ is the number of examples with
$y_i \le k$. At the minimum,

$$
\sum_i \sigma(\theta_k - s_i) = N_{\le k}.
$$

The left side is strictly increasing in $\theta_k$ and the same function for every $k$. The right side does not
decrease with $k$. So $\theta_k \le \theta_{k+1}$, with equality exactly when class $k + 1$ is empty. The sum of these
losses over $k$ is the logistic all-threshold loss of the <Gloss name="consistent-rank-logits" /> output layer, and
Cao et al. prove the same ordering for it <Cite id="cao2020coral" />. Chu and Keerthi prove the corresponding result
for the hinge all-threshold loss, support vector ordinal regression with implicit constraints (SVOR-IMC)
<Cite id="chu2005svor" />.

### When the order can fail

- **An empty class.** Let class $k$ have no examples in the cumulative logit model. Then $\theta_{k-1}$ appears only in
  the upper term $\sigma(\theta_{k-1} - s_i)$ of class $k - 1$, which it increases, and $\theta_k$ only in the lower term
  $\sigma(\theta_k - s_i)$ of class $k + 1$, which it decreases. Neither term sees the sign of $\theta_k - \theta_{k-1}$.
  The likelihood formula improves as $\theta_{k-1} \to +\infty$ and $\theta_k \to -\infty$, so unconstrained descent
  drives the pair past each other and apart without bound. The class that would pay for the crossing has no example to
  pay it.
- **Fits that use only neighbouring classes.** The immediate-threshold loss fits $\theta_k$ to classes $k$ and $k + 1$
  only <Cite id="rennie2005" />. A rare class $k + 1$ is outweighed on both sides: class $k$ pulls $\theta_k$ up past it,
  and class $k + 2$ pulls $\theta_{k+1}$ down past it. The worked example below has this failure. Chu and Keerthi's
  support vector ordinal regression with explicit constraints (SVOR-EXC) uses this loss and imposes
  $\theta_k \le \theta_{k+1}$ as constraints for this reason <Cite id="chu2005svor" /> <Cite id="chu2007svor" />.
- **Separate binary classifiers with their own weights.** Frank and Hall train one classifier for each target
  $y > k$ <Cite id="frank2001" />. With a weight vector per split, the estimates of $P(y > k \mid \xvec)$ need not
  decrease in $k$ at every input, and the differenced class probabilities can be negative. The failure comes from the
  separate weights. With a shared score, the argument above orders the thresholds.
- **Gradient steps that overshoot.** Even when the minimiser is ordered, a gradient step of fixed size can carry one
  threshold past its neighbour. For the cumulative logit the iterate then leaves the domain of the likelihood.

The ordering constraint is therefore needed in four situations: a class is empty, a loss fits each threshold to its
neighbours only, splits have their own weights, or the optimiser can step past the boundary.

## Mechanism

### The Lagrangian and the KKT conditions

The constraints are linear, so the feasible set $\set{\thetavec : \theta_1 \le \dots \le \theta_{K-1}}$ is a convex
cone. Attach a multiplier $\mu_k \ge 0$ to each constraint. The Lagrangian is

$$
\Lcal(\thetavec, \muvec) = L(\thetavec) + \sum_{k=1}^{K-2} \mu_k(\theta_k - \theta_{k+1}).
$$

The constraint gradients are $\nabla g_k = \evec_k - \evec_{k+1}$, where $\evec_k$ is the $k$-th standard basis vector.
They are linearly independent, so a constraint qualification holds at every point. For a convex $L$, such as the
negative log-likelihood of the cumulative logit or any convex threshold loss, the <Gloss name="kkt" /> conditions are
necessary and sufficient for a minimum <Cite id="boyd2004" />:

1. **Stationarity.** $\nabla L(\thetavec) + \sum_k \mu_k(\evec_k - \evec_{k+1}) = \zeros$.
2. **Primal feasibility.** $\theta_k \le \theta_{k+1}$ for every $k$.
3. **Dual feasibility.** $\mu_k \ge 0$ for every $k$.
4. **Complementary slackness.** $\mu_k(\theta_k - \theta_{k+1}) = 0$ for every $k$.

Write $\partial_j L = \partial L / \partial\theta_j$. Threshold $\theta_j$ appears in constraint $j$ with coefficient
$+1$ and in constraint $j - 1$ with coefficient $-1$. Component $j$ of stationarity is therefore

$$
\partial_j L + \mu_j - \mu_{j-1} = 0, \qquad \mu_0 = \mu_{K-1} = 0.
$$

Summing components $1$ to $k$ telescopes the multipliers and gives each one in closed form:

$$
\mu_k = -\sum_{j=1}^{k} \partial_j L, \qquad k = 1, \dots, K - 2.
$$

Summing all $K - 1$ components gives $\sum_{j=1}^{K-1} \partial_j L = 0$. Shifting every threshold by the same amount
never violates a constraint, so the loss must be stationary in that direction.

### Reading the conditions

The quantity $-\sum_{j \le k} \partial_j L$ is the rate at which the loss falls when the block
$\theta_1, \dots, \theta_k$ moves up together while $\theta_{k+1}, \dots, \theta_{K-1}$ stay fixed. It is the net
push of the data on the lower block towards the upper one.

- **Inactive constraint.** If $\theta_k < \theta_{k+1}$, complementary slackness gives $\mu_k = 0$. The net push on
  the lower block is zero, and the thresholds sit where the unconstrained loss puts them.
- **Active constraint.** If $\mu_k > 0$, complementary slackness gives $\theta_k = \theta_{k+1}$. The two thresholds
  merge into one cut point. The interval $(\theta_k, \theta_{k+1}]$ of class $k + 1$ has zero width, so the model
  never predicts class $k + 1$. The data push the lower block upward with force $\mu_k$, and the constraint holds it.
- **Dual feasibility is the direction test.** A merge is optimal only when the data push the thresholds towards each
  other. A negative $\mu_k$ would mean the data want the pair apart. The constraint would then be inactive.

The multiplier is also a sensitivity. Impose a margin, $\theta_{k+1} - \theta_k = \epsilon$ on an active constraint,
and let $L^*(\epsilon)$ be the optimal loss. Differentiating $\theta_{k+1}^*(\epsilon) - \theta_k^*(\epsilon) = \epsilon$
gives $\theta_{k+1}^{*\prime} - \theta_k^{*\prime} = 1$. With one active constraint, stationarity gives
$\partial_k L = -\mu_k$ and $\partial_{k+1} L = \mu_k$, and $\partial_j L = 0$ at every other threshold. By the chain rule,

$$
\frac{dL^*}{d\epsilon} = \partial_k L\,\theta_k^{*\prime} + \partial_{k+1} L\,\theta_{k+1}^{*\prime} = \mu_k(\theta_{k+1}^{*\prime} - \theta_k^{*\prime}) = \mu_k.
$$

Requiring a gap of $\epsilon$ between two merged thresholds raises the optimal loss at rate $\mu_k$. The same argument
is the sensitivity result of <NoteLink to="lagrange-multipliers" />, applied to one ordering constraint.

### Worked example

Take $K = 4$ and ten scores: three examples of class 1 at $s = 0$, one example of class 2 at $s = 1$, three examples of
class 3 at $s = 2$ and three examples of class 4 at $s = 3$. Fit the thresholds with the logistic immediate-threshold
loss, in which threshold $\theta_k$ separates class $k$ from class $k + 1$ and sees no other class:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(\thetavec) ={}& \underbrace{3\log(1 + e^{-\theta_1}) + \log(1 + e^{\theta_1 - 1})}_{\theta_1 \text{: classes 1 and 2}} \\
&+ \underbrace{\log(1 + e^{1 - \theta_2}) + 3\log(1 + e^{\theta_2 - 2})}_{\theta_2 \text{: classes 2 and 3}} \\
&+ \underbrace{3\log(1 + e^{2 - \theta_3}) + 3\log(1 + e^{\theta_3 - 3})}_{\theta_3 \text{: classes 3 and 4}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Each threshold appears in its own two terms. The partial derivatives are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_1 L &= -3\sigma(-\theta_1) + \sigma(\theta_1 - 1), \\
\partial_2 L &= -\sigma(1 - \theta_2) + 3\sigma(\theta_2 - 2), \\
\partial_3 L &= -3\sigma(2 - \theta_3) + 3\sigma(\theta_3 - 3).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Separate fits.** Setting $\partial_1 L = 0$ gives $3\sigma(-\theta_1) = \sigma(\theta_1 - 1)$. With $u = e^{\theta_1}$,
$\sigma(-\theta_1) = 1/(1 + u)$ and $\sigma(\theta_1 - 1) = u/(u + e)$, so $3(u + e) = u(1 + u)$, which is
$u^2 - 2u - 3e = 0$. The positive root is $u = 1 + \sqrt{1 + 3e} = 4.026$, so $\theta_1 = \log 4.026 = 1.393$. The
reflection $s \mapsto 2 - s$ swaps classes 1 and 3 and maps the problem for $\theta_2$ onto the problem for $\theta_1$,
so $\theta_2 = 2 - 1.393 = 0.607$. Classes 3 and 4 have equal counts, so $\theta_3 = 2.5$, their midpoint. The first
two thresholds cross by $0.785$. Class 1 outnumbers the single class-2 example three to one, so it pulls $\theta_1$
past $s = 1$; class 3 pulls $\theta_2$ below $s = 1$ in the same way. The loss is $5.993$.

**What the crossing breaks.** Read as a cumulative logit model, the crossed thresholds give class 2 the probability

$$
P(y = 2 \mid s) = \sigma(\theta_2 - s) - \sigma(\theta_1 - s) = \sigma(0.607 - s) - \sigma(1.393 - s),
$$

which is negative at every score, because $\theta_2 < \theta_1$ and $\sigma$ is increasing. At $s = 1$ it is
$\sigma(-0.393) - \sigma(0.393) = 0.403 - 0.597 = -0.194$, its minimum. The binary decisions contradict each other too.
For $0.607 < s < 1.393$, threshold 1 answers "$y \le 1$" and threshold 2 answers "$y \ge 3$". Counting the thresholds
below the score still assigns the class-2 example to class 2, but that count hides two incompatible answers.

**KKT.** The constraints are $g_1 = \theta_1 - \theta_2 \le 0$ and $g_2 = \theta_2 - \theta_3 \le 0$. The separate fit
violates $g_1$, so constraint 1 must be active. Try the active set $\set{1}$: $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = t$ and $\mu_2 = 0$.
Stationarity is $\partial_1 L(t) + \mu_1 = 0$, $\partial_2 L(t) - \mu_1 = 0$ and $\partial_3 L(\theta_3) = 0$.

- Adding the first two eliminates $\mu_1$: $-3\sigma(-t) + \sigma(t - 1) - \sigma(1 - t) + 3\sigma(t - 2) = 0$. The
  left side is increasing in $t$, and $t = 1$ makes it zero, since $\sigma(0) - \sigma(0) = 0$ and
  $3\sigma(-1) = 3\sigma(1 - 2)$. So $t = 1$.
- The multiplier is $\mu_1 = -\partial_1 L(1) = 3\sigma(-1) - \sigma(0) = 3(0.2689) - 0.5 = 0.307 \ge 0$.
- The third equation gives $\theta_3 = 2.5$ as before. Constraint 2 holds with slack, $1 < 2.5$, and the closed form
  $\mu_2 = -(\partial_1 L + \partial_2 L) = 0$ agrees with $\mu_2 = 0$.

Every condition holds and the loss is convex, so this is the constrained optimum: $\thetavec = (1, 1, 2.5)$ with
$\muvec = (0.307, 0)$. The merged cut point sits on the class-2 example, and the model never predicts class 2. The
loss is $2\big(3\log(1 + e^{-1}) + \log 2\big) + 6\log(1 + e^{-0.5}) = 3.266 + 2.845 = 6.110$, which is $0.117$ above
the crossed fit. By the sensitivity result, requiring a gap $\theta_2 - \theta_1 = \epsilon$ raises the optimal loss at
rate $0.307$ per unit of $\epsilon$ near $\epsilon = 0$. Complementary slackness holds in both forms: constraint 1 is
active with $\mu_1 > 0$, and constraint 2 is inactive with $\mu_2 = 0$.

**Two examples in class 2.** Stationarity for $\theta_1$ becomes $3\sigma(-\theta_1) = 2\sigma(\theta_1 - 1)$, which
is $2u^2 - u - 3e = 0$ with root $u = (1 + \sqrt{1 + 24e})/4 = 2.284$. So $\theta_1 = 0.826$ and, by the reflection,
$\theta_2 = 1.174$. The separate fits are ordered, both constraints are inactive, and $\muvec = (0, 0)$.

**An empty class 2.** Remove the class-2 example. Then $\theta_1$ sees only class 1, through $3\log(1 + e^{-\theta_1})$,
which decreases in $\theta_1$. And $\theta_2$ sees only class 3, through $3\log(1 + e^{\theta_2 - 2})$, which increases
in $\theta_2$. Without the constraint there is no minimiser: $\theta_1 \to +\infty$ and $\theta_2 \to -\infty$. With the
constraint, the same case analysis gives $t = 1$ and $\mu_1 = 3\sigma(-1) = 0.807$. The multiplier grows as the class
between the merged thresholds shrinks: $0$ with two examples, $0.307$ with one, $0.807$ with none. The cumulative logit
fitted to classes 1 and 3 alone has the same two terms, so it has the same solution.

**The all-threshold loss on the same data.** Adding the terms that compare every class with every threshold gives the
all-threshold loss. Its unconstrained minimum is $(0.488, 1.081, 2.737)$, ordered without a constraint, as the
implicit-constraint result predicts.

### Implementation in PyTorch

The runnable example in the [Code tab](ordering-constraints-on-ordinal-thresholds/code) fits the worked example with
each method: 4000 gradient steps of size 0.2 from $\thetavec = (0.75, 1.5, 2.25)$, with $\wvec$ fixed. Four functions
carry the ideas. The model's class probabilities are differences of cumulative probabilities:

```python
def class_probabilities(s: Tensor, theta: Tensor) -> Tensor:
    """P(y = k | s) = σ(θ_k − s) − σ(θ_{k−1} − s), shape (N, K). A class between crossed thresholds goes negative."""
    cdf = torch.sigmoid(theta[None, :] - s[:, None])  # P(y ≤ k | s), shape (N, K − 1)
    zeros, ones = torch.zeros_like(cdf[:, :1]), torch.ones_like(cdf[:, :1])
    return torch.diff(torch.cat([zeros, cdf, ones], dim=1), dim=1)
```

`torch.diff` subtracts neighbouring cumulative probabilities. Nothing in it checks the order, so crossed thresholds
produce negative probabilities silently.

The reparameterised method optimises $\theta_1$ and increments $\delta_j$ instead of the thresholds:

```python
def ordered(theta1: Tensor, delta: Tensor) -> Tensor:
    """θ_1 followed by softplus increments: every value of (θ_1, δ) gives strictly ordered thresholds."""
    return torch.cat([theta1.reshape(1), theta1 + torch.cumsum(F.softplus(delta), dim=0)])
```

The projected method takes a plain gradient step on $\thetavec$ and then projects it onto the ordered cone:

```python
            for p, g in zip(params, grads, strict=True):
                p -= lr * g
            if method is Method.projected:
                theta.copy_(pool_adjacent_violators(theta))
```

```python
def pool_adjacent_violators(v: Tensor) -> Tensor:
    """Euclidean projection onto θ_1 ≤ … ≤ θ_{K−1}: replace each out-of-order run by its mean."""
    blocks: list[tuple[float, int]] = []  # (sum, count) of each pooled run
    for value in v.tolist():
        blocks.append((value, 1))
        while len(blocks) > 1 and blocks[-2][0] / blocks[-2][1] > blocks[-1][0] / blocks[-1][1]:
            total, count = blocks.pop()
            blocks[-1] = (blocks[-1][0] + total, blocks[-1][1] + count)
    return torch.tensor([total / count for total, count in blocks for _ in range(count)], dtype=v.dtype)
```

Each new value joins the list as a block of one. While the last two blocks are out of order, they are pooled into one
block whose value is their mean.

The Lagrangian method minimises the loss plus an augmented Lagrangian term, and updates the multipliers every 100
steps:

```python
def augmented_penalty(theta: Tensor, mu: Tensor, rho: float) -> Tensor:
    """The augmented Lagrangian term for g ≤ 0 with multipliers μ ≥ 0 and penalty weight ρ."""
    return (torch.clamp(mu + rho * constraints(theta), min=0) ** 2 - mu**2).sum() / (2 * rho)


def update_multipliers(theta: Tensor, mu: Tensor, rho: float) -> Tensor:
    """First-order multiplier update: μ grows while a constraint is violated and falls to 0 when it is slack."""
    return torch.clamp(mu + rho * constraints(theta), min=0)
```

The term is $\frac{1}{2\rho}\sum_k \big(\max(0, \mu_k + \rho g_k)^2 - \mu_k^2\big)$ with $\rho = 1$. Its gradient
is $\sum_k \max(0, \mu_k + \rho g_k)\nabla g_k$. At a minimiser of the loss plus the term, the gradient of the loss
therefore cancels $\sum_k \mu_k^{+}\nabla g_k$ with $\mu_k^{+} = \max(0, \mu_k + \rho g_k)$, which is the
stationarity condition with the updated multipliers. The update keeps $\mu_k^{+} \ge 0$. A slack constraint,
$g_k < 0$, drives its multiplier to zero; a violated one, $g_k > 0$, raises it. At a fixed point the multipliers stop
changing, which forces $g_k = 0$ wherever $\mu_k > 0$ (complementary slackness) and $g_k \le 0$ wherever
$\mu_k = 0$ (primal feasibility).

The unconstrained fit reaches the separate fits and their negative class-2 probability. The projected and Lagrangian

fit crosses $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$. It gives class 2 a negative probability at every input, and its binary

## Cost

The <Gloss name="kkt" /> view does not require a special solver. The constraints form a chain, and the cheapest exact
method is projected gradient descent:

$$
\thetavec \leftarrow \Pi\big(\thetavec - \eta\nabla L(\thetavec)\big),
$$

where $\Pi$ is the Euclidean projection onto the ordered cone and $\eta > 0$ is a step size. That projection is
isotonic regression of the vector $\thetavec - \eta\nabla L$ with unit weights. The pool adjacent violators algorithm
computes it in $O(K)$ time by replacing each run of out-of-order values with its mean <Cite id="ayer1955" />. Pool
adjacent violators is itself an active-set method for the <Gloss name="kkt" /> conditions of that projection: a pooled
run is a set of active constraints <Cite id="best1990" />. The same algorithm is used for
<NoteLink to="isotonic-calibration" />.

A point is a fixed point of projected gradient descent exactly when it satisfies the <Gloss name="kkt" /> conditions.
The projection $\Pi(\zvec)$ of $\zvec$ is the point $\thetavec$ with $\zvec - \thetavec = \sum_k \nu_k \nabla g_k$ for
some $\nu_k \ge 0$ that vanish on inactive constraints. At a fixed point $\thetavec = \Pi(\thetavec - \eta\nabla L)$,
so $-\eta\nabla L = \sum_k \nu_k \nabla g_k$, which is stationarity with $\mu_k = \nu_k / \eta$. Each projection step
therefore also yields multiplier estimates.

The cost of the thresholds is small next to the cost of $\wvec$. A step on $\thetavec$ costs $O(N + K)$ for $N$
examples. The hinge-loss versions are <NoteLink to="quadratic-programming">quadratic programmes</NoteLink>, in which
the ordering constraints are $K - 2$ further linear inequalities.

## Trade-offs

Several other methods keep the thresholds in order. They differ in whether they solve the constrained problem exactly,
whether they can represent a tie, and whether they report a multiplier.

- **Reparameterisation.** Set $\theta_k = \theta_1 + \sum_{j<k} e^{\delta_j}$, or use softplus increments
  $\log(1 + e^{\delta_j})$. The problem becomes unconstrained in $(\theta_1, \deltavec)$ and every iterate is strictly
  ordered, so a gradient step can never cross. A tie needs $\delta_j \to -\infty$, and the gradient
  $\partial L/\partial\delta_j = e^{\delta_j}\sum_{k > j}\partial_k L$ vanishes along the way, so descent towards a tie
  slows. Reparameterisation cannot represent a merged pair and gives no multiplier. It suits the cumulative logit with
  every class observed, where the optimum is strictly ordered anyway, and neural output layers trained by stochastic
  <NoteLink to="gradient">gradients</NoteLink>.
- **Projection after each step.** <NoteLink to="proximal-gradient-methods">Projected gradient descent</NoteLink>, above,
  converges to the exact constrained optimum and can reach a tie. Projecting only the unconstrained optimum once is
  not the same. With six class-3 examples at $s = 2$ in the worked example, the separate fits give
  $(\theta_1, \theta_2) = (1.393, 0.019)$, their projection is the mean $0.706$, and the constrained optimum is
  $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 0.635$. The mean weights both thresholds equally; the optimum weights
  them by the curvature of the loss.
- **Penalty or barrier.** A quadratic penalty $\tfrac{\rho}{2}\max(0, \theta_k - \theta_{k+1})^2$ leaves a violation
  of about $\mu_k/\rho$: in the worked example, $\rho = 100$ gives $\theta_1 - \theta_2 = 0.0031$, and
  $\rho(\theta_1 - \theta_2) = 0.306$ estimates $\mu_1 = 0.307$. A log barrier $-\tfrac1t\log(\theta_{k+1} - \theta_k)$
  keeps a gap of about $1/(t\mu_k)$: $t = 100$ gives a gap of $0.031$. Both approach the constrained optimum only as
  $\rho$ or $t$ grows. See <NoteLink to="penalty-and-barrier-methods" />.
- **Sorting.** Sorting the fitted thresholds, or rearranging crossing cumulative probability curves, guarantees an
  order <Cite id="chernozhukov2010" />. For losses tied to specific thresholds it changes which threshold is which. In
  the worked example, sorting $(1.393, 0.607, 2.5)$ into $(0.607, 1.393, 2.5)$ gives loss $6.486$, above both the
  crossed fit, $5.993$, and the constrained optimum, $6.110$.

The <Gloss name="kkt" /> formulation is the one that describes the constrained optimum exactly. It says which classes
the data merge, it prices each merge through $\mu_k$, and its multipliers come from projected descent at no extra
cost. Reparameterisation is simpler to implement when no tie is expected.
