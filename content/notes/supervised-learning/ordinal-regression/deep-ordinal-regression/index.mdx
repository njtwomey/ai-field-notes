<Definition>
Deep <NoteLink to="ordinal-regression">ordinal regression</NoteLink> trains a neural network $\hvec = g(\xvec)$ with an output layer and loss that respect the order
of the labels $1 < \dots < K$. Three designs dominate. CORAL (consistent rank logits) outputs $q_k = \sigma(\wvec^\top\hvec + \beta_k)$ for
$k = 1, \dots, K - 1$ with one shared $\wvec$ and trains each unit on the target $\indicator[y > k]$
<Cite id="cao2020coral" />. CORN (conditional ordinal regression for neural networks) outputs conditional probabilities $P(y > k \mid y > k - 1)$ and multiplies them
<Cite id="shi2023corn" />. SORD (soft ordinal labels) keeps a softmax output and trains it against a soft target
$t_k \propto \exp(-\alpha\,\phi(k, y))$ that decays with a distance $\phi$ <Cite id="diaz2019sord" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

A network with a $K$-way softmax and <NoteLink to="cross-entropy-and-perplexity">cross-entropy loss</NoteLink> treats the classes as nominal. Cross-entropy against a one-hot
target is $-\log p_y$, which depends only on the probability of the true class. Predicting age 70 for a 20-year-old
costs the same as predicting 21 if both leave the same mass on 20. A single regression output with rounding keeps the
order, but assumes equal spacing between classes and gives no distribution. The designs below keep a distribution and
put the order into the output layer or the target.

## Binary heads: OR-CNN and CORAL

**Independent heads.** Niu et al. replace the softmax with $K - 1$ sigmoid units, unit $k$ trained with binary
cross-entropy on $\indicator[y > k]$, and predict $\hat y = 1 + \sum_k \indicator[q_k > \tfrac12]$
<Cite id="niu2016" />. This is the <NoteLink to="binary-decomposition-for-ordinal-regression">Frank and Hall
decomposition</NoteLink> with a shared backbone. Each unit has its own weight vector, so nothing stops $q_2 > q_1$ at
some input. The estimates of $P(y > k)$ are then not monotone in $k$, and the counting rule and the probabilities
disagree.

**Shared weights.** CORAL gives the $K - 1$ units one weight vector and separate biases:
$q_k(\xvec) = \sigma(\wvec^\top\hvec + \beta_k)$ <Cite id="cao2020coral" />. If $\beta_1 \ge \beta_2 \ge \dots \ge
\beta_{K-1}$, then for every input

$$
q_k - q_{k+1} = \sigma(\wvec^\top\hvec + \beta_k) - \sigma(\wvec^\top\hvec + \beta_{k+1}) \ge 0,
$$

because $\sigma$ is increasing. The differences $q_{k-1} - q_k$ are then valid class probabilities. Cao et al. prove
that the minimiser of the summed binary cross-entropy has ordered biases, so no constraint is needed during training
<Cite id="cao2020coral" />. The loss is exactly the logistic <Gloss name="all-threshold-loss">all-threshold loss</Gloss> of
<NoteLink to="threshold-losses-for-ordinal-regression" />, with $-\beta_k$ as the threshold and $\wvec^\top\hvec$ as the
score.

CORAL buys consistency with flexibility in the last layer: every split uses the same direction in feature space. A
deep backbone can learn a representation in which one direction suffices, which the linear models in the figure below
cannot.

## Conditional heads: CORN

CORN keeps $K - 1$ independent units but changes what each one estimates <Cite id="shi2023corn" />. Unit $k$ estimates
the <NoteLink to="conditional-probability">conditional probability</NoteLink> $c_k(\xvec) = P(y > k \mid y > k - 1, \xvec)$ and is trained only on the examples with
$y > k - 1$. The unconditional probabilities follow from the chain rule of probability:

$$
P(y > k \mid \xvec) = \prod_{j=1}^{k} c_j(\xvec).
$$

Each factor lies in $[0, 1]$, so multiplying by one more factor cannot increase the product: $P(y > k + 1) =
P(y > k)\, c_{k+1} \le P(y > k)$. The estimates are monotone by construction, whatever the weights. CORN is the
continuation-ratio decomposition of <NoteLink to="continuation-ratio-and-adjacent-category-models" /> with a separate
weight vector per level, which gives it the flexibility that CORAL gives up.

## Soft targets: SORD

SORD keeps the ordinary softmax and changes the target <Cite id="diaz2019sord" />. For true class $y$ and a distance
$\phi(k, y)$, such as $\abs{k - y}$ or $(k - y)^2$, the target is

$$
t_k = \frac{\exp(-\alpha\,\phi(k, y))}{\sum_{m=1}^{K} \exp(-\alpha\,\phi(m, y))},
$$

and the loss is the cross-entropy $-\sum_k t_k \log p_k$. Its <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> with respect to the logits is $\pvec - \tvec$,
the same form as for one-hot targets. The loss now charges a prediction by where its mass sits: mass on a class near
$y$ is partly rewarded, mass far from $y$ is not. As $\alpha \to \infty$ the target becomes one-hot; as $\alpha \to 0$
it becomes uniform.

<SoftLabels />

Uniform label smoothing also softens the target, but spreads the removed mass evenly over all classes. It regularises
confidence without encoding order.

## Unimodal outputs

A different approach constrains the predicted distribution to have one peak. Beckham and Pal let the network output a
single number $p = \sigma(\cdot) \in (0, 1)$ and set
$P(y = k) = \binom{K-1}{k-1} p^{k-1}(1 - p)^{K-k}$, a <NoteLink to="binomial-distribution">binomial distribution</NoteLink> over the classes, or use a Poisson form
with a learnt temperature <Cite id="beckham2017" />. The distribution is unimodal for every input, so the network
cannot put mass on classes 1 and 5 while skipping 3.

## Trade-offs

| Head               | Output units | Consistent $P(y > k)$ | Split directions | Loss                         |
| ------------------ | ------------ | --------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------- |
| softmax, one-hot   | $K$          | not defined           | free             | cross-entropy                |
| independent (OR-CNN) | $K - 1$    | no                    | free             | $K - 1$ binary cross-entropies |
| CORAL              | $K - 1$      | yes, at the optimum   | shared           | $K - 1$ binary cross-entropies |
| CORN               | $K - 1$      | yes, always           | free             | conditional binary cross-entropies |
| SORD               | $K$          | not defined           | free             | cross-entropy to a soft target |
| binomial unimodal  | 1            | yes                   | one score        | negative log-likelihood       |

- **Prediction rule.** For binary heads, counting $q_k > \tfrac12$ and taking the median of the implied distribution
  agree when the heads are consistent. For softmax heads, the median minimises expected absolute error and the argmax
  minimises the 0–1 error.
- **Imbalance.** The outer binary tasks are imbalanced. Niu et al. and Cao et al. allow per-task weights in the loss.
