<Definition>
A structural support vector machine predicts $\hat{\yvec} = \argmax_{\yvec} \lambdavec^\top \Phib(\xvec, \yvec)$ for a
joint feature map $\Phib$ of the input and the whole output. Given a loss $\Delta(\yvec, \yvec') \ge 0$ between outputs,
it trains the weights by

$$
\min_{\lambdavec}\ \frac{1}{2}\norm{\lambdavec}^2 + \frac{C}{M} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \max_{\yvec} \Big(
\Delta(\yvec_m, \yvec) + \lambdavec^\top\Phib(\xvec_m, \yvec) - \lambdavec^\top\Phib(\xvec_m, \yvec_m) \Big),
$$

which requires the true output to outscore every other output by at least its loss <Cite id="tsochantaridis2005" />.
</Definition>

## The joint feature map

For sequences, use the same feature functions as the <NoteLink to="conditional-random-field">conditional random
field</NoteLink> and sum them along the chain:

$$
\Phib(\xvec, \yvec) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \fvec(y_{n-1}, y_n, \xvec, n).
$$

The score $\lambdavec^\top \Phib(\xvec, \yvec)$ is then exactly the log of the CRF's unnormalised weight for $\yvec$,
$\sum_n \log\psivec_n(y_n) + \sum_n \log\Psimat_n(y_n, y_{n+1})$. The structural SVM and the CRF share the model and the
decoder; they differ only in the training loss. Prediction is the Viterbi path.

## The margin constraints

Written with slack variables $\xi_m \ge 0$, the objective asks, for every training sequence $m$ and every other output
$\yvec$,

$$
\lambdavec^\top\Phib(\xvec_m, \yvec_m) - \lambdavec^\top\Phib(\xvec_m, \yvec) \ \ge\ \Delta(\yvec_m, \yvec) - \xi_m.
$$

This is **margin rescaling**: a worse output must be beaten by a wider margin. The alternative, **slack rescaling**,
keeps the margin at 1 and scales the slack instead, $\ \ge 1 - \xi_m / \Delta(\yvec_m, \yvec)$. Slack rescaling ignores
outputs whose score is already far below, but its most violated constraint is harder to find. Margin rescaling is the
more common choice.

There is one constraint per possible output, $\abs{\Ycal}^N$ of them per sequence. The smallest slack that satisfies all
of them is the largest violation,

$$
\xi_m = \max_{\yvec}\Big(\Delta(\yvec_m, \yvec) + \lambdavec^\top\Phib(\xvec_m, \yvec)\Big) - \lambdavec^\top\Phib(\xvec_m,
\yvec_m),
$$

which is the structured hinge loss in the definition. Evaluating it is a single maximisation over outputs.

## Loss-augmented inference

The maximisation is tractable when the loss decomposes over the chain. The Hamming loss counts wrong labels,
$\Delta(\yvec_m, \yvec) = \sum_n \indicator[y_n \neq y_{m,n}]$. Adding it to the score gives

$$
\Delta(\yvec_m, \yvec) + \lambdavec^\top\Phib(\xvec_m, \yvec) = \sum_n \big( \log\psivec_n(y_n) + \indicator[y_n \neq
y_{m,n}] \big) + \sum_n \log\Psimat_n(y_n, y_{n+1}).
$$

This is a chain score with modified node potentials $\psivec'_n(y) = \psivec_n(y)\, e^{\indicator[y \neq y_{m,n}]}$: every
wrong label gets a bonus of 1 in log space. Running Viterbi on $\psivec'_n$ finds the most violated constraint in
$O(N\abs{\Ycal}^2)$. Any loss that is a sum over positions, or over neighbouring pairs, works the same way.

### Worked example

Use the three-position chain of the conditional random field note: $\psivec_1 = (2, 1)$, $\psivec_2 = (1, 1)$,
$\psivec_3 = (1, 3)$ and $\Psimat = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$, read as $e^{\text{score}}$. Suppose
the true labels are $\yvec_m = (1, 1, 1)$.

- **Score of the truth.** $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 = 18$, so $\lambdavec^\top\Phib(\xvec_m, \yvec_m) = \log
  18 = 2.890$.
- **Loss-augmented maximiser.** Enumerating all eight sequences, $\log(\text{weight}) + \Delta$ is largest for $(2, 2,
  2)$: $\log 27 + 3 = 6.296$. Viterbi on $\psivec'_1 = (2, e)$, $\psivec'_2 = (1, e)$, $\psivec'_3 = (1, 3e)$ finds the
  same sequence.
- **Hinge loss.** $6.296 - 2.890 = 3.405$. The truth is not even the highest-scoring sequence, since $(2, 2, 2)$ scores
  $\log 27$, so the loss is large.

## Training

**Cutting planes.** Keep a small working set of constraints. Repeatedly solve the quadratic programme on the working
set, then run loss-augmented inference on each training sequence and add its most violated constraint if it is violated
by more than a tolerance $\varepsilon$. The number of iterations is polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$ and independent of the
exponential number of outputs <Cite id="tsochantaridis2005" />.

**Subgradient descent.** The objective is convex but not differentiable. With $\hat{\yvec}_m$ the loss-augmented
maximiser, a subgradient is

$$
\lambdavec - \frac{C}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M} \Big( \Phib(\xvec_m, \yvec_m) - \Phib(\xvec_m, \hat{\yvec}_m) \Big),
$$

so each stochastic step pushes the weights toward the true output's features and away from those of the most violating
one.

**Max-margin Markov networks.** Taskar, Guestrin and Koller formulated the same margin-rescaled objective with the
Hamming loss for Markov networks and solved it through a dual that factorises over the graph's cliques, so that it needs
marginals rather than whole outputs <Cite id="taskar2003" />.

## Relation to the CRF and the perceptron

All three train the same score $s(\yvec) = \lambdavec^\top\Phib(\xvec, \yvec)$ and differ in the per-sequence loss:

| Model | Loss for one sequence |
| --- | --- |
| CRF (log loss) | $\log\sum_{\yvec} e^{s(\yvec)} - s(\yvec_m)$ |
| Structural SVM (hinge) | $\max_{\yvec}\big(s(\yvec) + \Delta(\yvec_m, \yvec)\big) - s(\yvec_m)$ |
| Softmax-margin | $\log\sum_{\yvec} e^{s(\yvec) + \Delta(\yvec_m, \yvec)} - s(\yvec_m)$ |
| Structured perceptron | $\max_{\yvec} s(\yvec) - s(\yvec_m)$ |

The log-sum-exp is a smooth maximum, so the CRF's loss is a smoothed perceptron loss, and softmax-margin is a smoothed
hinge. Softmax-margin upper-bounds both the hinge, because a log-sum-exp is at least its largest term, and the log loss,
because $\Delta \ge 0$ <Cite id="gimpel2010" />. In the worked example the log loss is $\log(92/18) = 1.631$, the hinge
3.405 and the softmax-margin 3.794.

The structured perceptron updates $\lambdavec \leftarrow \lambdavec + \Phib(\xvec_m, \yvec_m) - \Phib(\xvec_m,
\hat{\yvec}_m)$ with $\hat{\yvec}_m$ the ordinary Viterbi path <Cite id="collins2002" />. It is the subgradient step of
the hinge with no loss augmentation and no regularisation. Averaging the weights over all updates makes it competitive
with the regularised methods at a fraction of the cost.

## Pitfalls

- **No probabilities.** A structural SVM ranks outputs but gives no calibrated $P(\yvec \mid \xvec)$ or marginals. Use a
  CRF when downstream decisions need probabilities.
- **The loss must decompose.** Loss-augmented inference is as cheap as decoding only when $\Delta$ splits over the
  factors of the model. A loss such as F1 over a whole sequence needs a more expensive search.
- **The margin scale is set by $\Delta$.** Rescaling the loss changes the solution, and $C$ must be tuned together with it.
