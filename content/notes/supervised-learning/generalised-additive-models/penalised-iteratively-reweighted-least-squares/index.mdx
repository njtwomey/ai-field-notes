<Definition>
**Penalised iteratively reweighted least squares** (PIRLS) computes the coefficients of a generalised additive model
with a non-Gaussian response for fixed <NoteLink to="additive-model-smoothing-parameter-selection">smoothing parameters</NoteLink>. From the current linear predictor $\etavec =
\Xmat\betavec$ it forms a working response $\zvec$ and working weights $\Wmat$, then sets the next $\betavec$ to the
penalised weighted least-squares fit
$(\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat + \Smat_\lambda)^{-1}\Xmat^\top\Wmat\zvec$. It repeats until the penalised deviance stops
changing <Cite id="wood2017" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

A GAM with fixed bases has model matrix $\Xmat$ ($n \times p$), coefficients $\betavec$ and total penalty
$\Smat_\lambda = \sum_j\lambda_j\Smat_j$ (see <NoteLink to="generalised-additive-model">generalised additive
model</NoteLink>). The response $y_i$ has an exponential-dispersion density with mean $\mu_i$, variance
$\phi V(\mu_i)$ and link $g(\mu_i) = \eta_i = \xvec_i^\top\betavec$. The coefficients maximise the penalised
log-likelihood $\ell(\betavec) - \betavec^\top\Smat_\lambda\betavec/(2\phi)$. Multiplying by $-2\phi$ and adding a
constant, they minimise the **penalised deviance**

$$
\Pcal(\betavec) = D(\betavec) + \betavec^\top\Smat_\lambda\betavec, \qquad D(\betavec) = 2\phi\brack{\ell_{\text{sat}}
- \ell(\betavec)},
$$

where $\ell_{\text{sat}}$ is the log-likelihood of the saturated model with $\mu_i = y_i$. Only the Gaussian family
with the identity link has a closed-form minimiser. <NoteLink to="generalised-additive-models-in-practice">pyGAM</NoteLink>'s model classes fix the family and link
<Cite id="serven2018" />:

| Class          | Family           | Link     | $V(\mu)$      | Weight $w_i$         | Working response $z_i$                  |
| -------------- | ---------------- | -------- | ------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| `LinearGAM`    | Gaussian         | identity | $1$           | $1$                  | $y_i$                                   |
| `LogisticGAM`  | binomial         | logit    | $\mu(1 - \mu)$| $\mu_i(1 - \mu_i)$   | $\eta_i + (y_i - \mu_i)/(\mu_i(1 - \mu_i))$ |
| `PoissonGAM`   | Poisson          | log      | $\mu$         | $\mu_i$              | $\eta_i + (y_i - \mu_i)/\mu_i$          |
| `GammaGAM`     | gamma            | log      | $\mu^2$       | $1$                  | $\eta_i + (y_i - \mu_i)/\mu_i$          |
| `InvGaussGAM`  | inverse Gaussian | log      | $\mu^3$       | $1/\mu_i$            | $\eta_i + (y_i - \mu_i)/\mu_i$          |

The weights and working responses follow from the general formulas below. The class `GAM(distribution=...,
link=...)` accepts other combinations.

## Mechanism

**The <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink>.** Write $\ell_i = (y_i\theta_i - b(\theta_i))/\phi + c(y_i, \phi)$ with $\mu_i = b'(\theta_i)$ and
$V(\mu_i) = b''(\theta_i)$. By the <NoteLink to="derivative">chain rule</NoteLink> through $\theta_i$, $\mu_i$ and $\eta_i$,

$$
\frac{\partial\ell_i}{\partial\betavec} = \frac{y_i - \mu_i}{\phi}\,\frac{d\theta_i}{d\mu_i}\,\frac{d\mu_i}{d\eta_i}\,
\xvec_i = \frac{y_i - \mu_i}{\phi V(\mu_i)}\,\frac{d\mu_i}{d\eta_i}\,\xvec_i,
$$

using $d\theta/d\mu = 1/b''(\theta) = 1/V(\mu)$. Define the working weight and working response

$$
w_i = \frac{(d\mu_i/d\eta_i)^2}{V(\mu_i)}, \qquad z_i = \eta_i + (y_i - \mu_i)\frac{d\eta_i}{d\mu_i}.
$$

Then $(y_i - \mu_i)(d\mu_i/d\eta_i)/V(\mu_i) = w_i(z_i - \eta_i)$, and the gradients of the log-likelihood and of the
penalised deviance are

$$
\nabla\ell = \frac1\phi\Xmat^\top\Wmat(\zvec - \etavec), \qquad \nabla\Pcal = -2\Xmat^\top\Wmat(\zvec - \etavec) +
2\Smat_\lambda\betavec,
$$

with $\Wmat = \diag(w_1, \dots, w_n)$.

**The curvature.** The expected Hessian of $-\ell$ is the <Gloss name="fisher-information">Fisher information</Gloss> $\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat/\phi$, derived in
<NoteLink to="iteratively-reweighted-least-squares">IRLS</NoteLink>. The penalty is quadratic with Hessian
$2\Smat_\lambda$. So the expected Hessian of $\Pcal$ is $2(\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat + \Smat_\lambda)$.

**The step.** Fisher scoring moves by the expected Hessian's inverse times the negative gradient:

$$
\betavec^+ = \betavec + \paren{\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat + \Smat_\lambda}^{-1}\brack{\Xmat^\top\Wmat(\zvec - \etavec) -
\Smat_\lambda\betavec}.
$$

Multiply out, using $\etavec = \Xmat\betavec$. The terms in $\betavec$ inside the bracket and outside it combine to
$(\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat + \Smat_\lambda)\betavec - \Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat\betavec - \Smat_\lambda\betavec = \zeros$, which
leaves

$$
\betavec^+ = \paren{\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat + \Smat_\lambda}^{-1}\Xmat^\top\Wmat\zvec.
$$

This is the minimiser of $\norm{\Wmat^{1/2}(\zvec - \Xmat\betavec)}^2 + \betavec^\top\Smat_\lambda\betavec$: a
Gaussian penalised fit to the working data $\zvec$ with weights $\Wmat$. Every non-Gaussian GAM is fitted by a
sequence of Gaussian ones, and the penalty enters each exactly as it does for Gaussian data.

**<NoteLink to="generalised-linear-model">Canonical links</NoteLink>.** With the canonical link, $\theta_i = \eta_i$ and the Hessian of $\ell$ does not involve $y$, so
the observed and expected information are equal. PIRLS is then <NoteLink to="newtons-method">Newton's method</NoteLink> and converges quadratically near the
optimum. For a non-canonical link, such as the log link of `GammaGAM`, Fisher scoring converges linearly. mgcv uses full
Newton steps instead by replacing $w_i$ with $\alpha_iw_i$ and $z_i - \eta_i$ with $(z_i - \eta_i)/\alpha_i$, where
$\alpha_i = 1 + (y_i - \mu_i)\paren{V'(\mu_i)/V(\mu_i) + g''(\mu_i)/g'(\mu_i)}$ <Cite id="wood2017" />.

**Algorithm.**

1. Start from $\mu_i^{(0)} = y_i$, moved away from the boundary of the mean space (for binary data, $(y_i + 0.5)/2$),
   and $\eta_i^{(0)} = g(\mu_i^{(0)})$.
2. Compute $w_i$ and $z_i$ at the current $\etavec$.
3. Solve the penalised weighted least-squares problem for $\betavec^+$.
4. If $\Pcal(\betavec^+) > \Pcal(\betavec)$, or some $\mu_i$ is invalid, replace $\betavec^+$ by $(\betavec +
   \betavec^+)/2$ and check again (**step halving**).
5. Stop when $\abs{\Pcal(\betavec^+) - \Pcal(\betavec)} < \epsilon\,\abs{\Pcal(\betavec^+)}$; otherwise go to 2.

**Why step halving works.** The step direction is $\deltavec = -\Hmat^{-1}\nabla\Pcal$ with $\Hmat = 2(\Xmat^\top\Wmat
\Xmat + \Smat_\lambda)$ positive definite. The directional derivative of $\Pcal$ along it is $\nabla\Pcal^\top\deltavec =
-\nabla\Pcal^\top\Hmat^{-1}\nabla\Pcal < 0$ away from the optimum. So $\Pcal(\betavec + t\deltavec) < \Pcal(\betavec)$
for all small enough $t > 0$, and halving $t$ finds such a step after finitely many tries.

**Convexity.** With a canonical link, $-\ell$ is convex in $\betavec$ because its Hessian
$\Xmat^\top\diag(V(\mu_i))\Xmat/\phi$ is <Gloss name="positive-semi-definite">positive semi-definite</Gloss>. Adding the positive semi-definite penalty keeps
$\Pcal$ convex. If $\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat + \Smat_\lambda$ is positive definite, $\Pcal$ is strictly convex, and PIRLS
with step halving converges to its unique minimiser from any start.

**A stable solve.** Forming $\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat$ squares the <NoteLink to="condition-number">condition number</NoteLink> of $\Wmat^{1/2}\Xmat$. Any $\Emat$
with $\Emat^\top\Emat = \Smat_\lambda$ turns the penalised problem into <Gloss name="ols">ordinary least squares</Gloss> on augmented data,

$$
\norm{\begin{pmatrix}\Wmat^{1/2}\zvec \\ \zeros\end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix}\Wmat^{1/2}\Xmat \\
\Emat\end{pmatrix}\betavec}^2 = \norm{\Wmat^{1/2}(\zvec - \Xmat\betavec)}^2 + \betavec^\top\Smat_\lambda\betavec,
$$

which is solved by a <Gloss name="qr">QR decomposition</Gloss> of the stacked matrix. pyGAM takes the QR decomposition of
$\Wmat^{1/2}\Xmat = \Qmat\Rmat$ and a <Gloss name="svd">singular value decomposition</Gloss> of $\binom{\Rmat}{\Emat}$, following Wood
<Cite id="wood2017" />, and stops when the relative change in $\betavec$ falls below `tol=1e-4` or after `max_iter=100`
iterations. It does not halve steps <Cite id="serven2018" />.

## Worked example: a logistic GAM

Four hundred binary responses are drawn with $\pr(y = 1 \mid x) = \sigma(2\sin 2\pi x)$ for $x$ uniform on $[0, 1]$,
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function. pyGAM fits `LogisticGAM(s(0, n_splines=12, lam=1.0))`: 12 cubic <NoteLink to="b-splines">B-splines</NoteLink>
with a second-order difference penalty and $\lambda = 1$, plus an intercept. The code below takes pyGAM's model matrix
and penalty and runs PIRLS by hand, with $w_i = \mu_i(1 - \mu_i)$ and $z_i = \eta_i + (y_i - \mu_i)/w_i$ from the
table.

```python
import numpy as np
from pygam import LogisticGAM, s

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
n = 400
x = rng.uniform(0, 1, n)
y = rng.binomial(1, 1 / (1 + np.exp(-2 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * x))))

gam = LogisticGAM(s(0, n_splines=12, lam=1.0)).fit(x[:, None], y)
X = gam._modelmat(x[:, None]).toarray()  # 12 B-splines and an intercept column
S = gam._P().toarray() + np.sqrt(np.finfo(float).eps) * np.eye(13)  # λ DᵀD, plus pyGAM's tiny ridge


def deviance(mu):
    return -2 * np.sum(y * np.log(mu) + (1 - y) * np.log(1 - mu))


beta = np.zeros(13)
for it in range(1, 6):
    eta = X @ beta
    mu = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-eta))
    w = mu * (1 - mu)  # working weights
    z = eta + (y - mu) / w  # working response
    beta = np.linalg.solve(X.T @ (w[:, None] * X) + S, X.T @ (w * z))
    mu = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-X @ beta))
    print(it, round(deviance(mu) + beta @ S @ beta, 6), round(deviance(mu), 4))

w = mu * (1 - mu)
H = X.T @ (w[:, None] * X)
print("edf:", np.trace(np.linalg.solve(H + S, H)), "pyGAM:", gam.statistics_["edof"])
print("max |beta - pyGAM|:", np.abs(beta - gam.coef_).max())
print("sum(mu - y):", (mu - y).sum(), "sum(x (mu - y)):", (x * (mu - y)).sum())
```

Starting from $\betavec = \zeros$, so $\mu_i = 0.5$ and $w_i = 0.25$, the iterations give (pyGAM 0.12.0, NumPy 2.5):

| Iteration | Penalised deviance $\Pcal$ | Deviance $D$ |
| --------- | -------------------------- | ------------ |
| 1         | 393.496810                 | 390.1204     |
| 2         | 388.579130                 | 383.9775     |
| 3         | 388.520921                 | 383.7369     |
| 4         | 388.520905                 | 383.7340     |
| 5         | 388.520905                 | 383.7340     |

The decrease in $\Pcal$ is $4.9$, then $0.058$, then $1.6 \times 10^{-5}$, then below $10^{-6}$: once close, the error
is roughly squared at each step, as for Newton's method with the canonical logit link. At convergence:

- the <NoteLink to="effective-degrees-of-freedom">effective degrees of freedom</NoteLink> $\trace\paren{(\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat + \Smat)^{-1}\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat}$ are 5.4969,
  and pyGAM reports 5.4969;
- the largest difference from pyGAM's coefficients is $7.8 \times 10^{-8}$;
- $\sum_i(\hat\mu_i - y_i) = -3.8 \times 10^{-9}$ and $\sum_i x_i(\hat\mu_i - y_i) = 3.3 \times 10^{-8}$. The
  difference penalty leaves constants and lines unpenalised, so the fit reproduces the number of events and their
  first moment in $x$ (the conservation of moments of <NoteLink to="p-splines">P-splines</NoteLink>), up to the tiny
  ridge.

The penalised deviance of 388.52 exceeds the deviance of 383.73 by $\hat\betavec^\top\Smat\hat\betavec = 4.79$, the
price of smoothness at $\lambda = 1$.

## Separation

In unpenalised <NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink>, if some direction $\vvec$ has $\xvec_i^\top\vvec > 0$ whenever $y_i = 1$ and
$\xvec_i^\top\vvec < 0$ whenever $y_i = 0$, then $D(c\vvec) \to 0$ as $c \to \infty$. The deviance has no minimiser
and the coefficients diverge. A flexible smooth separates binary data much more easily than a line does.

The penalty changes this. Along the ray $c\vvec$ the penalised deviance is $D(c\vvec) + c^2\,\vvec^\top\Smat_\lambda
\vvec$.

- If $\vvec^\top\Smat_\lambda\vvec > 0$, the second term grows without bound, so $\Pcal$ cannot decrease forever along
  $\vvec$. The minimiser is finite.
- If $\vvec$ lies in the null space of $\Smat_\lambda$, the penalty is zero along the whole ray and $\Pcal(c\vvec) \to
  0$. The minimiser does not exist, whatever $\lambda$ is.

With a second-derivative or second-difference penalty, the null space holds the straight lines. So a logistic GAM
still diverges when a threshold on one feature separates the classes. Two checks with 200 points and 12 B-splines at
$\lambda = 1$:

- $y = 1$ exactly when $x > 0.5$. A line separates the data. After 10 iterations $\max_i\abs{\hat\eta_i} = 129$, the
  penalty is 0 and the deviance is 1.5, still falling. pyGAM stops at its iteration limit and prints "did not
  converge".
- $y = 1$ exactly when $\abs{x - 0.5} < 0.2$. No line separates the data, only a bump does, and every bump has
  curvature. PIRLS converges with $\max_i\abs{\hat\eta_i} = 12$, penalty 19.4 and deviance 34.7.

Remedies are a penalty on the null space as well (the double penalty of
<NoteLink to="additive-model-checking-and-term-selection">term selection</NoteLink>), a small ridge penalty on all
coefficients, or removing the term that separates the data and reporting the separation.

## Cost

Each iteration forms $\Wmat^{1/2}\Xmat$ and decomposes it in $O(np^2)$ time and solves a $p \times p$ system in
$O(p^3)$. For a GAM with 5 smooths of 10 to 20 <NoteLink to="basis-expansions">basis functions</NoteLink>, $p$ is below 100 and an iteration is fast even for
$n = 10^5$. PIRLS usually converges in 4 to 10 iterations. The smoothing parameters are chosen in an outer loop, and
every trial value of $\lambda$ needs a full PIRLS fit, warm-started from the previous one. mgcv nests PIRLS inside a
Newton optimisation of the <Gloss name="reml" /> or Laplace-approximate <NoteLink to="law-of-total-probability">marginal likelihood</NoteLink> over $\log\lambda$ <Cite id="wood2011" />;
pyGAM refits on a grid of $\lambda$ values.

## Trade-offs

- **Fisher scoring or Newton.** Fisher weights are always positive, so every step is a well-posed least-squares
  problem. Full Newton weights $\alpha_iw_i$ converge faster for non-canonical links but can be negative, and then
  need extra care <Cite id="wood2017" />.
- **Step halving.** It guarantees that the penalised deviance falls at every accepted step. Implementations without it,
  such as pyGAM, can oscillate on hard problems.
- **By-products.** At convergence, $(\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat + \Smat_\lambda)^{-1}\phi$ is the Bayesian posterior
  covariance of $\betavec$, and the trace of $(\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat + \Smat_\lambda)^{-1}\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat$ gives
  the effective degrees of freedom; see <NoteLink to="additive-model-inference-and-effective-degrees-of-freedom">GAM
  inference</NoteLink>.
- **Backfitting.** Local scoring wraps backfitting inside the same IRLS loop and solves each penalised
  weighted least-squares problem one term at a time <Cite id="hastie1990" />. Solving for all coefficients at once, as
  here, converges in fewer iterations when features are correlated and gives the covariance matrix directly.
