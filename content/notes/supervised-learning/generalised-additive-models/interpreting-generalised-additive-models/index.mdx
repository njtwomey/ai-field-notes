<Definition>
In a <NoteLink to="generalised-additive-model">generalised additive model</NoteLink> $g(\mu) = \alpha + \sum_j
f_j(x_j)$, the **partial effect** of feature $x_j$ is the fitted smooth $\hat f_j$: the change in the linear predictor
$g(\mu)$ as $x_j$ varies with the other features fixed. Each $\hat f_j$ is centred to mean zero over the data, so it
shows effects relative to an average observation. Software that does not centre, such as pyGAM, shows each smooth
up to an arbitrary constant. **Concurvity** is the nonlinear analogue of collinearity: one
smooth term can be closely approximated by a combination of the others <Cite id="hastie1990" />.
</Definition>

## Reading a smooth term

A plot of $\hat f_j(x_j)$ against $x_j$ shows the shape of the effect: where it rises, where it levels off, where it
turns. The vertical axis is on the link scale.

- With the identity link, a change of 1 in $\hat f_j$ is a change of 1 in the predicted mean.
- With a log link, it multiplies the predicted mean by $e$; a difference $\hat f_j(b) - \hat f_j(a)$ is a log rate
  ratio between $x_j = b$ and $x_j = a$.
- With a logit link, the difference is a log odds ratio.

On the response scale the effects no longer add. With a log link they multiply, and with a logit link the effect of
$x_j$ on the probability depends on the other features.

Each term plot usually carries two more layers:

- **Partial residuals** $r_{ij} = \hat f_j(x_{ij}) + (z_i - \hat\eta_i)$, where $z_i$ is the IRLS working response and
  $\hat\eta_i$ the fitted linear predictor. For Gaussian data this is $y_i - \hat\alpha - \sum_{k \ne j}\hat
  f_k(x_{ik})$: the data with every other term removed. The smooth should run through their middle. A pattern the
  smooth misses points to too much smoothing or a missing interaction.
- **Credible bands.** Pointwise bands $\hat f_j(x) \pm 1.96\,\operatorname{se}(x)$ come from the Bayesian posterior
  covariance of the coefficients. They allow for smoothing bias, and their coverage averaged across the function is
  close to nominal. Their derivation and their limits are in
  <NoteLink to="additive-model-inference-and-effective-degrees-of-freedom">GAM inference</NoteLink>.

The **effective degrees of freedom** of each term summarise its complexity. A value near 1 means the term is close to a
straight line; a value near the number of basis functions means the basis may be too small.

## Partial dependence of an additive model

The partial dependence function of a fitted model $\hat F$ with respect to $x_j$ averages predictions over the observed
values of the other features:

$$
\operatorname{PD}_j(x) = \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \hat F(x, \xvec_{i,-j}).
$$

On the link scale an additive model gives

$$
\operatorname{PD}_j(x) = \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\brack{\hat\alpha + \hat f_j(x) + \sum_{k \ne j}\hat f_k(x_{ik})} =
\hat\alpha + \hat f_j(x),
$$

because each centred $\hat f_k$ averages to zero over the data. So the plotted smooth is the partial dependence, up to
the intercept. For black-box models the two differ, and the difference measures interaction. For a GAM there are no
interactions to hide, and the term plot shows everything the model knows about $x_j$ (on the link scale).

## Concurvity

The smooths are identified by the part of each feature's variation that the others cannot reproduce. Suppose $x_2$ is
nearly a smooth function of $x_1$. Then $f_1(x_1) + f_2(x_2)$ can be rewritten as $\tilde f_1(x_1) + \tilde f_2(x_2)$ in
many ways with almost the same fitted values. The data cannot tell these apart, so:

- the individual smooths have wide confidence bands, even when the overall fit is precise;
- small changes to the data or the smoothing parameters move signal from one term to the other;
- backfitting needs many sweeps, because each smooth absorbs part of the other's change at every step
  <Cite id="buja1989" />.

Concurvity is measured by how much of $\hat f_j$ lies in the space spanned by the other terms' bases. mgcv reports,
for each term, the ratio $\norm{\Pmat_{-j}\hat\fvec_j}^2/\norm{\hat\fvec_j}^2$ and worst-case versions of it, where
$\Pmat_{-j}$ projects onto the span of the other terms <Cite id="wood2017" />. Values near 1 signal trouble. The
remedies are the same as for collinearity: drop or combine terms, replace the pair by a two-dimensional smooth
$f(x_1, x_2)$, or accept that only the sum is well determined.

## Pitfalls

- **Extrapolated shape.** At the ends of each feature's range the bands widen and the curve is set by the penalty,
  not the data. A rug of the observed $x_j$ values under each plot shows where the data are.
- **Causal reading.** "With the other features fixed" describes the model. Changing $x_j$ in the world usually changes
  correlated features too.
- **Overreading wiggles.** A smooth with many effective degrees of freedom may be following noise. Check whether a
  simpler shape fits inside the confidence band.
