<Definition>
Three kinds of <Gloss name="gam" /> term extend the basic smooth $f_j(x_j)$ <Cite id="wood2017" />:

- A **cyclic smooth** of a covariate on a period $[a, b)$ satisfies $f(a) = f(b)$, $f'(a) = f'(b)$ and $f''(a) = f''(b)$,
  so that it joins itself smoothly when the covariate wraps around.
- A **factor term** for a categorical covariate $g$ with levels $1, \dots, L$ adds a coefficient $\beta_g$ per level,
  usually with the ridge penalty $\lambda\sum_g\beta_g^2$.
- A **by-variable** $z$ multiplies a smooth, giving the term $f(x)\,z$. With a factor $g$ as the by-variable, the model
  has one smooth per level, $f_g(x)$.
</Definition>

## Cyclic smooths

Time of day, day of week, day of year, wind direction and phase angle are periodic: 23:59 is next to 00:00. An ordinary
spline on $[0, 24)$ treats the two ends of the range as unrelated, so its fitted values at 23:59 and 00:00 can differ.
Each end also rests on data from one side only, which makes the fit there more variable. A cyclic smooth treats the
covariate as a point on a circle.

**The constraints.** A <NoteLink to="regression-splines">cubic spline</NoteLink> is twice continuously differentiable at every knot; its third <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> may jump
there. Extend $f$ periodically, $f(x + b - a) = f(x)$. The extension is a cubic spline on the whole line if and only if
the join at $b \equiv a$ behaves like any other knot: value, first and second derivative continuous. That is exactly
$f(a) = f(b)$, $f'(a) = f'(b)$ and $f''(a) = f''(b)$. A cubic spline with knots $a = t_1 < \dots < t_K = b$ has $K - 1$
cubic pieces with 4 coefficients each and $3(K - 2)$ continuity conditions at the interior knots, so $K + 2$ free
parameters. The three cyclic conditions leave $K - 1$, one per distinct knot on the circle. mgcv's `bs = "cc"` is this
cyclic cubic regression spline <Cite id="wood2017" />.

**A periodic <NoteLink to="b-splines">B-spline basis</NoteLink>.** With $K$ evenly spaced knots on the circle and spacing $h = (b - a)/K$, take the
cardinal cubic B-spline $M_3$ (support four knot intervals) and wrap it:

$$
B_k(x) = \sum_{m \in \integers}M_3\paren{\frac{x - a}{h} - k + mK}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, K - 1.
$$

Each $B_k$ is a shifted copy of one bump. Near the boundary the bump is split, with part at the start of the range and
part at the end. Every $B_k$ is a cubic spline on the circle, so every combination satisfies the three conditions
automatically. The $K$ functions sum to 1. This is mgcv's `bs = "cp"` and pyGAM's `basis='cp'`.

**A periodic penalty.** A <NoteLink to="p-splines">P-spline</NoteLink> penalises squared second differences of
neighbouring coefficients. On the circle, coefficient $K - 1$ neighbours coefficient 0, so the difference operator wraps:
$(\Dmat_c\betavec)_k = \beta_{k-1} - 2\beta_k + \beta_{k+1}$ with indices taken modulo $K$. $\Dmat_c$ is a circulant
matrix. Its eigenvectors are the discrete Fourier vectors $v_{m,k} = e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}mk/K}$, since

$$
(\Dmat_c v_m)_k = \paren{e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}m/K} - 2 + e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}m/K}}v_{m,k} = \paren{2\cos\frac{2\pi m}{K} -
2}v_{m,k}.
$$

The <NoteLink to="smoothing-penalties-and-curvature">penalty matrix</NoteLink> $\Dmat_c^\top\Dmat_c$ therefore has eigenvalues $\paren{2 - 2\cos(2\pi m/K)}^2$ for $m = 0, \dots,
K - 1$. Only $m = 0$ gives zero, so the null space is the constant vector alone. An ordinary second-difference penalty
also leaves linear trends unpenalised. A linear trend cannot wrap around a circle, so as $\lambda \to \infty$ the cyclic
fit tends to a constant, not a line. **Worked example.** For $K = 12$ the smallest non-zero eigenvalue is
$(2 - 2\cos(\pi/6))^2 = 0.0718$, the penalty on one full cycle per period, and the largest is $4^2 = 16$, for the
alternating pattern at $m = 6$.

**pyGAM's construction.** pyGAM rescales the covariate by the **edge knots**, which default to the minimum and maximum of
the data, and wraps it with $x \bmod 1$. It builds $K + 3$ ordinary B-splines on evenly spaced knots, folds the last
three columns onto the first three, and drops the last three <Cite id="serven2018" />. Its default penalty for
`basis='cp'` is a wrapped second-difference matrix built in `penalties.derivative(periodic=True)`.

## Factor terms

A categorical covariate $g$ with $L$ levels enters through indicator columns, $\Xmat_{ig} = 1$ if observation $i$ is at
level $g$. The term is $\beta_{g_i}$. pyGAM's `f(feature)` codes the levels as consecutive integers, builds the indicators
as degree-0 B-splines on unit-width bins, and applies the ridge penalty $\lambda\sum_g\beta_g^2$ by default
<Cite id="serven2018" />.

**Identifiability.** The $L$ indicator columns sum to the intercept column, so without a penalty the model is not
identifiable: adding $c$ to the intercept $\alpha$ and subtracting $c$ from every $\beta_g$ leaves every fitted value
unchanged. The classical fix drops one level (treatment contrasts, pyGAM's `coding='dummy'`). A ridge penalty fixes it
differently. Among all the equivalent parameter vectors $(\alpha + c, \beta_g - c)$, the penalty $\sum_g(\beta_g - c)^2$
is smallest at $c = \bar\beta$. So the penalised solution always has $\sum_g\hat\beta_g = 0$: each level is reported as a
deviation from the intercept, with no level singled out.

## Random effects are smooths

A **random intercept** model has $y_i = \alpha + b_{g_i} + \varepsilon_i$ with $b_g \iid \Gauss(0, \sigma_b^2)$ and
$\varepsilon_i \iid \Gauss(0, \sigma^2)$. The joint log-density of $\yvec$ and $\bvec$ is, up to constants,

$$
-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_i\paren{y_i - \alpha - b_{g_i}}^2 - \frac{1}{2\sigma_b^2}\sum_g b_g^2.
$$

Multiply by $-2\sigma^2$. Maximising over $\bvec$ is minimising $\sum_i(y_i - \alpha - b_{g_i})^2 + \lambda\sum_g b_g^2$
with $\lambda = \sigma^2/\sigma_b^2$: the factor term with its ridge penalty. The penalised estimate is the posterior
mode, which for this Gaussian model is also the best linear unbiased predictor. The reverse reading holds for every
smooth: a penalty $\lambda\betavec^\top\Smat\betavec$ is a Gaussian prior on $\betavec$ with precision $\lambda\Smat/\sigma^2$
on the range of $\Smat$. Estimating $\lambda$ by <Gloss name="reml" /> is estimating the variance component $\sigma_b^2$ by REML, which is
how mgcv's `s(g, bs = "re")` works <Cite id="wood2017" />.

**Shrinkage.** Set the derivative with respect to $b_g$ to zero, with $n_g$ observations at level $g$ and mean
$\bar y_g$: $n_g(\bar y_g - \alpha - b_g) = \lambda b_g$, so

$$
\hat b_g = \frac{n_g}{n_g + \lambda}\paren{\bar y_g - \hat\alpha}.
$$

Each level's deviation is shrunk towards zero, more when the level has few observations or the variance ratio $\lambda$
is large. **Worked example.** Three groups of $n_g = 4$ observations have means 10, 12 and 17, and $\lambda = \sigma^2 /
\sigma_b^2 = 4/2 = 2$. With equal group sizes the estimates sum to zero and $\hat\alpha = \bar y = 13$. The raw deviations
$(-3, -1, 4)$ are multiplied by $4/(4 + 2) = 2/3$, giving $\hat\bvec = (-2, -0.667, 2.667)$.

## By-variables and varying coefficients

A **varying-coefficient model** lets the coefficient of a covariate $z$ change smoothly with another covariate $x$
<Cite id="hastie1993" />:

$$
g(\mu_i) = \alpha + f(x_i)\,z_i + \dots
$$

For example, the effect of temperature $z$ on electricity demand may depend on the hour of day $x$. With
$f(x) = \sum_k\beta_kB_k(x)$, the term is $\sum_k\beta_k\,B_k(x_i)z_i$: the model matrix is the smooth's basis with row
$i$ multiplied by $z_i$, $\diag(\zvec)\Bmat$. The penalty on $\betavec$ is unchanged, so the term is fitted like any other
smooth. mgcv writes it `s(x, by = z)`. pyGAM writes it `s(0, by=1)`, which multiplies the basis columns by feature 1.

The smooth is not centred. A constant $f(x) = c$ contributes $cz_i$, a linear effect of $z$, which is not confounded with
the intercept unless $z$ is constant. Centring $f$ would wrongly force the average effect of $z$ to zero.

**Factor-by smooths.** With a factor $g$ as the by-variable, the term is $\sum_{\ell}\indicator[g_i = \ell]\,f_\ell(x_i)$:
a separate smooth of $x$ for each level, each with its own <NoteLink to="additive-model-smoothing-parameter-selection">smoothing parameter</NoteLink>. In mgcv, `s(x, by = g)` centres each
$f_\ell$, so the model must also contain `g` as a <NoteLink to="factorial-designs">main effect</NoteLink> to carry the level means. pyGAM has no factor-by syntax.
The same model is built from one `s(0, by=j)` term per level, where feature $j$ is that level's indicator column. When
the levels are many and exchangeable, such as subjects, mgcv's `bs = "fs"` fits one curve per level with a shared
smoothing parameter and penalised null space, so the curves behave as <NoteLink to="hierarchical-models">random effects</NoteLink>.

## Assumptions

- A cyclic term assumes the period is known exactly and the effect repeats identically in every period.
- A random-effect reading of a factor term assumes exchangeable levels drawn from a common Gaussian distribution.
- A varying-coefficient term assumes the effect of $z$ is linear at each fixed $x$.

## Pitfalls

- **The period comes from the data in pyGAM.** The edge knots default to the smallest and largest observed value. For
  day of year observed on days 1 to 364 the period is then 363 days, not 365. Pass `edge_knots=[0, 365]` explicitly.
- **pyGAM's periodic penalty is not circulant.** In pyGAM 0.12.0 the wrapped difference matrix differs from
  $\Dmat_c^\top\Dmat_c$ in the rows near the wrap. A constant coefficient vector receives a penalty of 4 instead of 0, and
  as $\lambda \to \infty$ the fit tends to a pattern with four <NoteLink to="effective-degrees-of-freedom">effective degrees of freedom</NoteLink> (including the intercept)
  instead of a constant. Checking a cyclic term's behaviour at large $\lambda$ reveals this.
- **Cyclic terms without the cycle in the data.** A cyclic smooth forces the fit to wrap even if the data cover a single
  season. It is then extrapolating the join.
- **A factor-by smooth without the factor.** In mgcv the level-specific smooths are centred. Omitting the factor main
  effect forces all levels to the same mean.
