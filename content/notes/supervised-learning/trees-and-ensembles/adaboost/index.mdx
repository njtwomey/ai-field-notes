<Definition>
**AdaBoost** (discrete AdaBoost, or AdaBoost.M1) takes labels $y_i \in \set{-1, +1}$ and a weak learner that returns
a classifier $G(\xvec) \in \set{-1, +1}$ given weighted data. Starting from weights $w_i = 1/n$, it repeats for
$m = 1, \dots, M$:

1. Fit $G_m$ to the training data with weights $w_i$.
2. Compute the weighted error $\text{err}_m = \sum_i w_i \indicator[y_i \ne G_m(\xvec_i)] / \sum_i w_i$.
3. Set $\alpha_m = \log\frac{1 - \text{err}_m}{\text{err}_m}$.
4. Multiply the weight of each misclassified point by $e^{\alpha_m}$.

The final classifier is $\sgn\big(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\alpha_m G_m(\xvec)\big)$ <Cite id="freund1997" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A **weak learner** is one whose weighted error is below $1/2$ on any weighting of the data: better than a coin, but
perhaps only slightly. Schapire showed that a weak learner can always be boosted into an arbitrarily accurate one
<Cite id="schapire1990" />. AdaBoost is the practical form of that result. Each round concentrates the weights on
the points the ensemble currently gets wrong, so the next weak learner is forced to attend to them. Each learner's
vote $\alpha_m$ grows as its error falls: $\alpha_m = 0$ at $\text{err}_m = 1/2$ and $\alpha_m \to \infty$ as
$\text{err}_m \to 0$. The weak learner is typically a **stump**, a <NoteLink to="decision-tree">decision tree</NoteLink>
with one split, or a tree with a few leaves.

## AdaBoost as forward stagewise additive modelling

For years AdaBoost's success was explained through learning theory. Friedman, Hastie and Tibshirani showed that it is
an ordinary statistical procedure: it fits an <NoteLink to="generalised-additive-model">additive model</NoteLink> $f(\xvec) = \sum_m \beta_m G_m(\xvec)$ by greedy
forward stagewise minimisation of the **exponential loss** $L(y, f) = e^{-yf}$ <Cite id="friedman2000" />.

**Forward stagewise fitting** adds one term at a time and never revisits earlier terms. At step $m$, with the current
model $f_{m-1}$, it solves

$$
(\beta_m, G_m) = \argmin_{\beta, G} \sum_{i=1}^n \exp\big(-y_i\,[f_{m-1}(\xvec_i) + \beta G(\xvec_i)]\big).
$$

The factor $\exp(-y_i f_{m-1}(\xvec_i))$ does not depend on $\beta$ or $G$. Call it $w_i^{(m)}$. The objective becomes
$\sum_i w_i^{(m)} e^{-\beta y_i G(\xvec_i)}$. Since $y_i G(\xvec_i) = +1$ for a correct point and $-1$ for a wrong one,

$$
\sum_i w_i^{(m)} e^{-\beta y_i G(\xvec_i)} = e^{-\beta}\sum_{y_i = G(\xvec_i)} w_i^{(m)} + e^{\beta}\sum_{y_i \ne G(\xvec_i)} w_i^{(m)}
= (e^{\beta} - e^{-\beta})\sum_i w_i^{(m)}\indicator[y_i \ne G(\xvec_i)] + e^{-\beta}\sum_i w_i^{(m)}.
$$

**The classifier.** For any $\beta > 0$ the factor $e^\beta - e^{-\beta}$ is positive, so the best $G$ minimises the
weighted misclassification count $\sum_i w_i^{(m)}\indicator[y_i \ne G(\xvec_i)]$. This is step 1 of AdaBoost.

**The coefficient.** Divide the objective by $\sum_i w_i^{(m)}$ and write $\text{err}_m$ for the weighted error of
$G_m$. The objective is $e^{-\beta}(1 - \text{err}_m) + e^{\beta}\,\text{err}_m$. Its <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> in $\beta$ is
$-e^{-\beta}(1 - \text{err}_m) + e^{\beta}\text{err}_m$, which is zero when $e^{2\beta} = (1 - \text{err}_m)/\text{err}_m$:

$$
\beta_m = \frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1 - \text{err}_m}{\text{err}_m}.
$$

**The weights.** The next step's weights are $w_i^{(m+1)} = w_i^{(m)} e^{-\beta_m y_i G_m(\xvec_i)}$. Using
$-y_i G_m(\xvec_i) = 2\,\indicator[y_i \ne G_m(\xvec_i)] - 1$,

$$
w_i^{(m+1)} = w_i^{(m)}\, e^{2\beta_m \indicator[y_i \ne G_m(\xvec_i)]}\, e^{-\beta_m}.
$$

The factor $e^{-\beta_m}$ is the same for every point and cancels when the weights are normalised. With
$\alpha_m = 2\beta_m$, this is step 4 of AdaBoost, and $\sgn(\sum_m \beta_m G_m) = \sgn(\sum_m \alpha_m G_m)$. Every
step of AdaBoost is therefore a step of forward stagewise minimisation of the exponential loss <Cite id="friedman2000" />.

## What the exponential loss estimates

The minimiser of the expected exponential loss, at each $\xvec$, solves

$$
\min_f\ \expect\brack{e^{-yf} \mid \xvec} = \Pr(y = 1 \mid \xvec)\,e^{-f} + \Pr(y = -1 \mid \xvec)\,e^{f}.
$$

Setting the derivative to zero gives

$$
f^*(\xvec) = \frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\Pr(y = 1 \mid \xvec)}{\Pr(y = -1 \mid \xvec)},
$$

half the log-odds. With infinite data, AdaBoost's score $f = \sum_m \beta_m G_m = \tfrac12\sum_m \alpha_m G_m$ estimates
half the log-odds, and $\Pr(y = 1 \mid \xvec) = 1/(1 + e^{-2f(\xvec)})$ turns it into a probability. The binomial deviance of <NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink>,
$\log(1 + e^{-2yf})$, has the same population minimiser <Cite id="friedman2000" />. The two losses agree near a margin
$yf = 0$ and differ for large negative margins: the exponential loss grows as $e^{-yf}$, the deviance only linearly.

## Worked example: one round of reweighting

Ten points have equal weights $0.1$. The first stump misclassifies three of them, so $\text{err}_1 = 0.3$ and
$\alpha_1 = \log(0.7/0.3) = 0.847$. The three misclassified weights become $0.1 \cdot 7/3 = 0.233$ each, a total of
$0.7$; the seven correct weights stay $0.1$, also a total of $0.7$. After normalisation each misclassified point
weighs $1/6 \approx 0.167$ and each correct point $1/14 \approx 0.071$.

The misclassified points now hold exactly half the weight. This is general: after reweighting, the misclassified
points' share is $\text{err}_m e^{\alpha_m} / (\text{err}_m e^{\alpha_m} + 1 - \text{err}_m) = 1/2$. The stump just
fitted has weighted error $1/2$ on the new weights, no better than a coin, so the next round must find a different
one.

## Training error falls exponentially

The indicator of a mistake is bounded by the exponential loss: $\indicator[y \ne \sgn f] \le e^{-yf}$. So the training
error of $f_M$ is at most the average exponential loss, which the derivation above lets us compute exactly.

<Derivation title="The training-error bound">
Normalise weights to sum to 1 at each step and write $Z_m$ for the normaliser of step $m$, the sum of the unnormalised
weights $w_i^{(m)} e^{-\beta_m y_i G_m(\xvec_i)}$. At the optimal $\beta_m$,

$$
Z_m = e^{-\beta_m}(1 - \text{err}_m) + e^{\beta_m}\text{err}_m = 2\sqrt{\text{err}_m(1 - \text{err}_m)}.
$$

Unrolling the updates from $w_i^{(1)} = 1/n$ gives $\frac{1}{n}e^{-y_i f_M(\xvec_i)} = w_i^{(M+1)}\prod_{m=1}^M Z_m$.
Summing over $i$, and using that the final weights sum to 1,

$$
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \indicator[y_i \ne \sgn f_M(\xvec_i)] \le \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n e^{-y_i f_M(\xvec_i)} =
\prod_{m=1}^M 2\sqrt{\text{err}_m(1 - \text{err}_m)}.
$$

Write $\text{err}_m = 1/2 - \gamma_m$. Then $2\sqrt{\text{err}_m(1 - \text{err}_m)} = \sqrt{1 - 4\gamma_m^2}
\le e^{-2\gamma_m^2}$, using $1 + x \le e^x$. The training error is at most $\exp(-2\sum_m \gamma_m^2)$
<Cite id="freund1997" />.
</Derivation>

If every weak learner beats a coin by at least $\gamma$, the training error is at most $e^{-2M\gamma^2}$ and reaches
zero after $O(\log n / \gamma^2)$ rounds. In the worked example, $\gamma_1 = 0.2$ and the first factor is
$2\sqrt{0.21} = 0.917$.

## Assumptions

- The weak learner must accept weights, or the data must be resampled in proportion to them.
- Each weak learner must beat chance on the weighted data. If $\text{err}_m \ge 1/2$, $\alpha_m \le 0$ and the
  algorithm stops.
- The procedure is for two classes. SAMME and AdaBoost.MH extend it to $K$ classes.

## Pitfalls

- **Label noise and outliers.** The exponential loss penalises a point with margin $yf = -5$ by $e^5 \approx 148$. A
  mislabelled point gains weight every round it stays wrong and eventually dominates the fit. <NoteLink to="gradient-boosting">Gradient boosting</NoteLink> with
  the binomial deviance, whose penalty grows linearly, is more robust <Cite id="friedman2000" />.
- **Test error and $M$.** Test error often keeps falling after training error reaches zero, but AdaBoost can overfit
  noisy data. Choose $M$ on held-out data.
- **Calibration.** The score estimates half the log-odds only in the limit of infinite data. In practice, probabilities
  from boosted trees are pushed away from 0 and 1 and need <NoteLink to="classifier-calibration">calibration</NoteLink> before use <Cite id="niculescumizil2005" />.
