<Definition>
**Iteratively reweighted least squares** (IRLS) computes the maximum-likelihood coefficients $\hat\betavec$ of a
<NoteLink to="generalised-linear-model">generalised linear model</NoteLink>. From the current linear predictor
$\eta_i = \xvec_i^\top\betavec$ and mean $\mu_i = g^{-1}(\eta_i)$ it forms a working response $z_i$ and a weight
$w_i$, and sets the next $\betavec$ to the weighted least-squares fit of $\zvec$ on $\Xmat$. It repeats until the
coefficients stop changing <Cite id="nelder1972" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

The score equations of the generalised linear model

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{y_i - \mu_i}{a(\phi)\, V(\mu_i)}\, \frac{d\mu_i}{d\eta_i}\, \xvec_i = \zeros
$$

are nonlinear in $\betavec$, because $\mu_i$, $V(\mu_i)$ and $d\mu_i/d\eta_i$ all depend on it. Here $V$ is the
variance function, $g$ the link and $a(\phi)$ the dispersion factor. For a general design matrix there is no closed form
except for the Gaussian family with the identity link.

## Mechanism

Define, at the current coefficients,

$$
w_i = \frac{(d\mu_i/d\eta_i)^2}{V(\mu_i)}, \qquad z_i = \eta_i + (y_i - \mu_i)\,\frac{d\eta_i}{d\mu_i}.
$$

$z_i$ is the **working response**: a first-order <NoteLink to="taylor-series">Taylor expansion</NoteLink> of $g(y_i)$ about $\mu_i$, since
$g(y_i) \approx g(\mu_i) + (y_i - \mu_i) g'(\mu_i)$ and $g'(\mu) = d\eta/d\mu$. Let $\Wmat = \diag(w_1, \dots, w_n)$.
The update is

$$
\betavec^{(t+1)} = \paren{\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat}^{-1}\Xmat^\top\Wmat\zvec.
$$

**Derivation.** Write the score as a <NoteLink to="matrix-multiplication">matrix product</NoteLink>. Since $(d\mu_i/d\eta_i)/V(\mu_i) = w_i\,(d\eta_i/d\mu_i)$,

$$
\uvec(\betavec) = \frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\betavec} = \frac{1}{a(\phi)}\sum_i w_i\,(y_i - \mu_i)
\frac{d\eta_i}{d\mu_i}\,\xvec_i = \frac{1}{a(\phi)}\Xmat^\top\Wmat(\zvec - \etavec).
$$

The <Gloss name="fisher-information">Fisher information</Gloss> is the covariance of the score. The $y_i$ are independent with $\var(y_i) = a(\phi)V(\mu_i)$,
so

$$
\Imat(\betavec) = \sum_i \frac{\var(y_i)}{a(\phi)^2 V(\mu_i)^2}\paren{\frac{d\mu_i}{d\eta_i}}^2 \xvec_i\xvec_i^\top =
\frac{1}{a(\phi)}\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat.
$$

**Fisher scoring** is <NoteLink to="newtons-method">Newton's method</NoteLink> with the expected information $\Imat$ in place of the negative <NoteLink to="hessian">Hessian</NoteLink>:
$\betavec^{(t+1)} = \betavec^{(t)} + \Imat^{-1}\uvec$. Substituting both expressions, $a(\phi)$ cancels:

$$
\betavec^{(t+1)} = \betavec^{(t)} + (\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat)^{-1}\Xmat^\top\Wmat(\zvec - \etavec) =
(\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat)^{-1}\Xmat^\top\Wmat\zvec,
$$

using $\etavec = \Xmat\betavec^{(t)}$. This is the weighted least-squares solution for response $\zvec$ and weights
$\Wmat$ <Cite id="mccullagh1989" />.

**Canonical link.** With the canonical link $\theta_i = \eta_i$, the log-likelihood is $\sum_i (y_i\eta_i -
b(\eta_i))/a(\phi)$, and its Hessian $-\Xmat^\top\diag(b''(\eta_i))\Xmat/a(\phi)$ contains no $y_i$. The observed and
expected information are equal, so Fisher scoring is Newton's method. Here $w_i = b''(\eta_i) = V(\mu_i)$, since
$d\mu/d\eta = b''(\theta)$. For <NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink>, $w_i = \mu_i(1 - \mu_i)$.

**Starting values.** The iteration needs a starting $\etavec$, not a starting $\betavec$. A common choice sets
$\mu_i^{(0)} = y_i$, adjusted away from the boundary, for example $(y_i + 0.5)/2$ for binary data, and
$\eta_i^{(0)} = g(\mu_i^{(0)})$.

## Worked example: an intercept-only Poisson model

Fit $\log\mu = \beta$ to counts with sample mean $\bar y = 3$. The maximum-likelihood answer is $\hat\beta = \log 3 =
1.0986$. With the log link, $d\mu/d\eta = \mu = e^\beta$, $V(\mu) = \mu$, so $w_i = \mu$ and
$z_i = \beta + (y_i - \mu)/\mu$. All weights are equal, and weighted least squares on a column of ones returns the
mean of $\zvec$:

$$
\beta^{(t+1)} = \beta^{(t)} + \frac{\bar y - e^{\beta^{(t)}}}{e^{\beta^{(t)}}}.
$$

This is Newton's method for $e^\beta = \bar y$. From $\beta^{(0)} = 0$:

| $t$ | $\beta^{(t)}$ | error $\beta^{(t)} - \log 3$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | $-1.099$ |
| 1 | 2 | $0.901$ |
| 2 | 1.4060 | $0.307$ |
| 3 | 1.1414 | $0.0428$ |
| 4 | 1.0995 | $9.0 \times 10^{-4}$ |
| 5 | 1.0986 | $4.1 \times 10^{-7}$ |

Once close, the error is roughly squared at each step: <NoteLink to="root-finding">quadratic convergence</NoteLink>, as for any Newton method.

## Cost

Each iteration forms $\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat$ in $O(np^2)$ time and solves a $p \times p$ system in $O(p^3)$, for
$n$ observations and $p$ coefficients. A <Gloss name="qr">QR decomposition</Gloss> of $\Wmat^{1/2}\Xmat$ is more stable than the normal
equations and costs the same order. Typical fits converge in 4 to 10 iterations, so IRLS is fast for moderate $p$. For
very large $p$ or $n$, first-order methods such as <Gloss name="sgd">stochastic gradient descent</Gloss> avoid forming the $p \times p$ matrix.

## Trade-offs

- **No global guarantee.** Newton and Fisher-scoring steps can overshoot, and with a non-canonical link a step can
  leave the valid mean space, for example a negative Poisson mean under the identity link. <NoteLink to="penalised-iteratively-reweighted-least-squares">Step halving</NoteLink> fixes both: if
  the deviance rises or a mean becomes invalid, move halfway back to the previous iterate and try again.
- **Divergence under separation.** For binary data with a separating hyperplane, the maximum does not exist. IRLS
  weights $\mu_i(1 - \mu_i)$ go to zero and the coefficients grow without bound.
- **By-products.** At convergence, $(\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat)^{-1}$ scaled by $\phi$ is the estimated covariance of
  $\hat\betavec$, so <NoteLink to="standard-error">standard errors</NoteLink> come at no extra cost.
- **Reuse.** The same scheme, with other weights, fits robust M-estimators and quasi-likelihood models
  <Cite id="green1984" />. Adding a <NoteLink to="smoothing-penalties-and-curvature">penalty matrix</NoteLink> to $\Xmat^\top\Wmat\Xmat$ gives penalised IRLS, the inner loop of
  <NoteLink to="generalised-additive-model">generalised additive model</NoteLink> fitting.
