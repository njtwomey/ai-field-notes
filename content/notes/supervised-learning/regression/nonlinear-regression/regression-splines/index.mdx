<Definition>
A **spline of degree $q$** with knots $\xi_1 < \dots < \xi_K$ is a function that is a polynomial of degree at most $q$
on each interval between consecutive knots and has $q - 1$ continuous derivatives everywhere. A **regression spline**
is a spline fitted to data by least squares, with the knots fixed in advance. Cubic splines, $q = 3$, are the usual
choice <Cite id="deboor1978" />.
</Definition>

## Truncated power basis

The cubic splines with knots $\xi_1, \dots, \xi_K$ form a vector space of dimension $K + 4$. One basis is

$$
1,\; x,\; x^2,\; x^3,\; (x - \xi_1)^3_+,\; \dots,\; (x - \xi_K)^3_+,
$$

where $(u)_+ = \max(u, 0)$. Each truncated cubic $(x - \xi_k)^3_+$ is zero left of $\xi_k$ and a cubic to the right.
Its first derivative $3(x - \xi_k)^2_+$ and second derivative $6(x - \xi_k)_+$ are continuous at $\xi_k$, and its
third derivative jumps from 0 to 6. A combination $\sum_k \theta_k (x - \xi_k)^3_+$ added to a cubic is therefore a
cubic spline, whose third derivative jumps by $6\theta_k$ at knot $k$.

These $K + 4$ functions are linearly independent, and the dimension count for piecewise cubics also gives $K + 4$, so
they span all cubic splines with these knots. The basis is easy to write down but poorly conditioned: for knots close
together, the columns $(x - \xi_k)^3_+$ are nearly equal, and $\Hmat^\top\Hmat$ is close to singular.

## B-spline basis

**B-splines** span the same space with functions that are local and well conditioned. Extend the knots with boundary
knots repeated at each end, $t_0 \le t_1 \le \dots$. The degree-0 B-splines are indicators of the knot intervals,
$B_{j,0}(x) = \indicator[t_j \le x < t_{j+1}]$. Higher degrees follow from the Cox–de Boor recursion
<Cite id="deboor1978" />:

$$
B_{j,q}(x) = \frac{x - t_j}{t_{j+q} - t_j} B_{j,q-1}(x) + \frac{t_{j+q+1} - x}{t_{j+q+1} - t_{j+1}}
B_{j+1,q-1}(x),
$$

with any term whose denominator is zero set to zero. Three properties follow by induction on $q$:

- **Local support.** $B_{j,q}$ is nonzero only on $[t_j, t_{j+q+1})$, which spans $q + 1$ knot intervals.
- **Non-negativity.** $B_{j,q}(x) \ge 0$.
- **Partition of unity.** $\sum_j B_{j,q}(x) = 1$ at every $x$ in the range.

Local support makes $\Hmat^\top\Hmat$ banded, with bandwidth $q + 1$. Each coefficient moves the fit only near its
own knots, so the least-squares problem is well conditioned and solves in time linear in $n$ and $K$.

## Natural cubic splines

A cubic spline fitted by least squares is erratic beyond the outermost knots, where the cubic pieces extrapolate with
little data to anchor them. A **natural cubic spline** is additionally required to be linear to the left of the first
knot and to the right of the last. Linearity means the second and third derivatives vanish there: two constraints at
each end. The dimension falls from $K + 4$ to $K$ <Cite id="hastie2009" />. The saved degrees of freedom can buy more
interior knots, and the linear tails make extrapolation less wild.

## Choosing knots

The knots control flexibility. The number of knots matters more than their exact positions, and common practice
places them at quantiles of $x$, so that each interval holds about the same number of points. The number is chosen by
cross-validation or an information criterion. When the function changes faster in one region, as in the figure,
knots concentrated there fit better with the same number of parameters.

An alternative avoids the choice: use many knots and add a penalty on the roughness of the fit. With B-splines and a
difference penalty on adjacent coefficients, this is a **P-spline** <Cite id="eilers1996" />. With a penalty on
$\int f''(x)^2\,dx$ and a knot at every data point, it is a <NoteLink to="smoothing-splines">smoothing
spline</NoteLink>. The figure's $\lambda$ slider adds such a penalty. The effective degrees of freedom then fall below the
basis size.

## Pitfalls

- **Knots outside the data.** An interval with no data leaves its coefficients undetermined, and $\Hmat^\top\Hmat$ is
  singular.
- **Degree for smoothness only.** Cubic splines look smooth to the eye because their second derivative is continuous.
  Higher degrees rarely help.
- **Interpreting coefficients.** Individual spline coefficients have no direct meaning. Interpret the fitted curve, or
  its derivative, instead.
