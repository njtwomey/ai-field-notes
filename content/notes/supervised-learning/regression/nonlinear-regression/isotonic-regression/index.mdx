<Definition>
Given observations $(x_i, y_i)$ sorted so that $x_1 \le x_2 \le \dots \le x_n$, and positive weights $w_i$,
**isotonic regression** finds fitted values $\hat f_1, \dots, \hat f_n$ that solve

$$
\min_{f_1, \dots, f_n} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i\,(y_i - f_i)^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad f_1 \le f_2 \le \dots \le f_n.
$$

The fitted function is non-decreasing in $x$ and has no other assumed shape <Cite id="ayer1955" /> <Cite
id="brunk1955" />. **Antitonic** regression imposes a non-increasing order instead.
</Definition>

## Structure of the solution

The feasible set $\set{\fvec : f_1 \le \dots \le f_n}$ is a closed convex cone, and the objective is a strictly convex
quadratic, so the solution exists and is unique. It is the weighted projection of $\yvec$ onto the cone.

The solution is constant on consecutive **blocks** of indices, and on each block it equals the weighted mean of the
responses in that block. The key step is that adjacent observations in the wrong order share a fitted value.
Suppose $y_i > y_{i+1}$ but $\hat f_i < \hat f_{i+1}$. Raising $\hat f_i$ alone by a small amount keeps the order,
so optimality requires $y_i \le \hat f_i$; otherwise the raise would reduce the error. Lowering $\hat f_{i+1}$ alone
likewise requires $y_{i+1} \ge \hat f_{i+1}$. Together these give $y_i \le \hat f_i < \hat f_{i+1} \le y_{i+1}$,
which contradicts $y_i > y_{i+1}$. So $\hat f_i = \hat f_{i+1}$. Two observations
forced to share a value $c$ contribute

$$
w_i(y_i - c)^2 + w_{i+1}(y_{i+1} - c)^2 = (w_i + w_{i+1})(\bar y - c)^2 + \text{const},
$$

with $\bar y$ their weighted mean. They act as a single observation at $\bar y$ with weight $w_i + w_{i+1}$. The same
argument applies to adjacent blocks.

An explicit formula also holds. Let $\operatorname{Av}(s, t)$ be the weighted mean of $y_s, \dots, y_t$. Then

$$
\hat f_i = \max_{s \le i}\;\min_{t \ge i}\;\operatorname{Av}(s, t).
$$

Equivalently, plot the cumulative sums $\paren{\sum_{j \le k} w_j, \sum_{j \le k} w_j y_j}$ for $k = 0, \dots, n$.
$\hat f$ is the slope of the greatest convex minorant of these points <Cite id="brunk1955" />.

## Pool adjacent violators

The pool-adjacent-violators algorithm (PAV) builds the blocks from left to right with a stack.

1. Push observation $i$ as a block with value $y_i$ and weight $w_i$.
2. While the top block's value is below the value of the block beneath it, pop both and push one block holding their
   weighted mean and summed weight.
3. After the last observation, each observation takes the value of its block.

Each observation is pushed once, and each merge removes one block, so there are at most $n - 1$ merges and PAV runs
in $O(n)$ time after an $O(n\log n)$ sort
<Cite id="deleeuw2009" />. Ties in $x$ are handled by first replacing tied observations with their weighted mean.

## Worked example

Responses $\yvec = (2, 5, 3, 1, 6, 4)$ at increasing $x$, all with weight 1.

| step | read | stack of blocks (value, size) after pooling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | (2, 1) |
| 2 | 5 | (2, 1), (5, 1) |
| 3 | 3 | $5 > 3$: pool to (4, 2). Stack: (2, 1), (4, 2) |
| 4 | 1 | $4 > 1$: pool to $(4 \cdot 2 + 1)/3 = 3$, giving (3, 3). $2 \le 3$. Stack: (2, 1), (3, 3) |
| 5 | 6 | (2, 1), (3, 3), (6, 1) |
| 6 | 4 | $6 > 4$: pool to (5, 2). Stack: (2, 1), (3, 3), (5, 2) |

The fit is $\hat\fvec = (2, 3, 3, 3, 5, 5)$. Step 4 shows a cascade: the new observation first merges with the block
$\set{5, 3}$, and the merged block is then checked against the block beneath it. The residual sum of squares is
$0 + 4 + 0 + 4 + 1 + 1 = 10$. The fitted values sum to 21, the same as the responses: each block preserves its sum.

## Statistical properties

For a bounded non-decreasing regression function and noise with finite variance, the <Gloss name="mse">mean squared error</Gloss> of isotonic
regression at the design points is of order $n^{-2/3}$ <Cite id="zhang2002" />. This is the minimax rate for monotone
functions, and it is achieved without a bandwidth, knots or a <NoteLink to="additive-model-smoothing-parameter-selection">smoothing parameter</NoteLink>. The price is a step function: the
fit is flat within blocks and jumps between them. Near the ends of the range it is biased and noisy, because the
extreme blocks are short.

## Uses

- **Probability calibration.** A classifier's scores are mapped to probabilities by an isotonic fit of the labels on
  the scores. See <NoteLink to="isotonic-calibration">isotonic calibration</NoteLink>.
- **Dose–response curves**, where the response must not fall as the dose rises.
- **Monotone constraints in other models.** Isotonic regression is the projection step in algorithms that estimate
  monotone single-index models, and a building block of shape-constrained density estimation.

## Pitfalls

- **Overfitting at small $n$.** With few observations per block, the steps follow the noise. A parametric or smooth
  monotone fit, such as a <NoteLink to="shape-constrained-additive-models">monotone spline</NoteLink>, has lower variance.
- **Wrong direction.** Fitting a non-decreasing function to decreasing data returns a single flat block at the overall
  mean. Check the direction first.
- **Prediction between points.** The fit is defined at the observed $x_i$. Between them, use the step value from the
  left or interpolate linearly; the choice is a convention, not part of the estimator.
