<Definition>
A shared Gaussian process latent variable model relates two views of the same $N$ items, $\Ymat \in \reals^{N \times
D_Y}$ and $\Zmat \in \reals^{N \times D_Z}$ with row $n$ of each describing item $n$, through one latent matrix
$\Xmat \in \reals^{N \times Q}$ <Cite id="shon2005" />. Each view has its own <Gloss name="gp">Gaussian process</Gloss> map from the latent
space, with its own kernel, and the views are independent given $\Xmat$:

$$
p(\Ymat, \Zmat \mid \Xmat) = \prod_{d=1}^{D_Y}\Gauss(\yvec_{:,d} \mid \zeros, \Kmat_Y)\prod_{d=1}^{D_Z}\Gauss(\zvec_{:,d}
\mid \zeros, \Kmat_Z),
$$

with $\Kmat_Y = k_Y(\Xmat, \Xmat) + \sigma_Y^2\eye$ and $\Kmat_Z = k_Z(\Xmat, \Xmat) + \sigma_Z^2\eye$. Manifold
relevance determination (MRD) integrates out $\Xmat$ variationally and gives $k_Y$ and $k_Z$ separate <Gloss name="ard" /> weights over
the same latent dimensions <Cite id="damianou2012" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Paired data are common: an image and its depth map, a video and its audio, a human pose and the silhouette it casts, a
human pose and the joint angles of a robot imitating it. Each view has its own high-dimensional representation, and
the pairing says that the two describe one underlying state. A single latent point per pair is a natural description
of that state. The two <NoteLink to="gaussian-process-latent-variable-model">GP-LVM</NoteLink> maps turn it into each
view.

Once fitted, the latent space translates between views. A new observation of one view is embedded by maximising its
likelihood over the latent point, and the other view's Gaussian process predicts the matching observation. Shon,
Grochow, Hertzmann and Rao used this to synthesise novel views of objects and to map human motion-capture poses to the
degrees of freedom of a humanoid robot <Cite id="shon2005" />.

## Shared and private variation

One latent space for both views assumes that all variation is shared. It rarely is. A silhouette does not show whether
the person faces towards or away from the camera, although the 3D pose does. A face image under different lighting
varies with the light, which a second face under the same lighting shares, and with identity, which it does not.
Forcing private variation into the shared space mixes the two.

Ek and colleagues split the latent space into three parts, $\Xmat = [\Xmat^s, \Xmat^Y, \Xmat^Z]$ <Cite id="ek2008" />.
The shared part $\Xmat^s$ feeds both kernels. The private part $\Xmat^Y$ feeds only $k_Y$, and $\Xmat^Z$ only $k_Z$.
Private dimensions capture ambiguity. When a silhouette is consistent with several poses, the pose view's private
dimensions index the alternatives, and inference from the silhouette alone returns several candidate poses. This
factorisation is fixed before training and fitted by <Gloss name="map-estimate">maximum a posteriori</Gloss> estimation of the latent points. The number
of shared and private dimensions is a discrete structural choice that the likelihood cannot make.

## Manifold relevance determination

MRD replaces the hard split by a soft one and lets the data choose it <Cite id="damianou2012" />. There is one latent
space $\Xmat$ with $Q$ dimensions. Each view has an ARD <Gloss name="rbf-kernel">squared exponential kernel</Gloss> with its own weights:

$$
k_Y(\xvec, \xvec') = (\sigma_{\text{ard}}^Y)^2\exp\paren{-\frac12\sum_{q=1}^{Q}w_q^Y(x_q - x_q')^2},
$$

and likewise $k_Z$ with weights $w_q^Z$. A dimension with $w_q^Y > \delta$ and $w_q^Z > \delta$, for a small threshold
$\delta$, is shared. A dimension with only $w_q^Y > \delta$ is private to $\Ymat$, and one with only $w_q^Z > \delta$
is private to $\Zmat$. A dimension relevant to both views with different weights is softly shared.

The weights can only be learned if unused dimensions are penalised, which the maximum-likelihood GP-LVM does not do.
MRD therefore uses the <NoteLink to="bayesian-gaussian-process-latent-variable-model">Bayesian GP-LVM</NoteLink> bound. With a factorised Gaussian
$q(\Xmat)$ and <NoteLink to="sparse-gaussian-processes">inducing variables</NoteLink> for each view, <NoteLink to="expectation">Jensen's inequality</NoteLink> gives

$$
\log p(\Ymat, \Zmat) \ge \Lcal_Y + \Lcal_Z - \KL\paren{q(\Xmat) \,\|\, p(\Xmat)},
$$

where $\Lcal_Y$ and $\Lcal_Z$ are the per-view bounds of the Bayesian GP-LVM, each computed from $\Psi$ statistics with
that view's kernel. The two views interact only through the shared $q(\Xmat)$. A latent dimension whose weight is zero
in both views costs nothing in either likelihood term, so the <Gloss name="kl" /> term returns it to the prior. The same bound extends to
more than two views by adding one term per view.

**Inference across views.** Given a new $\yvec_*$, MRD fits $q(\xvec_*)$ with the bound for $\Ymat$ alone. It then finds
the training latent points nearest to $\xvec_*$ in the shared dimensions only, and generates $\zvec_*$ from $p(\Zmat \mid
\Xmat)$ at a latent point that takes its shared coordinates from $\xvec_*$ and its $\Zmat$-private coordinates from a
neighbour. Each neighbour gives one candidate, which exposes the ambiguity of the mapping.

## Experiments

**Faces under varying light.** Two views each contained 192 images, the 64 illumination conditions of three subjects
from the Yale faces database, at $192 \times 168 = 32{,}256$ pixels per image <Cite id="damianou2012" />. Images were
paired only by illumination condition, not by identity. The learned weights switched on the first three latent
dimensions with about equal weight for both views: a shared space shaped like a hollow hemisphere, the geometry of the
light-source positions. Most other dimensions were private to one view. Two private dimensions of the first view
separated its three faces into three clusters. Sampling along a shared dimension moved the light source in generated
images of both views. Sampling in a private space morphed between faces of one view.

**Pose from silhouette.** The views were 63-dimensional 3D joint locations and 100-dimensional histogram-of-gradient
features of silhouettes, from 5 walking sequences totalling 649 frames, with a separate test sequence of 158 frames
<Cite id="damianou2012" />. A silhouette often matches several poses. The non-dynamical model cannot choose between
them because it treats test frames independently. A version with a Gaussian process prior over time on the latent
points forces the test sequence onto a smooth path, which resolves the ambiguity. The mean Euclidean error of the
predicted joint locations was 2.94 for MRD with dynamics and 4.67 without. The comparisons were 5.86 for <NoteLink to="linear-regression">linear
regression</NoteLink>, 4.27 for GP regression, 4.88 for nearest neighbours in silhouette space, 4.04 for nearest neighbours chosen
to be coherent over the sequence, and 5.13 for the MAP shared GP-LVM of Ek and colleagues.

## Relation to linear methods

<NoteLink to="canonical-correlation-analysis">Canonical correlation analysis</NoteLink> finds linear projections of two
views that are maximally correlated. It captures shared variation only, through <NoteLink to="matrix-vector-product">linear maps</NoteLink>, and gives no generative
model. The shared GP-LVM and MRD are nonlinear and generative: each view is a Gaussian process function of a common
latent space, and MRD also learns which latent dimensions are private to one view.

## Assumptions

- **Paired observations.** Row $n$ of every view describes the same item.
- **Conditional independence of views** given the latent point.
- **Smooth maps from the latent space to each view,** with Gaussian noise.

## Pitfalls

- **Thresholding the weights.** Deciding which dimensions are shared uses a threshold $\delta$ on learned weights,
  which are rarely exactly zero.
- **Many-to-one maps.** When one view does not determine the other, a single prediction averages incompatible
  outputs. Return several candidates from different neighbours, or add dynamics or other context to choose.
- **Cost grows with every view.** Each view has its own kernel and inducing variables, and the per-view terms are
  computed separately.
