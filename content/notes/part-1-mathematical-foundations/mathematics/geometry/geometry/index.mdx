<Definition>
**Geometry** studies spaces equipped with notions of distance, angle, volume, and transformation. A space may be:
- **Flat (Euclidean):** Constant zero curvature with global Cartesian coordinates and the Pythagorean norm.
- **Curved (Riemannian):** Locally Euclidean at every point through tangent spaces $T_p\Mcal$, with an inner product
  $g_p$ that varies smoothly from point to point.
- **Negatively curved (Hyperbolic):** Constant negative curvature where circle circumferences and ball volumes grow
  exponentially with radius, naturally accommodating tree-like hierarchical branching.
</Definition>

## Why geometry matters in machine learning

Standard algorithms often default to Euclidean distances $\norm{\xvec - \yvec}_2$ in raw coordinate space. This flat
assumption breaks down across modern representation learning:

1. **The Manifold Hypothesis:** High-dimensional observations (images, audio, text) do not fill their ambient vector
   space uniformly. They cluster tightly along lower-dimensional, nonlinear submanifolds $\Mcal \subset \reals^D$.
   Intrinsic shortest paths (geodesics) along the manifold reflect true semantic continuous variations, whereas
   Euclidean chords cut through empty, out-of-distribution space.
2. **Constrained Parameter Spaces:** Weight matrices often satisfy geometric constraints: unit-norm sphere
   $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ for spherical embeddings and weight normalization; orthogonal Stiefel manifolds
   $\mathrm{St}(k, n) = \set{\Wmat \in \reals^{n \times k} : \Wmat^\top\Wmat = \Imat}$ for orthogonal RNNs and vision
   transformers; and symmetric positive-definite (SPD) manifolds for covariance estimation.
3. **Statistical Manifolds (Information Geometry):** Families of probability distributions $\set{p_{\thetavec}}$ are not
   flat in parameter space $\thetavec$. Moving a parameter by $\Delta \theta$ has radically different statistical effect
   depending on local Fisher information. The natural gradient descends the true statistical Riemannian manifold
   invariant to parameterisation.
4. **Hierarchical and Graph Representations:** Complex relational datasets (word taxonomies, citation graphs, social
   networks) possess exponential volume expansion. Embedding trees into Euclidean space produces severe distortion;
   hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^d$ accommodates branching hierarchies with negligible distortion.

## Geometric spaces family map

| Space | Curvature | Distance formula | Volume growth | Typical ML application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Euclidean $\reals^d$** | $K = 0$ (flat) | $\norm{\xvec - \yvec}_2 = \sqrt{\sum_i (x_i - y_i)^2}$ | Polynomial $V(r) \propto r^d$ | Standard feature vectors, linear regression |
| **Spherical $\mathbb{S}^d$** | $K > 0$ (constant positive) | Great circle arc $d(u, v) = \arccos(u^\top v)$ | Bounded surface area | Directional statistics, unit-norm embeddings |
| **Hyperbolic $\mathbb{H}^d$** | $K < 0$ (constant negative) | $\operatorname{arcosh}\paren{1 + \frac{2\norm{u - v}^2}{(1-\norm{u}^2)(1-\norm{v}^2)}}$ | Exponential $V(r) \propto e^{(d-1)r}$ | Tree embeddings, knowledge graphs, WordNet |
| **Riemannian $(\Mcal, g)$** | Variable | Geodesic length $\inf \int \sqrt{g(\dot\gamma, \dot\gamma)}\, dt$ | Depends on Ricci curvature | Manifold optimization, surface normalising flows |
| **Statistical $(\Scal, I)$** | Variable (Fisher–Rao) | Fisher information distance $\int \sqrt{\dot\theta^\top I(\theta)\dot\theta}\, dt$ | Metric tensor is Fisher matrix $I(\theta)$ | Natural gradient descent, KL geometry |
