<Definition>
A **metric space** is a set $M$ equipped with a distance function $d: M \times M \to [0, \infty)$ satisfying four
fundamental axioms for all $x, y, z \in M$:

1. **Non-negativity:** $d(x, y) \ge 0$.
2. **Identity of indiscernibles:** $d(x, y) = 0 \iff x = y$.
3. **Symmetry:** $d(x, y) = d(y, x)$.
4. **Triangle inequality:** $d(x, z) \le d(x, y) + d(y, z)$.

A **geodesic** $\gamma: [0, 1] \to M$ is a curve that locally minimises the arc length between its endpoints:

$$
L(\gamma) = \int_0^1 \norm{\dot{\gamma}(t)}\, dt, \qquad d(x, y) = \inf_{\gamma(0)=x,\, \gamma(1)=y} L(\gamma).
$$
</Definition>

## Why geometry matters in machine learning

Standard Euclidean geometry assumes that space is flat, isotropic, and unconstrained. Yet real data representations rarely live in an unconstrained flat void:
- **Constrained embeddings:** Unit-norm vectors in cosine similarity and spherical embeddings live on the hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$. The true distance between two embeddings is not the Euclidean cord length through the interior of the sphere, but the **great-circle geodesic** along its curved surface.
- **Data manifolds:** High-dimensional images and natural speech occupy low-dimensional nonlinear submanifolds. Distances along the manifold (geodesic distance) capture semantic similarity, whereas Euclidean ambient distances shortcut across empty regions of space.
- **Information geometry:** The space of probability distributions is naturally curved by the Fisher information metric; moving a parameter by $\Delta \theta$ has radically different statistical impact depending on current parameter uncertainty.

## The metric axioms and semi-metrics

When evaluating models or comparing feature representations, whether a distance function satisfies the metric axioms determines which algorithms can be safely applied:

- **Pseudometrics:** Relax axiom 2, allowing distinct points $x \neq y$ to have $d(x, y) = 0$. In representation learning, an encoder that maps distinct inputs to identical embeddings induces a pseudometric on raw input space.
- **Quasimetrics:** Relax symmetry (axiom 3), so $d(x, y) \neq d(y, x)$. Common in directed graph shortest paths and non-symmetric divergences such as Kullback–Leibler divergence.
- **Semi-metrics:** Relax the triangle inequality (axiom 4). **Cosine distance** $d_{\cos}(u, v) = 1 - \cos\theta$ and squared Euclidean distance $d_2(u, v)^2$ violate the triangle inequality:
  For unit vectors $u = (1, 0)$, $v = (\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})$, and $w = (0, 1)$,
  $d_{\cos}(u, w) = 1.0$, whereas $d_{\cos}(u, v) + d_{\cos}(v, w) \approx 0.293 + 0.293 = 0.586 < 1.0$.
  Violating the triangle inequality breaks spatial indexing data structures (such as vantage-point trees, cover trees, and KD-trees) that rely on bounding balls to prune nearest-neighbour searches.

## Geodesics as solutions to variational problems

In Euclidean space, the shortest path between two points is a straight line $\gamma(t) = (1 - t)x + ty$. On a curved surface or in a space with position-dependent cost, the arc length functional depends on a local metric tensor $g(x)$:

$$
L(\gamma) = \int_0^1 \sqrt{g_{\gamma(t)}\paren{\dot{\gamma}(t), \dot{\gamma}(t)}}\, dt.
$$

By the calculus of variations, stationary paths of the kinetic energy $E(\gamma) = \frac{1}{2}\int g(\dot{\gamma}, \dot{\gamma})\, dt$ satisfy the **geodesic differential equation**:

$$
\ddot{\gamma}^k(t) + \sum_{i,j} \Gamma_{ij}^k(\gamma(t))\, \dot{\gamma}^i(t)\, \dot{\gamma}^j(t) = 0,
$$

where $\Gamma_{ij}^k$ are the Christoffel symbols of the Levi-Civita connection <Cite id="docarmo1992" />. The geodesic equation describes straight trajectories from the intrinsic perspective of an observer constrained to the manifold: acceleration is orthogonal to the surface.

## Worked example

Consider the two-dimensional plane equipped with a conformal metric tensor $g(x) = e^{2\phi(x)}\mathbf{I}_2$. The infinitesimal distance element is $ds = e^{\phi(x)}\sqrt{dx_1^2 + dx_2^2}$.

1. If $\phi(x) = 0$, $g = \mathbf{I}_2$, the metric is standard Euclidean, and geodesics are straight lines with $d(x, y) = \norm{x - y}_2$.
2. If $\phi(x) = -x_2$ on the upper half-plane $x_2 > 0$, the metric is the **Poincaré half-plane** metric $ds^2 = \frac{dx_1^2 + dx_2^2}{x_2^2}$.
   Geodesics between points $A = (-1, 1)$ and $B = (1, 1)$ are semicircles centered on the horizontal axis $x_2 = 0$. The path arcs upward into regions of larger $x_2$ because $1/x_2^2$ is smaller there, reducing total integrated path length below the Euclidean straight segment!

## Machine learning applications

- **Isomap and Manifold Learning:** Approximates intrinsic geodesic distances by finding shortest paths on a $k$-nearest-neighbour graph, preserving manifold topology where linear PCA fails.
- **Normalising Flows on Manifolds:** Trajectory flows parameterised by neural ODEs must follow vector fields tangent to the manifold, preserving Riemannian volume measures.
- **Metric Learning:** Transforms representations so that task-relevant pairs have small geodesic distance while impostors are separated beyond a margin.
