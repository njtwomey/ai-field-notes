<Definition>
A **smooth manifold** $\Mcal$ of dimension $d$ is a topological space that locally resembles $\reals^d$ through a
family of smooth coordinate charts. At every point $p \in \Mcal$, the vectors tangent to curves passing through $p$
form a $d$-dimensional vector space called the **tangent space** $T_p\Mcal$.

A **Riemannian manifold** $(\Mcal, g)$ equips each tangent space with a positive-definite inner product
$g_p: T_p\Mcal \times T_p\Mcal \to \reals$ that varies smoothly with $p$. In local coordinates,
$g_p(u, v) = u^\top G(p)\, v$, where $G(p) \in \reals^{d \times d}$ is the symmetric positive-definite **metric tensor**.
</Definition>

## Tangent spaces and the Riemannian metric

In unconstrained machine learning, parameters $\thetavec \in \reals^D$ can move in any direction. When parameters must
satisfy smooth constraints—such as unit-norm embeddings $\norm{\uvec} = 1$ or orthogonal matrices
$\Wmat^\top\Wmat = \Imat$—the valid configurations form a submanifold embedded in ambient space.

- **Tangent vectors:** If $\gamma: (-\epsilon, \epsilon) \to \Mcal$ is a smooth curve on the manifold with
  $\gamma(0) = p$, its velocity $\dot{\gamma}(0)$ is a tangent vector $v \in T_p\Mcal$. For the unit sphere
  $\mathbb{S}^{n-1} = \set{x \in \reals^n : \norm{x}^2 = 1}$, differentiating $x(t)^\top x(t) = 1$ shows that tangent
  vectors are precisely those orthogonal to the position vector: $T_p \mathbb{S}^{n-1} = \set{v \in \reals^n : p^\top v = 0}$.
- **Riemannian gradient:** Given a smooth loss function $f: \Mcal \to \reals$, its ambient Euclidean gradient
  $\nabla_{\mathrm{E}} f(p)$ may point off the manifold. The **Riemannian gradient** $\operatorname{grad} f(p) \in T_p\Mcal$
  is the orthogonal projection of $\nabla_{\mathrm{E}} f(p)$ onto the tangent space:
  $$
  \operatorname{grad} f(p) = \operatorname{Proj}_{T_p\Mcal}\paren{\nabla_{\mathrm{E}} f(p)}.
  $$
  For the sphere, $\operatorname{Proj}_{T_p \mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u) = (\Imat - p p^\top) u = u - (p^\top u) p$.

## The exponential map and retractions

In Euclidean gradient descent, one updates parameters by stepping along the negative gradient: $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \nabla f(\theta_t)$.
On a curved manifold, adding a tangent vector $p - \eta v$ escapes the manifold into ambient space.

### The exponential map

The **exponential map** $\exp_p: T_p\Mcal \to \Mcal$ maps a tangent vector $v$ to the point reached by following the
unique geodesic $\gamma(t)$ initialised at $\gamma(0) = p$ with velocity $\dot{\gamma}(0) = v$ for unit time $t = 1$:

$$
\exp_p(v) = \gamma(1).
$$

On the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$, geodesics are great circles, and the exponential map has the closed form:

$$
\exp_p(v) = \cos(\norm{v})\, p + \sin(\norm{v})\, \frac{v}{\norm{v}}.
$$

The inverse operation, the **logarithmic map** $\log_p(q) = v \in T_p\Mcal$, computes the tangent vector whose geodesic
connects $p$ to $q$. The intrinsic Riemannian distance is simply the tangent norm: $d_\Mcal(p, q) = \norm{\log_p(q)}_g$.

### Retractions

Computing the exact exponential map requires integrating the geodesic differential equations, which can be prohibitively
costly on complex manifolds. A **retraction** $\Rcal_p: T_p\Mcal \to \Mcal$ is any smooth, first-order approximation to
$\exp_p$ satisfying <Cite id="absil2008" />:

1. $\Rcal_p(0) = p$.
2. The derivative of $\Rcal_p$ at $0$ is the identity: $D\Rcal_p(0)[v] = v$.

For the sphere, the standard retraction normalises the ambient addition back to the surface:

$$
\Rcal_p(v) = \frac{p + v}{\norm{p + v}}.
$$

**Riemannian gradient descent** updates parameters using retractions: $p_{k+1} = \Rcal_{p_k}\paren{-\eta \operatorname{grad} f(p_k)}$.

## Worked example

Consider the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^2 \subset \reals^3$. At base point $p = (1, 0, 0)^\top$, let the ambient gradient
of a loss function be $g_{\mathrm{amb}} = (3, 2, 1)^\top$.

1. **Riemannian gradient:** The tangent space $T_p\mathbb{S}^2$ consists of vectors with $x_1 = 0$.
   Projecting $g_{\mathrm{amb}}$:
   $$
   \operatorname{grad} f(p) = g_{\mathrm{amb}} - (p^\top g_{\mathrm{amb}}) p = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} - 3 \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}.
   $$
2. **Tangent step:** With step size $\eta = 0.5$, the search vector is $v = -\eta \operatorname{grad} f(p) = (0, -1, -0.5)^\top$.
   Its tangent norm is $\norm{v} = \sqrt{0^2 + (-1)^2 + (-0.5)^2} = \sqrt{1.25} \approx 1.118$ radians.
3. **Exponential map:**
   $$
   p_{\text{new}} = \cos(1.118) \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \sin(1.118) \frac{1}{1.118} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \\ -0.5 \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0.437 \\ -0.804 \\ -0.402 \end{pmatrix}.
   $$
   Note that $\norm{p_{\text{new}}}^2 = 0.437^2 + 0.804^2 + 0.402^2 = 0.191 + 0.646 + 0.162 = 0.999 \approx 1.0$.
4. **Retraction:**
   $$
   p + v = (1, -1, -0.5)^\top, \quad \norm{p + v} = \sqrt{1 + 1 + 0.25} = 1.5, \quad \Rcal_p(v) = \begin{pmatrix} 0.667 \\ -0.667 \\ -0.333 \end{pmatrix}.
   $$
   The retraction remains on the sphere with zero transcendental function evaluations.

## Matrix manifolds in modern deep learning

1. **Stiefel Manifold $\mathrm{St}(k, n)$:** Matrices with $k$ orthonormal columns in $\reals^n$. Used to constrain
   weight matrices in recurrent neural networks to prevent exploding and vanishing gradients, and in transformer projection heads.
2. **Grassmann Manifold $\mathrm{Gr}(k, n)$:** The space of all $k$-dimensional linear subspaces of $\reals^n$, invariant
   to choice of basis. Used in subspace clustering and domain adaptation.
3. **Symmetric Positive-Definite (SPD) Manifold $\Scal_{++}^n$:** Covariance matrices and diffusion tensors. Under the
   affine-invariant Riemannian metric $d(A, B) = \norm{\log(A^{-1/2} B A^{-1/2})}_{\mathrm{F}}$, interpolation between
   covariances preserves positive definiteness without swelling artifacts.
