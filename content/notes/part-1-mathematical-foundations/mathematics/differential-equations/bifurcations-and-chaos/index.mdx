<Definition>
A **discrete-time dynamical system** (or map) iteratively applies a function $f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{X}$:
$$
x_{n+1} = f(x_n), \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots
$$
A **fixed point** $x^\ast$ satisfies $f(x^\ast) = x^\ast$. It is linearly stable if $|f'(x^\ast)| < 1$ and unstable if $|f'(x^\ast)| > 1$. A **bifurcation** is a qualitative topological change in the orbit structure as a parameter $r$ is varied <Cite id="strogatz2015" />.
</Definition>

## The logistic map and cobweb iteration

The canonical model for nonlinear population dynamics and chaos is the **logistic map** on the unit interval $[0, 1]$:

$$
x_{n+1} = r x_n (1 - x_n), \qquad r \in [0, 4].
$$

The fixed points satisfy $x^\ast = r x^\ast (1 - x^\ast)$, yielding:
1. The extinction fixed point $x_0^\ast = 0$, with derivative $f'(0) = r$. It is stable for $0 \le r < 1$ and loses stability at $r = 1$ in a transcritical bifurcation.
2. The carrying capacity fixed point $x_1^\ast = 1 - \frac{1}{r}$, valid for $r \ge 1$. Its derivative is:
$$
f'\paren{1 - \frac{1}{r}} = r\paren{1 - 2\paren{1 - \frac{1}{r}}} = 2 - r.
$$
Hence, $x_1^\ast$ is stable for $1 < r < 3$, where $|2 - r| < 1$.

### Cobweb iteration

An orbit starting at $x_0$ can be visualized graphically on the Cartesian plane $(x_n, x_{n+1})$:
1. Start at $(x_0, 0)$ and draw a vertical segment to $(x_0, f(x_0)) = (x_0, x_1)$.
2. Draw a horizontal segment to the identity line $y = x$ at $(x_1, x_1)$.
3. Repeat vertically to $(x_1, f(x_1)) = (x_1, x_2)$, and so forth.

When $|f'(x^\ast)| < 1$, the trajectory spirals inwards towards the intersection of $y = f(x)$ and $y = x$. When $f'(x^\ast) = -1$ (which occurs at $r = 3$), the system undergoes a **period-doubling (flip) bifurcation**.

## The period-doubling route to chaos

At $r = 3$, the fixed point $x_1^\ast$ repels, but the second iterate $f^{(2)}(x) = f(f(x))$ develops two new stable fixed points forming a 2-cycle $\{p_1, p_2\}$:

$$
f(p_1) = p_2, \qquad f(p_2) = p_1.
$$

The stability of the 2-cycle is determined by the chain rule:

$$
\frac{d}{dx} f^{(2)}(x)\Big|_{x=p_1} = f'(p_2) f'(p_1) = \frac{d}{dx} f^{(2)}(x)\Big|_{x=p_2}.
$$

As $r$ increases further:
- At $r_1 = 3$, period 1 doubles to period 2.
- At $r_2 = 1 + \sqrt{6} \approx 3.44949$, period 2 doubles to period 4.
- At $r_3 \approx 3.54409$, period 4 doubles to period 8.
- The cascade accumulates at the Feigenbaum point:
$$
r_\infty \approx 3.5699456\dots
$$

Mitchell Feigenbaum discovered that the ratio of successive parameter intervals converges to a universal mathematical constant independent of the specific map:

$$
\delta = \lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{r_k - r_{k-1}}{r_{k+1} - r_k} \approx 4.6692016\dots
$$

Any unimodal map with a quadratic maximum (including $x_{n+1} = r \sin(\pi x_n)$) exhibits exactly the same universal rate of period doubling <Cite id="strogatz2015" />.

## The Lyapunov exponent

Deterministic chaos requires trajectories that originate from infinitesimally close initial conditions to separate exponentially fast. If two initial states differ by $\delta_0$, after $n$ iterations their separation is approximately:

$$
|\delta_n| \approx |\delta_0| e^{\lambda n}.
$$

The **Lyapunov exponent** $\lambda$ measures this average rate of expansion:

$$
\lambda(x_0) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \ln \abs{\frac{df^n(x_0)}{dx}} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \ln \abs{f'(x_i)}.
$$

- If $\lambda < 0$: The orbit converges to a stable periodic attractor (cycle or fixed point).
- If $\lambda = 0$: The orbit is at a bifurcation threshold (neutral stability).
- If $\lambda > 0$: Trajectories diverge exponentially. The map is chaotic, and long-term prediction is impossible despite complete deterministic determinism.

For the logistic map at $r = 4$, the change of variables $x_n = \sin^2(\theta_n)$ conjugates the dynamics to the tent map, yielding an exact Lyapunov exponent $\lambda = \ln 2 \approx 0.693$.

### The Hénon attractor

The Hénon map $(x_n, y_n) \mapsto (x_{n+1}, y_{n+1})$ is a 2D model of dissipative dynamics:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{n+1} &= 1 - a x_n^2 + y_n, \\
y_{n+1} &= b x_n.
\end{aligned}
$$

The Jacobian determinant is constant:

$$
\det \Jmat = \det \begin{pmatrix} -2a x & 1 \\ b & 0 \end{pmatrix} = -b.
$$

For $0 < b < 1$ (typically $a = 1.4, b = 0.3$), the map contracts area uniformly by a factor of $b = 0.3$ per iteration. Repeated folding and stretching compresses phase space volume onto a fractal **strange attractor** with Hausdorff dimension $d_H \approx 1.26$ <Cite id="strogatz2015" />.

### The Chirikov standard map

In Hamiltonian (conservative) mechanics, phase volume is strictly preserved ($\det \Jmat = 1$). The standard map models a kicked rotor with position $\theta \pmod{2\pi}$ and momentum $p \pmod{2\pi}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{n+1} &= p_n + K \sin(\theta_n) \pmod{2\pi}, \\
\theta_{n+1} &= \theta_n + p_{n+1} \pmod{2\pi}.
\end{aligned}
$$

When $K = 0$, trajectories follow horizontal invariant circles (integrable motion). As $K$ increases, the Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser (KAM) theorem guarantees that robust irrational invariant tori survive, acting as barriers to diffusion. Beyond the critical stochasticity threshold $K_c \approx 0.971635$, the last bounding torus breaks, and orbits diffuse chaotically across the entire momentum cylinder.
