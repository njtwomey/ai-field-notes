<Definition>
The **inverse hyperbolic functions** invert $\sinh$, $\cosh$ restricted to $[0, \infty)$, and $\tanh$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\arsinh &: \reals \to \reals, & \arsinh x &= \log\paren{x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1}}, \\
\arcosh &: [1, \infty) \to [0, \infty), & \arcosh x &= \log\paren{x + \sqrt{x^2 - 1}}, \\
\artanh &: (-1, 1) \to \reals, & \artanh x &= \frac12\log\frac{1 + x}{1 - x}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\log$ is the <NoteLink to="exponential-and-logarithm">natural logarithm</NoteLink>. The prefix "ar" abbreviates
_area_: the value is the hyperbolic angle, twice the area of a hyperbolic sector <Cite id="roy2010" />.
</Definition>

## Solving for the logarithms

Each hyperbolic function is a rational function of $e^y$, so inverting it means solving a quadratic in $u = e^y$.

**arsinh.** Let $x = \sinh y = (e^y - e^{-y})/2$. Multiplying by $2e^y$ gives $u^2 - 2xu - 1 = 0$, with roots
$u = x \pm \sqrt{x^2 + 1}$. Since $\sqrt{x^2 + 1} > \abs{x}$, only the plus sign gives $u = e^y > 0$. Hence
$y = \log(x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1})$. $\sinh$ is increasing on the whole line, so no branch has to be chosen.

**arcosh.** Let $x = \cosh y$ with $y \ge 0$. The same steps give $u^2 - 2xu + 1 = 0$ and $u = x \pm \sqrt{x^2 - 1}$.
The two roots multiply to 1, so they are $e^{y}$ and $e^{-y}$; the condition $y \ge 0$ selects the larger, the plus
sign. $\cosh$ is even, so the restriction to $y \ge 0$ is the <NoteLink to="inverse-trigonometric-functions">principal
branch</NoteLink>, as for $\arccos$.

**artanh.** Let $x = \tanh y = (u^2 - 1)/(u^2 + 1)$ with $u = e^y$. Solving for $u^2$ gives
$u^2 = (1 + x)/(1 - x)$, so $y = \tfrac12\log\frac{1 + x}{1 - x}$, defined exactly when $\abs{x} < 1$.

<Derivation title="Derivatives">
Differentiating $\sinh y = x$ gives $\cosh y \cdot y' = 1$, and $\cosh y = \sqrt{1 + \sinh^2 y} = \sqrt{1 + x^2}$. The
same steps for the other two use $\sinh y = \sqrt{\cosh^2 y - 1}$ for $y \ge 0$ and $\tanh' = 1 - \tanh^2$:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\arsinh x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x^2}}, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\arcosh x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 - 1}}, \qquad
\frac{d}{dx}\artanh x = \frac{1}{1 - x^2}.
$$

The last differs from $\frac{d}{dx}\arctan x = 1/(1 + x^2)$ only in a sign. The
<NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> of $\arcosh$ is unbounded as $x \to 1$.
</Derivation>

## artanh and the logit

The **logit** is $\operatorname{logit} p = \log\frac{p}{1 - p}$, the inverse of the logistic sigmoid
$\sigma(t) = 1/(1 + e^{-t})$. Substituting $p = (1 + x)/2$ gives $1 - p = (1 - x)/2$ and

$$
\artanh x = \frac12\operatorname{logit}\frac{1 + x}{2}.
$$

This is the inverse of the identity $\tanh t = 2\sigma(2t) - 1$. artanh is the logit rescaled from $(0, 1)$ to
$(-1, 1)$: it maps a bounded quantity onto the whole real line, with the ends $\pm 1$ sent to $\pm\infty$.

That makes it the standard way to optimise a parameter confined to $(-1, 1)$, such as a correlation or the coefficient
of a stable autoregression: optimise $t \in \reals$ freely and set the parameter to $\tanh t$. The reverse direction
appears in <Gloss name="rl">reinforcement learning</Gloss>. A policy that samples an unbounded Gaussian $u$ and acts
with $a = \tanh u$ needs the density of $a$, which by the <NoteLink to="change-of-variables">change of
variables</NoteLink> formula is the density of $u = \artanh a$ divided by $\abs{da/du} = 1 - \tanh^2 u$
<Cite id="haarnoja2018" />.

## Fisher's z-transformation

Let $r$ be the sample <NoteLink to="pearson-correlation">Pearson correlation</NoteLink> of $n$ pairs drawn from a
bivariate normal distribution with correlation $\rho$. The distribution of $r$ is awkward: it is confined to
$(-1, 1)$, skewed when $\rho \ne 0$, and its variance, approximately $(1 - \rho^2)^2/n$ for large $n$, depends on the
unknown $\rho$. Fisher showed that

$$
z = \artanh r
$$

is approximately normal with mean $\artanh\rho$ and variance $1/(n - 3)$, for every $\rho$ <Cite id="fisher1921" />.

The reason is that artanh is the variance-stabilising transformation for $r$. By the first-order (delta-method)
approximation, $\var(g(r)) \approx g'(\rho)^2\var(r)$ for a smooth $g$. With $g = \artanh$,
$g'(\rho) = 1/(1 - \rho^2)$, so

$$
\var(z) \approx \frac{1}{(1 - \rho^2)^2}\cdot\frac{(1 - \rho^2)^2}{n} = \frac{1}{n},
$$

which no longer depends on $\rho$. The stretch $1/(1 - \rho^2)$ of artanh near $\pm 1$ exactly undoes the compression of
$r$ against its bounds. Fisher's finer calculation replaces $n$ with $n - 3$. A
<NoteLink to="confidence-intervals">confidence interval</NoteLink> for $\rho$ is built on the $z$ scale and mapped back
with $\tanh$.

## arsinh as a transformation of data

For large $\abs{x}$, $\sqrt{x^2 + 1} \approx \abs{x}$, so $\arsinh x \approx \sgn(x)\log(2\abs{x})$. Near zero,
$\arsinh x \approx x$. The function therefore behaves like a logarithm on large values of either sign and like the
identity near zero, and unlike $\log$ it is defined at zero and for negative values. Burbidge, Magee and Robb proposed
it, with a scale parameter, $\arsinh(\theta x)/\theta$, as an alternative to the log transform for skewed variables that
include zeros or negative values, such as wealth or income <Cite id="burbidge1988" />. Unlike $\log$, arsinh does not
turn a change of units into an additive constant: $\log(cx) = \log c + \log x$, but $\arsinh(cx) - \arsinh x$ depends on
$x$. The scale parameter $\theta$ sets where the switch from linear to logarithmic behaviour happens.

## arcosh and hyperbolic distance

Distances in hyperbolic space are arcosh of an algebraic expression. In the Poincaré ball model used for hierarchical
embeddings, the distance between points $\uvec$ and $\vvec$ inside the <NoteLink to="vector-norms">unit ball</NoteLink>
is

$$
d(\uvec, \vvec) = \arcosh\paren{1 + \frac{2\norm{\uvec - \vvec}^2}{(1 - \norm{\uvec}^2)(1 - \norm{\vvec}^2)}}
$$

<Cite id="nickel2017" />. The argument is at least 1 and equals 1 only when $\uvec = \vvec$. Near 1, $\arcosh(1 + \epsilon)
\approx \sqrt{2\epsilon}$, and the derivative $1/\sqrt{x^2 - 1}$ is unbounded, so implementations clamp the argument
slightly above 1 to keep <NoteLink to="gradient">gradients</NoteLink> finite (see
<NoteLink to="hyperbolic-geometry-and-embeddings">hyperbolic geometry and embeddings</NoteLink>).

## Worked example

A sample of $n = 28$ pairs has correlation $r = 0.6$. Find a 95% confidence interval for $\rho$.

- **Transform.** $z = \artanh 0.6 = \tfrac12\log(1.6/0.4) = \tfrac12\log 4 = \log 2 \approx 0.6931$.
- **<NoteLink to="standard-error">Standard error</NoteLink>.** $1/\sqrt{n - 3} = 1/\sqrt{25} = 0.2$.
- **Interval on the z scale.** $0.6931 \pm 1.96 \times 0.2 = (0.3011, 1.0851)$.
- **Back-transform.** $\tanh 0.3011 \approx 0.292$ and $\tanh 1.0851 \approx 0.796$, so the interval for $\rho$ is
  $(0.292, 0.796)$. It is asymmetric about $0.6$, longer towards 0, as the skewed distribution of $r$ requires.

For the logarithmic forms: $\arsinh 3 = \log(3 + \sqrt{10}) = \log 6.1623 \approx 1.8184$, and
$\sinh 1.8184 = (6.1623 - 0.1623)/2 = 3.0000$.

## Pitfalls

- **Correlations of exactly $\pm 1$.** $\artanh(\pm 1)$ is infinite. Clip $r$ to $[-1 + \epsilon, 1 - \epsilon]$ before
  transforming, or report the boundary case separately.
- **Cancellation in the log forms.** For large negative $x$, $x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1}$ subtracts nearly equal numbers. Use
  the library `asinh`, or the odd symmetry $\arsinh x = -\arsinh(-x)$. For $\artanh$ near 0 use
  $\tfrac12\operatorname{log1p}(2x/(1 - x))$ or the library `atanh`.
- **Notation.** $\sinh^{-1}x$ means $\arsinh x$, not $1/\sinh x$. NumPy spells the functions `arcsinh`, `arccosh` and
  `arctanh`, although they return areas, not arcs.
- **Back-transforming means.** The mean of $z$ maps back to $\tanh(\bar z)$, which is not the mean of the
  correlations. Average correlations on the $z$ scale only when that is the quantity wanted.
