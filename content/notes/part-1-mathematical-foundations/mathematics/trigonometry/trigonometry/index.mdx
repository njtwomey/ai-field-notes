<Definition>
Trigonometry studies angles and the functions that turn an angle into a position on a circle. Turning $\theta$ radians
anticlockwise from $(1, 0)$ around the unit circle reaches the point $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$. Every other
trigonometric function, identity and law is built from these two coordinates <Cite id="roy2010" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Machine learning uses trigonometry wherever angles, rotations or periodic signals appear. Cosine similarity measures the
angle between two embedding vectors. Sinusoidal positional encodings and rotary position embeddings give a transformer a
sense of order by rotating pairs of coordinates. Fourier features, random Fourier features and the whole of
<NoteLink to="fourier-analysis">Fourier analysis</NoteLink> rest on sines and cosines. Polar coordinates evaluate the
<NoteLink to="gaussian-integral">Gaussian integral</NoteLink> and drive the
<NoteLink to="box-muller-transform">Box–Muller method</NoteLink> for sampling normals. The hyperbolic tangent is an
activation function, and its inverse is Fisher's transformation of a correlation coefficient.

## Main ideas

**Radians make angles lengths.** An angle in <NoteLink to="angles-and-radians">radians</NoteLink> is the arc it cuts
from a circle of radius 1, so a full turn is $2\pi$. The <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink>
$\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x$ holds only in radians, which is why calculus and code
use them.

**Two coordinates on the unit circle.** <NoteLink to="sine-and-cosine" /> are the coordinates of the point at angle
$\theta$, with period $2\pi$ and $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$. The
<NoteLink to="tangent-and-reciprocal-trigonometric-functions">tangent</NoteLink> is the slope of the ray at that
angle, and secant, cosecant and cotangent are reciprocals.

**Rotations compose by adding angles.** The angle-sum formulas among the
<NoteLink to="trigonometric-identities">trigonometric identities</NoteLink> come from composing two rotations.
Double-angle, product-to-sum and harmonic-addition formulas follow from them. The
<NoteLink to="law-of-cosines-and-law-of-sines">law of cosines and law of sines</NoteLink> extend Pythagoras' theorem to
every triangle, and the law of cosines is the geometric form of the expansion of $\norm{\avec - \bvec}^2$ with a dot
product.

**Going back from a ratio to an angle.** The <NoteLink to="inverse-trigonometric-functions">inverse trigonometric
functions</NoteLink> invert sine, cosine and tangent on intervals where each is one-to-one. The two-argument function
$\atantwo(y, x)$ uses both signs to return the angle of a point in its correct quadrant.
<NoteLink to="polar-coordinates" /> describe a point by its distance and that angle, with area element $r\,dr\,d\theta$.

**Complex exponentials unify the circle.** <NoteLink to="eulers-formula" />, $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$,
turns rotations into multiplications and the angle-sum identities into the rule $e^{i(\alpha + \beta)} = e^{i\alpha}
e^{i\beta}$. It is the starting point of Fourier analysis.

**The hyperbola has its own trigonometry.** The <NoteLink to="hyperbolic-functions">hyperbolic functions</NoteLink>
$\cosh$, $\sinh$ and $\tanh$ parametrise the hyperbola $x^2 - y^2 = 1$ as cosine and sine parametrise the circle. Their
inverses, the <NoteLink to="inverse-hyperbolic-functions">inverse hyperbolic functions</NoteLink>, are logarithms.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="angles-and-radians">angles and radians</NoteLink>, then <NoteLink to="sine-and-cosine">sine and
cosine</NoteLink>, which every later note uses. The tangent, the identities and the laws of triangles each need only
sine and cosine. The inverse functions need the tangent, and polar coordinates need the inverse functions for
$\atantwo$. Euler's formula ties the circle to the <NoteLink to="exponential-and-logarithm">exponential</NoteLink>, and
the hyperbolic functions and their inverses come last because they rely on the exponential as well.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume algebra and the geometry of right triangles. Euler's formula and the
hyperbolic functions also use the exponential function and the <NoteLink to="taylor-series" /> from
<NoteLink to="functions-of-one-variable">functions of one variable</NoteLink>.

The <NoteLink to="derivatives-of-trigonometric-functions">derivatives of the trigonometric functions</NoteLink> note
differentiates these functions. In <NoteLink to="linear-algebra">linear algebra</NoteLink>, the
<NoteLink to="dot-product">dot product</NoteLink> measures the cosine of an angle between vectors.
In <NoteLink to="signal-processing">signal processing</NoteLink>, the <NoteLink to="fourier-series" /> and
<NoteLink to="fourier-transform" /> decompose signals into sines and cosines. In transformers, the
<NoteLink to="sinusoidal-positional-encoding">sinusoidal positional encoding</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="rotary-position-embedding">rotary position embedding</NoteLink> use the angle-sum identities, and
<NoteLink to="random-fourier-features">random Fourier features</NoteLink> approximate kernels with random cosines.
