<Definition>
The **hyperbolic cosine**, **hyperbolic sine** and **hyperbolic tangent** of a real number $x$ are

$$
\cosh x = \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2}, \qquad \sinh x = \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{2}, \qquad
\tanh x = \frac{\sinh x}{\cosh x} = \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{e^x + e^{-x}}.
$$

The reciprocals are $\sech x = 1/\cosh x$, $\csch x = 1/\sinh x$ and $\coth x = 1/\tanh x$. $\cosh$ is even and at
least 1, $\sinh$ is odd and increasing, and $\tanh$ is odd, increasing and takes values in $(-1, 1)$
<Cite id="roy2010" />.
</Definition>

## The unit hyperbola

$\cosh$ and $\sinh$ are the even and odd parts of $e^x$: $e^x = \cosh x + \sinh x$ and $e^{-x} = \cosh x - \sinh x$.
Multiplying the two equations gives

$$
\cosh^2 x - \sinh^2 x = e^x e^{-x} = 1.
$$

So the point $(\cosh t, \sinh t)$ lies on the **unit hyperbola** $x^2 - y^2 = 1$, and as $t$ runs over the real line it
traces the right branch, since $\cosh t \ge 1$. The analogy with the circle, $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$, is
exact in one more respect. On the unit circle the angle $\theta$ is twice the area of the circular sector it cuts off.
On the unit hyperbola the parameter $t$ is twice the area of the **hyperbolic sector** between the $x$-axis, the ray to
$(\cosh t, \sinh t)$ and the hyperbola. For this reason $t$ is called a hyperbolic angle.

<Derivation title="The hyperbolic sector has area t/2">
Take $t > 0$. The sector is the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(\cosh t, 0)$ and $(\cosh t, \sinh t)$, minus the
region under the hyperbola $y = \sqrt{x^2 - 1}$ from $x = 1$ to $x = \cosh t$. The triangle has area
$\tfrac12\cosh t\sinh t$. Substituting $x = \cosh u$, $dx = \sinh u\,du$, the region has area

$$
\int_1^{\cosh t} \sqrt{x^2 - 1}\,dx = \int_0^t \sinh^2 u\,du = \int_0^t \frac{\cosh 2u - 1}{2}\,du
= \frac{\sinh 2t}{4} - \frac{t}{2} = \frac{\cosh t\sinh t}{2} - \frac{t}{2},
$$

using $\sinh^2 u = (\cosh 2u - 1)/2$ and $\sinh 2t = 2\sinh t\cosh t$, both checked by expanding the exponentials. The
difference is $t/2$. Negative $t$ follows by reflection in the $x$-axis.
</Derivation>

The tangent line $x = 1$ to the hyperbola at $(1, 0)$ meets the ray through
$(\cosh t, \sinh t)$ at height $\sinh t/\cosh t = \tanh t$, by the same similar-triangle argument that makes
$\tan\theta$ a length on the tangent line to the circle. As $t \to \infty$ the ray approaches the asymptote $y = x$, so
$\tanh t \to 1$.

<HyperbolaSector />

The link to the circular functions is exact in the complex plane. <NoteLink to="eulers-formula">Euler's
formula</NoteLink> gives $\cos\theta = (e^{i\theta} + e^{-i\theta})/2$, so $\cos(ix) = (e^{-x} + e^x)/2 = \cosh x$, and
similarly $\sin(ix) = i\sinh x$.

## Identities

Each circular identity has a hyperbolic counterpart, found by expanding exponentials. The counterpart flips the sign of
every term that contains a product of two $\sinh$ factors (Osborn's rule):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cosh(a + b) &= \cosh a\cosh b + \sinh a\sinh b, \\
\sinh(a + b) &= \sinh a\cosh b + \cosh a\sinh b, \\
\tanh(a + b) &= \frac{\tanh a + \tanh b}{1 + \tanh a\tanh b}, \\
1 - \tanh^2 x &= \sech^2 x.
\end{aligned}
$$

To check the first, expand $\cosh a\cosh b + \sinh a\sinh b = \tfrac14\big[(e^a + e^{-a})(e^b + e^{-b}) +
(e^a - e^{-a})(e^b - e^{-b})\big]$. The cross terms $e^{a - b}$ and $e^{-a + b}$ cancel, leaving
$\tfrac12(e^{a + b} + e^{-a - b}) = \cosh(a + b)$. The last identity is $\cosh^2 x - \sinh^2 x = 1$ divided by
$\cosh^2 x$.

## Derivatives

Differentiating the exponentials term by term gives

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\sinh x = \cosh x, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\cosh x = \sinh x, \qquad
\frac{d}{dx}\tanh x = \frac{\cosh^2 x - \sinh^2 x}{\cosh^2 x} = 1 - \tanh^2 x = \sech^2 x,
$$

the last by the quotient rule. Unlike $\cos$, $\cosh$ differentiates without a sign change, so $\sinh$ and $\cosh$
solve $y'' = y$ where $\sin$ and $\cos$ solve $y'' = -y$. The formula $\tanh' = 1 - \tanh^2$ means that the derivative
can be computed from the output alone, which backpropagation exploits: the forward pass stores $\tanh x$ and the
backward pass needs nothing else.

$\cosh$ also describes a physical shape. A uniform chain hanging under gravity between two supports takes the shape
$y = a\cosh(x/a)$, the **catenary**.

## tanh as an activation function

The logistic sigmoid is $\sigma(x) = 1/(1 + e^{-x})$, with outputs in $(0, 1)$. Then

$$
2\sigma(2x) - 1 = \frac{2}{1 + e^{-2x}} - 1 = \frac{1 - e^{-2x}}{1 + e^{-2x}} = \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{e^x + e^{-x}} = \tanh x,
$$

multiplying numerator and denominator by $e^x$ in the third step. tanh is the sigmoid stretched to $(-1, 1)$ and
compressed horizontally by a factor of 2. Two consequences follow for neural networks.

- **Zero-centred outputs.** tanh outputs average near 0 when its inputs do, while sigmoid outputs are all positive. When
  every input to a unit has the same sign, the <NoteLink to="gradient">gradients</NoteLink> of that unit's incoming
  weights all share a sign too, and updates zig-zag. LeCun et al. recommend tanh over the sigmoid for hidden units for
  this reason, in the scaled form $1.7159\tanh(2x/3)$, which maps $\pm 1$ to $\pm 1$ <Cite id="lecun1998" />.
- **Slope 1 at the origin.** $\tanh'(0) = 1 - 0 = 1$, while $\sigma'(0) = \sigma(0)(1 - \sigma(0)) = 1/4$. Near 0, tanh
  behaves like the identity.

## Saturation and vanishing gradients

For large $\abs{x}$, $\tanh x$ is flat at $\pm 1$: the unit **saturates**. Its derivative decays exponentially,

$$
\tanh'(x) = \sech^2 x = \frac{4}{(e^x + e^{-x})^2} \approx 4e^{-2\abs{x}} \quad \text{for large } \abs{x}.
$$

At $x = 2$ the derivative is $0.071$; at $x = 3$ it is $0.0099$. Backpropagation multiplies the gradient by $\tanh'$ at
every saturated unit it passes through, so a path through $L$ saturated units shrinks the gradient by a factor such as
$0.071^L$. Glorot and Bengio observed hidden layers of deep tanh and sigmoid networks saturating one after another
during training, and proposed an initialisation that keeps pre-activations in the near-linear region
<Cite id="glorot2010" />. The problem is analysed in <NoteLink to="vanishing-and-exploding-gradients">vanishing and
exploding gradients</NoteLink>, and functions that do not saturate for positive inputs are compared in
<NoteLink to="activation-functions">activation functions</NoteLink>.

**Softsign**, $x/(1 + \abs{x})$, has the same range and slope 1 at 0, but approaches $\pm 1$ polynomially. Its
derivative $1/(1 + \abs{x})^2$ decays like $1/x^2$ rather than exponentially, so it saturates more gently; Glorot and
Bengio found it less prone to saturation than tanh <Cite id="glorot2010" />.

<TanhVersusSigmoid />

tanh remains in use where a bounded, signed output is the point.

- **Recurrent gates.** The standard <NoteLink to="long-short-term-memory">LSTM</NoteLink> cell squashes its candidate
  cell values and its output with tanh, and its gates with the sigmoid.
- **Soft capping.** Some <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss> bound attention or output logits $z$
  smoothly with $c\tanh(z/c)$, which is close to $z$ for $\abs{z} \ll c$ and never exceeds $c$ in magnitude
  <Cite id="gemmateam2024" /> (see <NoteLink to="query-key-normalisation-and-logit-soft-capping">logit
  soft-capping</NoteLink>).
- **Bounded actions.** A policy that outputs an unbounded Gaussian sample can pass it through tanh to respect action
  limits.

## Worked example

Compute $\tanh 1$ two ways.

- **From the definition.** $e \approx 2.71828$ and $e^{-1} \approx 0.36788$, so
  $\tanh 1 = (2.71828 - 0.36788)/(2.71828 + 0.36788) = 2.35040/3.08616 \approx 0.76159$.
- **From the sigmoid.** $\sigma(2) = 1/(1 + e^{-2}) = 1/1.13534 \approx 0.88080$, and $2 \times 0.88080 - 1 = 0.76159$.

The derivative there is $1 - 0.76159^2 \approx 0.41998$. Check: $\cosh 1 = 1.54308$ and $1/\cosh^2 1 = 0.41998$.

## Pitfalls

- **Overflow.** $(e^x - e^{-x})/(e^x + e^{-x})$ evaluates to `inf/inf = NaN` once $e^x$ overflows, near $x = 710$ in
  <NoteLink to="floating-point-arithmetic">double precision</NoteLink>. Use the library `tanh`, or $1 - 2/(e^{2x} + 1)$
  for $x \ge 0$.
- **Cancellation near 0.** $(e^x - e^{-x})/2$ loses digits for small $\abs{x}$, since the two terms nearly cancel. Use
  `sinh`, or `expm1`: $\sinh x = \tfrac12\big(\operatorname{expm1}(x) - \operatorname{expm1}(-x)\big)$.
- **Notation.** $\sinh^{-1}$ means the inverse function $\arsinh$, not $1/\sinh = \csch$.
- **Initialisation.** Inputs to tanh units with large variance saturate the units from the first step. Scale weights so
  that pre-activations start with variance near 1.
