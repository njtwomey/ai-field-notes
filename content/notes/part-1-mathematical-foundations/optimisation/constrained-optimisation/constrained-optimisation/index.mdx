<Definition>
Constrained optimisation minimises an objective $f_0(\xvec)$ over the points that
satisfy equality constraints $h_j(\xvec) = 0$ and inequality constraints $f_i(\xvec) \le 0$. At a constrained optimum
the gradient of the objective need not vanish. It is balanced instead by the gradients of the active constraints,
weighted by Lagrange multipliers <Cite id="boyd2004" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Constraints are common in machine learning. Probabilities must be nonnegative and sum to one, so maximum-entropy and
<Gloss name="mle">maximum-likelihood</Gloss> problems over distributions carry an equality constraint. The support
vector machine is a quadratic program, and its dual reveals the support vectors and the kernel form. Optimal transport
is a linear program. Fairness constraints, budget constraints in resource allocation,
<NoteLink to="trust-region-methods">trust regions</NoteLink> in policy optimisation and norm bounds on weights all
restrict the feasible set. The multipliers themselves carry meaning: each is the rate at which the optimal value changes
as its constraint is relaxed.

## Main ideas

**Gradients are parallel at the optimum.** <NoteLink to="lagrange-multipliers" /> handle equality constraints. At a
local optimum of $f$ subject to $g(\xvec) = 0$, $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$. The multiplier $\lambda$ measures how fast
the optimal value changes with the constraint level.

**Every problem has a dual.** <NoteLink to="lagrangian-duality" /> adds the constraints to the objective with
multipliers and minimises over $\xvec$. The result, the dual function, is a concave lower bound on the optimal value
for every nonnegative multiplier. The best bound equals the optimal value for convex problems that satisfy Slater's
condition.

**Optimality conditions with inequalities.** The <NoteLink to="karush-kuhn-tucker-conditions">KKT conditions</NoteLink>
extend Lagrange multipliers to inequality constraints. They add nonnegative multipliers and complementary slackness:
an inactive constraint has a zero multiplier. For convex problems satisfying Slater's condition they are necessary and
sufficient.

**Linear and quadratic programs.** A <NoteLink to="linear-programming">linear program</NoteLink> minimises a linear
objective over a polyhedron, and an optimum lies at a vertex. The simplex method walks along vertices, and
interior-point methods cross the interior. A <NoteLink to="quadratic-programming">quadratic program</NoteLink> has a
quadratic objective and linear constraints, and is convex when its matrix is positive semidefinite.

**Constraints become penalties.** <NoteLink to="penalty-and-barrier-methods" /> solve a sequence of unconstrained
problems. A penalty charges for violations, a logarithmic barrier blocks the boundary from inside, and the augmented
Lagrangian adds multiplier estimates to a penalty.
