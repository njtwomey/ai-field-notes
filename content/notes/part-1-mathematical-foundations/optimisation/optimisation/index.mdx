<Definition>
Optimisation finds the input $\xvec$ that minimises an objective $f(\xvec)$, possibly subject to constraints. Its
theory says when a minimum exists, how to recognise one and how fast an algorithm reaches it. Its algorithms use
function values, gradients or curvature, exactly or from noisy estimates <Cite id="boyd2004" />
<Cite id="nocedal2006" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Training a model is an optimisation problem: choose the parameters that minimise the loss on the training data, often
plus a regulariser. Neural networks are trained by stochastic first-order methods, classical models such as
<NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink> by <NoteLink to="quasi-newton-methods">quasi-Newton
methods</NoteLink>, and <Gloss name="svm">support vector machines</Gloss> through a constrained dual. Hyperparameters
are tuned by derivative-free search. Optimal transport, itself a linear program, compares distributions in generative
modelling and <NoteLink to="domain-adaptation">domain adaptation</NoteLink>.

## Main ideas

**Convexity separates easy from hard.** For a convex objective every local minimum is global, a zero gradient
certifies optimality, and rates of convergence can be proved. Most classical learning problems are convex, and deep
learning is not, but convex theory still explains the behaviour of its optimisers.

**Smoothness and curvature set the speed.** The smoothness constant $L$ limits the step size and the strong-convexity
constant $\mu$ sets the rate. Their ratio, the <NoteLink to="condition-number">condition number</NoteLink>, measures how
hard a problem is for gradient methods. Second-order methods use curvature to correct for poor conditioning.

**Constraints become multipliers.** At a constrained optimum the gradient of the objective is balanced by the gradients
of the <NoteLink to="karush-kuhn-tucker-conditions">active constraints</NoteLink>.
<NoteLink to="lagrange-multipliers">Lagrange multipliers</NoteLink> and the KKT conditions state this, and duality turns
a constrained problem into a bound that is often easier to optimise.

**Noise is a resource and a limit.** Stochastic gradients make each step cheap and set a noise floor on accuracy.
Step-size schedules, minibatch size and <NoteLink to="variance-reduction">variance reduction</NoteLink> control that
trade-off.

**Without gradients, search.** When only function values are available, simplex methods, annealing and evolution
strategies explore the landscape directly.
