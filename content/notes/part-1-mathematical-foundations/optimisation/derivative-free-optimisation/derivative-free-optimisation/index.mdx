<Definition>
Derivative-free optimisation minimises a function $f$ using only its values
$f(\xvec)$ at chosen points. It treats $f$ as a black box: no <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink>, no formula,
sometimes noisy evaluations. The methods differ in how they choose the next points to evaluate, from a deterministic
simplex of trial points to random populations drawn from an adapted distribution <Cite id="nocedal2006" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Many objectives in machine learning have no usable gradient. The validation accuracy of a model as a function of its
hyperparameters comes from a full training run. Simulators, compilers and physical experiments return a number, not a
<NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink>. Objectives with discrete choices or plateaus, such as the return of a
<Gloss name="rl">reinforcement-learning</Gloss> policy, have gradients that are zero or undefined almost everywhere.
Derivative-free methods also search for global minima of rugged landscapes where
<NoteLink to="first-order-methods">gradient methods</NoteLink> stop at the nearest local minimum.

## Main ideas

**Move a simplex of points.** The <NoteLink to="nelder-mead">Nelder–Mead method</NoteLink> keeps $n + 1$ points in
$n$ dimensions. Each step reflects the worst point through the centroid of the others, then expands, contracts or
shrinks according to the new value. It is simple and effective in a few dimensions, but has no general convergence
guarantee.

**Accept some uphill moves.** <NoteLink to="simulated-annealing" /> runs a random walk that always accepts downhill
moves and accepts an uphill move of size $\Delta$ with probability $e^{-\Delta/T}$. At fixed temperature $T$ it samples
the Boltzmann distribution $\propto e^{-f/T}$. Lowering $T$ slowly concentrates the walk on the global minima.

**Adapt a search distribution.** <NoteLink to="evolution-strategies-and-covariance-matrix-adaptation">Evolution
strategies</NoteLink> sample candidates from a Gaussian $\Gauss(\mvec, \sigma^2\Cmat)$ and move the distribution
towards the best of them. CMA-ES also adapts the covariance $\Cmat$ and the step size $\sigma$, learning the shape of
the landscape. Its updates approximate natural-gradient steps on the expected objective.

## How the notes fit together

The three notes are independent. <NoteLink to="nelder-mead" /> is deterministic and local.
<NoteLink to="simulated-annealing" /> adds randomness to escape local minima.
<NoteLink to="evolution-strategies-and-covariance-matrix-adaptation" /> is population-based and needs the
<NoteLink to="multivariate-normal-distribution">multivariate normal distribution</NoteLink> and the
<NoteLink to="score-function-estimator">score-function estimator</NoteLink>, so it is best read last.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume <NoteLink to="gradient-descent">gradient descent</NoteLink> for comparison, and for the later notes the
<NoteLink to="multivariate-normal-distribution">multivariate normal distribution</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="markov-chain" />.

Simulated annealing is a slowly cooled form of <NoteLink to="markov-chain-monte-carlo" />. Evolution strategies estimate
the gradient of the expected objective with the score-function estimator, so they connect to
<NoteLink to="stochastic-optimisation">stochastic optimisation</NoteLink> and to the
<NoteLink to="policy-gradient-theorem">policy gradient theorem</NoteLink> in reinforcement learning. For expensive
objectives such as hyperparameter tuning, <NoteLink to="hyperparameter-search">hyperparameter search</NoteLink> compares
grid and random search.
