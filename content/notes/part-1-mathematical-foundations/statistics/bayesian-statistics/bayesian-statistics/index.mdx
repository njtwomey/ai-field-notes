<Definition>
Bayesian statistics treats every unknown quantity, including a model's parameters, as a
<NoteLink to="random-variable">random variable</NoteLink>. A <Gloss name="prior">prior distribution</Gloss> states what
is believed before the data, the likelihood states how the data depend on the parameters, and Bayes' theorem combines
them into a posterior distribution. Every conclusion, from a point estimate to a prediction or a model comparison, is a
summary of that posterior <Cite id="gelman2013" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The posterior gives uncertainty for free: a full distribution over each parameter instead of a single estimate and a
<NoteLink to="standard-error">standard error</NoteLink>. Bayesian methods suit small samples, where a prior stabilises
estimates, and grouped data, where a hierarchical model shares strength between groups. In machine learning they
underlie Bayesian linear regression, <Gloss name="gp">Gaussian processes</Gloss>, Bayesian neural networks, Thompson
sampling for bandits and Bayesian optimisation. Regularisation has a Bayesian reading: a ridge penalty is a Gaussian
prior and a lasso penalty a Laplace prior, and the penalised estimate is the posterior mode.

## Main ideas

**Posterior is proportional to likelihood times prior.** <NoteLink to="bayesian-inference" /> computes
$p(\theta \mid y) \propto p(y \mid \theta)\,p(\theta)$. Predictions average the model over the posterior,
$p(\tilde y \mid y) = \int p(\tilde y \mid \theta)\,p(\theta \mid y)\,d\theta$, which accounts for uncertainty in
$\theta$.

**Some priors give closed-form posteriors.** With <NoteLink to="conjugate-priors">conjugate priors</NoteLink>, the
posterior stays in the prior's family. Beta–binomial, gamma–Poisson, normal–normal and normal–inverse-gamma updates add
<NoteLink to="sufficient-statistic">sufficient statistics</NoteLink> to the prior's hyperparameters, which act as
pseudo-observations.

**The prior is a modelling choice.** <NoteLink to="prior-choice" /> distinguishes informative priors, which encode real
knowledge, weakly informative priors, which exclude absurd values, and formal rules such as Jeffreys' prior, which is
invariant to reparameterisation.

**Intervals hold the parameter with stated probability.** A credible interval contains the parameter with posterior
probability $1 - \alpha$. The note on <NoteLink to="credible-intervals-and-highest-posterior-density">credible intervals
and HPD regions</NoteLink> compares the equal-tailed interval with the shortest region, the highest posterior density
region.

**Groups share a prior.** <NoteLink to="hierarchical-models" /> give group parameters a common prior whose own
parameters are learned from all groups. Each group's estimate is shrunk towards the grand mean, more strongly when its
own data are noisy. This partial pooling sits between fitting each group alone and pooling all groups together.

**Models are compared by their evidence.** The Bayes factor is the ratio of two models' marginal likelihoods. The note
on <NoteLink to="bayes-factors-and-model-comparison">Bayes factors and Bayesian model comparison</NoteLink> shows how it
turns prior odds into posterior odds, how strongly it depends on the priors, and how <Gloss name="loo">leave-one-out
cross-validation</Gloss> and WAIC compare models by predictive accuracy instead.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume <NoteLink to="bayes-theorem" />, the <NoteLink to="beta-distribution">beta distribution</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="gamma-distribution">gamma distribution</NoteLink>, and the <NoteLink to="gaussian-distribution" />. The
comparison with frequentist methods uses <NoteLink to="confidence-intervals">confidence intervals</NoteLink> and the
<NoteLink to="p-value">p-value</NoteLink>.

Most posteriors have no closed form. <NoteLink to="markov-chain-monte-carlo" /> samples them,
<NoteLink to="variational-inference">variational inference</NoteLink> approximates them by optimisation, and the
<NoteLink to="laplace-approximation" /> fits a Gaussian at the mode. <NoteLink to="probabilistic-programming" />
automates all three. <NoteLink to="bayesian-linear-regression" /> and <NoteLink to="thompson-sampling">Thompson
sampling</NoteLink> apply the conjugate updates to regression and bandits.
