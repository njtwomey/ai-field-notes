<Definition>
Statistics draws conclusions about a population, or about the process that generated some data, from a finite sample.
Where probability starts from a known distribution and describes the data it produces, statistics starts from the
data and infers the distribution. Its results are estimates with stated uncertainty, decisions with controlled error
rates, and designs that make data informative <Cite id="wasserman2004" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Every claim about a model's performance is a statistical claim. A test-set accuracy is an estimate and needs a
<NoteLink to="confidence-intervals">confidence interval</NoteLink>. Saying that one model beats another needs a test,
and comparing many models needs a correction for <NoteLink to="multiple-testing">multiple testing</NoteLink>. An A/B
test of a product change is a <NoteLink to="randomised-controlled-experiments">randomised experiment</NoteLink>.
Monitoring a deployed model for drift is a <NoteLink to="two-sample-testing">two-sample test</NoteLink>. The fitting of
models is itself estimation: <Gloss name="mle">maximum likelihood</Gloss>, <NoteLink to="bayesian-inference">Bayesian
inference</NoteLink> and the <NoteLink to="bootstrap">bootstrap</NoteLink> are statistical methods that machine learning
inherited and scaled up.

## Main ideas

**Estimates come with uncertainty.** An estimator is a <NoteLink to="random-variable">random variable</NoteLink> with a
sampling distribution. Its <NoteLink to="standard-error">standard error</NoteLink> and the confidence intervals built
from it say how far the estimate is likely to be from the truth.

**Tests control error rates.** A hypothesis test rejects a null hypothesis when the data would be surprising under it.
The <NoteLink to="significance-level">significance level</NoteLink> bounds false rejections, power measures true
detections, and the sample size trades one against the other.

**Two schools of inference.** Frequentist methods treat parameters as fixed and reason about repeated samples. Bayesian
methods treat parameters as random and reason about the posterior. Both appear in the notes, and the comparison between
confidence and <NoteLink to="credible-intervals-and-highest-posterior-density">credible intervals</NoteLink> makes the
difference concrete.

**Simulation replaces formulas.** The bootstrap and <NoteLink to="permutation-tests">permutation tests</NoteLink>
compute standard errors, intervals and <NoteLink to="p-value">p-values</NoteLink> by recomputing a statistic on
resampled data, for statistics whose distribution has no closed form.

**Design comes before analysis.** Randomisation makes causal conclusions possible, blocking reduces variance, and
<NoteLink to="censoring">censoring</NoteLink> and confounding must be handled in the analysis when the design cannot
remove them.
