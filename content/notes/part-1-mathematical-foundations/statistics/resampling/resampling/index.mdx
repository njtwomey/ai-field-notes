<Definition>
Resampling methods estimate the uncertainty of a statistic by recomputing it on many altered copies of the data. The
copies leave observations out, draw them with replacement, simulate them from a fitted model or shuffle their labels.
The spread of the recomputed values replaces a formula for the sampling distribution, so the methods work for
statistics whose distribution has no closed form <Cite id="efron1993" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Most quantities in machine learning evaluation have no textbook standard error: an F1 score, an area under a curve, a
ranking metric, the difference between two models on the same test set. The bootstrap gives confidence intervals for all
of them. Permutation tests decide whether two models or two groups differ without assuming normality. Leave-one-out
ideas from the jackknife reappear in cross-validation and influence measures. Bagging and
<NoteLink to="random-forest">random forests</NoteLink> train each model on a bootstrap resample.

## Main ideas

**Leave one out.** The <NoteLink to="jackknife">jackknife</NoteLink> recomputes an estimator $n$ times, each time
without one observation. The spread of the $n$ values estimates the variance, and their mean corrects the $1/n$ term of
the bias. It fails for statistics that are not smooth functions of the data, such as the median.

**Resample with replacement.** The <NoteLink to="bootstrap">bootstrap</NoteLink> treats the sample as a stand-in for the
population and draws $B$ resamples of size $n$ from it with replacement. The recomputed statistics approximate its
sampling distribution and give standard errors and percentile, basic and BCa intervals. It fails for extremes and for
dependent data unless the resampling respects the dependence.

**Simulate from a fitted model.** The <NoteLink to="parametric-bootstrap-and-bootstrap-hypothesis-tests">parametric
bootstrap</NoteLink> draws new data sets from a model fitted to the data. A bootstrap hypothesis test simulates from a
distribution that satisfies the null, usually the recentred data, and uses a studentised statistic.

**Shuffle the labels.** A <NoteLink to="permutation-tests">permutation test</NoteLink> compares a statistic with its
values under relabellings that the null hypothesis makes equally likely. Under exchangeability its p-value is exact for
any statistic and any sample size. With $B$ random permutations, $b$ of them at least as extreme, the p-value
$(1 + b)/(1 + B)$ is valid.

## How the notes fit together

Start with the <NoteLink to="jackknife">jackknife</NoteLink>, the oldest and simplest method, then the
<NoteLink to="bootstrap">bootstrap</NoteLink>, which generalises it. The note on the
<NoteLink to="parametric-bootstrap-and-bootstrap-hypothesis-tests">parametric bootstrap and bootstrap hypothesis
tests</NoteLink> extends the bootstrap from intervals to tests. <NoteLink to="permutation-tests" /> answer the testing
question directly and can be read after the p-value alone.
