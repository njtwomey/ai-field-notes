<Definition>
Let $V_1 \sim \ChiSq_{d_1}$ and $V_2 \sim \ChiSq_{d_2}$ be independent. The ratio

$$
F = \frac{V_1/d_1}{V_2/d_2}
$$

has an F distribution with $d_1$ numerator and $d_2$ denominator degrees of freedom, written $F \sim \FDist(d_1, d_2)$.
Its density is

$$
p(x \mid d_1, d_2) = \frac{1}{\mathrm{B}\paren{\frac{d_1}{2}, \frac{d_2}{2}}} \paren{\frac{d_1}{d_2}}^{d_1/2} x^{d_1/2 -
1}
\paren{1 + \frac{d_1}{d_2}x}^{-(d_1 + d_2)/2}, \qquad x > 0.
$$
</Definition>

## Where it comes from

The F distribution compares two variance estimates. Let $S_1^2$ and $S_2^2$ be sample variances from independent
Gaussian samples of sizes $n_1$ and $n_2$ with common variance $\sigma^2$. Each $(n_i - 1)S_i^2/\sigma^2$ is
$\ChiSq_{n_i - 1}$, so the unknown $\sigma^2$ cancels in the ratio:

$$
\frac{S_1^2}{S_2^2} \sim \FDist(n_1 - 1, n_2 - 1).
$$

The density follows from the beta distribution. A $\ChiSq_k$ variable is $\GammaD(k/2, 1/2)$, so $V_1$ and $V_2$ are
independent gammas with a common rate, and $X = V_1/(V_1 + V_2)$ is
<NoteLink to="beta-distribution">$\Beta(d_1/2, d_2/2)$</NoteLink>. Since
$F = \frac{d_2}{d_1}\cdot\frac{V_1}{V_2} = \frac{d_2}{d_1}\cdot\frac{X}{1 - X}$, invert to $X = d_1F/(d_1F + d_2)$ and
change variables. The <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> is $dx/dF = d_1 d_2/(d_1F + d_2)^2$, so

$$
p(F) = \frac{x^{d_1/2 - 1}(1 - x)^{d_2/2 - 1}}{\mathrm{B}\paren{\frac{d_1}{2}, \frac{d_2}{2}}}
\cdot\frac{d_1 d_2}{(d_1F + d_2)^2},
\qquad x = \frac{d_1F}{d_1F + d_2},\ 1 - x = \frac{d_2}{d_1F + d_2}.
$$

Collecting the powers of $d_1F$, $d_2$ and $d_1F + d_2$ gives the density in the definition.

For example, two independent samples of 10 observations give sample variances with ratio $S_1^2/S_2^2 = 2.5$. Under
equal variances this ratio is $\FDist(9, 9)$, and $P(\FDist(9, 9) > 2.5) \approx 0.094$. A ratio of 2.5 is not unusual
with samples this small; the 5% critical value is $3.18$.

Ronald Fisher derived the distribution of a closely related statistic in the 1920s <Cite id="fisher1924" />, and George
Snedecor tabulated the ratio in its present form and named it F after Fisher <Cite id="johnson1995" />.

## Properties

**Mean and variance.** $V_1$ and $V_2$ are independent, so $\expect[F] = \frac{d_2}{d_1}\expect[V_1]\,\expect[1/V_2]$.
With $\expect[V_1] = d_1$ and $\expect[1/V_2] = 1/(d_2 - 2)$ for $d_2 > 2$ (from the gamma moment formula, as for the
Student t), the mean is $d_2/(d_2 - 2)$. For the second moment, $\expect[V_1^2] = \var(V_1) + d_1^2 = d_1(d_1 + 2)$ and
$\expect[1/V_2^2] = 1/\paren{(d_2 - 2)(d_2 - 4)}$ for $d_2 > 4$. So

$$
\expect[F^2] = \frac{d_2^2}{d_1^2} \cdot \frac{d_1(d_1 + 2)}{(d_2 - 2)(d_2 - 4)},
$$

and subtracting $\expect[F]^2$ gives the variance in the table. The mean does not depend on $d_1$.

**Location near 1.** Under the <NoteLink to="null-and-alternative-hypotheses">null hypothesis</NoteLink> of equal
variances, both numerator and denominator estimate the same quantity, so $F$ is centred near 1. The mean $d_2/(d_2 - 2)$
tends to 1 as $d_2$ grows.

**Reciprocal.** If $F \sim \FDist(d_1, d_2)$, then $1/F = (V_2/d_2)/(V_1/d_1) \sim \FDist(d_2, d_1)$, by the definition
with the roles swapped. Lower-tail quantiles follow from
upper-tail quantiles with the degrees of freedom swapped.

**Limit.** As $d_2 \to \infty$, $V_2/d_2 \to 1$, so $d_1 F \to \ChiSq_{d_1}$ in distribution.


## Relations to other distributions

- **χ².** The definition. See the <NoteLink to="chi-squared-distribution">χ² distribution</NoteLink>.
- **Student t.** If $T \sim$ <NoteLink to="student-t-distribution">$\StudentT_\nu$</NoteLink>, then
  $T^2 \sim \FDist(1, \nu)$. A two-sided t-test is an F-test with one numerator degree of freedom.
- **Beta.** If $F \sim \FDist(d_1, d_2)$, then
  $\dfrac{d_1 F}{d_1 F + d_2} \sim \Beta\paren{\tfrac{d_1}{2}, \tfrac{d_2}{2}}$. This is how the cdf above is computed.

## In machine learning

- **Nested linear models.** The F-test compares a <NoteLink to="linear-regression">linear regression</NoteLink> with
  $p_0$ coefficients to a larger one with $p_1$ coefficients. With residual sums of squares $\text{RSS}_0$ and
  $\text{RSS}_1$ on $n$ points,
  $\dfrac{(\text{RSS}_0 - \text{RSS}_1)/(p_1 - p_0)}{\text{RSS}_1/(n - p_1)} \sim \FDist(p_1 - p_0, n - p_1)$ under the
  smaller model <Cite id="wasserman2004" />.
- **<Gloss name="anova" /> and feature scoring.** One-way ANOVA tests whether group means differ with an F statistic.
  scikit-learn's `f_classif` and `f_regression` score features this way.

## Pitfalls

- **Order of the degrees of freedom.** $\FDist(d_1, d_2)$ and $\FDist(d_2, d_1)$ differ. The numerator's degrees of
  freedom come first.
- **Sensitivity to non-normality.** The variance-ratio F-test assumes Gaussian data and is badly affected by heavy
  tails. Levene's or the Brown–Forsythe test are more robust ways to compare variances.
