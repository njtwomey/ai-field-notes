<Definition>
A multivariate distribution is a joint distribution of several <NoteLink to="random-variable">random
variables</NoteLink>, treated as one random vector or matrix. Besides the spread of each component, it describes how the
components depend on one another. The named multivariate distributions extend the Gaussian to vectors, the beta to
probability vectors, and the chi-squared and gamma to positive-definite matrices
<Cite id="bishop2006" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Data in machine learning are vectors, so models need distributions over vectors. The multivariate normal is the noise
model of <NoteLink to="linear-regression">linear regression</NoteLink>, the components of a Gaussian mixture, the prior
and posterior of a <Gloss name="gp">Gaussian process</Gloss>, the latent space of a <Gloss name="vae">variational
autoencoder</Gloss> and the <NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">Laplace approximation</NoteLink> to a posterior. The
Dirichlet is the prior over topic proportions in <Gloss name="latent-dirichlet-allocation">latent Dirichlet
allocation</Gloss> and over class probabilities in Bayesian classifiers. The Wishart is the prior over the
<Gloss name="precision-matrix">precision matrix</Gloss> of a Gaussian, and the distribution of the scatter matrix of
Gaussian data.

## Main ideas

**A mean vector and a covariance matrix define a Gaussian.** The
<NoteLink to="multivariate-normal-distribution">multivariate normal distribution</NoteLink> on $\reals^d$ has a mean
$\muvec \in \reals^d$ and a positive-definite covariance $\Sigmamat \in \reals^{d \times d}$. Its density contours are
ellipsoids whose axes are the eigenvectors of $\Sigmamat$. It stays Gaussian under linear maps, marginalisation and
conditioning, and the conditional mean is linear in the observed components. These closure properties make inference in
linear-Gaussian models exact.

**A distribution over probability vectors.** The <NoteLink to="dirichlet-distribution" /> lives on the simplex of
vectors with nonnegative entries that sum to one. It has one positive parameter per category and generalises the
<NoteLink to="beta-distribution">beta distribution</NoteLink> to $K$ categories. It is the conjugate prior for
categorical and multinomial data, so its parameters act as prior counts.

**A distribution over covariance matrices.** The <NoteLink to="wishart-distribution" /> lives on positive-definite
matrices. With $\nu$ degrees of freedom and scale matrix $\Vmat$, it is the distribution of the scatter matrix
$\sum_{i=1}^{\nu} \xvec_i \xvec_i^\top$ of independent zero-mean Gaussian vectors with covariance $\Vmat$. It
generalises the chi-squared distribution to matrices and is the conjugate prior for the precision matrix of a
multivariate normal.

**Dependence lives in the off-diagonal entries.** The covariance of a Gaussian records linear dependence between
components. The entries of a Dirichlet vector are negatively correlated, because they must sum to one. A Wishart matrix
describes how a whole covariance structure varies.

## How the notes fit together

Start with the <NoteLink to="multivariate-normal-distribution">multivariate normal distribution</NoteLink>, which uses
the most linear algebra. The <NoteLink to="dirichlet-distribution" /> can be read independently after the beta
distribution. The <NoteLink to="wishart-distribution" /> builds on both the multivariate normal and the chi-squared
distribution, and comes last.
