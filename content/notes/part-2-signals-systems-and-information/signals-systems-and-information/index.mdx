<Definition>
**Signals, systems and information** collects three engineering theories that machine learning borrows from.
*Information theory* measures uncertainty and dependence, and sets the limits of compression and communication
<Cite id="cover2006" />. *Signal processing* represents, filters and analyses sampled signals, mostly through their
frequency content <Cite id="oppenheim2010" />. *Control theory* models dynamical systems and designs feedback that
makes them behave <Cite id="astrom2021" />. All three describe systems by how they transform an input into an output.
</Definition>

## What it is for

These theories supply three things to machine learning.

- **Losses and measures.** Cross-entropy, the Kullback–Leibler divergence and mutual information are the standard
  training objectives and analysis tools of probabilistic models. They come from information theory, where they have
  exact operational meanings as code lengths and rates.
- **<NoteLink to="data-preparation">Data preparation</NoteLink>.** Audio, sensor streams, physiological recordings and
  other measured signals are sampled, filtered, resampled and turned into spectrograms before a model sees them. Signal
  processing says what each step keeps and what it destroys.
- **Model structure.** <NoteLink to="convolutional-layer">Convolutional layers</NoteLink> are learned filters,
  structured state-space sequence models are <NoteLink to="linear-gaussian-state-space-models">linear dynamical
  systems</NoteLink> from control theory, and reinforcement learning solves the optimal control problem when the model
  is unknown.

## Main ideas

**Uncertainty has a unit.** Information theory measures uncertainty in bits. The <NoteLink to="entropy" /> of a source
is the shortest average code length for it, and the <NoteLink to="kullback-leibler-divergence" /> is the extra code
length paid for using the wrong distribution. <NoteLink to="mutual-information" /> measures how much one variable tells
about another. These quantities turn into theorems about what is possible: the
<NoteLink to="source-coding-theorem" />, <NoteLink to="channel-capacity" /> and
<NoteLink to="rate-distortion-theory" />.

**Linear time-invariant systems are described by frequency.** A system that is linear and does not change over time is
fixed by its impulse response, and acts by <NoteLink to="convolution" />. Sinusoids pass through such a system with
only their amplitude and phase changed, so the <NoteLink to="fourier-transform" /> turns convolution into
multiplication. This one fact organises filter design, spectral estimation, sampling theory and much of control.

**Sampling and finite records have limits.** A band-limited signal is recovered exactly from samples at more than twice
its bandwidth, and faster content <NoteLink to="aliasing">aliases</NoteLink>. A finite record blurs the spectrum, and
time and <NoteLink to="zero-padding-and-resolution">frequency resolution</NoteLink> trade against each other by the
<NoteLink to="time-frequency-uncertainty">uncertainty bound</NoteLink>. Wavelets and adaptive decompositions choose
different trade-offs at different scales.

**Optimal estimation and adaptation.** When signals are random, the best linear filter follows from their second-order
statistics: the <NoteLink to="wiener-filter" /> for estimation, the <NoteLink to="matched-filter" /> for detection,
the <NoteLink to="kalman-filter" /> for a state that evolves. The <NoteLink to="least-mean-squares-filter" /> learns
such a filter online by stochastic gradient descent.

**Feedback makes systems robust, and can make them unstable.** Control theory closes a loop from measured outputs to
inputs. A loop must first be stable, then accurate and robust to model error. Stability is decided by pole locations
or by a Lyapunov function, robustness by margins or worst-case gains, and the best controller for a cost by dynamic
programming or Pontryagin's principle.

## How the notes fit together

The Part has three topics, which can be read in any order.

- <NoteLink to="information-theory" /> needs only probability. It is the most directly used in machine learning and is
  a good place to start.
- <NoteLink to="signal-processing" /> starts with <NoteLink to="signals-and-systems" /> and
  <NoteLink to="fourier-analysis" />, then branches into <NoteLink to="digital-filters" />,
  <NoteLink to="multirate-processing-and-filter-banks" />, <NoteLink to="time-frequency-analysis" />,
  <NoteLink to="wavelets" />, <NoteLink to="sparse-approximation">sparse representations</NoteLink>,
  <NoteLink to="spectral-estimation" />, <NoteLink to="adaptive-and-statistical-signal-processing" /> and
  <NoteLink to="audio-signal-processing" />.
- <NoteLink to="control-theory" /> starts with <NoteLink to="system-modelling" /> and
  <NoteLink to="stability-analysis" />, then follows a frequency-domain path through <NoteLink to="classical-control" />
  and a state-space path through <NoteLink to="state-space-control" />, <NoteLink to="optimal-control" /> and
  <NoteLink to="model-predictive-control" />, with <NoteLink to="robust-and-nonlinear-control" /> and
  <NoteLink to="autoregressive-exogenous-models-and-prediction-error-methods">system identification</NoteLink>
  alongside.

The topics share vocabulary. The <NoteLink to="transfer-function-and-block-diagrams">transfer function</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="frequency-response">frequency response</NoteLink> appear in discrete time in signal processing and in
continuous time in control. The Kalman filter is both a statistical signal processing method and the observer of optimal
control. <NoteLink to="quantisation">Quantisation</NoteLink> and transform coding connect signal processing to the
coding theorems of information theory.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The Part assumes the probability of Part I, in particular <NoteLink to="random-variable" />,
<NoteLink to="expectation" /> and <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance" />, and its
<NoteLink to="linear-algebra">linear algebra</NoteLink> and calculus, especially <NoteLink to="eigendecomposition" />,
complex numbers and <NoteLink to="eulers-formula" />, and <NoteLink to="ordinary-differential-equations" />.

Its ideas return throughout the rest of the book. Information measures define the losses of Part IV and the objectives
of <NoteLink to="variational-inference" /> and generative models. Signal processing underlies the preparation of
audio, sensor and time-series data in Part III and the time-series models of Part VII. Control theory leads into
<NoteLink to="reinforcement-learning" />, and its linear state-space models reappear as the
<NoteLink to="structured-state-space-models" /> of <NoteLink to="neural-architectures" />.
