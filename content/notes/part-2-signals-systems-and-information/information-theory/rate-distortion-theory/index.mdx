<Definition>
Given an information source $X \sim p(x)$, a reconstruction alphabet $\hat{\Xcal}$, and a distortion measure $d(x, \hat{x}) \ge 0$, the **rate-distortion function** $R(D)$ is the minimum <NoteLink to="mutual-information">mutual information</NoteLink> between the source $X$ and reproduction $\hat{X}$ over all conditional distributions $p(\hat{x} \mid x)$ satisfying an expected distortion constraint $\expect[d(X, \hat{X})] \le D$:

$$
R(D) = \min_{p(\hat{x} \mid x):\, \sum_{x, \hat{x}} p(x) p(\hat{x} \mid x) d(x, \hat{x}) \le D} I(X; \hat{X}).
$$

Shannon's lossy source coding theorem establishes that $R(D)$ is the infimum of rates achievable by lossy block codes operating under distortion budget $D$ <Cite id="shannon1948" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

When compressing data losslessly (such as text or executable code), Shannon's <NoteLink to="source-coding-theorem">source coding theorem</NoteLink> proves that the description rate cannot be reduced below the source <NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink> $H(X)$ without infinite probability of error.

However, continuous signals like audio, images, and video naturally tolerate slight degradation that is imperceptible to human senses or acceptable for downstream tasks. **Rate-distortion theory** formalizes this trade-off: if we allow an average fidelity loss $D$ (measured by squared error, Hamming distance, or perceptual loss), how many fewer bits per symbol do we need to store or transmit?

As $D \to 0$, $R(D)$ recovers the lossless entropy limit $H(X)$. As the distortion tolerance $D$ increases, the required rate $R(D)$ decreases monotonically and convexly until reaching $0$ at the trivial distortion $D_{\max} = \min_{\hat{x}} \expect[d(X, \hat{x})]$, where the compressor transmits zero information and simply outputs the constant centroid.

## Binary Source with Hamming Distortion

For an independent and identically distributed Bernoulli source $X \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ with $p \le 1/2$ and Hamming distortion $d(x, \hat{x}) = \mathbf{1}_{\{x \ne \hat{x}\}}$, the rate-distortion function has a classic closed form:

$$
R(D) = \begin{cases}
H_2(p) - H_2(D), & 0 \le D \le p, \\
0, & D > p,
\end{cases}
$$

where $H_2(q) = -q \log_2 q - (1 - q) \log_2(1 - q)$ is the binary entropy function.

When no distortion is tolerated ($D = 0$), $R(0) = H_2(p)$ bits/symbol. When the allowed error rate equals the marginal probability $D = p$, the receiver can simply guess $\hat{x} = 0$ every time without receiving any bits, achieving rate $R = 0$ at distortion $p$.

## Gaussian Source with Mean Squared Error

For a continuous Gaussian source $X \sim \Ncal(0, \sigma^2)$ under squared-error distortion $d(x, \hat{x}) = (x - \hat{x})^2$:

$$
R(D) = \begin{cases}
\frac{1}{2}\log_2 \frac{\sigma^2}{D}, & 0 \le D \le \sigma^2, \\
0, & D > \sigma^2.
\end{cases}
$$

Each additional bit of rate halves the reconstruction mean squared error (a 6 dB improvement in signal-to-noise ratio per bit).

## Computing $R(D)$ via the Blahut–Arimoto Algorithm

Because the objective $I(X; \hat{X})$ is convex in the conditional distribution $p(\hat{x} \mid x)$ and the constraint set is convex, the optimization can be formulated using a Lagrange multiplier $\beta \ge 0$ on the distortion:

$$
\min_{p(\hat{x} \mid x)} \left\{ I(X; \hat{X}) + \beta \expect[d(X, \hat{X})] \right\}.
$$

The **Blahut–Arimoto rate-distortion algorithm** alternates between computing the output marginal $q(\hat{x}) = \sum_x p(x) p(\hat{x} \mid x)$ and updating the conditional probabilities:

$$
p^{(k+1)}(\hat{x} \mid x) = \frac{q^{(k)}(\hat{x}) e^{-\beta d(x, \hat{x})}}{\sum_{\hat{x}'} q^{(k)}(\hat{x}') e^{-\beta d(x, \hat{x}')}}.
$$

Varying the slope parameter $\beta \in (0, \infty)$ traces the entire boundary of the rate-distortion region $R(D)$ point by point <Cite id="cover2006" />.
