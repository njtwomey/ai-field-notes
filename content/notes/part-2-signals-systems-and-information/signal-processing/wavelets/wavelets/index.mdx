<Definition>
A **wavelet** is a short oscillating function $\psi(t)$ with zero mean. A wavelet transform correlates a signal with
copies of $\psi$ shifted in time and stretched or compressed in scale. Small scales give short wavelets that locate fast
events precisely; large scales give long wavelets that resolve low frequencies finely. The discrete form, built on
nested approximation spaces, gives orthogonal bases computed in $O(N)$ operations <Cite id="mallat2009" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The <Gloss name="stft">short-time Fourier transform</Gloss> uses one window length for every frequency. Many signals
need different resolutions at different frequencies: a click lasts milliseconds while a slow trend lasts minutes.
Wavelets adapt the window to the scale, giving fine time resolution at high frequencies and fine
<NoteLink to="zero-padding-and-resolution">frequency resolution</NoteLink> at low ones.

Wavelet coefficients of piecewise smooth signals and images are sparse: most are near zero, and the few large ones
sit at edges and jumps. Sparsity is what makes wavelets work for compression, as in the JPEG 2000 image standard, and
for denoising by thresholding <Cite id="daubechies1992" />. In machine learning, wavelet coefficients serve as
features for time series and images, and the sparsity they exhibit underlies <NoteLink to="compressed-sensing" />.

## Main ideas

**Scale replaces frequency.** The <NoteLink to="continuous-wavelet-transform" />
$W(a, b) = a^{-1/2} \int x(t)\, \psi^*((t - b)/a)\, dt$ measures how much the signal near time $b$ resembles the
wavelet at scale $a$. Its magnitude, the scalogram, is a time-scale picture of the signal. A wavelet must satisfy an
admissibility condition for the transform to be invertible.

**Powers of two and a filter-bank pyramid.** The <NoteLink to="discrete-wavelet-transform" /> samples scales at powers
of two. Mallat's pyramid computes it by repeatedly splitting the approximation with a low-pass and a high-pass filter
and downsampling by two. For orthogonal wavelets the transform is orthogonal and costs $O(N)$.

**Nested approximations.** A <NoteLink to="multiresolution-analysis" /> is a sequence of spaces of signals at
successively finer resolution, each spanned by shifts of one scaling function. The two-scale equation ties the scaling
function to a filter, and the wavelets span the detail added between one level and the next. This structure explains
why the filter bank computes the transform.

**Families trade properties.** <NoteLink to="wavelet-families" /> compares support, vanishing moments, smoothness and
symmetry. Haar is the shortest; Daubechies wavelets have the most vanishing moments for their support; symlets,
coiflets and biorthogonal wavelets relax other constraints. Morlet and Mexican hat wavelets are used with the
continuous transform.

**Adapting the basis and exploiting sparsity.** <NoteLink to="wavelet-packets" /> split the detail bands too, giving
a library of orthonormal bases from which a best-basis search picks the sparsest. <NoteLink to="wavelet-denoising" />
shrinks small coefficients towards zero and inverts the transform; the universal threshold $\sigma\sqrt{2 \ln N}$
removes nearly all pure-noise coefficients while keeping those that carry edges. Soft thresholding is the same
operation as the proximal step of the <NoteLink to="lasso" />.
