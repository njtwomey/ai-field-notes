<Definition>
**Multirate processing** handles signals at more than one sampling rate within one system. Its two basic operations
are downsampling by an integer $M$, which keeps every $M$-th sample, and upsampling by an integer $L$, which inserts
$L - 1$ zeros between samples. A **filter bank** uses them to split a signal into frequency bands, process each band at
a reduced rate, and recombine the bands <Cite id="vaidyanathan1993" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Signals often arrive at one rate and are needed at another. Audio recorded at 44.1 kHz is resampled to 48 kHz for
playback or to 16 kHz for a speech model. A signal whose band of interest is narrow can be processed at a much lower
rate after filtering, which cuts the computation. Splitting a signal into subbands lets a coder spend bits where the
ear or the eye notices errors, and lets an analysis examine each band separately <Cite id="crochiere1981" />.

Neural networks change rates too. Strided convolutions and pooling downsample feature maps, and, as noted in
<NoteLink to="pooling-and-strides" />, striding without a low-pass filter aliases high frequencies. Upsampling layers
in decoders insert samples and must suppress the resulting spectral images. Resampling audio to the rate a model was
trained on is a routine preprocessing step for speech and audio models.

## Main ideas

**Rate changes fold or copy the spectrum.** Downsampling by $M$ stretches the spectrum and overlays $M$ shifted copies,
so content above $\pi/M$ aliases. Upsampling by $L$ compresses the spectrum and creates $L - 1$ images. A decimator
places a low-pass filter before the downsampler and an interpolator places one after the upsampler.
<NoteLink to="decimation-and-interpolation" /> derives both spectra and the filters that make the operations safe.

**Filter at the low rate.** A filter followed by a downsampler computes many outputs only to discard them. The
<NoteLink to="polyphase-decomposition" /> splits a filter into $M$ subfilters of every $M$-th coefficient. With the
noble identities, which move a filter across a rate changer, the subfilters run at the low rate and the cost falls by a
factor of $M$.

**Rational conversion needs one filter.** Changing the rate by $L/M$ upsamples by $L$, filters once with cutoff
$\min(\pi/L, \pi/M)$, and downsamples by $M$. <NoteLink to="sample-rate-conversion" /> shows how a polyphase
implementation computes only the outputs that are kept, which keeps the conversion from 44.1 kHz to 48 kHz cheap.

**Analysis and synthesis banks.** A <NoteLink to="filter-banks">filter bank</NoteLink> splits a signal with analysis
filters, usually downsamples each band, and recombines with synthesis filters. A uniform DFT filter bank modulates one
prototype low-pass filter to $K$ centre frequencies and runs as a polyphase network followed by an <Gloss name="fft" />.

**Aliasing can be cancelled exactly.** Each downsampled band aliases, yet the bank as a whole can reconstruct its input
exactly up to a delay. <NoteLink to="perfect-reconstruction-filter-banks" /> derives the conditions for two channels:
synthesis filters that cancel the aliasing, and a remaining transfer function that is a pure delay. Iterated on the
low band, orthogonal two-channel banks of this kind compute the discrete wavelet transform.
