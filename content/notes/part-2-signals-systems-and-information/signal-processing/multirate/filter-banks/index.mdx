<Definition>
A **filter bank** is a set of filters $H_0, \dots, H_{K-1}$ whose outputs, the **subband signals**, divide the
spectrum of the input into bands. In a **maximally decimated** or **critically sampled** bank each subband is
downsampled by $M = K$, so the total sample rate is unchanged. A **synthesis bank** of filters $G_k$, after
upsampling, recombines the subbands <Cite id="vaidyanathan1993" />.
</Definition>

## Analysis and synthesis

The analysis bank computes $v_k = (h_k * x)$ downsampled by $M$. The synthesis bank upsamples each $v_k$ by $M$,
filters with $g_k$ and sums. Between them, a coder can quantise each band to a different precision, or an equaliser
can scale each band.

In the $z$-domain, each branch applies $H_k$, downsamples and upsamples, then applies $G_k$. Downsampling followed by
upsampling by $M$ replaces $V(z)$ by $\frac1M \sum_{l=0}^{M-1} V\paren{z W^l}$ with $W = e^{-i2\pi/M}$: it keeps the
samples at multiples of $M$, and the sum of roots of unity is the comb that does so. The output is therefore

$$
\hat X(z) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{l=0}^{M-1} X\paren{zW^l} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} G_k(z)\, H_k\paren{zW^l}.
$$

The $l = 0$ term is the wanted, filtered signal. The terms $l \ne 0$ are <NoteLink to="aliasing">aliasing</NoteLink>.
Designing a bank means choosing filters so the aliasing terms cancel and the $l = 0$ term is a pure delay; the
<NoteLink to="perfect-reconstruction-filter-banks">perfect-reconstruction note</NoteLink> derives the conditions for two
channels.

## Uniform DFT filter banks

The most used design modulates a single prototype lowpass $h[n]$ to equally spaced centre frequencies:

$$
h_k[n] = h[n]\, e^{i 2\pi k n / K}, \qquad H_k(z) = H\paren{z\, e^{-i 2\pi k / K}}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, K - 1.
$$

The $k$-th output at time $n$ is $\sum_m h[m]\, x[n - m]\, e^{i 2\pi k m / K}$. Write $m = rK + q$ and use
$e^{i2\pi k (rK + q)/K} = e^{i2\pi kq/K}$:

$$
y_k[n] = \sum_{q=0}^{K-1} e^{i 2\pi k q / K} \underbrace{\sum_r h[rK + q]\, x[n - rK - q]}_{u_q[n]}.
$$

The inner sums $u_q$ are the outputs of the $K$ <NoteLink to="polyphase-decomposition">polyphase components</NoteLink>
of the prototype. The outer sum is an inverse DFT of length $K$ over $q$. So all $K$ channels together cost one pass
through the polyphase network, which costs as much as filtering once with the prototype, plus one $K$-point
<Gloss name="fft" />. This is how channelisers in radio receivers and many audio coders are built
<Cite id="crochiere1981" />.

The short-time Fourier transform is a <Gloss name="dft" /> filter bank whose prototype is the analysis window,
time-reversed; see <NoteLink to="short-time-fourier-transform">short-time Fourier transform</NoteLink>.

**Worked example.** A $K = 8$ channel DFT bank with a 64-tap prototype, decimated by $M = 8$. Filtering with eight
separate 64-tap filters costs $8 \times 64 = 512$ multiplications per input sample before decimation. With the
polyphase network running at the low rate, the eight 8-tap components cost 64 multiplications per block of eight
inputs, and an 8-point FFT adds about $\tfrac{8}{2} \log_2 8 = 12$ complex multiplications per block. Per input
sample that is $8$ real multiplications plus $1.5$ complex ones, instead of 512.

## Worked example: a signal in bands

Take half a second of audio sampled at $f_s = 8$ kHz, made of four events in turn: a 500 Hz tone, a chirp rising
from 1 to 3.5 kHz, a click, and finally two tones at 1.5 kHz and 3 kHz sounding together. Pass it through a
$K = 16$ channel DFT filter bank. Channel $k$ is centred on $k f_s / K = 500k$ Hz and is $f_s / K = 500$ Hz wide, so
the bank reads the spectrum in 500 Hz slices, and each slice is a signal in time:

- **The tone** at 500 Hz sits in band 1. For the first 0.15 s, band 1 carries a steady output and the others are
  nearly silent.
- **The chirp** moves through the bands. Its <NoteLink to="instantaneous-frequency">instantaneous frequency</NoteLink>
  rises by 12.5 kHz per second, so it crosses one 500 Hz band every 40 ms, and bands 2 to 7 light up in turn.
- **The click** lasts one millisecond. A signal that short has a broad spectrum, so every band responds at once, for
  about the length of the prototype filter.
- **The two final tones** land in different bands, 3 and 6. They are separate signals in the bank's output although
  they overlap completely in time.

A tone between two band centres appears in both neighbouring bands, because the prototype's passband is not a
brick wall: its response falls smoothly across each band edge. A tone at 700 Hz, near the 750 Hz edge between bands 1
and 2, shows up in both. Longer prototypes with steeper edges reduce this leakage.

This is the same picture a spectrogram draws, and for the same reason: a short-time Fourier transform is a DFT filter
bank. The bank's band width sets its <NoteLink to="zero-padding-and-resolution">frequency resolution</NoteLink>, and the
prototype's length sets how far an event is smeared in time. Doubling $K$ halves the band width and doubles the
prototype length, which trades time resolution for frequency resolution, as the channel slider shows.

**Putting the bands back together.** Summing all $K$ undecimated channels gives

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} y_k[n] = \sum_j h[j]\, x[n - j] \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} e^{i 2\pi k j / K} = K \sum_{r} h[rK]\, x[n - rK],
$$

because the inner sum of roots of unity is $K$ when $K$ divides $j$ and 0 otherwise. So the bands add back to the input
exactly when the prototype is a **Nyquist($K$) filter**: its taps at a spacing of $K$ from its centre $D$ are zero,
except $h[D] = 1/K$. Then $\sum_k y_k[n] = x[n - D]$, a pure delay. A
<NoteLink to="finite-impulse-response-filter-design">windowed sinc</NoteLink> with cutoff $\pi/K$ has this property by
construction: $\operatorname{sinc}\paren{(j - D)/K}$ vanishes at every nonzero multiple of $K$ from the centre, and the
window leaves the zeros in place. In the figure the recombined signal matches the delayed input to
<NoteLink to="floating-point-arithmetic">rounding error</NoteLink>. Once the channels are decimated, exact
reconstruction needs more: the synthesis filters must also cancel the aliasing terms of the previous section.

## Other designs

- **Cosine-modulated filter banks** use real cosine modulation and are the basis of MPEG audio coding and the
  modified discrete cosine transform. See <NoteLink to="perceptual-audio-coding">perceptual audio coding</NoteLink>.
- **Tree-structured banks** iterate a two-channel split, usually on the lowpass branch only, giving octave bands:
  this is the <NoteLink to="discrete-wavelet-transform">discrete wavelet transform</NoteLink>.
- **Auditory filter banks** space bands on a perceptual scale, such as the
  <NoteLink to="mel-filter-bank">mel filter bank</NoteLink> or the
  <NoteLink to="gammatone-filter-bank">gammatone filter bank</NoteLink>. They are usually not decimated or inverted.

## Pitfalls

- **Critical sampling turns subband errors into aliasing.** With $M = K$, any error in the aliasing cancellation, such
  as quantising a subband, reappears as aliasing in the output. Oversampled banks ($M < K$) trade rate for robustness.
- **Prototype stopband attenuation bounds the separation between channels** and, in a critically sampled bank, the
  residual aliasing.
