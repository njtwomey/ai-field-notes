<Definition>
**Time-frequency analysis** represents a signal as a function of time and frequency together, showing which frequencies
are present at each moment. The <NoteLink to="fourier-transform">Fourier transform</NoteLink> gives the frequencies of a
whole signal but not when they occur. Time-frequency methods either analyse short windowed segments, spread the signal's
energy over a time-frequency plane, or split the signal into components each with its own instantaneous frequency
<Cite id="mallat2009" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Most real signals are non-stationary: speech changes phoneme every few tens of milliseconds, music changes note, a
machine's vibration drifts as it wears, and an EEG changes with the state of the brain. A single spectrum averages
these changes away. A time-frequency representation keeps them, which makes events such as onsets, chirps and
transients visible and measurable.

In machine learning the spectrogram, the squared magnitude of the short-time Fourier transform, is the standard input
for audio and speech models, usually on a mel frequency scale as in the <NoteLink to="mel-spectrogram" />.
<NoteLink to="spectrogram-augmentation" /> masks bands of time and frequency in it to regularise training. Empirical
mode decomposition was introduced for nonlinear, non-stationary series <Cite id="huang1998" />, and its modes are
used as inputs to models of such series.

## Main ideas

**Time and <NoteLink to="zero-padding-and-resolution">frequency resolution</NoteLink> trade against each other.** A
signal cannot be concentrated in both time and frequency: the spreads satisfy $\sigma_t\sigma_f \ge 1/(4\pi)$, with
equality only for Gaussians. <NoteLink to="time-frequency-uncertainty" /> derives the bound and shows that it limits
every windowed analysis.

**Windowed Fourier transforms tile the plane.** The <NoteLink to="short-time-fourier-transform" /> takes the
<Gloss name="dft" /> of successive windowed frames. The window length fixes a single time and frequency resolution
everywhere in the plane. <NoteLink to="overlap-add-and-overlap-save" /> computes long
<NoteLink to="convolution">convolutions</NoteLink> block by block and inverts the short-time transform. The
<NoteLink to="constant-q-transform" /> uses geometrically spaced frequencies with windows that shorten as frequency
rises, so every bin has the same ratio of frequency to bandwidth; with 12 bins per octave its bins are musical
semitones.

**A single component has an instantaneous frequency.** The phase of the analytic signal, from the
<NoteLink to="hilbert-transform" />, defines an <NoteLink to="instantaneous-frequency" />. It is meaningful for one
narrowband component and misleading for a sum of components.

**Quadratic distributions sharpen the picture at a cost.** The <NoteLink to="wigner-ville-distribution" /> localises a
linear chirp perfectly but adds oscillating cross-terms between components; spectrograms are smoothed versions of it.
<NoteLink to="reassignment-and-synchrosqueezing" /> move spectrogram energy to its local centre of gravity, so tones
and impulses collapse onto sharp lines. Synchrosqueezing reassigns in frequency only and remains invertible.

**Adaptive decompositions let the data choose the components.**
<NoteLink to="empirical-mode-decomposition" /> splits a signal into intrinsic mode functions by repeatedly subtracting
the mean of spline envelopes, with no fixed basis. The <NoteLink to="hilbert-huang-transform" /> then reads an
instantaneous amplitude and frequency from each mode. <NoteLink to="ensemble-empirical-mode-decomposition" /> averages
over added noise to cure mode mixing, <NoteLink to="multivariate-empirical-mode-decomposition" /> decomposes several
channels jointly, and <NoteLink to="variational-mode-decomposition" /> replaces the algorithm by an optimisation over
band-limited modes.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="time-frequency-uncertainty" /> and the <NoteLink to="short-time-fourier-transform" />. Then
two branches follow. The Fourier branch continues with <NoteLink to="overlap-add-and-overlap-save" />, the
<NoteLink to="constant-q-transform" />, the <NoteLink to="wigner-ville-distribution" /> and
<NoteLink to="reassignment-and-synchrosqueezing" />. The adaptive branch starts at
<NoteLink to="instantaneous-frequency" />, then <NoteLink to="empirical-mode-decomposition" /> and the
Hilbert–Huang transform, then the three EMD variants and variational mode decomposition. Reassignment uses
instantaneous frequency, so it joins the two branches.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume <NoteLink to="fourier-analysis" />, in particular the DFT, windows and the Hilbert transform.
<NoteLink to="wavelets" /> offer a different tiling of the plane, with fine time resolution at high frequencies and
fine frequency resolution at low ones. <NoteLink to="audio-signal-processing" /> builds its features on the
spectrogram and the constant-Q transform. For stationary signals, <NoteLink to="spectral-estimation" /> estimates a
single spectrum with controlled variance.
