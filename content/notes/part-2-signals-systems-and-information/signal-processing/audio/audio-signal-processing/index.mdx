<Definition>
**Audio signal processing** applies signal processing to sound: speech, music and environmental audio sampled as a
pressure waveform. Its methods are shaped by human hearing, which resolves frequency finely at low frequencies and
coarsely at high ones, and perceives loudness roughly logarithmically. Most audio analysis starts from a short-time
spectrum warped to a perceptual frequency scale <Cite id="muller2015" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A raw waveform at 16 or 44.1 kHz has thousands of samples per second, and its sample values vary with phase in ways the
ear ignores. Audio processing turns it into representations that keep what matters for hearing: which frequencies are
present over time, their pitch and harmony, and when events start. Those representations feed speech recognition,
speaker identification, music information retrieval, hearing aids and audio codecs.

In machine learning, the log-mel spectrogram is the standard input for speech and audio networks, MFCCs were the
standard features of speech recognisers for decades <Cite id="davis1980" />, and SpecAugment is a standard
regulariser for speech models. Classical methods such as non-negative matrix factorisation for source separation and
spectral subtraction for denoising remain the baselines that learned systems are compared with.

## Main ideas

**Perceptual frequency scales.** <NoteLink to="auditory-scales" /> warp frequency so that equal steps sound roughly
equal: the mel scale, the Bark scale of critical bands and the ERB-number scale. All are close to linear below about
1 kHz and close to logarithmic above.

**<NoteLink to="filter-banks">Filter banks</NoteLink> modelled on the ear.** A <NoteLink to="mel-filter-bank" /> sums a
power spectrum under overlapping triangular filters equally spaced in mel. A
<NoteLink to="gammatone-filter-bank" /> models cochlear filtering with filters whose bandwidths follow the ERB scale.

**From spectrogram to compact features.** A <NoteLink to="mel-spectrogram" /> passes a short-time power spectrum through
a mel filter bank and takes the logarithm. The <NoteLink to="mel-frequency-cepstral-coefficients" /> apply a
<Gloss name="dct">discrete cosine transform</Gloss> to the log mel energies and keep about 13 coefficients, a compact
and nearly decorrelated description of the spectral envelope. <NoteLink to="spectral-features" /> summarise each frame
in a few numbers, such as the spectral centroid, flatness and flux. <NoteLink to="chroma-features" /> fold a spectrum
onto the 12 pitch classes and describe harmony.

**Musical structure.** <NoteLink to="pitch-estimation" /> finds the fundamental period of a harmonic sound by
autocorrelation, YIN, the cepstrum or the harmonic product spectrum, with octave errors as the main failure.
<NoteLink to="onset-detection-and-beat-tracking" /> builds an onset envelope from spectral flux, picks its peaks, and
aligns a beat sequence to its periodicity by dynamic programming.

**Cleaning, separating and compressing.** <NoteLink to="spectral-subtraction-and-denoising" /> removes an estimate of
the noise power from each frame and reduces the resulting musical noise with Wiener gains.
<NoteLink to="audio-source-separation" /> recovers individual sources from a mixture, by factorising the spectrogram or
by exploiting several microphones. <NoteLink to="perceptual-audio-coding" /> quantises an MDCT spectrum coarsely where
a psychoacoustic model predicts the error will be masked, as MP3 and AAC do.

**Augmenting spectrograms.** <NoteLink to="spectrogram-augmentation" /> warps time and masks random frequency bands and
time spans of log-mel spectrograms during training.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="auditory-scales" />, then the <NoteLink to="mel-filter-bank" />, the
<NoteLink to="mel-spectrogram" /> and the <NoteLink to="mel-frequency-cepstral-coefficients" />, which form a chain.
The <NoteLink to="gammatone-filter-bank" /> is an alternative to the mel filter bank. Spectral features, chroma, pitch
and onset detection each need only the short-time spectrum and can be read in any order. Denoising, source separation
and perceptual coding are applications that combine several earlier notes. Spectrogram augmentation needs only the mel
spectrogram.
