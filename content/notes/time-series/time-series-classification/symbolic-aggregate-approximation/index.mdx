<Definition>
Symbolic aggregate approximation (SAX) <Cite id="lin2003" /> represents a time series $\xvec = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ by a
word of $w$ symbols drawn from an alphabet of size $a$. It z-normalises $\xvec$ to mean 0 and standard deviation 1,
replaces each of $w$ equal segments by its mean (the piecewise aggregate approximation, PAA), and maps each mean to
the symbol of the interval it falls in. The intervals are cut at the $a - 1$ quantiles of the standard normal
distribution, so that each symbol is equally likely. A distance between two words, MINDIST, lower-bounds the
Euclidean distance between the original series <Cite id="lin2007" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A time series of a thousand real numbers is costly to store, compare and index. SAX compresses it into a short string,
such as `acda`. Strings can be hashed, counted, sorted and stored in tree indexes, and algorithms from text processing
apply to them directly.

A compressed representation is useful for search only if it cannot rule out a true match. SAX guarantees this: the
distance between two words is never larger than the distance between the series they came from. A search can discard
every candidate whose word is already too far from the query's word, without ever reading the full series, and it
loses no true matches.

## Construction

**Piecewise aggregate approximation.** For $n$ divisible by $w$, segment $i$ covers the $n / w$ consecutive points
$x_{(i-1)n/w + 1}, \dots, x_{i n / w}$, and its PAA value is their mean:

$$
\bar x_i = \frac{w}{n} \sum_{k = (i-1)n/w + 1}^{i n / w} x_k, \qquad i = 1, \dots, w.
$$

The vector $\bar\xvec = (\bar x_1, \dots, \bar x_w)$ is the PAA of $\xvec$.

**Breakpoints.** The breakpoints $\beta_1 < \dots < \beta_{a-1}$ are the quantiles
$\beta_r = \Phi^{-1}(r / a)$ of the standard normal distribution, with cumulative distribution function $\Phi$. Set
$\beta_0 = -\infty$ and $\beta_a = +\infty$. For $a = 4$ the breakpoints are $-0.674$, $0$ and $0.674$.

**Symbols.** PAA value $\bar x_i$ receives symbol $r \in \set{1, \dots, a}$, written as the $r$-th letter, when
$\beta_{r-1} < \bar x_i \le \beta_r$. Z-normalised series have PAA values that are roughly Gaussian, so the
equiprobable cut gives each letter roughly the same frequency <Cite id="lin2003" />.

**MINDIST.** Two symbols $r \le s$ are at distance

$$
\mathrm{dist}(r, s) =
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } s - r \le 1, \\
\beta_{s-1} - \beta_r & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
$$

and two words $\hat\xvec$, $\hat{\yvec}$ of length $w$ from series of length $n$ are at distance

$$
\mathrm{MINDIST}(\hat\xvec, \hat{\yvec}) = \sqrt{\frac{n}{w}} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{w} \mathrm{dist}(\hat x_i, \hat y_i)^2}.
$$

The symbol distances form an $a \times a$ table that is computed once, so MINDIST costs $O(w)$ table look-ups.

## Why MINDIST is a lower bound

Let $\xvec$ and $\yvec$ be z-normalised series of length $n$, with PAA vectors $\bar\xvec$, $\bar{\yvec}$ and
SAX words $\hat\xvec$, $\hat{\yvec}$. The claim is

$$
\mathrm{MINDIST}(\hat\xvec, \hat{\yvec}) \le \sqrt{\frac{n}{w}} \norm{\bar\xvec - \bar{\yvec}}
\le \norm{\xvec - \yvec}.
$$

**Step 1: PAA lower-bounds the Euclidean distance.** Take one segment of $\ell = n / w$ points, and write
$e_k = x_k - y_k$ for its differences and $\bar e = \bar x_i - \bar y_i$ for their mean. Adding and subtracting the
mean inside the square,

$$
\sum_{k} e_k^2 = \sum_{k} \paren{\bar e + (e_k - \bar e)}^2 = \ell \, \bar e^2 + \sum_{k} (e_k - \bar e)^2
\ge \ell \, \bar e^2,
$$

because the cross term $2 \bar e \sum_k (e_k - \bar e)$ is zero. Summing over the $w$ segments gives
$\norm{\xvec - \yvec}^2 \ge \ell \norm{\bar\xvec - \bar{\yvec}}^2$, which is the second inequality. When
the segments are unequal, the same step gives $\norm{\xvec - \yvec}^2 \ge \sum_i \ell_i (\bar x_i - \bar y_i)^2$ with
$\ell_i$ the length of segment $i$.

**Step 2: the symbol distance lower-bounds the PAA difference.** Take one segment, with symbols $r = \hat x_i$ and
$s = \hat y_i$, and suppose $r < s$. By the symbol rule, $\bar x_i \le \beta_r$ and $\bar y_i > \beta_{s-1}$. If
$s - r \ge 2$, then $\bar y_i - \bar x_i > \beta_{s-1} - \beta_r = \mathrm{dist}(r, s) > 0$. If $s - r \le 1$, the
distance is 0, which bounds any absolute difference. In both cases
$\mathrm{dist}(\hat x_i, \hat y_i) \le \abs{\bar x_i - \bar y_i}$. Squaring, summing over segments and multiplying by
$n / w$ gives the first inequality.

Adjacent symbols get distance 0 because two PAA values on either side of one breakpoint can be arbitrarily close.
MINDIST is well below the Euclidean distance for small $a$ and $w$. As $a$ grows, the breakpoints crowd together,
the symbol distance approaches the PAA difference, and MINDIST approaches the scaled PAA distance.

## Worked example

Take $n = 8$, $w = 4$ and $a = 4$, with breakpoints $-0.674$, $0$, $0.674$. The series $(2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 7, 4, 1)$
z-normalises to

$$
\xvec = (-1.091, -0.655, 0.218, 0.655, 1.528, 1.091, -0.218, -1.528).
$$

Averaging pairs gives the PAA $\bar\xvec = (-0.873, 0.436, 1.309, -0.873)$. The first value is below $-0.674$, so it
is `a`. The second lies in $(0, 0.674]$, so it is `c`. The third is above $0.674$, so it is `d`. The word is `acda`.

The series $(7, 6, 4, 2, 1, 3, 5, 8)$ z-normalises to
$\yvec = (1.091, 0.655, -0.218, -1.091, -1.528, -0.655, 0.218, 1.528)$, with PAA
$(0.873, -0.655, -1.091, 0.873)$ and word `dbad`.

| Segment | $\bar x_i$ | $\bar y_i$ | Symbols | dist                                         |
| ------- | ---------- | ---------- | ------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1       | $-0.873$   | $0.873$    | a, d    | $\beta_3 - \beta_1 = 0.674 - (-0.674) = 1.349$ |
| 2       | $0.436$    | $-0.655$   | c, b    | $0$ (adjacent)                               |
| 3       | $1.309$    | $-1.091$   | d, a    | $1.349$                                      |
| 4       | $-0.873$   | $0.873$    | a, d    | $1.349$                                      |

MINDIST is $\sqrt{8/4} \times \sqrt{3 \times 1.349^2} = 3.304$. The scaled PAA distance is
$\sqrt{2} \, \norm{\bar\xvec - \bar{\yvec}} = 5.108$, and the Euclidean distance is $\norm{\xvec - \yvec} = 5.623$. The
chain $3.304 \le 5.108 \le 5.623$ holds as the proof requires.

## iSAX: indexing with variable cardinality

With $a = 2^b$, the breakpoints for alphabet size $2^b$ are a subset of those for $2^{b+1}$: each interval splits in
two at the new breakpoint. Writing symbols in binary, a symbol at cardinality $2^b$ is the first $b$ bits of the
symbol at cardinality $2^{b+1}$. Indexable SAX (iSAX) <Cite id="shieh2008" /> uses this to let each position of a word
carry its own cardinality.

The iSAX index is a tree. Each node holds a word whose positions may have different cardinalities, and covers every
series whose full-resolution word starts with those bits at each position. When a leaf holds more series than a
threshold, one position's cardinality is doubled, and the leaf splits into two children that differ in the new bit.
MINDIST extends to words of mixed cardinality by comparing each position at the lower of the two cardinalities. Exact
nearest-neighbour search visits nodes in order of MINDIST to the query and prunes every node whose bound exceeds the
best distance found so far. Shieh and Keogh used the index on collections of time series of terabyte scale
<Cite id="shieh2008" />.

## Uses

- **Indexing and exact search.** The lower bound makes SAX safe for exact nearest-neighbour search, as above.
- **Hashing and counting.** Identical words collide in a hash table. Fast shapelets use SAX words with random masking
  to find subsequences that occur often in one class and rarely in another <Cite id="rakthanmanon2013" />.
- **Text methods.** Words and word counts feed bag-of-words classifiers and string algorithms such as suffix trees.

## Assumptions

- The PAA values of z-normalised data are approximately Gaussian. If they are not, the symbols are not equiprobable,
  and hashing and counting become less efficient. The lower bound holds regardless of the distribution.
- Each series is z-normalised before conversion. SAX discards level and scale.

## Pitfalls

- **Near-constant series.** A series with a standard deviation near 0 has its noise amplified by z-normalisation into
  an arbitrary word. A common remedy is to give any series whose standard deviation falls below a threshold a flat word.
- **Loss of shape within a segment.** PAA keeps only segment means. A spike and a flat stretch with the same mean get
  the same symbol.
- **Choice of $w$ and $a$.** Small values give loose bounds and many collisions; large values give tight bounds and
  few collisions, which weakens the benefit of hashing.
