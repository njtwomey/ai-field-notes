<Definition>
A **hierarchical time series** is a collection of $n$ series in which some are sums of others, such as sales by store,
region and country. Stack all series at time $t$ in $\yvec_t \in \reals^n$ and the $m$ most disaggregated (bottom)
series in $\bvec_t \in \reals^m$. The **summing matrix** $\Smat$ ($n \times m$, entries 0 or 1) gives
$\yvec_t = \Smat\bvec_t$. Forecasts are **coherent** when they satisfy the same constraint. **Reconciliation** maps
base forecasts $\hat\yvec_h$ of every series, produced independently, to coherent forecasts
$\tilde\yvec_h = \Smat\Gmat\hat\yvec_h$ for some $m \times n$ matrix $\Gmat$ <Cite id="hyndman2011" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Forecasting each series separately uses the best model for each level: the total is smooth and easy to forecast,
the bottom series are noisy. But independent forecasts do not add up, and plans made at different levels then
contradict each other. Reconciliation keeps one forecast per bottom series, $\Gmat\hat\yvec_h$, formed from the base
forecasts at all levels, and sums it up with $\Smat$. Coherent forecasts are exactly the vectors in the <NoteLink to="rank-and-null-space">column space</NoteLink> of
$\Smat$, so reconciliation is a projection of $\hat\yvec_h$ onto that $m$-dimensional subspace.

For a total $T$ with two children $A$ and $B$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} y_T \\ y_A \\ y_B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} y_A \\ y_B \end{bmatrix}.
$$

## Single-level methods

- **Bottom-up.** Forecast only the bottom series and add them up: $\Gmat = [\zeros_{m \times (n-m)}, \eye_m]$. Nothing
  is lost by aggregation, but bottom series are often noisy or intermittent, and their forecasts ignore the
  information in the aggregates.
- **Top-down.** Forecast the total and split it with proportions $\pvec$, often historical shares:
  $\Gmat = [\pvec, \zeros_{m \times (n-1)}]$. It is simple and stable, but it cannot reflect different dynamics in the
  children, such as one growing and one shrinking.
- **Middle-out** forecasts one intermediate level, sums upward and splits downward.

## Unbiasedness

Suppose the base forecasts are unbiased, $\expect[\hat\yvec_h] = \Smat\expect[\bvec_{t+h}]$. The reconciled forecasts
are unbiased when $\expect[\Smat\Gmat\hat\yvec_h] = \Smat\Gmat\Smat\expect[\bvec_{t+h}]$ equals
$\Smat\expect[\bvec_{t+h}]$ for every mean. Since $\Smat$ has full column rank, this holds exactly when

$$
\Gmat\Smat = \eye_m.
$$

The constraint says that already-coherent forecasts are left unchanged. Bottom-up satisfies it. Top-down does not:
$\Gmat\Smat = \pvec\ones^\top$, which has rank 1, so top-down forecasts are biased whenever the children's shares
change <Cite id="hyndman2011" />.

## Minimum trace reconciliation

Let $\Wmat_h = \cov(\yvec_{t+h} - \hat\yvec_h)$ be the covariance of the base forecast errors. If $\Gmat\Smat = \eye$,
the reconciled error is $\yvec_{t+h} - \Smat\Gmat\hat\yvec_h = \Smat\Gmat(\yvec_{t+h} - \hat\yvec_h)$, using
$\yvec_{t+h} = \Smat\Gmat\Smat\bvec_{t+h} = \Smat\Gmat\yvec_{t+h}$. Its covariance is

$$
\cov(\yvec_{t+h} - \tilde\yvec_h) = \Smat\Gmat\Wmat_h\Gmat^\top\Smat^\top.
$$

The **MinT** reconciliation chooses $\Gmat$ to minimise the trace of this matrix, the sum of the error variances of
all $n$ series, subject to $\Gmat\Smat = \eye$. The solution is <Cite id="wickramasuriya2019" />

$$
\Gmat^* = \paren{\Smat^\top\Wmat_h^{-1}\Smat}^{-1}\Smat^\top\Wmat_h^{-1}.
$$

**Why $\Gmat^*$ is optimal.** $\Gmat^*$ satisfies the constraint, since $\Gmat^*\Smat = \eye$. Any other feasible
$\Gmat$ can be written $\Gmat = \Gmat^* + \Dmat$ with $\Dmat\Smat = \zeros$. Expanding,

$$
\Gmat\Wmat_h\Gmat^\top = \Gmat^*\Wmat_h\Gmat^{*\top} + \Dmat\Wmat_h\Gmat^{*\top} + \Gmat^*\Wmat_h\Dmat^\top +
\Dmat\Wmat_h\Dmat^\top.
$$

The cross term vanishes: $\Gmat^*\Wmat_h\Dmat^\top = \paren{\Smat^\top\Wmat_h^{-1}\Smat}^{-1}\Smat^\top\Dmat^\top =
\paren{\Smat^\top\Wmat_h^{-1}\Smat}^{-1}(\Dmat\Smat)^\top = \zeros$. So
$\Gmat\Wmat_h\Gmat^\top = \Gmat^*\Wmat_h\Gmat^{*\top} + \Dmat\Wmat_h\Dmat^\top$, and the second term is <NoteLink to="positive-definite-matrices">positive
semidefinite</NoteLink>. Multiplying by $\Smat$ on the left and $\Smat^\top$ on the right keeps the ordering. Every reconciled
series' error variance is therefore smallest under $\Gmat^*$, and so is the trace. This is the same argument as the
Gauss–Markov theorem: $\Gmat^*$ is the <Gloss name="gls">generalised least-squares</Gloss> estimate of $\bvec$ in the regression
$\hat\yvec_h = \Smat\bvec + \text{error}$.

## Estimating $\Wmat_h$

$\Wmat_h$ is an $n \times n$ covariance, often with $n$ in the thousands, and must be estimated. Common choices
<Cite id="wickramasuriya2019" /> <Cite id="hyndman2021" />:

- **<Gloss name="ols" />:** $\Wmat_h \propto \eye$. The reconciliation is the <NoteLink to="orthogonal-projection">orthogonal projection</NoteLink> onto the column space of $\Smat$
  <Cite id="hyndman2011" />.
- **WLS, structural scaling:** $\Wmat_h$ diagonal with entry $i$ equal to the number of bottom series that series $i$
  sums. It needs no residuals, so it works when some series are too short to estimate variances.
- **WLS, variance scaling:** $\Wmat_h$ diagonal with the in-sample one-step residual variances.
- **MinT-shrink:** the sample covariance of one-step residuals, shrunk towards its diagonal so that it is invertible
  and stable. The $h$-step covariance is assumed proportional to the one-step covariance.

## Worked example

A total and two children have base forecasts $\hat y_T = 100$, $\hat y_A = 55$ and $\hat y_B = 40$. They are
incoherent by $5$. Suppose the base error variances are $16$, $4$ and $9$, with errors uncorrelated.

| Method | $\tilde y_T$ | $\tilde y_A$ | $\tilde y_B$ | Sum of error variances |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bottom-up | 95.00 | 55.00 | 40.00 | 26.00 |
| Top-down, shares 0.6 and 0.4 | 100.00 | 60.00 | 40.00 | (biased) |
| OLS | 98.33 | 56.67 | 41.67 | 19.33 |
| MinT with the true $\Wmat$ | 97.24 | 55.69 | 41.55 | 16.83 |

OLS splits the discrepancy of 5 equally: the total falls by $1.67$ and each child rises by $1.67$. MinT moves each
forecast in proportion to its uncertainty. The total, with variance 16, moves by $2.76$; $A$, with variance 4, moves
by only $0.69$. The bottom-up variance of the total is $4 + 9 = 13$, larger than MinT's $7.17$, because bottom-up
ignores the total's own forecast.

## Pitfalls

- **Unreconciled evaluation.** Compare methods on all levels. Bottom-up can win at the bottom and lose badly at the
  top.
- **Negative forecasts.** Reconciliation is linear and can produce negative values for non-negative series. Constrained
  versions solve a quadratic programme instead.
- **Covariance estimation.** A full sample covariance with $n$ larger than the number of residuals is singular. Use
  shrinkage or a diagonal $\Wmat_h$.
- **Probabilistic forecasts.** Reconciling means does not make quantiles coherent. Reconcile sample paths, or
  distributions, rather than individual quantiles.
