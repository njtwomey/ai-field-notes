<Definition>
In the <NoteLink to="linear-gaussian-state-space-models">linear-Gaussian state-space model</NoteLink> $\zvec_t = \Amat\zvec_{t-1} + \wvec_t$, $\xvec_t = \Cmat\zvec_t + \vvec_t$,
with $\wvec_t \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Qmat)$ and $\vvec_t \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Rmat)$, the **smoothing distribution** is
$p(\zvec_t \mid \xvec_{1:T}) = \Gauss(\muvec_{t \mid T}, \Pmat_{t \mid T})$ for $t = 1, \dots, T$: the posterior of
each state given the whole sequence. The **Rauch–Tung–Striebel (RTS) smoother** computes it with the
<NoteLink to="kalman-filter">Kalman filter</NoteLink> followed by the backward recursion

$$
\Gmat_t = \Pmat_{t \mid t}\Amat^\top\Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}^{-1}, \qquad
\muvec_{t \mid T} = \muvec_{t \mid t} + \Gmat_t\paren{\muvec_{t+1 \mid T} - \muvec_{t+1 \mid t}}, \qquad
\Pmat_{t \mid T} = \Pmat_{t \mid t} + \Gmat_t\paren{\Pmat_{t+1 \mid T} - \Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}}\Gmat_t^\top,
$$

run for $t = T - 1, \dots, 1$ from $\muvec_{T \mid T}$ and $\Pmat_{T \mid T}$ <Cite id="rauch1965" />.
</Definition>

## Filtering, prediction and smoothing

The subscript $t \mid s$ means "the state at time $t$, given observations up to time $s$". Three problems differ only
in $s$:

- **Prediction**, $s < t$: forecast a future state.
- **Filtering**, $s = t$: estimate the current state from the past and present. The Kalman filter solves it online.
- **Smoothing**, $s > t$: estimate a past state using later observations too.

Smoothing is the right problem whenever the whole record is available before the estimate is needed: offline
analysis of a recorded trajectory, reconstructing a signal, or the E-step of fitting the model by
<Gloss name="em-algorithm">expectation maximisation</Gloss>.

Smoothing has three variants. **Fixed-interval** smoothing estimates every $\zvec_t$ from a fixed record $\xvec_{1:T}$;
this note is about it. **Fixed-lag** smoothing estimates $\zvec_{t - L}$ from $\xvec_{1:t}$, trading a delay of $L$
steps for accuracy in an online system. **Fixed-point** smoothing refines the estimate of one chosen state as data keep
arriving.

## Derivation

The filter has already computed, for every $t$, the filtered $\muvec_{t \mid t}$, $\Pmat_{t \mid t}$ and the
predicted $\muvec_{t+1 \mid t}$, $\Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}$. At $t = T$ the smoothing and filtering distributions coincide.
The recursion goes backwards from there in two steps.

**Step 1: condition on the next state.** Given $\zvec_{t+1}$, the state $\zvec_t$ is independent of the later
observations $\xvec_{t+1:T}$, because every path from $\zvec_t$ to them in the chain passes through $\zvec_{t+1}$. So

$$
p(\zvec_t \mid \zvec_{t+1}, \xvec_{1:T}) = p(\zvec_t \mid \zvec_{t+1}, \xvec_{1:t}).
$$

Given $\xvec_{1:t}$, the pair $(\zvec_t, \zvec_{t+1})$ is jointly Gaussian. $\zvec_t$ has the filtered mean and
covariance. $\zvec_{t+1} = \Amat\zvec_t + \wvec_{t+1}$ has the predicted mean and covariance, and its covariance with
$\zvec_t$ is $\cov(\zvec_t, \Amat\zvec_t) = \Pmat_{t \mid t}\Amat^\top$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \zvec_t \\ \zvec_{t+1} \end{bmatrix} \Bigm| \xvec_{1:t} \sim
\Gauss\paren{\begin{bmatrix} \muvec_{t \mid t} \\ \muvec_{t+1 \mid t} \end{bmatrix},
\begin{bmatrix} \Pmat_{t \mid t} & \Pmat_{t \mid t}\Amat^\top \\ \Amat\Pmat_{t \mid t} & \Pmat_{t+1 \mid t} \end{bmatrix}}.
$$

Conditioning on $\zvec_{t+1}$ (the Gaussian conditioning formula, whose covariance is a
<NoteLink to="schur-complement">Schur complement</NoteLink>) gives

$$
\zvec_t \mid \zvec_{t+1}, \xvec_{1:t} \sim
\Gauss\paren{\muvec_{t \mid t} + \Gmat_t(\zvec_{t+1} - \muvec_{t+1 \mid t}),\;
\Pmat_{t \mid t} - \Gmat_t\Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}\Gmat_t^\top}, \qquad
\Gmat_t = \Pmat_{t \mid t}\Amat^\top\Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}^{-1}.
$$

The **smoother gain** $\Gmat_t$ ($d \times d$) is the coefficient of the regression of $\zvec_t$ on $\zvec_{t+1}$.

**Step 2: average over the next state.** Suppose $\zvec_{t+1} \mid \xvec_{1:T} \sim \Gauss(\muvec_{t+1 \mid T},
\Pmat_{t+1 \mid T})$, known from the previous backward step. Then

$$
p(\zvec_t \mid \xvec_{1:T}) = \int p(\zvec_t \mid \zvec_{t+1}, \xvec_{1:t})\, p(\zvec_{t+1} \mid \xvec_{1:T})\,
d\zvec_{t+1}.
$$

Step 1 wrote $\zvec_t$ as a linear function of $\zvec_{t+1}$ plus independent Gaussian noise of covariance
$\Pmat_{t \mid t} - \Gmat_t\Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}\Gmat_t^\top$. Averaging over $\zvec_{t+1}$ therefore gives a Gaussian
with mean $\muvec_{t \mid t} + \Gmat_t(\muvec_{t+1 \mid T} - \muvec_{t+1 \mid t})$ and covariance
$\Gmat_t\Pmat_{t+1 \mid T}\Gmat_t^\top + \Pmat_{t \mid t} - \Gmat_t\Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}\Gmat_t^\top$. These are the
RTS equations in the definition.

The recursion corrects each filtered estimate by how much the smoothed estimate of the next state differs from its
prediction. When later data move $\muvec_{t+1 \mid T}$ away from $\muvec_{t+1 \mid t}$, the correction passes back to
$\zvec_t$ in proportion to how strongly the two states covary.

The backward pass uses only the filter's stored means and covariances, not the observations. Its cost is
$O(T d^3)$, the same order as the filter. The smoother also gives the lag-one cross-covariance
$\cov(\zvec_t, \zvec_{t+1} \mid \xvec_{1:T}) = \Gmat_t\Pmat_{t+1 \mid T}$, which the M-step of expectation
maximisation for the model's parameters needs <Cite id="sarkka2013" />.

## Why smoothing never increases uncertainty

$\Pmat_{t \mid T} \preceq \Pmat_{t \mid t}$ for every $t$: the smoothed covariance is never larger, in the
<NoteLink to="positive-definite-matrices">positive semidefinite</NoteLink> order, than the filtered one. By induction from $t = T$, where the two are equal: suppose
$\Pmat_{t+1 \mid T} \preceq \Pmat_{t+1 \mid t+1}$. The filter's update never increases uncertainty and its prediction
never decreases it, so $\Pmat_{t+1 \mid t+1} \preceq \Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}$. Hence
$\Pmat_{t+1 \mid T} - \Pmat_{t+1 \mid t} \preceq \zeros$, the correction term
$\Gmat_t(\Pmat_{t+1 \mid T} - \Pmat_{t+1 \mid t})\Gmat_t^\top$ is negative semidefinite, and
$\Pmat_{t \mid T} \preceq \Pmat_{t \mid t}$.

The smoothed variance is smallest in the middle of the record, where a state has observations on both sides. At $t = T$ there is
nothing after, and the two coincide. Near $t = 1$ there is little data before, so the smoothed variance also rises
there.

## Worked example

Continue the scalar <NoteLink to="random-walk">random walk</NoteLink> of the Kalman filter note: $A = C = 1$, $Q = R = 1$, prior $\Gauss(0, 1)$,
observations $x_{1:4} = (1, 3, 2, 2.5)$. For a random walk the prediction is the previous filtered mean,
$\mu_{t+1 \mid t} = \mu_{t \mid t}$, and $P_{t+1 \mid t} = P_{t \mid t} + 1$. The filter gives
$\mu_{t \mid t} = (0.667, 2.125, 2.048, 2.327)$ and $P_{t \mid t} = (0.667, 0.625, 0.619, 0.618)$. The backward pass
starts from $\mu_{4 \mid 4} = 2.327$, $P_{4 \mid 4} = 0.618$:

| $t$ | $G_t = P_{t \mid t} / P_{t+1 \mid t}$ | $\mu_{t \mid 4}$ | $P_{t \mid 4}$ | $P_{t \mid t}$ |
| --- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| 4   |                                       | 2.327            | 0.618          | 0.618          |
| 3   | 13/34 = 0.382                         | 2.155            | 0.473          | 0.619          |
| 2   | 5/13 = 0.385                          | 2.136            | 0.455          | 0.625          |
| 1   | 2/5 = 0.4                             | 1.255            | 0.473          | 0.667          |

At $t = 3$: $\mu_{3 \mid 4} = 2.048 + 0.382 \times (2.327 - 2.048) = 2.155$ and
$P_{3 \mid 4} = 0.619 + 0.382^2 \times (0.618 - 1.619) = 0.473$. The largest change is at $t = 1$. The filter put
$z_1$ at 0.667, between the prior mean 0 and $x_1 = 1$. The later observations, all near 2 to 3, pull the smoothed
estimate up to 1.255. The smoothed variances are smallest in the middle, at $t = 2$.

## Relations

- **The hidden Markov model <Gloss name="forward-backward">forward–backward algorithm</Gloss>.** The Kalman filter is the forward pass of a chain with Gaussian states, and
  the RTS smoother is a backward pass. The <NoteLink to="hidden-markov-model">hidden Markov model</NoteLink>'s
  backward pass usually propagates messages $\beta_t$ and multiplies them into the forward ones. The RTS form instead
  propagates the smoothed posterior itself, which avoids representing a Gaussian message that may not be normalisable.
- **Two-filter smoothing.** Running one filter forwards and one backwards, and combining their estimates at each $t$,
  gives the same smoothed distribution.

## Pitfalls

- **Invertibility.** $\Gmat_t$ needs $\Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}^{-1}$. With $\Qmat$ singular and a deterministic state
  component, $\Pmat_{t+1 \mid t}$ can be singular. A pseudo-inverse or a square-root implementation is then needed.
- **The smoother inherits the model.** Wrong $\Qmat$ or $\Rmat$ produce the same errors as in the filter: too little
  process noise gives a smoothed path that is too straight.
- **Offline only.** The smoothed estimate at $t$ changes whenever data after $t$ arrive. An online system that needs
  some of the benefit uses a fixed-lag smoother and accepts the delay.
