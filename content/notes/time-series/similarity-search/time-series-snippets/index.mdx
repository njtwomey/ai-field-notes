<Definition>
Let $A$ and $B$ be time series of lengths $n_A$ and $n_B$, and $L$ a subsequence length. Let $P^{AB}$ and $P^{BA}$ be
their two join <NoteLink to="matrix-profile">matrix profiles</NoteLink> under z-normalised Euclidean distance, and
$P^{ABBA}$ their concatenation. MPdist is the $k$-th smallest value of $P^{ABBA}$, with
$k = \lceil \tau (n_A + n_B) \rceil$ for a small fraction $\tau$ (0.05 in the original paper), or the largest value if
$P^{ABBA}$ has fewer than $k$ entries <Cite id="gharghabi2018" />. Time series snippets are $s$ subsequences of a
series, chosen greedily so that every part of the series is close under MPdist to at least one of them
<Cite id="imani2018" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Euclidean distance between two whole series compares point $t$ of one with point $t$ of the other. A small shift in
time or a different order of the same activities makes two similar series far apart.
<NoteLink to="dynamic-time-warping">Dynamic time warping</NoteLink> allows local stretching but still aligns the two
series from start to end.

MPdist compares series by their parts. Two series are similar if a set fraction of their subsequences have a close match
somewhere in the other series, in any position and any order. The join profiles $P^{AB}$ and $P^{BA}$ record, for every
subsequence of each series, the distance to its best match in the other. Taking a small order statistic of their union,
rather than the maximum or the mean, tolerates unmatched subsequences: the $k$-th smallest value is small whenever at
least $k$ entries are small. Taking the $k$-th smallest rather than the smallest prevents one lucky match from making
the two series look identical.

## Details

### MPdist

1. Compute $P^{AB}$ ($n_A - L + 1$ entries) and $P^{BA}$ ($n_B - L + 1$ entries). One pass over the diagonals of the
   distance matrix yields both.
2. Concatenate them into $P^{ABBA}$ and sort.
3. Return the $k$-th smallest value, $k = \lceil \tau (n_A + n_B) \rceil$.

The cost is that of one <NoteLink to="matrix-profile-joins">AB-join</NoteLink>, $O(n_A n_B)$. MPdist is not a metric:
two different series that consist of the same subsequences in a different order have MPdist 0, and the triangle
inequality can fail. It is a dissimilarity suited to clustering and
<NoteLink to="nearest-neighbour-search">nearest-neighbour search</NoteLink> where the order of events within a series
should not matter <Cite id="gharghabi2018" />.

The parameter $\tau$ sets how much of the two series may lack a counterpart. With $\tau = 0.05$, MPdist is small if at
least 5 % of the combined subsequences have close matches. The inner subsequence length $L$ is shorter than the series
being compared and sets the scale of the parts that must match.

### Snippets

A long series, such as a day of accelerometer data, often consists of a few recurring behaviours. Motifs report the
closest repeated pairs, which may be rare. Snippets report the typical behaviours and the share of the series each one
represents <Cite id="imani2018" />.

1. Split the series into non-overlapping candidate segments of length $m$.
2. For each candidate $c$, compute its MPdist profile $M_c$: the MPdist between the candidate and every length-$m$
   subsequence of the series, using an inner length $L < m$.
3. Greedily choose snippets. The first is the candidate whose profile has the smallest area,
   $\sum_i M_{c,i}$. Each next one is the candidate that most reduces the area under the elementwise minimum of the
   chosen profiles, $\sum_i \min_{c \in S} M_{c,i}$, where $S$ is the set chosen so far.
4. Report, for each snippet, the fraction of positions $i$ at which its profile is the minimum. The fractions sum to 1
   and describe how much of the series each snippet represents.

The greedy choice needs one MPdist profile per candidate, about $n/m$ profiles for a series of length $n$.

## Worked example

Take three series of 100 points each, sampled at unit steps with added Gaussian noise of
<NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink> 0.1: $A$ is a sine of period 25; $B$ is the same
sine shifted by 9 steps; $C$ is a square wave of period 25 in phase with $A$. Use $L = 20$ and $\tau = 0.05$, so
$k = \lceil 0.05 \cdot 200 \rceil = 10$ of the 162 joined profile values.

| Pair     | Euclidean distance after z-normalisation | MPdist |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------ |
| $A$, $B$ | 18.04                                    | 0.561  |
| $A$, $C$ | 4.78                                     | 1.660  |

Euclidean distance ranks the square wave as the closer series, because it is in phase with $A$ and the phase shift of
$B$ moves every point. MPdist ranks the shifted sine as closer, because every length-20 window of $B$ has a near copy
in $A$ at a different offset, while no window of the square wave looks like a window of a sine.

## Assumptions

- **Order does not matter.** MPdist deliberately ignores the order of subsequences. If the order of events carries the
  meaning, as in a sequence of steps in a procedure, MPdist discards it.
- **A shared scale.** The inner length $L$ must be long enough to capture the recurring shapes and short enough that
  each series contains many of them.

## Pitfalls

- **Not a metric.** Indexing structures that rely on the triangle inequality do not apply.
- **Very different lengths.** When one series is much shorter, $k$ is dominated by the longer series' length, and the
  short series' profile makes up a small share of $P^{ABBA}$.
- **Snippet length.** Snippets of length $m$ describe behaviours of that duration. A behaviour much longer than $m$ is
  split across several snippets.
