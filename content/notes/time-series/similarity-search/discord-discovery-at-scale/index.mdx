<Definition>
A time series discord of length $m$ is the subsequence $T_i$ whose z-normalised distance to its nearest
non-overlapping neighbour is largest: $\argmax_i P_i$, where $P$ is the <NoteLink to="matrix-profile">matrix profile</NoteLink> <Cite id="yeh2016" />. A
left discord uses the <NoteLink to="streaming-and-incremental-matrix-profile">left matrix profile</NoteLink> $P^L_i$, the distance to the nearest earlier subsequence, and so asks
whether $T_i$ is unlike everything before it. MERLIN finds discords of every length in a range
<Cite id="nakamura2020" />. DAMP (Discord Aware Matrix Profile) finds left discords while computing only a small part
of the left matrix profile <Cite id="lu2022" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A discord is an anomaly defined by the data alone. No model of normal behaviour is fitted and no labels are needed.
If every other stretch of a series has a close match somewhere and one stretch does not, that stretch is the
discord. Two parameters remain: the length $m$, and the reference against which "somewhere" is judged.

The reference matters when an anomaly repeats. Two identical abnormal beats in an electrocardiogram are each other's
nearest neighbour, so both have small matrix profile values, and neither is the discord. This is the twin freak
problem. The left matrix profile avoids it for the first occurrence: when the first abnormal beat appears, no earlier
subsequence matches it. The second occurrence matches the first and is, correctly, no longer new.

## Details

### Discords of every length: MERLIN

The best discord length is rarely known, and a discord at one length need not be one at another. MERLIN returns the
top discord for every length in a range $[m_{\min}, m_{\max}]$ <Cite id="nakamura2020" />. It builds on a two-phase
search that takes a distance threshold $r$:

1. **Candidate selection.** Scan the series once. Keep $T_i$ as a candidate unless it has a match closer than $r$
   among the subsequences already scanned. When a new subsequence matches a kept candidate within $r$, remove that
   candidate too.
2. **Refinement.** Scan again and compute each surviving candidate's exact nearest-neighbour distance, removing any
   candidate that turns out to have a match within $r$. The survivors are exactly the subsequences whose
   nearest-neighbour distance exceeds $r$, and the largest of them is the discord.

If $r$ is below the discord distance, the search is correct but keeps many candidates and is slow. If $r$ is above
it, no candidate survives and the search must be repeated with a smaller $r$. MERLIN chooses $r$ at each length from
the discord distances already found at nearby lengths, which are close to the new one, and lowers it when a search
returns nothing. The only inputs are the length range.

### Trillion-scale left discords: DAMP

DAMP processes subsequences in time order after a training prefix and keeps the best-so-far discord score
$b$, the largest left-profile value found so far <Cite id="lu2022" />. For each new subsequence $T_i$, DAMP only decides
whether $P^L_i > b$, which is cheaper than computing $P^L_i$.

- **Backward processing.** Compute the distance profile of $T_i$ against the chunk of the past immediately before
  it, of length a small multiple of $m$, with <Gloss name="mass" />. If some distance in that chunk is below $b$, then
  $P^L_i < b$, and $T_i$ cannot be the top discord. Stop. Otherwise double the chunk length and search further back.
  If the search reaches the start of the series, the minimum found is the exact $P^L_i$, which then becomes the new
  $b$ if it is larger.
- **Forward pruning.** Compute the distance profile of $T_i$ against a chunk of the future. Any future subsequence
  $T_j$ with $d(i, j) < b$ has an earlier match closer than $b$, namely $T_i$, so $P^L_j < b$ and $T_j$ can be skipped
  when its turn comes.

Most subsequences in real data resemble their recent past, so the backward search usually stops in the first chunk,
and forward pruning skips many subsequences entirely. The cost per point is then close to constant, and DAMP scales to
trillions of points and to streams arriving faster than a full matrix profile could be updated <Cite id="lu2022" />.
The output is an approximate left matrix profile, exact for every subsequence that could be the top discord.

### Benchmarks

Wu and Keogh examined the benchmark datasets most used to evaluate <NoteLink to="time-series-anomaly-types">time series anomaly</NoteLink> detectors and found four
flaws <Cite id="wu2021" />.

- **Triviality.** Many benchmark anomalies can be found with one line of code, such as a threshold on the absolute
  first difference. Success on them says little about a sophisticated method.
- **Unrealistic anomaly density.** Some datasets label a large share of points, or many contiguous regions, as
  anomalous, which blurs the distinction between <NoteLink to="anomaly-detection">anomaly detection</NoteLink> and classification.
- **Mislabelled ground truth.** Some labels mark normal data as anomalous, or miss clear anomalies.
- **Run-to-failure bias.** In many datasets the anomalies sit near the end of the series, so a detector that favours
  late positions scores well.

They proposed a new archive of datasets, each with one anomaly, designed to avoid these flaws <Cite id="wu2021" />.
Discord methods such as DAMP are evaluated on it <Cite id="lu2022" />.

## Worked example

In the figure's default series, 480 points of heartbeat-like beats contain the same abnormal beat at positions 204
and 407 (counting from 0), with $m = 40$ and a training prefix of 120 samples.

- The full matrix profile at the two abnormal beats is 1.373 and 1.374: each is the other's nearest neighbour. Its
  maximum, 4.326, lies at position 339, a normal beat distorted by noise and length jitter.
- The left matrix profile at the first abnormal beat is 6.487, because no earlier subsequence resembles it. Its
  maximum, 6.656, lies at position 219, a window overlapping the same beat. At the second abnormal beat the left
  profile is 1.374, since the first one is now an earlier match.

The full matrix profile misses the twin anomaly; the left matrix profile finds its first occurrence.

## Assumptions

- **Anomalies are unusual shapes.** Discords compare z-normalised shapes. An anomaly that is only a change of level or
  scale is invisible to them.
- **Enough normal history.** The left matrix profile needs a training prefix long enough to contain the normal
  behaviour. Before that, every new pattern looks novel.

## Pitfalls

- **Repeated anomalies.** The full matrix profile misses anomalies that occur twice; the left matrix profile flags
  only the first occurrence. Counting anomalies needs another method.
- **Noise as discord.** In a series with no true anomaly, the top discord is the noisiest stretch. The discord score
  must be compared with the distribution of the profile, not read as a probability.
- **Benchmark claims.** A detector's reported advantage on a flawed benchmark may not survive on data without trivial
  or mislabelled anomalies <Cite id="wu2021" />.
