<Definition>
The matrix profile family is a set of time-series data-mining methods that share one computation. That computation is
the all-pairs similarity join over the subsequences of a series. Every subsequence of length $m$ is compared with
every other one under z-normalised Euclidean distance, and only the distance to its nearest neighbour and that
neighbour's position are kept. Most members were published by Eamonn Keogh's group at UC Riverside and collaborators
as a numbered paper series, "Matrix Profile I" onwards <Cite id="keogh-matrix-profile" />.
</Definition>

## The shared core

Every member computes some variant of the same quantity. Write $T_i$ for the length-$m$ subsequence of a series $x$
starting at position $i$, and $d(i, j)$ for the Euclidean distance between $T_i$ and $T_j$ after each is z-normalised to
mean 0 and <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink> 1. The
<NoteLink to="matrix-profile">matrix profile</NoteLink> stores $P_i = \min_{j} d(i, j)$ over positions $j$ outside an
exclusion zone around $i$. Its index $I_i$ stores the minimising $j$.

Three facts make the family possible.

- $d(i, j)^2 = 2m\paren{1 - \rho_{ij}}$, where $\rho_{ij}$ is the <NoteLink to="pearson-correlation">Pearson
  correlation</NoteLink> of $T_i$ and $T_j$. The distance needs only a dot product and the means and standard deviations
  of the two subsequences.
- The dot products of one subsequence with all others are a convolution. The <Gloss name="fft" /> computes them in
  $O(n \log n)$ for a series of length $n$. This is the <NoteLink to="distance-profile-and-mass">distance profile and
  MASS</NoteLink>.
- Along a diagonal of the distance matrix, the dot product changes by one subtraction and one addition. All $n^2$
  distances therefore cost $O(n^2)$ time, independent of $m$, and $O(n)$ memory.

The members differ in what they compute from the join (motifs, discords, chains, segment boundaries), in which pairs
they join (one series, two series, many dimensions, many lengths) and in how they schedule the work (exactly, in
anytime order, incrementally, on <Gloss name="gpu" plural />).

## Progression

### One join for many problems

Matrix Profile I reframed motif discovery (repeated patterns), discord discovery (anomalies) and similarity joins
between two series as the same computation <Cite id="yeh2016" />. Before it, each problem had its own algorithms,
indexes and parameters. The matrix profile answers all of them from one vector of $n - m + 1$ numbers, with $m$ as the
only parameter. STAMP, the first algorithm, computes one row of the distance matrix at a time with MASS and processes
rows in random order, so an approximate profile is available at any moment. STAMPI updates the profile as points
arrive.

### Faster exact algorithms

Matrix Profile II replaced the $O(n \log n)$ FFT per row with an $O(1)$ update per entry along diagonals. STOMP costs
$O(n^2)$ in total and runs on GPUs <Cite id="zhu2016" />. SCRIMP kept the diagonal update but processed diagonals in
random order, which restores the anytime property. PreSCRIMP adds a cheap approximate first pass <Cite id="zhu2018" />.
SCAMP rewrote the update in terms of centred covariances, which stays accurate in
<NoteLink to="floating-point-arithmetic">single precision</NoteLink> over long diagonals, and distributed the work
across GPUs and cloud machines <Cite id="zimmerman2019" />. The note on <NoteLink to="matrix-profile-algorithms">matrix
profile algorithms</NoteLink> derives each update.

### Streams, joins and many dimensions

The join need not be a self-join. Joining two series $A$ and $B$ finds what they share and what each has that the
other lacks. Joining each subsequence only with earlier ones gives the left matrix profile, which can be maintained as
points arrive (<NoteLink to="streaming-and-incremental-matrix-profile">streaming matrix profile</NoteLink>). mSTAMP
extends the join to multidimensional series and chooses how many dimensions a motif spans <Cite id="yeh2017" />
(<NoteLink to="matrix-profile-joins">joins and multidimensional profiles</NoteLink>).

### New primitives from the index

The profile index $I$ holds more structure than the profile values. Time series chains follow left and right nearest
neighbours to find a pattern that evolves (<NoteLink to="time-series-chains">time series chains</NoteLink>). FLUSS
counts the index links that cross each position; few crossings mark a regime boundary
(<NoteLink to="matrix-profile-segmentation">segmentation</NoteLink>). MPdist turns two join profiles into a distance
between whole series, and snippets use it to summarise a series
(<NoteLink to="time-series-snippets">snippets</NoteLink>).

### Every length

A single $m$ is a guess about the length of the pattern. MOEN enumerated motifs of all lengths with lower bounds across
lengths <Cite id="mueen2013" />, before the numbered series. VALMOD computes the motifs over a length range with
similar bounds <Cite id="linardi2018" />. The pan matrix profile stacks matrix profiles for every length on a common
scale <Cite id="madrid2019" />. Consensus motifs extend the search from one series to a set
<Cite id="kamgar2019" />. MERLIN does for discords what the pan matrix profile does for motifs
<Cite id="nakamura2020" />. These are collected in <NoteLink to="motif-discovery-variants">motif discovery
variants</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="discord-discovery-at-scale">discord discovery at scale</NoteLink>.

### Anomalies at scale

DAMP computes the left matrix profile only where it can matter for the top discord. It searches backward from each
new subsequence for a close enough earlier match and prunes future subsequences that are already too similar to the
current one. The result scales discord discovery to trillions of points and to streams that arrive faster than a
matrix profile could be updated <Cite id="lu2022" />. Alongside it, Wu and Keogh showed that popular anomaly
benchmarks are flawed and can make weak detectors look strong <Cite id="wu2021" />.

## Relations to other time-series methods

Matrix profile methods compare raw subsequences under z-normalised Euclidean distance. Two other lines of work make
different choices. <NoteLink to="dynamic-time-warping">Dynamic time warping</NoteLink> allows local stretching of the
time axis at a higher cost per comparison. <NoteLink to="symbolic-aggregate-approximation">SAX</NoteLink> discretises
subsequences into words, which trades exactness for speed and indexing. <NoteLink to="shapelets">Shapelets</NoteLink>
use the same nearest-subsequence distance as the matrix profile to separate classes; Matrix Profile I describes
shapelet discovery as a join between the series of two classes <Cite id="yeh2016" />.
