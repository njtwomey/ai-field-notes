<Definition>
A **seasonal ARIMA** model with period $m$, written ARIMA($p$, $d$, $q$)($P$, $D$, $Q$)$_m$, is

$$
\Phi(B^m)\,\phi(B)\,(1 - B)^d(1 - B^m)^D\,x_t = c + \Theta(B^m)\,\theta(B)\,\varepsilon_t,
$$

where $\phi$ and $\theta$ are the ordinary <Gloss name="ar" /> and MA polynomials of degrees $p$ and $q$, and
$\Phi(z) = 1 - \Phi_1z - \dots - \Phi_Pz^P$ and $\Theta(z) = 1 + \Theta_1z + \dots + \Theta_Qz^Q$ are the seasonal
polynomials, evaluated at $B^m$. $D$ is the number of seasonal differences $1 - B^m$. All roots of $\phi$, $\theta$,
$\Phi$ and $\Theta$ lie outside the unit circle, and $\varepsilon_t \sim \operatorname{WN}(0, \sigma^2)$
<Cite id="box2015" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Monthly data have two kinds of memory. This month relates to last month, and this January relates to last January.
A seasonal <NoteLink to="autoregressive-integrated-moving-average-model">ARIMA model</NoteLink> gives each kind its own ARIMA structure. The seasonal polynomials act on lags that are multiples
of $m$, so a seasonal AR(1) term $\Phi_1$ links $x_t$ to $x_{t-12}$ directly, with a single parameter.

The model is **multiplicative**: the ordinary and seasonal polynomials multiply. Multiplying produces interaction
terms. The product $(1 + \theta B)(1 + \Theta B^{12}) = 1 + \theta B + \Theta B^{12} + \theta\Theta B^{13}$ has a
coefficient at lag 13 without any new parameter. The interaction says that the effect of last month's shock on this
month is the same in every month, and that last year's shock enters with the same one-month echo.

## Seasonal differencing

The seasonal difference $(1 - B^m)x_t = x_t - x_{t-m}$ removes a fixed seasonal pattern and a seasonal unit root
(<NoteLink to="differencing-and-unit-roots">differencing and unit roots</NoteLink>). The polynomial $1 - z^m$ has $m$
roots on the unit circle, at the $m$-th roots of unity $e^{2\pi ik/m}$. Each root corresponds to one seasonal frequency
$k/m$ cycles per step, including frequency 0. So $(1 - B^m)$ contains the ordinary difference $(1 - B)$ as a factor:
$1 - z^m = (1 - z)(1 + z + \dots + z^{m-1})$. A seasonal difference therefore also removes a stochastic trend.

In practice $D$ is 0 or 1, and $d + D$ rarely exceeds 2. A strong, stable seasonal pattern usually calls for $D = 1$.

## The airline model

Box and Jenkins fitted the monthly totals of international airline passengers, 1949–1960, on the log scale, with
the model ARIMA(0, 1, 1)(0, 1, 1)$_{12}$ <Cite id="box2015" />. It became a standard benchmark and is called the
**airline model**:

$$
w_t = (1 - B)(1 - B^{12})x_t = (1 + \theta B)(1 + \Theta B^{12})\varepsilon_t
= \varepsilon_t + \theta\varepsilon_{t-1} + \Theta\varepsilon_{t-12} + \theta\Theta\varepsilon_{t-13}.
$$

The doubly differenced series $w_t$ is an MA(13) with only two free parameters. Its autocovariances follow from the
MA formula $\gamma(h) = \sigma^2\sum_ja_ja_{j+h}$ with coefficients $a_0 = 1$, $a_1 = \theta$, $a_{12} = \Theta$ and
$a_{13} = \theta\Theta$ (<NoteLink to="moving-average-model">moving-average model</NoteLink>):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma(0) &= \sigma^2(1 + \theta^2)(1 + \Theta^2), \\
\gamma(1) &= \sigma^2(a_0a_1 + a_{12}a_{13}) = \sigma^2\theta(1 + \Theta^2), \\
\gamma(11) &= \sigma^2a_1a_{12} = \sigma^2\theta\Theta, \\
\gamma(12) &= \sigma^2(a_0a_{12} + a_1a_{13}) = \sigma^2\Theta(1 + \theta^2), \\
\gamma(13) &= \sigma^2a_0a_{13} = \sigma^2\theta\Theta,
\end{aligned}
$$

and every other lag is 0. Dividing by $\gamma(0)$:

$$
\rho(1) = \frac{\theta}{1 + \theta^2}, \qquad \rho(12) = \frac{\Theta}{1 + \Theta^2}, \qquad \rho(11) = \rho(13) = \rho(1)\rho(12).
$$

The <Gloss name="acf" /> has spikes at lags 1 and 12, and smaller spikes at 11 and 13 whose size is the product of the two. Those side
lobes are the fingerprint of a multiplicative seasonal MA.

## Worked example

Take $\theta = -0.4$ and $\Theta = -0.6$. The lag-1 autocorrelation is $-0.4/1.16 = -0.345$ and the lag-12
autocorrelation is $-0.6/1.36 = -0.441$. The side lobes are $\rho(11) = \rho(13) = (-0.345)(-0.441) = 0.152$, positive
because both factors are negative. All other autocorrelations are 0.

With $\alpha = 1 + \theta = 0.6$ the non-seasonal part behaves like <Gloss name="ses">simple exponential smoothing</Gloss> of the level. With
$1 + \Theta = 0.4$ the seasonal part smooths the seasonal pattern across years. A negative $\Theta$ close to $-1$ means
the seasonal pattern is nearly fixed; the seasonal difference then nearly cancels against the seasonal MA factor.

## Identification

The ACF and <Gloss name="pacf" /> are read at two scales <Cite id="hyndman2021" />.

- **Seasonal lags** $m, 2m, 3m, \dots$ identify $P$ and $Q$. A seasonal MA($Q$) gives ACF spikes at lags
  $m, \dots, Qm$ and none after. A seasonal AR($P$) gives PACF spikes at $m, \dots, Pm$ and an ACF that decays across
  seasonal lags.
- **Short lags** $1, 2, \dots$ identify $p$ and $q$ as for an ordinary ARMA model.
- **Side lobes** around seasonal lags are expected from the multiplication and do not call for extra terms.

<NoteLink to="information-criteria">Information criteria</NoteLink> then compare the candidate models, fitted to the same differenced data
(<NoteLink to="box-jenkins-method">Box–Jenkins method</NoteLink>).

## Assumptions

- The seasonal period $m$ is known and fixed.
- After ordinary and seasonal differencing, the series is a stationary, invertible multiplicative <Gloss name="arma" /> process.
- The multiplicative structure holds; an additive model with free coefficients at lags 1, 12 and 13 is an alternative
  when it does not.

## Pitfalls

- **Long periods.** With weekly data ($m = 52$) or daily data with an annual cycle ($m = 365$), seasonal differencing
  discards a whole year of data and seasonal polynomials need very long lags. Fourier terms in a
  <NoteLink to="regression-with-autoregressive-moving-average-errors">regression with ARMA errors</NoteLink> handle long periods with a few
  parameters.
- **Multiple seasonalities** (hourly data with daily and weekly cycles) do not fit one period $m$.
- **Over-differencing** with both $d = 1$ and $D = 1$ when one suffices introduces a non-invertible MA factor. Check for
  $\hat\theta$ or $\hat\Theta$ near $-1$.
