<Definition>
An autoregressive language model outputs, at each step $t$, logits $\zvec \in \reals^V$ over a vocabulary of $V$
tokens, and the next-token distribution $p(x_t = i \mid x_{<t}) = \operatorname{softmax}(\zvec)_i$. A decoding strategy
is a rule that chooses $x_t$ from these logits, appends it to the prefix and repeats until an end token or a length
limit. Deterministic strategies search for a high-probability sequence; stochastic strategies sample from a modified
distribution <Cite id="holtzman2020" />.
</Definition>

## Search

**Greedy decoding** takes $x_t = \argmax_i z_i$ at every step. It is fast and deterministic. It does not find the most
probable sequence, because a locally best token can lead to poor continuations.

**Beam search** keeps the $B$ highest-scoring prefixes, extends each by every token, and keeps the $B$ best extensions by
total log-probability. Greedy decoding is beam search with $B = 1$. The note on
<NoteLink to="sequence-to-sequence">sequence-to-sequence models</NoteLink> gives a worked example and the length
normalisation that beam search needs.

Search suits tasks with one correct output, such as translation. For open-ended generation it produces dull and
repetitive text. The probability of a repeated phrase tends to grow with each repetition, so maximising probability
drives the text into loops <Cite id="holtzman2020" />. Human text is not the most probable text: its tokens often have
modest probability under the model.

## Sampling

Ancestral sampling draws $x_t \sim p(\cdot \mid x_{<t})$ at each step. Pure sampling from a large vocabulary produces
incoherent text, because the thousands of individually unlikely tokens in the tail together carry substantial
probability, and one bad draw derails the rest <Cite id="holtzman2020" />. The strategies below reshape the distribution
before sampling.

### Temperature

Divide the logits by a temperature $T > 0$ before the softmax:

$$
p_T(i) = \frac{e^{z_i / T}}{\sum_{j} e^{z_j / T}}.
$$

The ratio of two probabilities is $p_T(i) / p_T(j) = e^{(z_i - z_j)/T}$. With $T < 1$ each logit gap counts for more, so
the distribution sharpens toward the largest logit; as $T \to 0$ it concentrates on the argmax, and sampling becomes
greedy decoding. With $T > 1$ the gaps shrink; as $T \to \infty$ the distribution becomes uniform. $T = 1$ is the model's
own distribution. Equivalently, $p_T \propto p^{1/T}$: temperature raises every probability to the same power and
renormalises. The order of the tokens never changes.

**Entropy rises with temperature.** Write $\beta = 1/T$ and $Z(\beta) = \sum_j e^{\beta z_j}$. Then $\log p_\beta(i) =
\beta z_i - \log Z(\beta)$, and the <NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink> is

$$
\entropy(\beta) = -\sum_i p_\beta(i) \log p_\beta(i) = \log Z(\beta) - \beta\, \expect_\beta[z].
$$

The derivative of $\log Z$ is $\frac{d}{d\beta}\log Z(\beta) = \sum_i z_i e^{\beta z_i} / Z(\beta) = \expect_\beta[z]$,
and the derivative of the mean is $\frac{d}{d\beta}\expect_\beta[z] = \var_\beta[z]$. So

$$
\frac{d\entropy}{d\beta} = \expect_\beta[z] - \expect_\beta[z] - \beta \var_\beta[z] = -\beta \var_\beta[z] \le 0.
$$

Entropy falls as $\beta$ grows, so it rises with $T$. It is constant only when all logits are equal. The same rescaling,
fitted on held-out data rather than set by hand, is <NoteLink to="temperature-scaling">temperature scaling</NoteLink> for
calibration.

### Top-k sampling

Keep the $k$ tokens with the largest logits, set the others to probability zero, renormalise and sample. Fan et al. used
$k = 10$ for story generation <Cite id="fan2018" />. A fixed $k$ does not adapt to the context. When the model is
confident, $k$ tokens include implausible ones; when many continuations are reasonable, $k$ tokens exclude some of them.

### Nucleus (top-p) sampling

Sort the tokens by probability and keep the smallest set $\Vcal^{(p)}$ whose total probability is at least $p$:

$$
\Vcal^{(p)} = \text{smallest prefix of the sorted tokens with} \sum_{i \in \Vcal^{(p)}} p(i) \ge p.
$$

Renormalise over $\Vcal^{(p)}$ and sample <Cite id="holtzman2020" />. The number of tokens kept adapts to the shape of
the distribution: one or two tokens when the model is confident, hundreds when it is not. Holtzman et al. found that
nucleus sampling produced text whose statistics, such as its perplexity under the model and its vocabulary diversity,
were closer to human text than greedy decoding, beam search, pure sampling or top-$k$ sampling.

Implementations usually apply temperature first, then top-$k$, then top-$p$, and renormalise once at the end.

## Worked example

Take four tokens with logits $\zvec = (2, 1, 0, -1)$.

- **$T = 1$.** $p = (0.644, 0.237, 0.087, 0.032)$, with entropy 1.37 bits.
- **$T = 0.5$.** The logits double to $(4, 2, 0, -2)$ and $p = (0.865, 0.117, 0.016, 0.002)$, with entropy 0.66 bits.
- **$T = 2$.** The logits halve and $p = (0.455, 0.276, 0.167, 0.102)$, with entropy 1.80 bits.
- **Top-$k$ with $k = 2$, at $T = 1$.** Keep 0.644 and 0.237, which sum to 0.881, and renormalise to $(0.731, 0.269)$.
- **Top-$p$ with $p = 0.9$, at $T = 1$.** The cumulative sums are 0.644, 0.881 and 0.968. Two tokens reach only 0.881, so
  three are kept, and renormalising gives $(0.665, 0.245, 0.090)$.

Top-$k$ with $k = 3$ would give the same result here. On a flatter distribution top-$p$ keeps more tokens; on a sharper
one, fewer.

## Pitfalls

- **Temperature 0 is a limit, not a value.** Implementations treat $T = 0$ as greedy decoding. Very small positive $T$
  overflows a naive softmax unless the maximum logit is subtracted first (see
  <NoteLink to="softmax-and-log-sum-exp">softmax and log-sum-exp</NoteLink>).
- **Settings interact.** At low temperature the nucleus shrinks, so top-$p$ may already keep one token; at high
  temperature top-$k$ may be the only thing standing between the model and its tail.
- **Truncation changes the distribution.** Samples no longer come from the model, so sample-based estimates, such as the
  average log-probability of generated text, are biased.
- **Search and sampling suit different tasks.** Constrained outputs (translation, code, extraction) favour greedy or beam
  search; open-ended outputs favour truncated sampling.
