<Definition>
The **Cox proportional hazards model** specifies the hazard of a unit with features $\xvec \in \reals^p$ as

$$
h(t \mid \xvec) = h_0(t)\exp(\betavec^\top\xvec),
$$

where $h_0(t)$ is a **baseline hazard** left completely unspecified and $\betavec \in \reals^p$ are coefficients. The
ratio of the hazards of two units does not depend on time, so their hazards are proportional. $\betavec$ is estimated
without estimating $h_0$, from the **partial likelihood** <Cite id="cox1972" />.
</Definition>

## Interpretation

For two units whose features differ only in $x_j$, by one unit,

$$
\frac{h(t \mid \xvec + \evec_j)}{h(t \mid \xvec)} = e^{\beta_j}
$$

at every time $t$. $e^{\beta_j}$ is the **hazard ratio**. A hazard ratio of 0.5 for a treatment means that, at any
time, a treated patient still at risk has half the event rate of an untreated one. It does not mean that survival
times double, and it does not give the absolute risk, which needs $h_0$. There is no intercept: a constant would be
absorbed into $h_0$.

## The partial likelihood

Assume no tied event times. Order the observed event times $t_{(1)} < \dots < t_{(k)}$, let $i_j$ be the unit that
fails at $t_{(j)}$, and let $R_j$ be the **risk set**, the units still at risk just before $t_{(j)}$. Given that exactly
one unit in $R_j$ fails at $t_{(j)}$, the probability that it is unit $i_j$ is its hazard divided by the total hazard of
the risk set:

$$
\frac{h_0(t_{(j)})\exp(\betavec^\top\xvec_{i_j})}{\sum_{l \in R_j}h_0(t_{(j)})\exp(\betavec^\top\xvec_l)} =
\frac{\exp(\betavec^\top\xvec_{i_j})}{\sum_{l \in R_j}\exp(\betavec^\top\xvec_l)}.
$$

The baseline hazard cancels because it multiplies every term. The **partial likelihood** is the product of these
conditional probabilities over event times:

$$
L_P(\betavec) = \prod_{j=1}^k\frac{\exp(\betavec^\top\xvec_{i_j})}{\sum_{l \in R_j}\exp(\betavec^\top\xvec_l)}.
$$

Censored units never appear in a numerator, but they appear in the denominators of every event time up to their
<NoteLink to="censoring">censoring</NoteLink> time. Cox argued that the factors omitted from the full likelihood carry little information about
$\betavec$ when $h_0$ is arbitrary. $L_P$ can be treated as a likelihood: its maximiser is consistent and
asymptotically normal, with covariance given by the inverse of the observed information <Cite id="cox1975" />.

## Score and information

The log partial likelihood is $\ell_P(\betavec) = \sum_j\brack{\betavec^\top\xvec_{i_j} - \log\sum_{l \in R_j}
e^{\betavec^\top\xvec_l}}$. Define the weights $\pi_{jl} = e^{\betavec^\top\xvec_l}/\sum_{m \in R_j}
e^{\betavec^\top\xvec_m}$ over the risk set and the weighted feature mean $\bar\xvec_j = \sum_{l \in R_j}\pi_{jl}
\xvec_l$. Then

$$
\uvec(\betavec) = \sum_{j=1}^k\paren{\xvec_{i_j} - \bar\xvec_j}, \qquad \Imat(\betavec) = \sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{l \in
R_j}\pi_{jl}\paren{\xvec_l - \bar\xvec_j}\paren{\xvec_l - \bar\xvec_j}^\top.
$$

The score compares each failing unit's features with the risk-weighted average of those at risk. The information is a
sum of weighted covariance matrices, <Gloss name="positive-semi-definite">positive semi-definite</Gloss>, so $\ell_P$ is concave and <NoteLink to="newtons-method">Newton's method</NoteLink> converges to
the unique maximum when it exists.

**Link to the log-rank test.** With a single group indicator $x \in \set{0, 1}$ and $\beta = 0$, all weights are equal,
and $\bar x_j = n_{1j}/n_j$, the fraction of the risk set in group 1. The score is then $\sum_j (x_{i_j} -
n_{1j}/n_j) = O_1 - E_1$, and the information is $\sum_j (n_{1j}/n_j)(1 - n_{1j}/n_j) = V$. The score test of $\beta
= 0$ is the <NoteLink to="log-rank-test">log-rank test</NoteLink>.

## Tied event times

With $d_j > 1$ events at $t_{(j)}$, the exact <NoteLink to="conditional-probability">conditional probability</NoteLink> sums over all subsets of size $d_j$ of the risk
set, which is expensive. **Breslow's approximation** uses the same denominator for every tied event
<Cite id="breslow1974" />:

$$
\frac{\exp\paren{\betavec^\top\svec_j}}{\paren{\sum_{l \in R_j}e^{\betavec^\top\xvec_l}}^{d_j}},
$$

where $\svec_j$ is the sum of the features of the $d_j$ failing units. **Efron's approximation** removes a growing
fraction of the tied units' risk from successive denominators and is closer to the exact result when ties are many
<Cite id="efron1977" />.

## Baseline hazard

Once $\hat\betavec$ is known, **Breslow's estimator** of the cumulative baseline hazard is

$$
\hat H_0(t) = \sum_{j:\, t_{(j)} \le t}\frac{d_j}{\sum_{l \in R_j}\exp(\hat\betavec^\top\xvec_l)},
$$

the <NoteLink to="nelson-aalen-estimator">Nelson–Aalen estimator</NoteLink> with each unit at risk weighted by its
relative hazard <Cite id="breslow1974" />. The predicted survival of a unit with features $\xvec$ is
$\hat S(t \mid \xvec) = \exp\paren{-\hat H_0(t)e^{\hat\betavec^\top\xvec}}$.

## Worked example

Treated (A, $x = 1$) and control (B, $x = 0$) follow-up times in months, with $^+$ marking censoring:

- A: $6, 9^+, 11, 14, 18^+, 21$
- B: $2, 4, 5^+, 7, 8, 12$

There are no ties. Maximising $\ell_P(\beta)$ numerically gives $\hat\beta = -1.645$, a hazard ratio
$e^{\hat\beta} = 0.193$ for treatment. The observed information at $\hat\beta$ gives a <NoteLink to="standard-error">standard error</NoteLink> of 0.854, so the
Wald statistic is $z = -1.93$ with <NoteLink to="p-value">p-value</NoteLink> 0.054. The likelihood-ratio statistic, $2\brack{\ell_P(\hat\beta) -
\ell_P(0)} = 4.21$, has p-value 0.040. The score statistic at $\beta = 0$ is $4.49$, equal to the log-rank statistic
for the same data. With six patients per arm, the three tests disagree about significance at the 5% level. They agree
only asymptotically.

## Checking proportional hazards

The model's central assumption is that hazard ratios are constant in time. Checks include:

- **Schoenfeld residuals.** For each event, the residual is $\xvec_{i_j} - \bar\xvec_j$ <Cite id="schoenfeld1982" />.
  If the effect of $x_m$ changes over time, its scaled residuals trend against time. Grambsch and Therneau give a test
  based on their correlation with time <Cite id="grambsch1994" />.
- **Log cumulative hazard plots.** For a categorical feature, the curves $\log\hat H(t)$ of the groups should be
  parallel.

When the assumption fails, a time-varying coefficient $\beta_j(t)$, a stratified model with a separate baseline for
each stratum, or an <NoteLink to="parametric-survival-models">accelerated failure time model</NoteLink> may fit instead.

## Pitfalls

- **Hazard ratios are not risk ratios.** A hazard ratio of 0.5 can correspond to a small difference in five-year
  survival if events are rare.
- **Time-varying features.** Features measured after baseline must enter as time-varying covariates, evaluated in each
  risk set at the event time. Using a value from the future, such as "received a transplant at some point",
  creates immortal time bias.
- **Non-collapsibility.** Adding a prognostic feature changes the hazard ratio of another feature even when the two are
  independent. Hazard ratios from models with different features are not directly comparable.
