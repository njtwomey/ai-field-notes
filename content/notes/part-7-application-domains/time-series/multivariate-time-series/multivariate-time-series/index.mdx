<Definition>
A **multivariate time series** is a vector $\yvec_t \in \reals^k$ of $k$ series observed at the same times, such as
output, inflation and interest rates. Multivariate models describe how each component depends on the past of all
components, and whether the components share long-run movements.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Related series carry information about each other. Interest rates help forecast inflation; the price of a commodity in
one market tracks its price in another. A joint model forecasts all components together, measures how a shock to one
variable spreads to the others, and tests whether one series helps predict another. These questions are central in
macroeconomics and finance and recur wherever several sensors or markets are observed at once
<Cite id="lutkepohl2005" />.

## Main ideas

**Regress every series on the past of all series.** <NoteLink to="vector-autoregression" /> (VAR) writes $\yvec_t$ as a
constant plus matrix-weighted sums of its last $p$ values plus noise. Each equation is fitted by least squares. The
model is stable when the eigenvalues of its companion matrix lie inside the unit circle. Impulse responses trace the
effect of a one-off shock to one variable on all variables over the following periods.

**Predictive value is testable.** <NoteLink to="granger-causality" /> says $x$ Granger-causes $y$ if past values of $x$
reduce the error of forecasting $y$ beyond what past values of $y$ achieve. An F test compares the two regressions.
The test concerns prediction: a third series that drives both $x$ and $y$ can produce Granger causality without any
causal link.

**Wandering series can move together.** <NoteLink to="cointegration" /> describes non-stationary series with a
stationary linear combination: each wanders like a <NoteLink to="random-walk">random walk</NoteLink>, but
$y_t - \beta x_t$ stays near a constant. Regressing one unrelated random walk on another gives spurious significance, so
cointegration must be tested, not assumed. The Engle–Granger test checks the regression residuals for a unit root
<Cite id="engle1987" />.

**Deviations from equilibrium are corrected.** The <NoteLink to="error-correction-model" /> writes the change in $y_t$
as short-run responses to changes in its drivers plus a term that pulls $y_t$ back towards the long-run relation. The
Granger representation theorem states that every cointegrated system has this form.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="vector-autoregression" />, which sets the notation. Granger causality is a test within that
model and can be read next. Cointegration is a separate thread for non-stationary series; it requires differencing and
unit roots, and the error-correction model builds directly on it.
