<Definition>
A **state-space model** describes a time series $\xvec_1, \dots, \xvec_T$ through an unobserved state $\zvec_t$. The
state follows a Markov transition $p(\zvec_t \mid \zvec_{t-1})$, and each observation depends only on the current
state through $p(\xvec_t \mid \zvec_t)$. Inference recovers the state from the observations: **filtering** computes
$p(\zvec_t \mid \xvec_{1:t})$ and **smoothing** computes $p(\zvec_t \mid \xvec_{1:T})$.
</Definition>

## What it is for

State-space models track quantities that are measured indirectly and with noise: the position of a vehicle from GPS
fixes, the level and trend of a demand series, the volatility of a price. The same framework handles missing
observations, irregular sampling and several sensors at once, because each observation enters only through its
likelihood. <Gloss name="arima" /> and exponential-smoothing models can be written in state-space form, so the filters
below also compute their likelihoods and forecasts <Cite id="sarkka2013" />.

## Main ideas

**The linear-Gaussian model is solved exactly.** <NoteLink to="linear-gaussian-state-space-models" /> sets
$\zvec_t = \Amat\zvec_{t-1} + \wvec_t$ and $\xvec_t = \Cmat\zvec_t + \vvec_t$ with Gaussian noise. Every filtering and
smoothing distribution is then Gaussian, and the <Gloss name="em-algorithm">expectation-maximisation</Gloss> algorithm
learns $\Amat$, $\Cmat$ and the noise covariances in closed form <Cite id="bishop2006" />.

**Filtering alternates prediction and correction.** The <NoteLink to="kalman-filter" /> pushes the current estimate
through the dynamics, then corrects it by the gain times the innovation, the gap between the new observation and its
prediction. The <NoteLink to="kalman-smoother" /> adds a backward pass that revises each estimate with later
observations.

**Nonlinear models need approximations.** <NoteLink to="extended-and-unscented-kalman-filters" /> keep a Gaussian
estimate. The extended filter linearises the model with Jacobians at the current mean; the unscented filter passes a
few deterministic sigma points through the nonlinear functions. Both fail when the true posterior is far from
Gaussian, for instance when it has several modes.

**Samples replace Gaussians.** The <NoteLink to="particle-filter" /> represents the filtering distribution by weighted
samples. It moves them through the dynamics, reweights them by the likelihood of each new observation and resamples
them when a few weights dominate. It applies to any model that can be simulated, at a cost that grows with the number
of particles.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="linear-gaussian-state-space-models" />, which sets out the model and the inference tasks. The
Kalman filter comes next and the smoother after it. The extended and unscented filters require the Kalman filter. The
particle filter requires only Bayes' theorem and can be read on its own.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume the <NoteLink to="multivariate-normal-distribution" />, <NoteLink to="bayes-theorem" /> and, for the
extended filter, the <NoteLink to="jacobian" />. The <NoteLink to="hidden-markov-model" /> is the state-space model with
a discrete state, and its <Gloss name="forward-backward">forward-backward algorithm</Gloss> is the discrete counterpart
of the Kalman smoother. The state equations of <NoteLink to="control-theory">control theory</NoteLink> are in
<NoteLink to="state-space-representation" />. Within this Part, the state-space forms of
<NoteLink to="autoregressive-and-moving-average-models" /> and <NoteLink to="exponential-smoothing" /> use the Kalman
filter for estimation, <NoteLink to="stochastic-volatility" /> needs filtering for its likelihood, and
<NoteLink to="deep-state-space-models" /> let a neural network set the parameters of a linear-Gaussian model. The
structured state-space layers of <NoteLink to="structured-state-space-models" /> share the linear recurrence but are
deterministic sequence layers, not probabilistic models.
