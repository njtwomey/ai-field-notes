<Definition>
A **time series** is a sequence of observations $x_1, \dots, x_n$ recorded in time order, in which each value usually
depends on the values before it. Time-series analysis models that dependence to forecast future values, to estimate
hidden quantities such as a level or a volatility, to find changes, repeated patterns and anomalies, and to classify
whole series.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Sales, energy demand, prices, sensor readings, web traffic and <NoteLink to="physiological-signals">physiological
signals</NoteLink> all arrive as time series. Organisations forecast them to plan stock, staff and capacity; engineers
monitor them for faults; scientists test whether one series helps predict another. The time order breaks the
independence assumption of most statistics and machine learning, so the models, the evaluation and even the train–test
split must respect it <Cite id="hyndman2021" />.

## Main ideas

**Dependence must be stable to be learned.** <NoteLink to="foundations-of-time-series" /> defines
<NoteLink to="stationarity">stationarity</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="white-noise-and-random-walk">white noise</NoteLink>
and the <NoteLink to="random-walk">random walk</NoteLink>, the
<NoteLink to="autocorrelation-and-partial-autocorrelation">autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation
functions</NoteLink>, decomposition into trend and season, and differencing. These tools recur in every later chapter.

**Classical forecasting models.** <NoteLink to="autoregressive-and-moving-average-models" /> write a series as a linear
filter of white noise and build models by the <NoteLink to="box-jenkins-method">Box–Jenkins</NoteLink> cycle
<Cite id="box2015" />. <NoteLink to="exponential-smoothing" /> updates a level, trend and season by weighted averages
and gives them a likelihood through the <NoteLink to="error-trend-seasonal-models">ETS models</NoteLink>. Both families
have state-space forms.

**Hidden states and changing variance.** <NoteLink to="state-space-models" /> explain observations through a hidden
state, estimated exactly by the <NoteLink to="kalman-filter">Kalman filter</NoteLink> for linear-Gaussian models and
approximately by extended, unscented and <NoteLink to="particle-filter">particle filters</NoteLink>.
<NoteLink to="volatility-models" /> such as <Gloss name="garch" /> describe a variance that changes over time.
<NoteLink to="multivariate-time-series" /> model several series jointly with <Gloss name="var-model">vector
autoregression</Gloss>, <NoteLink to="granger-causality">Granger causality</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="cointegration">cointegration</NoteLink> <Cite id="hamilton1994" />.

**Forecasts need intervals and honest tests.** <NoteLink to="forecasting-practice" /> gives
<NoteLink to="prediction-intervals">prediction intervals</NoteLink>, probabilistic forecasts, rolling-origin
cross-validation and reconciliation of forecasts across a hierarchy.

**Finding structure in a series.** <NoteLink to="change-point-detection" /> locates times at which the generating
distribution changes, offline or as data arrive. The <NoteLink to="matrix-profile-family" /> finds repeated patterns
and anomalies from each subsequence's nearest-neighbour distance. <NoteLink to="time-series-classification" /> labels
whole series by <Gloss name="dtw">dynamic time warping</Gloss>, symbolic words or shapelets.

**Neural forecasters learn across many series.** <NoteLink to="neural-forecasting-models" /> fit one network to many
related series, from recurrent likelihood models such as <Gloss name="deepar" /> to <Gloss name="n-beats" />, the
<Gloss name="tft">Temporal Fusion Transformer</Gloss> and pretrained foundation models that forecast new series without
training.

## How the notes fit together

Start with the foundations chapter, which every other chapter requires. <Gloss name="arima" /> and exponential smoothing
come next and can be read in either order. State-space models connect them and are needed for
<NoteLink to="stochastic-volatility">stochastic volatility</NoteLink>. Volatility and multivariate models extend the
autoregressive idea and can follow ARIMA directly. Forecasting practice applies to any forecaster and is best read after
one model family. Change points, the <NoteLink to="matrix-profile">matrix profile</NoteLink> and classification form a
separate data-mining thread that needs only the foundations. Neural forecasting comes last, since it compares itself
with the classical models.
