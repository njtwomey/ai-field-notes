<Definition>
A time series motif of length $m$ is a pair of non-overlapping subsequences with small z-normalised distance. The
<NoteLink to="matrix-profile">matrix profile</NoteLink> $P$ gives the best pair directly: $\argmin_i P_i$ and its
nearest neighbour <Cite id="yeh2016" />. The variants in this note relax one assumption each. Top-$k$ discovery returns
several distinct motifs. MOEN and VALMOD (Variable Length Motif Discovery) search a range of lengths
<Cite id="mueen2013" /> <Cite id="linardi2018" />. The pan matrix profile computes the matrix profile at every length
<Cite id="madrid2019" />. Consensus motifs look for one pattern shared by every series in a set
<Cite id="kamgar2019" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

The top motif of a single matrix profile answers a narrow question: which two subsequences of length $m$ are most
alike? Real questions are broader. A series may contain several different repeated behaviours. The right length is
rarely known in advance. A pattern of interest may occur once in each of many recordings rather than twice in one.
Each variant widens the question while keeping the matrix profile's exactness and its one-computation structure.

## Details

### Top-$k$ motifs

Take the minimum of $P$ and its neighbour as the first motif. Set $P$ to $\infty$ within an exclusion zone around both
members, so that the next minimum is not a shifted copy of the same pattern. Repeat $k$ times. Each motif can also be
widened to a set: every subsequence within a radius of the pair, for example twice the pair's distance, joins the motif.
The cost after the matrix profile is $O(kn)$, plus one <NoteLink to="distance-profile-and-mass">distance
profile</NoteLink> per motif for the radius search.

### Motifs over a range of lengths: MOEN and VALMOD

Computing a separate matrix profile for each length from $m_{\min}$ to $m_{\max}$ costs $O(n^2)$ per length. MOEN
enumerates the best motif of every length in the range with lower bounds <Cite id="mueen2013" />. The distance
between two subsequences at length $m$ bounds, from below, their distance at longer lengths after a correction for the
change in normalisation. Most pairs can then be discarded at longer lengths without recomputing their distance.

VALMOD (Variable Length Motif Discovery) computes the full matrix profile at $m_{\min}$ and keeps, for each
subsequence, a few of its smallest distance-profile entries together with lower bounds for longer lengths
<Cite id="linardi2018" />. At each longer length it updates only those candidates. When the best updated candidate is
below the smallest lower bound of the discarded ones, the new profile entry is exact; otherwise that distance profile
is recomputed. VALMOD reports a variable-length matrix profile in which each entry is the best motif distance over all
lengths, normalised by $\sqrt{m}$ so that lengths compare.

### All lengths: the pan matrix profile

The pan matrix profile stacks the matrix profiles for every length in a range into a two-dimensional array, one row
per length <Cite id="madrid2019" />. The figure divides each row by $2\sqrt{m}$, the largest possible z-normalised
distance at length $m$, so every row lies in $[0, 1]$. Dark cells mark subsequences with close matches at that
length, and reading down a column shows over which lengths a subsequence has a close match.

The pan matrix profile addresses the choice of $m$ by showing all choices at once. A motif that is dark over a wide
band of lengths is robust to the choice. The companion algorithm SKIMP computes the rows in an anytime order: it
starts with lengths spread across the range and fills in between them, so the overall picture is visible early and
refines as rows are added.

### Consensus motifs: Ostinato

Given a set of series $T^{(1)}, \dots, T^{(K)}$, a consensus motif is a subsequence that has a close match in every
other series. For a candidate subsequence $S$ in series $a$, its radius is the largest of its nearest-neighbour
distances into the other series,

$$
r(S) = \max_{b \ne a} \min_{j} d\paren{S, T^{(b)}_j} .
$$

The consensus motif minimises $r$ over all candidates in all series <Cite id="kamgar2019" />. A small radius means the
pattern occurs, closely, in every series. Each inner minimum is an entry of an
<NoteLink to="matrix-profile-joins">AB-join</NoteLink> profile between series $a$ and series $b$. A candidate can be
abandoned as soon as one of its nearest-neighbour distances exceeds the best radius found so far, which makes the search
much cheaper than computing every join in full.

## Worked example

**Consensus motif.** Generate three series of 50 points of Gaussian noise with
<NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink> 0.3 and add a 12-point pattern, a sine period with
rising amplitude scaled by 2, at positions 16, 30 and 20 (counting from 1). Search all length-12 subsequences of all
three series. The consensus motif is the subsequence at position 31 of the second series, one step from its planted
copy, with radius 0.992: its nearest neighbours in the first and third series are at most 0.992 away.

**Pan matrix profile.** In the figure's default series, a sawtooth of length 20 is planted at positions 41 and 322 and a
slower wave of length 72 at positions 122 and 389 (counting from 0). At $m = 20$ the top motif is the sawtooth pair, at
positions 41 and 322. At $m = 72$ the top motif is the wave pair, starting at 108 and 376: each window starts 13 or 14
samples before its copy and covers most of it. A matrix profile at either single length would report only one of the two
patterns.

## Assumptions

- **Z-normalised Euclidean distance.** All variants compare shapes point by point. A motif whose occurrences differ by
  local stretching needs a warping distance instead.
- **Lower bounds need z-normalisation.** The bounds that make MOEN and VALMOD efficient are derived for z-normalised
  distances and do not transfer to other distances unchanged.

## Pitfalls

- **Comparing lengths.** Raw distances grow with $m$. Profiles at different lengths must be normalised before their
  minima are compared, or longer motifs always look worse.
- **Trivial variants.** Without exclusion zones, the second-best motif is usually the first shifted by one sample.
- **Consensus from outliers.** The radius is a maximum, so one series without the pattern makes every candidate's
  radius large. A radius taken over all but the farthest one or two series tolerates such outliers, at the cost of a
weaker guarantee.
