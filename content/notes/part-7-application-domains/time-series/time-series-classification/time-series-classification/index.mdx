<Definition>
**Time-series classification** learns a map from a whole series $x_1, \dots, x_n$ to one of a finite set of classes,
from a training set of labelled series. The class usually depends on the shape of the series, or of a short pattern
inside it, rather than on any single value, and the informative pattern may appear at different times or speeds in
different series.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Examples are recognising a gesture from accelerometer readings, a heart condition from an electrocardiogram, a fault
from a vibration signal, or a species from the outline of a leaf traced as a one-dimensional series. A generic
classifier that treats the $n$ values as independent features ignores their order and fails when the pattern shifts in
time. The methods here measure similarity between shapes, or extract shape features, so that a standard classifier can
work on them <Cite id="bagnall2017" />.

## Main ideas

**Compare whole series under a flexible alignment.** <NoteLink to="dynamic-time-warping" /> (DTW) matches two series by
the cheapest monotone alignment of their points, so a pattern that is stretched or shifted still matches. A
one-nearest-neighbour classifier under DTW, with a constraint on how far the alignment may stray, is the standard
baseline. Lower bounds make the search over a training set fast.

**Compress series into symbols.** <NoteLink to="symbolic-aggregate-approximation" /> (SAX) averages a z-normalised
series over $w$ segments and maps each mean to a letter. The resulting words support hashing,
<NoteLink to="bag-of-words">bag-of-words</NoteLink> classifiers and a distance that never exceeds the Euclidean
distance, so it can prune searches without losing the true nearest neighbour.

**Classify by a short discriminative pattern.** <NoteLink to="shapelets" /> are subsequences whose minimum distance to
a series predicts its class. They give interpretable classifiers: the shapelet shows which local shape decides the
label. The <NoteLink to="shapelet-transform" /> picks the best $k$ shapelets and turns each series into $k$ distances
for any classifier. <NoteLink to="learned-shapelets" /> fit the shapelets themselves by gradient descent, jointly with a
logistic classifier.

**Progress is measured on shared benchmarks.**
<NoteLink to="time-series-classification-benchmarks" /> describes the UCR archive of 128 univariate datasets and the
comparison of classifiers by average rank. The 2017 bake-off found that few methods beat one-nearest-neighbour DTW;
ensembles over several representations and random convolutional features later led on accuracy and speed
<Cite id="dau2019" />.
