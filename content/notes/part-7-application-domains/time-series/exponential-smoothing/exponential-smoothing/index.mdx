<Definition>
**Exponential smoothing** forecasts a time series by keeping a small set of components, such as a level, a slope and
seasonal indices, and updating each one at every step as a weighted average of its old value and the newest
observation. Unrolling the update gives weights on past observations that decay geometrically with their age, which is
the origin of the name.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Exponential smoothing produces fast, automatic forecasts for large numbers of series, such as the weekly demand for
every product in a retailer's catalogue. Each method has one to three smoothing constants, fitted per series. The
methods need no <NoteLink to="stationarity">stationarity</NoteLink> transformation and update in constant time as data
arrive. They are a standard benchmark in forecasting competitions and in business forecasting software
<Cite id="gardner1985" />.

## Main ideas

**A level updated by a weighted average.** <NoteLink to="simple-exponential-smoothing" /> keeps a level
$\ell_t = \alpha y_t + (1 - \alpha)\ell_{t-1}$, with smoothing constant $\alpha$ between 0 and 1. A large $\alpha$
follows recent values; a small $\alpha$ averages over a long history. The forecast for every horizon is the last level.

**Each extra pattern adds one component.** <NoteLink to="holt-linear-trend" /> adds a slope $b_t$, smoothed by its own
constant, so forecasts extrapolate a straight line. Its damped version shrinks the slope at each step, so long-horizon
forecasts level off. <NoteLink to="holt-winters" /> adds $m$ seasonal indices for a season of period $m$. The additive
form suits a seasonal swing of fixed size, and the multiplicative form a swing proportional to the level.

**The methods are state-space models.** <NoteLink to="error-trend-seasonal-models" /> recasts every method as a model
with one noise term that drives both the observation and the state updates. The models are named by their error, trend
and season components, such as ETS(A, Ad, M). The model form gives a likelihood, so parameters are fitted by maximum
likelihood, models are compared by <NoteLink to="information-criteria">information criteria</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="prediction-intervals">prediction intervals</NoteLink> follow from the model <Cite id="hyndman2008" />.

**Links to <Gloss name="arima" /> and the <NoteLink to="kalman-filter">Kalman filter</NoteLink>.** Simple exponential
smoothing gives the same forecasts as an ARIMA(0, 1, 1) model, and it is the steady-state Kalman filter for a
<NoteLink to="random-walk">random walk</NoteLink> observed with noise. The linear ETS models are special cases of ARIMA
models, while the multiplicative ones have no ARIMA counterpart <Cite id="hyndman2021" />.

## How the notes fit together

The notes form a chain. Start with simple exponential smoothing; Holt's method requires it, Holt–Winters requires
Holt's method, and the ETS note requires both. Each note adds one component and one smoothing constant to the one
before.
