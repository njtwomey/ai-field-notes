<Definition>
**Forecasting practice** covers the steps that turn a fitted time-series model into forecasts that can be used and
trusted: stating the uncertainty of each forecast, checking accuracy on data the model has not seen without leaking the
future, and keeping the forecasts of related series consistent with each other.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A forecast drives a decision: how much stock to order, how many staff to roster, how much capacity to reserve. A point
forecast alone cannot size a safety margin, so the uncertainty must be stated. A forecaster chosen by an evaluation
that saw the future will fail when deployed. Forecasts made separately for stores, regions and the whole company will
not add up, and planners at different levels then act on contradictory numbers. The notes here address each of these
problems <Cite id="hyndman2021" />.

## Main ideas

**State the uncertainty as an interval.** <NoteLink to="prediction-intervals" /> gives a range that contains the future
value with a stated probability. For <NoteLink to="linear-models">linear models</NoteLink> with Gaussian errors the
interval width grows with the horizon through the model's impulse-response weights. Residual bootstraps and conformal
methods drop the Gaussian assumption.

**Or as a whole distribution.** <NoteLink to="probabilistic-forecasting" /> gives a predictive distribution as a
density, a set of quantiles or sample paths. A good forecast distribution is calibrated, so its stated probabilities
match observed frequencies, and as sharp as calibration allows. Quantile forecasts are trained with the pinball loss,
whose expected value is minimised at the true quantile <Cite id="gneiting2014" />.

**Evaluate in time order.** <NoteLink to="time-series-cross-validation" /> moves a forecast origin through the series,
trains on everything before it and tests on the next $h$ values. The training window either expands or slides. Random
$k$-fold splits would train on future values and overstate accuracy <Cite id="tashman2000" />.

**Make a hierarchy coherent.** <NoteLink to="hierarchical-forecast-reconciliation" /> handles series that sum to
totals, such as product sales that add up to category and company sales. It maps independently produced base forecasts
onto forecasts that add up. Bottom-up and top-down are simple fixed choices; the minimum-trace (MinT) method chooses the
mapping that minimises the total forecast error variance.

## How the notes fit together

The four notes are independent and can be read in any order. Prediction intervals come first because the
probabilistic-forecasting note generalises them from one interval to a whole distribution. Time-series
cross-validation applies to every forecaster in the Part. Reconciliation is needed only when the series form a
hierarchy or grouping.
