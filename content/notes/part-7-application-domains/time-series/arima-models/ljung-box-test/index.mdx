<Definition>
The **Ljung–Box test** is a **portmanteau test**: it tests the first $h$ autocorrelations of a series together rather
than one at a time <Cite id="ljung1978" />. Applied to the residuals of a fitted <Gloss name="arma" />($p$, $q$) model,
it checks whether the model has captured the series' linear dependence. The statistic sums squared sample
autocorrelations with weights that correct their small-sample variance, and is compared with a
<NoteLink to="chi-squared-distribution">chi-squared distribution</NoteLink> with $h - p - q$ degrees of freedom.
</Definition>

## Hypotheses

- $H_0$: the series is <NoteLink to="white-noise-and-random-walk">white noise</NoteLink> up to lag $h$,
  $\rho(1) = \rho(2) = \dots = \rho(h) = 0$. For residuals: the model is adequate.
- $H_1$: at least one of $\rho(1), \dots, \rho(h)$ is non-zero.

The test is a lack-of-fit test. A rejection says that some linear structure remains. It does not say which.

## Assumptions

- Under $H_0$ the series is iid with finite variance, or the model's innovations are. The chi-squared approximation
  needs $n$ large relative to $h$.
- When the test is applied to residuals, the model must be an ARMA model fitted by an efficient method (least squares
  or maximum likelihood), for the degrees-of-freedom correction to hold <Cite id="box1970" />.
- The number of lags $h$ is chosen before looking at the result.

## Test statistic

Let $r_k$ be the sample autocorrelation at lag $k$ of $n$ values
(<NoteLink to="autocorrelation-and-partial-autocorrelation">ACF and PACF</NoteLink>). Under iid noise each $r_k$ is
approximately $\Gauss(0, 1/n)$ and different lags are approximately independent. So $\sqrt n\,r_k$ are approximately
independent standard normals, and the sum of their squares is approximately chi-squared with $h$ degrees of freedom.
This is the **Box–Pierce statistic** <Cite id="box1970" />:

$$
Q_{\mathrm{BP}} = n\sum_{k=1}^{h}r_k^2 \;\dot\sim\; \chi^2_h.
$$

The $1/n$ variance is only a large-sample value. For iid Gaussian data a more exact calculation gives

$$
\var(r_k) \approx \frac{n - k}{n(n + 2)},
$$

which is smaller than $1/n$, noticeably so at large lags. The factor $n - k$ counts the products in $\hat\gamma(k)$. The
factor $n + 2$ comes from the moments of a ratio of two <NoteLink to="positive-definite-matrices">quadratic
forms</NoteLink> in normal variables, $\hat\gamma(k)$ over $\hat\gamma(0)$. Standardising each $r_k$ by this variance
instead of $1/n$ gives the **Ljung–Box statistic**:

$$
Q_{\mathrm{LB}} = n(n + 2)\sum_{k=1}^{h}\frac{r_k^2}{n - k}.
$$

Its null distribution is much closer to $\chi^2_h$ in samples of 50 to 100, where the Box–Pierce statistic is too
small and the test rejects too rarely <Cite id="ljung1978" />.

**Degrees of freedom for residuals.** Fitting an ARMA($p$, $q$) model makes the residuals fit the data by construction,
which shrinks their low-lag autocorrelations. Box and Pierce showed that the residual autocorrelations behave like
$h$ normal variables subject to $p + q$ linear constraints, so the statistic is approximately $\chi^2_{h-p-q}$
<Cite id="box1970" />. The constant and regression coefficients do not reduce the degrees of freedom.

## Decision rule

Reject $H_0$ at level $\alpha$ when $Q_{\mathrm{LB}} > \chi^2_{h-p-q,\,1-\alpha}$, the $1 - \alpha$ quantile of the
chi-squared distribution. Equivalently, reject when the <NoteLink to="p-value">p-value</NoteLink>
$\Pr(\chi^2_{h-p-q} > Q_{\mathrm{LB}})$ is below $\alpha$.

A common choice is $h = 10$ for non-seasonal data and $h = 2m$ for seasonal data with period $m$, with $h \le n/5$
<Cite id="hyndman2021" />. Too small an $h$ misses dependence at longer lags. Too large an $h$ dilutes a few large
autocorrelations among many small ones and loses power.

## Worked example

A model is fitted to $n = 100$ observations. The first five residual autocorrelations are
$r = (0.15, -0.10, 0.08, 0.12, -0.05)$. The terms $n(n + 2)r_k^2/(n - k) = 10200r_k^2/(100 - k)$ are:

| $k$ | $r_k$   | $10200r_k^2/(100 - k)$ |
| --- | ------- | ---------------------- |
| 1   | 0.15    | 2.318                  |
| 2   | $-0.10$ | 1.041                  |
| 3   | 0.08    | 0.673                  |
| 4   | 0.12    | 1.530                  |
| 5   | $-0.05$ | 0.268                  |

The sum is $Q_{\mathrm{LB}} = 5.830$. The Box–Pierce value is $100 \times 0.0558 = 5.580$, slightly smaller.

- **Raw series** (no model fitted, $h = 5$ degrees of freedom): the 5% critical value is $11.07$, and the p-value is
  $0.32$. White noise is not rejected.
- **Residuals of an AR(1) fit** ($5 - 1 = 4$ degrees of freedom): the critical value is $9.49$, and the p-value is
  $0.21$. The model is not rejected.

No single $r_k$ here exceeds the $\pm 1.96/\sqrt{100} = \pm 0.196$ band either. The portmanteau test combines evidence
that is individually weak; with larger autocorrelations spread over several lags it can reject even when no single
lag stands out.

## Effect size

The test has no standard <NoteLink to="effect-size">effect size</NoteLink>. The residual autocorrelations $r_k$ are the
effect: they measure how much one-step predictability the model leaves unused. An $r_1$ of 0.1 means that regressing
residuals on their previous value would explain about $0.1^2 = 1\%$ of their variance.

## Pitfalls

- **Correlation is not dependence.** The test detects linear dependence only. Residuals with
  <NoteLink to="volatility-clustering">volatility clustering</NoteLink> can pass. Applying the test to squared residuals
  (the McLeod–Li test) checks for that.
- **Degrees of freedom.** Using $h$ instead of $h - p - q$ for residuals makes the test conservative; using $h - p - q$
  on a raw series makes it anti-conservative. With $h \le p + q$ the test is undefined.
- **Large samples reject trivially.** With $n$ in the tens of thousands, autocorrelations of 0.02 are significant but
  irrelevant for forecasting. Look at the size of $r_k$.
- **Not a model-selection tool.** Many models pass. Choose among them with
  <NoteLink to="information-criteria">information criteria</NoteLink> or out-of-sample accuracy
  (<NoteLink to="box-jenkins-method">Box–Jenkins method</NoteLink>).
