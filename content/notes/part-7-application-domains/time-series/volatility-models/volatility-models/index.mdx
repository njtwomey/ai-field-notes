<Definition>
A **volatility model** describes a series of returns $r_t = \sigma_t\varepsilon_t$ whose conditional standard deviation
$\sigma_t$, the volatility, changes over time, with $\varepsilon_t$ independent noise of unit variance. The models
differ in how $\sigma_t$ evolves: as a deterministic function of past returns (ARCH and GARCH), or as a latent process
with its own noise (stochastic volatility).
</Definition>

## What it is for

The returns of stocks, currencies and commodities are close to uncorrelated, so their mean is hard to forecast. Their
size is not: calm periods and turbulent periods each persist. Volatility models forecast that size. The forecasts feed
risk measures such as value at risk, option prices and portfolio weights. The same models describe any series whose
noise level varies, such as electricity prices or sensor errors <Cite id="engle1982" />.

## Main ideas

**Uncorrelated is not independent.** <NoteLink to="volatility-clustering" /> describes the stylised fact behind the
chapter. Returns $r_t$ show almost no autocorrelation, but their absolute values $\abs{r_t}$ and squares $r_t^2$ stay
positively autocorrelated for many lags. The conditional variance therefore changes over time, and the unconditional
distribution has heavier tails than a Gaussian.

**Variance as an autoregression in squared returns.** The <NoteLink to="arch-model" /> sets
$\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \sum_{i=1}^q \alpha_i r_{t-i}^2$, so a large shock raises the next variance. The
<NoteLink to="garch-model" /> adds lagged variances, $\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha r_{t-1}^2 + \beta\sigma_{t-1}^2$.
One extra parameter replaces a long ARCH lag polynomial, and the persistence $\alpha + \beta$ sets how slowly a shock
to volatility decays <Cite id="bollerslev1986" />. Both models have closed-form likelihoods.

**Volatility with its own noise.** The <NoteLink to="stochastic-volatility" /> model lets the log-variance follow an
<NoteLink to="autoregressive-model">AR(1)</NoteLink> process with an independent shock. Volatility is then a hidden
state, so the likelihood requires filtering: the model is a nonlinear state-space model, fitted by
<NoteLink to="kalman-filter">Kalman-filter</NoteLink> approximations or by <NoteLink to="monte-carlo-integration">Monte
Carlo methods</NoteLink>.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The models reuse the autoregressive idea of <NoteLink to="autoregressive-and-moving-average-models" />, applied to the
squared series instead of the series itself. Stochastic volatility relies on <NoteLink to="state-space-models" /> and
the <NoteLink to="particle-filter" />. Heavy tails, the other stylised fact of returns, connect to the
<NoteLink to="student-t-distribution" /> often used for $\varepsilon_t$ <Cite id="hamilton1994" />.
