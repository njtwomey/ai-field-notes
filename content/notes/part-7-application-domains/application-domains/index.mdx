<Definition>
An **application domain** is a family of problems whose data and use set their own methods. Part VII collects six:
time series, online experimentation, recommendation and retrieval, natural language processing, computer vision, and
games. In each, the general learning methods of the earlier Parts meet a particular data structure, a particular
output and a particular way of judging success.
</Definition>

## What it is for

General methods leave the hard choices open. A forecaster must respect time order; an experiment must estimate a
causal effect on live users; a recommender must rank millions of items from feedback its own past choices produced;
a text classifier must turn strings into numbers; a vision model must cope with pixels, scale and perspective. Each
domain has developed models, evaluation protocols and pitfalls that the general theory does not predict. This Part
records them, and links each back to the foundations it uses.

## Main ideas

**Order in time.** <NoteLink to="time-series" /> covers <NoteLink to="stationarity">stationarity</NoteLink> and
autocorrelation, <Gloss name="arima" /> and <NoteLink to="exponential-smoothing">exponential smoothing</NoteLink>,
<Gloss name="state-space-model">state-space models</Gloss> and the <NoteLink to="kalman-filter">Kalman
filter</NoteLink>, volatility and multivariate models, <NoteLink to="forecasting-practice">forecasting
practice</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="change-point-detection">change-point detection</NoteLink>, the
<NoteLink to="matrix-profile">matrix profile</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="time-series-classification">time-series
classification</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="neural-forecasting-models">neural forecasters</NoteLink>
<Cite id="hyndman2021" />. Its defining constraint is that the future may not inform the past, in models or in
evaluation.

**Causal effects on live users.** <NoteLink to="online-experimentation" /> designs and analyses
<Gloss name="ab-test" plural />, lists the threats to their validity and the pitfalls of running them at scale, gives
sequential tests that let an experiment stop early, and covers multi-armed and
<NoteLink to="contextual-bandit">contextual bandits</NoteLink> that adapt traffic while they learn
<Cite id="kohavi2020" />.

**Ranking from biased feedback.** <NoteLink to="recommendation-and-retrieval" /> learns to rank items for a user or a
query: <NoteLink to="collaborative-filtering">collaborative filtering</NoteLink>, feature-interaction, sequential and
graph models, <NoteLink to="ranking-losses">ranking losses</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="click-models">click
models</NoteLink> and debiasing, and evaluation beyond accuracy. Its data are produced by the system being trained, so
bias and <NoteLink to="feedback-loops">feedback loops</NoteLink> are central.

**Text.** <NoteLink to="natural-language-processing" /> turns text into tokens and vectors, models the probability of
word sequences, and poses the standard tasks of classification, inference, translation,
<NoteLink to="summarisation">summarisation</NoteLink>, <Gloss name="qa">question answering</Gloss> and token tagging
<Cite id="jurafsky2025" />.

**Images.** <NoteLink to="computer-vision" /> covers <NoteLink to="image-processing">classical image
processing</NoteLink>, learned recognition of images, objects and pixels, and the <NoteLink to="camera-geometry">camera
geometry</NoteLink> that recovers depth and 3D structure <Cite id="szeliski2022" />.

**Games.** <NoteLink to="ray-casting" /> renders a first-person view of a grid world, and
<NoteLink to="neural-ray-casting" /> trains a coordinate network to replace the ray caster, so that the network's
weights store the world.

## How the notes fit together

The topics are independent of one another and can be read in any order. Each opens with an overview that maps its
chapters. Time series and online experimentation lean on statistics from Part I. Recommendation, language and vision
lean on the neural architectures of Part VI. Links between the topics are specific: bandits and online experiments are
the main way recommenders are tested, text and image encoders supply features to recommenders, and the multimodal
learning topic, still planned, joins language and vision.
