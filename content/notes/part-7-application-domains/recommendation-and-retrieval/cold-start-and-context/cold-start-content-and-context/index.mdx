<Definition>
This chapter covers recommendation that uses more than the <NoteLink to="explicit-and-implicit-feedback">user–item
interaction matrix</NoteLink>. **Content** means features of items and users, such as text, images, tags and
demographics. **Context** means the situation of a request, such as time, place and device. Both let a recommender score
users and items with little or no history, the **cold-start** case, and adapt a recommendation to the moment it is made.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A collaborative-filtering model learns one embedding per user ID and item ID from that ID's interactions. A new item
added this morning, or a user on their first visit, has no interactions and so no usable embedding. Catalogues with
fast turnover, such as news, fashion and classified ads, are dominated by such items. Separately, the same user wants
different things on a weekday morning on a phone than on a weekend evening on a television. The notes here supply the
missing signal from features, other behaviours and the request itself <Cite id="schein2002coldstart" />.

## Main ideas

**The failure being fixed.** <NoteLink to="cold-start-problem" /> defines user and item cold start and surveys the
remedies: replace or supplement IDs with features, session behaviour, auxiliary behaviours, graph neighbours or
<Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss>.

**Recommend by similarity of content.** <NoteLink to="content-based-filtering" /> embeds items from their metadata, with
<Gloss name="tf-idf" />, text and image encoders or categorical embeddings, and recommends items close to those a user
liked. New items can be recommended at once. The cost is over-specialisation: the user is shown more of what they
already know.

**Combine content and collaboration.** <NoteLink to="hybrid-recommenders" /> mix the two signals. In LightFM each user
and item embedding is the sum of its features' embeddings, including an ID feature. A new item gets a vector from its
tags at once, and an established item behaves as in matrix factorisation <Cite id="kula2015lightfm" />.

**Borrow from denser behaviours.** <NoteLink to="multi-behaviour-recommendation" /> learns from several interaction
types, such as views, clicks, add-to-cart events and purchases, with multi-task networks that share experts or
embeddings. A user with no purchases but many views is cold for purchase prediction but warm for the shared
representation.

**Condition on the request.** <NoteLink to="context-aware-recommendation" /> lets the situation of a request change the
recommendation, by filtering the training data before modelling, filtering the output after it, or feeding context as
features alongside user and item embeddings.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="cold-start-problem" />, which motivates the rest. Content-based filtering and hybrid
recommenders form a pair and are best read in that order. The multi-behaviour and context-aware notes are independent
of each other and of the first three.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume <NoteLink to="collaborative-filtering" />. Item features are often text, encoded by the methods of
<NoteLink to="text-representation-pipeline" />, or images, encoded by models such as <NoteLink to="clip" />. Side and
context features are modelled jointly in <NoteLink to="feature-interaction-models" />. A user's recent session is a
strong cold-start signal, used by <NoteLink to="sequential-recommendation-models" />, and graph neighbours supply
another, in <NoteLink to="graph-based-recommendation" />.
