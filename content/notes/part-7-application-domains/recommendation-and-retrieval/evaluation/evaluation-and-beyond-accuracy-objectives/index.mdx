<Definition>
**Evaluating a recommender or retrieval system** means measuring how useful its ranked lists are, offline on held-out
or judged data, or online on live traffic. **Beyond-accuracy objectives** are properties of the lists that relevance
metrics ignore: diversity within a list, novelty and coverage of the catalogue, and fairness of quality and exposure
across users and providers.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A ranked list is good in several ways at once. It should put relevant items near the top, but a list of ten near
copies of one film is accurate and useless, and a system that only ever shows best-sellers starves the rest of the
catalogue. Offline metrics decide which models reach an online test, so a misleading offline evaluation wastes
experiments or ships worse models. The notes here cover the methodology of evaluation, the metrics for relevance and
for the other objectives, and methods that trade relevance for diversity <Cite id="manning2008" />.

## Main ideas

**Three ways to evaluate.** <NoteLink to="evaluating-retrieval-systems" /> describes batch evaluation on a test
collection of queries, judged documents and a metric; user studies; and online evaluation from clicks. Each metric
models a different user, so the better of two rankings can depend on the metric chosen.

**Ranking metrics offline, business metrics online.** <NoteLink to="recommendation-metrics" /> collects precision and
recall at $k$, hit rate, MAP, <Gloss name="mrr" />, <Gloss name="ndcg" /> and ERR for offline use, and click-through,
conversion and retention for online tests.

**Evaluation can manufacture progress.** <NoteLink to="progress-and-baselines-in-recommendation" /> shows that untuned
baselines, inconsistent splits and sampled metrics can make a new method look better than it is. Many published neural
recommenders lose to carefully tuned simple methods. <NoteLink to="unbiased-offline-evaluation" /> corrects a second
distortion: metrics computed on logged interactions favour models that imitate the
<NoteLink to="logging-policies-and-propensities">logging policy</NoteLink>, and weighting each outcome by the inverse of
its observation propensity removes this.

**Model judges.** <NoteLink to="language-model-as-a-judge" /> uses a <Gloss name="llm">large language model</Gloss> to
rate or compare outputs in place of human raters. For recommenders it can judge fit to a user profile. Judges must be
validated against human ratings and have known biases towards position, length and their own outputs.

**Objectives beyond relevance.** <NoteLink to="beyond-accuracy-metrics" /> defines intra-list diversity, catalogue
coverage, concentration of exposure, novelty and serendipity <Cite id="vargas2011novelty" />.
<NoteLink to="fairness-in-recommendation" /> applies fairness to both sides of the market: equal recommendation
quality across user groups, and exposure for items or providers in line with their relevance.

**Re-ranking for diversity.** <NoteLink to="diversified-re-ranking" /> reorders a relevance-ranked list so that it
covers several interests, greedily by maximal marginal relevance or by a <Gloss name="dpp">determinantal point
process</Gloss> that scores whole sets. <NoteLink to="diversified-recommendation-on-youtube" /> is a case study of a
determinantal point process deployed on a homepage feed.
