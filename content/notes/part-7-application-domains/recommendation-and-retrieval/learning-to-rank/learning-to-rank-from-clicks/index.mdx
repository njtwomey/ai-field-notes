<Definition>
**Learning to rank from clicks** fits a model that orders documents or items by relevance, using logged clicks
instead of human relevance judgements. A click is evidence of relevance only for results the user examined, and the
chance of examination falls with rank, so the methods model or correct for examination before they use clicks as
labels.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Search engines, product search and recommenders log billions of clicks at almost no cost, while expert judgements
are slow and expensive. Clicks are biased: the first result is clicked more than the fifth even when they are equally
relevant, because more users look at it. A ranker trained naively on clicks learns to keep the current order, whatever
its quality. The notes here describe how users click, and how to train and design rankers that learn relevance
despite that behaviour <Cite id="chuklin2015clickmodels" />.

## Main ideas

**Model how users scan a list.** <NoteLink to="click-models" /> are probabilistic models of examination and clicking.
The position-based model multiplies a probability of examining rank $k$ by the probability that the result is relevant.
The cascade model has users scan down the list and stop at the first click. Fitted to logs, the models separate
position effects from relevance.

**Reweight clicks by <NoteLink to="propensity-in-recommendation">examination propensity</NoteLink>.**
<NoteLink to="unbiased-learning-to-rank" /> weights each click by the inverse of the probability that its position was
examined. The weighted loss is an unbiased estimate of the loss on true relevance, at the cost of higher variance. The
propensities come from randomised swaps of results, from click models, or are learned jointly with the ranker
<Cite id="joachims2017ultr" />.

**Rank structured documents by field.** <NoteLink to="field-interactions-in-neural-ranking" /> covers documents with
several fields, such as a title, ingredients and steps. Scoring every pair of fields grows quadratically in the number
of fields and adds noise. Scoring query–field pairs, or a subset of pairs correlated with clicks, ranks better.

## How the notes fit together

Read <NoteLink to="click-models" /> first; its examination probabilities are the propensities that
<NoteLink to="unbiased-learning-to-rank" /> divides by. The field-interaction note concerns model architecture rather
than bias, and can be read on its own.
