<Definition>
The **recommendation problem** is to choose, for a user and a request, a short ranked list from a catalogue of items,
such that the user values the items listed. The foundations are the statement of that problem, the kinds of feedback
from which preferences are learned, and the system architecture that makes the choice fast enough for catalogues of
millions of items.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Streaming services, shops, news sites and social feeds show each user a personal selection from far more items than
anyone could browse. The selection is learned from logged behaviour. What that behaviour means, and what it leaves
out, shapes every later modelling choice: whether a missing rating is a dislike, whether to predict a rating or a
ranking, and how to rank a million items in a few milliseconds. The notes here fix these terms before the model
families of the later chapters.

## Main ideas

**What a recommender does.** <NoteLink to="recommender-systems" /> defines the system, its inputs and outputs, and its
two classical families: <NoteLink to="content-based-filtering">content-based filtering</NoteLink>, which recommends
items similar to those a user liked, and collaborative filtering, which recommends what similar users liked. It also
maps every later chapter of this topic.

**Stated and revealed preferences.** <NoteLink to="explicit-and-implicit-feedback" /> separates ratings that users give
on purpose from clicks, views and purchases recorded as they use a product. Implicit feedback is abundant but noisy and
one-sided: a missing interaction may mean dislike or that the user never saw the item. Models for the two kinds of data
differ in their losses and in how they treat missing entries.

**A competition that set the methods.** The <NoteLink to="netflix-prize" /> offered one million US dollars for a 10% cut
in the error of Netflix's rating predictor. It made <NoteLink to="matrix-factorisation-for-recommendation">latent-factor
models</NoteLink> the dominant method and showed the gains from blending many models <Cite id="koren2009mf" />. Its
winning blend was never fully deployed, and the field later moved from rating prediction to ranking from implicit
feedback.

**Retrieve, rank, re-rank.** <NoteLink to="multi-stage-recommendation" /> describes the architecture of large
recommenders. A cheap retrieval stage cuts millions of items to hundreds of candidates, an expensive ranking model
orders them, and a re-ranking stage adjusts the final list for diversity, fairness and business rules
<Cite id="covington2016youtube" />.
