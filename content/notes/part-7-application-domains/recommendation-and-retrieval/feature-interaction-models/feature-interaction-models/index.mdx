<Definition>
A **feature-interaction model** scores an impression, such as an advert shown to a user on a page, from many sparse
categorical features: user ID, item ID, device, time, publisher and so on. Its central component models how pairs, or
higher-order combinations, of features change the prediction together, using a learned embedding for each feature so
that combinations never seen in training still receive a sensible score.
</Definition>

## What it is for

<Gloss name="click-through-rate">Click-through-rate</Gloss> prediction for advertising and ranking for feeds and shops
work on tabular logs with millions of rare categorical values. A logistic regression on one-hot features cannot capture
that a feature matters only in combination with another, such as a product category at a particular hour. Adding every
pair as a crossed feature explodes the parameter count and leaves most pairs unobserved. Feature-interaction models
factorise the pairwise weights so that they generalise. They also extend collaborative filtering with side information
about users, items and context <Cite id="rendle2010fm" />.

## Main ideas

**Factorise the pairwise weights.** A <NoteLink to="factorisation-machines">factorisation machine</NoteLink> (FM) adds
to a linear model a weight for every pair of features, written as the inner product of the two features' embeddings. All
pairwise terms are computed in time linear in the number of features. With only user and item IDs as features, the FM
reduces to matrix factorisation.

**Make interactions depend on fields.** Features fall into fields such as user, item and publisher.
<NoteLink to="field-aware-factorisation-machines" /> give each feature one embedding per other field, which won
click-prediction contests at the cost of many more parameters and quadratic time.
<NoteLink to="field-weighted-factorisation-machines" /> keep one embedding per feature and learn one scalar weight per
pair of fields, a small fraction of the field-aware model's parameters.

**Memorise and generalise.** <NoteLink to="wide-and-deep" /> sums the outputs of a linear model over hand-crossed
features, which memorises specific co-occurrences, and of a feed-forward network over embeddings, which generalises to
new combinations. The two parts are trained jointly through one logistic loss <Cite id="cheng2016wide" />.

**Learn the crosses too.** <NoteLink to="deepfm" /> replaces the hand-built wide part with an FM that shares its
embeddings with the deep network, so no manual crosses are needed <Cite id="guo2017deepfm" />. The same note covers DCN,
xDeepFM and DLRM, which build explicit higher-order or dot-product interactions in other ways.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="factorisation-machines" />, which every other note in the chapter extends. The field-aware
and field-weighted notes are two answers to the same limitation and are best read in that order. Wide & Deep can be
read independently; DeepFM requires both the FM and Wide & Deep.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume <NoteLink to="logistic-regression" />, embeddings of categorical features, and the matrix
factorisation of <NoteLink to="collaborative-filtering" />. The losses these models train with are in
<NoteLink to="recommendation-loss-families" />, and the click logs they learn from carry the position effects treated
in <NoteLink to="learning-to-rank-from-clicks" />. Side features also help with new users and items, the subject of
<NoteLink to="cold-start-content-and-context" />. Interactions between the fields of a document in neural ranking are
in <NoteLink to="field-interactions-in-neural-ranking" />.
