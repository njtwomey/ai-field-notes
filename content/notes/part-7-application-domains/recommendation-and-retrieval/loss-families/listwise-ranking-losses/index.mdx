<Definition>
A **listwise ranking loss** takes the scores $\svec = (s_1, \dots, s_n)$ that a model gives to all $n$ candidates of one
query or user, together with their relevance labels $\yvec = (y_1, \dots, y_n)$, and returns a single number for the
whole list. It is not a sum of independent per-item or per-pair terms. The main families are cross-entropies between the
softmax of the scores and a target distribution (<Gloss name="listnet" />, softmax cross-entropy), likelihoods of the
ideal order under the Plackett–Luce model (<Gloss name="listmle" />), and smoothed versions of a ranking metric
(ApproxNDCG, SoftRank, NeuralNDCG).
</Definition>

## Intuition

A ranking metric such as <Gloss name="ndcg" /> is a property of the whole list. Item $i$'s contribution depends on its
rank, and its rank depends on every other score. A <NoteLink to="pointwise-recommendation-losses">pointwise
loss</NoteLink> fits each label on its own. A pairwise loss scores each pair on its own. Neither sees that a mistake at
rank 1 costs more than a mistake at rank 50.

A listwise loss couples all the scores through a normaliser. The softmax $p_i = e^{s_i} / \sum_k e^{s_k}$ is the
standard coupling: raising one score lowers every other item's probability. The
<NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> of a softmax loss then concentrates on the items with the highest scores.
Those are the items at the top of the list, where the metrics are decided.

## Softmax cross-entropy and ListNet

**ListNet** defines a probability distribution over permutations from the scores <Cite id="cao2007" />. The full
distribution has $n!$ outcomes. ListNet uses its marginal over the first position instead, the **top-one
probability**. For the exponential transformation of scores that marginal is the softmax:

$$
P_{\svec}(i) = \frac{e^{s_i}}{\sum_{k=1}^n e^{s_k}} \eqdef p_i.
$$

A target distribution $\tvec$ over the same $n$ items comes from the labels. The loss is the
<NoteLink to="cross-entropy-and-perplexity">cross-entropy</NoteLink> from $\tvec$ to $\pvec$:

$$
L(\svec, \tvec) = -\sum_{i=1}^n t_i \ln p_i.
$$

The choice of target names the variant.

| Variant | Target $t_i$ | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ListNet (original) | $e^{y_i} / \sum_k e^{y_k}$ | <Cite id="cao2007" /> |
| Softmax cross-entropy, label-normalised | $y_i / \sum_k y_k$ | <Cite id="bruch2019softmax" />, binary $y_i$ |
| Single positive $+$ | $\indicator[i = +]$ | multiclass logistic loss |
| XE-NDCG | $(2^{y_i} - \gamma_i) / \sum_k (2^{y_k} - \gamma_k)$, $\gamma_k \sim \Unif(0, 1)$ | <Cite id="bruch2021xendcg" /> |

The label-normalised target puts zero mass on irrelevant items. ListNet's softmax of the labels gives an irrelevant
item mass $e^0 / \sum_k e^{y_k}$, which is positive. XE-NDCG uses the NDCG gains $2^{y_i}$ and a random offset
$\gamma_i$ drawn afresh at each step. Bruch shows that this target makes the loss a convex bound on NDCG
<Cite id="bruch2021xendcg" />.

**Gradient.** Write $\ln p_i = s_i - \ln\sum_k e^{s_k}$. Because $\sum_i t_i = 1$,

$$
L = -\sum_i t_i s_i + \ln\sum_{k} e^{s_k}.
$$

The first term is linear in the scores, with <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> $-t_i$ with respect to
$s_i$. The derivative of $\ln\sum_k e^{s_k}$ with respect to $s_i$ is $e^{s_i}/\sum_k e^{s_k} = p_i$. So

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial s_i} = p_i - t_i.
$$

<NoteLink to="gradient-descent">Gradient descent</NoteLink> raises item $i$ when the model gives it less top-one
probability than the target does and lowers it otherwise. The gradients sum to $\sum_i p_i - \sum_i t_i = 0$. The loss
is therefore unchanged by adding a constant to every score, and the scores carry order but no absolute scale. The loss
is convex in $\svec$, because log-sum-exp is convex and the other term is linear.

An irrelevant item $j$ (with $t_j = 0$) is pushed down with force $p_j$. That force is largest for the irrelevant items
the model scores highest. Those are the items that sit at the top of the list and hurt the metrics most. A
<NoteLink to="pairwise-ranking-losses">pairwise</NoteLink> logistic loss pushes the same item $j$ below a relevant item
$i$ with force $\sigma(s_j - s_i)$, which depends only on the pair; the softmax force $p_j$ also depends on how $s_j$
compares with all other scores.

## One positive: the softmax over a candidate set

Recommendation data often has one positive per list: the item clicked from a slate, the next item in a session, or
the true pair in a batch of user–item pairs. The target is then one-hot on the positive $+$, and the loss reduces to

$$
L = -\ln\frac{e^{s_+}}{\sum_{k \in \Ccal} e^{s_k}} = -s_+ + \ln\sum_{k \in \Ccal} e^{s_k},
$$

with gradient $p_+ - 1$ on the positive and $p_j$ on every other candidate. This is the loss of
<NoteLink to="multinomial-logistic-regression">multinomial logistic regression</NoteLink> with the candidate set $\Ccal$
as the classes. With <NoteLink to="two-tower-model">in-batch negatives</NoteLink> and a temperature it is the
<Gloss name="infonce" /> loss of <NoteLink to="contrastive-learning">contrastive learning</NoteLink>.

The candidate set $\Ccal$ decides what the model learns to beat.

- **The slate.** $\Ccal$ is the items shown on the page. The loss learns to reorder what an earlier stage retrieved.
- **The whole catalogue.** $\Ccal$ is every item, as in <NoteLink to="sequential-recommendation-models">next-item
  prediction</NoteLink>. A full softmax uses every item as a negative, and <NoteLink to="sasrec">SASRec</NoteLink>
  trained with it outperforms the same model trained with one sampled negative and <Gloss name="bce">binary
  cross-entropy</Gloss> <Cite id="klenitskiy2023" />.
- **A sample.** When the catalogue is too large to normalise over, $\Ccal$ is the positive plus sampled negatives.
  This is <NoteLink to="sampled-softmax-and-negative-sampling">sampled softmax</NoteLink>
  <Cite id="covington2016youtube" />. If each of $m$ negatives is drawn from a proposal $q$, the sampled sum
  $\sum_j e^{s_j}$ over-represents frequently sampled items. Dividing each sampled term by its expected count
  $m q_j$ makes the sum an unbiased estimate of the full normaliser. In the logits that weight is the shift
  $s_j - \ln(m q_j)$, the **<Gloss name="logq-correction">logQ correction</Gloss>** <Cite id="yi2019sampling" />.

With several positives $\Pcal$ and target $t_i = 1/\abs{\Pcal}$ on each, the loss is the average of
$-\ln p_i$ over the positives. Every positive then competes against the other positives in the shared normaliser. A
common variant removes that competition: each positive $i$ gets its own term
$-\ln\paren{e^{s_i} / \paren{e^{s_i} + \sum_{j \notin \Pcal} e^{s_j}}}$, contrasting it only with the negatives.

## ListMLE: likelihood of the ideal order

**ListMLE** treats the scores as the log-strengths of a <NoteLink to="plackett-luce-model">Plackett–Luce
model</NoteLink>. The model builds a ranking top-down: it picks the first item with probability proportional to
$e^{s_i}$, removes it, picks the second from the rest in the same way, and so on. Let $\pi$ be a permutation that sorts
the items by decreasing label, with $\pi(k)$ the item at position $k$. ListMLE minimises the negative log-likelihood of
$\pi$ <Cite id="xia2008" />:

$$
L(\svec, \pi) = -\sum_{k=1}^{n} \ln\frac{e^{s_{\pi(k)}}}{\sum_{l=k}^{n} e^{s_{\pi(l)}}}
= \sum_{k=1}^{n}\paren{-s_{\pi(k)} + \ln\sum_{l=k}^{n} e^{s_{\pi(l)}}}.
$$

Each term is a single-positive softmax loss: the item that belongs at position $k$ against every item that belongs
below it. A ranking drawn from this model has the same distribution as the order of the scores after adding independent
standard Gumbel noise to each, by the <NoteLink to="gumbel-max-trick">Gumbel-max trick</NoteLink> applied at every
step.

**Gradient.** Write $p^{(k)}_i = e^{s_i}/\sum_{l \ge k} e^{s_{\pi(l)}}$ for the softmax over the items remaining at
step $k$, and $\kappa(i)$ for the position of item $i$ in $\pi$. Item $i$ appears in the normaliser of every step
$k \le \kappa(i)$ and is the chosen item at step $\kappa(i)$. Each step is a single-positive softmax loss, whose
gradient is $p^{(k)}_i$ minus one for the chosen item. Summing over steps,

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial s_i} = \sum_{k=1}^{\kappa(i)} p^{(k)}_i \; - \; 1.
$$

The top item gets $p^{(1)}_{\pi(1)} - 1 < 0$ and is always pushed up. The last item gets
$\sum_{k<n} p^{(k)}_{\pi(n)} > 0$ and is always pushed down. The gradients again sum to zero, since each step
contributes $\sum_i p^{(k)}_i - 1 = 0$.

Xia et al. show that ListMLE is consistent for the permutation-level 0–1 loss and that its loss is convex and
differentiable <Cite id="xia2008" />.

ListMLE needs a full order. With graded or binary labels many permutations sort the labels, and the loss forces one
arbitrary order among tied items. Common fixes follow.

- **Random tie-breaking.** Draw a fresh permutation consistent with the labels at each step, so no tie order is
  learned systematically.
- **Top-$k$ ListMLE.** Keep only the first $k$ steps of the likelihood. Xia, Liu and Li show that this truncated loss
  is consistent for the top-$k$ ranking <Cite id="xia2009topk" />. With one positive and $k = 1$, top-$k$ ListMLE is
  the single-positive softmax loss.
- **Position-aware ListMLE (p-ListMLE).** Weight step $k$ by a decreasing $\alpha(k)$, so that mistakes at the top
  cost more than mistakes in the tail <Cite id="lan2014positionaware" />. Plain ListMLE gives every step weight one,
  and a list of $n$ items has $n - 1$ informative steps, most of them about the order of the tail.

## Smoothing the metric

A second group of listwise losses starts from the metric. NDCG with gains $g_i = 2^{y_i} - 1$ and ranks $\rho_i$ (1 for
the top) is $\frac{1}{\operatorname{IDCG}}\sum_i g_i / \log_2(1 + \rho_i)$, where IDCG is the <Gloss name="dcg" /> of
the ideal order. The rank $\rho_i = 1 + \sum_{j \ne i}\indicator[s_j > s_i]$ is a step function of the scores. Replacing
the steps with smooth functions gives a differentiable metric, and its negative is the loss.

- **ApproxNDCG** replaces each indicator with a sigmoid of temperature $T$
  <Cite id="qin2010approx" />:
  $$
  \hat\rho_i = 1 + \sum_{j \ne i}\sigma\paren{\frac{s_j - s_i}{T}}, \qquad
  \operatorname{ApproxNDCG} = \frac{1}{\operatorname{IDCG}}\sum_i \frac{g_i}{\log_2(1 + \hat\rho_i)}.
  $$
  As $T \to 0$, $\hat\rho_i \to \rho_i$ away from ties and ApproxNDCG tends to NDCG.
- **SoftRank** treats each score as a Gaussian <NoteLink to="random-variable">random variable</NoteLink> with mean $s_i$
  and a shared variance, computes the distribution of every item's rank from the pairwise probabilities
  $\prob(S_j > S_i)$, and optimises the expected NDCG under that distribution <Cite id="taylor2008softrank" />. The rank
  distributions cost $O(n^3)$ per list.
- **NeuralNDCG** relaxes the sorting operator itself <Cite id="pobrotyn2021neuralndcg" />. **NeuralSort** replaces
  the permutation matrix that sorts $\svec$ with a row-stochastic matrix $\hat{\Pmat}$ whose row $k$ is
  $\operatorname{softmax}\paren{\paren{(n + 1 - 2k)\svec - \Amat_{\svec}\ones}/\tau}$, where
  $(\Amat_{\svec})_{ij} = \abs{s_i - s_j}$ <Cite id="grover2019neuralsort" />. Row $k$ is a distribution over which item
  sits at position $k$. NeuralNDCG scales $\hat{\Pmat}$ towards a doubly stochastic matrix with
  <NoteLink to="entropic-regularisation-and-sinkhorn">Sinkhorn</NoteLink> iterations, forms the relaxed gains
  $\hat{\Pmat}\gvec$ by position, and discounts them. As $\tau \to 0$, $\hat{\Pmat}$ tends to the true sorting matrix.

Each smoothing has a temperature that trades bias against gradient quality. A large temperature gives a smooth
function that differs from the metric. A small one gives a close approximation whose gradient is near zero almost
everywhere, which is the original problem. None of these losses is convex.

<Derivation title="Why row k of NeuralSort picks the k-th largest score">
Fix $k$ and consider $f_k(x) = (n + 1 - 2k)x - \sum_{j=1}^n \abs{x - s_j}$ as a function of a real number $x$. When
$x$ lies strictly between two neighbouring scores with $m$ scores above it and $n - m$ below, the sum
$\sum_j \abs{x - s_j}$ has slope $(n - m) - m$. The slope of $f_k$ there is
$(n + 1 - 2k) - (n - 2m) = 2(m - k) + 1$. It is positive when $m \ge k$ and negative when $m \le k - 1$. So $f_k$
rises as $x$ climbs towards the $k$-th largest score and falls beyond it, and among the scores themselves $f_k$ is
largest at the $k$-th largest. At $x = s_i$ the term $\abs{s_i - s_i}$ is zero, so $f_k(s_i)$ is the $i$-th entry of
$(n + 1 - 2k)\svec - \Amat_{\svec}\ones$ <Cite id="grover2019neuralsort" />.

Row $k$ of the true sorting matrix is the one-hot vector on $\argmax_i f_k(s_i)$. Replacing the $\argmax$ with a
softmax of temperature $\tau$ gives row $k$ of $\hat{\Pmat}$. Each row is a probability vector, so $\hat{\Pmat}$ is
row-stochastic. Its columns need not sum to one, which is why NeuralNDCG adds Sinkhorn scaling.
</Derivation>

## Worked example

One query has four items with graded labels $\yvec = (2, 0, 1, 0)$. The model scores them
$\svec = (0.5, 1.0, 0.0, -0.5)$ and ranks them 2, 1, 3, 4. The NDCG of that order is 0.659. The softmax of the scores
is $\pvec = (0.276, 0.455, 0.167, 0.102)$, with $\ln\sum_k e^{s_k} = 1.787$.

**Softmax cross-entropy, target $y_i/\sum_k y_k$.** The target is $\tvec = (0.667, 0, 0.333, 0)$. The loss is
$-(0.667 \ln 0.276 + 0.333 \ln 0.167) = 1.454$. Its minimum over $\svec$ is the
<NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink> of $\tvec$, 0.637, reached only in the limit where irrelevant scores go to
$-\infty$. The gradient is

$$
\pvec - \tvec = (-0.391,\ 0.455,\ -0.166,\ 0.102).
$$

The largest push is downward on item 2, the irrelevant item ranked first.

**ListNet, target $\operatorname{softmax}(\yvec)$.** The target is $(0.610, 0.083, 0.225, 0.083)$. The loss is 1.441
against a minimum of 1.049, and the gradient is $(-0.334, 0.373, -0.057, 0.019)$. The softmax of the labels leaves
mass 0.083 on each irrelevant item, so item 4, which already has probability 0.102, is barely pushed down.

**ListMLE.** The labels sort as 1, 3, then the tied items 2 and 4. Breaking the tie by index gives
$\pi = (1, 3, 2, 4)$. The step probabilities of the chosen items are

| Step $k$ | Remaining items | Chosen item | $p^{(k)}$ of chosen item | $-\ln p^{(k)}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1, 3, 2, 4 | 1 | 0.276 | 1.287 |
| 2 | 3, 2, 4 | 3 | 0.231 | 1.464 |
| 3 | 2, 4 | 2 | 0.818 | 0.201 |
| 4 | 4 | 4 | 1 | 0 |

The loss is 2.953. The gradient formula gives, for item 3, $0.167 + (0.231 - 1) = -0.601$, and for item 4,
$0.102 + 0.140 + 0.182 = 0.424$. The full gradient is $(-0.724, 0.901, -0.601, 0.424)$. Step 3 contributes 0.201 to
the loss and 0.182 to item 4's push. That part only enforces the arbitrary tie-break between two irrelevant items.

The three losses agree on the direction for every item: raise items 1 and 3, lower items 2 and 4. They differ in how
hard they push the bottom of the list.

## Assumptions

- **A list is a unit.** The loss needs all candidates of a query or user in one batch. Lists of unequal length give
  softmax losses of unequal scale, since $\ln n$ is the loss of uniform scores over $n$ items.
- **Labels within a list are comparable.** Every listwise loss compares items only within the same list, so labels
  must be consistent within a list but need not be across lists.
- **The candidate set matches serving.** A model trained against the items on a slate learns to reorder slates. A
  model trained against sampled catalogue items learns to retrieve. The candidate set of the loss should match the
  stage at which the model is used.

## Pitfalls

- **Scores are not probabilities.** Softmax, ListNet and ListMLE are all unchanged by adding a constant to every score
  in a list. The scores of different lists are on no common scale, and $\sigma(s_i)$ is not a click probability.
- **ListNet's target is ambiguous.** Libraries often call the label-normalised target "ListNet" or "softmax loss",
  while Cao et al. used the softmax of the labels. The two targets put different mass on irrelevant items and give
  different gradients.
- **Lists with no positive.** With target $y_i/\sum_k y_k$ the target is undefined when every label is zero. Such lists
  carry no ranking information for these losses and are usually dropped.
- **Temperature schedules.** ApproxNDCG and NeuralNDCG need a temperature small enough to track the metric and large
  enough to give gradients. A value tuned for lists of 10 items may be wrong for lists of 1,000.
- **Sampled negatives without correction.** A sampled softmax without the logQ correction learns scores biased against
  popular items, which are sampled as negatives most often.
