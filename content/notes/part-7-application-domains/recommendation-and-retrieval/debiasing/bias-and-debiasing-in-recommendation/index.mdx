<Definition>
**Bias in recommendation** is any systematic difference between the feedback a recommender logs and the preferences
it is meant to learn. The difference arises because users can react only to what the system showed them, in the
positions it chose. **Debiasing** methods recover preferences from such data, mostly by modelling the probability that
each interaction could be observed and correcting for it.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A recommender trains and is evaluated on its own logs. An item never shown has no clicks, whatever its quality; an
item shown first collects clicks partly for its position. A model fitted naively to these logs learns the old
system's choices as if they were preferences, and an offline evaluation on the same logs rewards models that copy the
old system. The notes here name the biases, show how they compound over retraining, and give the estimators that
correct them <Cite id="chen2023biassurvey" />.

## Main ideas

**One problem with many names.** <NoteLink to="biases-in-recommender-feedback" /> treats selection, position, exposure
and popularity bias as forms of one problem: the presentation policy confounds relevance. Corrections therefore come
from causal inference rather than from more data of the same kind.

**Biases compound over time.** <NoteLink to="feedback-loops-in-recommendation" /> shows how a recommender that retrains
on interactions it caused ranks already-favoured items higher at each round, so exposure concentrates and unexplored
items starve. <NoteLink to="popularity-bias" /> is the most visible result: popular items dominate the data and are
over-recommended. Causal methods such as popularity-bias deconfounding remove popularity's effect during training.

**Propensities separate exposure from preference.** <NoteLink to="propensity-in-recommendation" /> defines the
propensity as the probability that an interaction could be observed at all: that a position was examined, an item
exposed or an action chosen. Dividing each observation by its propensity gives an unbiased estimate of the
quantity under uniform exposure <Cite id="schnabel2016treatments" />. <NoteLink to="estimating-item-propensities" />
works through estimating exposure from click counts alone, with a Poisson factor model fitted in closed form and a
Gamma–Poisson EM.

**Many levels of intervention.** The <NoteLink to="debiasing-toolkit" /> groups methods by where they act: re-weighting
the data, modelling the causal graph, removing bias from representations with adversaries, distilling from a small
unbiased sample, and contrastive or simulated training.

**Evaluate a new policy on old logs.** <NoteLink to="counterfactual-evaluation-of-recommenders" /> estimates the reward
a new recommender would earn by reweighting the production system's logged rewards, with clipping, self-normalisation
and models of the reward to control variance. <NoteLink to="slate-off-policy-evaluation" /> extends this to pages of
several items, where the number of orderings is so large that estimators must assume structure, such as rewards that add
up across positions.
