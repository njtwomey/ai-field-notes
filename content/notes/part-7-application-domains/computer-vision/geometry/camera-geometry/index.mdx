<Definition>
**Camera geometry** models how a camera maps points in a three-dimensional scene to pixels, and inverts that map when
several images are available. Points and lines are written in homogeneous coordinates, so projection and changes of view
become <NoteLink to="matrix-multiplication">matrix multiplications</NoteLink> that hold up to scale. The central objects
are the camera matrix, the homography and the fundamental matrix.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Geometry turns images into measurements. It removes lens distortion, stitches photos into panoramas, measures depth
from a stereo pair, tracks a camera through a scene for augmented reality and robot navigation, and reconstructs
buildings and terrain from photo collections. Most of its estimation problems are linear in homogeneous coordinates,
solved by a singular value decomposition, then made robust to wrong matches by RANSAC and refined by nonlinear least
squares <Cite id="hartley2004" />.

## Main ideas

**Projection is a matrix.** The <NoteLink to="pinhole-camera-model" /> maps a world point to a pixel by a rotation and
translation that place the camera in the world, followed by the intrinsic matrix $\Kmat$ that holds the focal lengths
and principal point. Lens distortion is modelled separately as a nonlinear correction.

**Two views of a plane are related by a homography.** A <NoteLink to="homography" /> is an invertible $3 \times 3$
matrix with 8 degrees of freedom. It relates two images of a planar surface, or two images from a camera that only
rotates. It is estimated from four or more point matches by the direct linear transform, which reduces to a singular
value decomposition.

**Calibration recovers the intrinsics.** <NoteLink to="camera-calibration" /> photographs a known planar pattern from
several angles. Each view gives a homography and two linear constraints on $\Kmat$, so three views determine it before
a nonlinear refinement that also fits the distortion.

**Two general views constrain each other along lines.** <NoteLink to="epipolar-geometry-and-stereo" /> shows that a
point in one image must lie on a known line in the other. The fundamental matrix encodes this constraint and becomes
the essential matrix when the intrinsics are known. In a rectified stereo pair, depth is inversely proportional to the
horizontal disparity between matched pixels.

**Many views give the scene and the cameras.** <NoteLink to="structure-from-motion" /> matches features across
overlapping images, estimates relative poses, triangulates 3D points and refines all cameras and points jointly by
bundle adjustment, a nonlinear least-squares fit of reprojection error <Cite id="szeliski2022" />.

## How the notes fit together

Read the <NoteLink to="pinhole-camera-model" /> first; every other note uses its notation. The homography comes next,
and calibration builds on it. Epipolar geometry requires the camera model but not calibration. Structure from motion
combines all of them and comes last.
