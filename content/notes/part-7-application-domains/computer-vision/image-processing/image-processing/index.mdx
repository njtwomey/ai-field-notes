<Definition>
**Image processing** transforms an image, a grid of pixel intensities $f(x, y)$, into another image or into a set of
measurements such as edges, lines or keypoints. Its operations are fixed rules rather than learned models: a kernel
convolved with the image, a shape slid over it, a vote in a parameter space, or a search over paths of pixels.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Image processing prepares images for later stages and extracts the structure that geometry and recognition use. Blurring
removes noise before differentiation. Edges and lines outline objects and lanes. Pyramids let one detector work at every
size. Local features find the same physical point in two photographs, which is the first step of panorama stitching,
<NoteLink to="camera-calibration">camera calibration</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="structure-from-motion">3D
reconstruction</NoteLink>. The convolutional networks of modern recognition learn filters that resemble these
hand-designed ones, so the classical operations also explain what the first layers of a network compute
<Cite id="szeliski2022" />.

## Main ideas

**Filtering is convolution with a kernel.** <NoteLink to="image-filtering" /> replaces each pixel by a weighted sum of
its neighbours, with the same kernel everywhere. Box and Gaussian kernels blur; separable kernels cost $2k$ instead of
$k^2$ operations per pixel. <NoteLink to="morphological-operations" /> are the nonlinear counterpart for binary images:
erosion and dilation by a small shape, combined into opening and closing to remove specks and fill holes.

**Edges are large gradients.** <NoteLink to="edge-detection" /> smooths with a Gaussian, estimates the intensity
gradient with derivative kernels and keeps the local maxima across each edge. The Canny detector links edges with two
thresholds. The <NoteLink to="hough-transform" /> then finds straight lines among edge points: each point votes for
every line through it, and lines appear as peaks in the vote accumulator.

**Content decides what to remove.** <NoteLink to="seam-carving" /> resizes an image by deleting connected paths of
low-energy pixels, found by dynamic programming, so that objects keep their shape while background shrinks.

**Structures have a size.** <NoteLink to="image-pyramids-and-scale-space" /> builds Gaussian and Laplacian pyramids by
repeated blurring and subsampling. Scale space treats blurring as a continuous parameter, the solution of the heat
equation, and scale-normalised derivatives select the size of each structure.

**Distinctive points match across views.** <NoteLink to="feature-detection-and-descriptors" /> finds corners where the
structure tensor has two large eigenvalues (Harris), adds invariance to scale and rotation (SIFT), or uses fast binary
tests (ORB). Each keypoint gets a descriptor vector, and matching descriptors links images of the same scene
<Cite id="lowe2004" />.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="image-filtering" />, which every other note uses. Edge detection follows, and the Hough
transform builds on its output. Morphology and seam carving are independent. Pyramids and scale space come before
feature detection, since SIFT finds keypoints in scale space.
