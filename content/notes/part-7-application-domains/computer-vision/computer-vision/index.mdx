<Definition>
**Computer vision** computes descriptions of the world from images and video: what objects are present, where they
are, and how the scene and the camera are arranged in three dimensions. It combines fixed signal-processing operations
on pixel grids, models learned from labelled images, and the projective geometry of cameras.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Cameras are cheap, and images record far more than any other sensor of similar cost. Computer vision turns that
record into decisions: sorting photos, reading scans and documents, guiding vehicles and robots, inspecting products,
mapping land from satellites and placing virtual objects in a live camera view. Some of these need labels, which
recognition supplies; others need measurements of position and depth, which geometry supplies
<Cite id="szeliski2022" />.

## Main ideas

**Fixed operations expose structure.** <NoteLink to="image-processing" /> covers the classical toolkit: linear filters
and morphology, <NoteLink to="edge-detection">edge detection</NoteLink> and the <NoteLink to="hough-transform">Hough
transform</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="image-pyramids-and-scale-space">image pyramids and scale space</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="seam-carving">seam carving</NoteLink>, and local features such as <Gloss name="sift" /> that match points
between images. These operations need no training, and they explain what the early layers of a trained network compute.

**Learned models assign meaning.** <NoteLink to="visual-recognition" /> labels whole images, boxes and pixels:
<NoteLink to="image-classification">image classification</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="object-detection">object
detection</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="semantic-segmentation">semantic segmentation</NoteLink>, and
<NoteLink to="instance-and-panoptic-segmentation">instance and panoptic segmentation</NoteLink>. A shared backbone, a
convolutional network or a <Gloss name="vit">vision transformer</Gloss>, is pretrained on a large labelled set and
reused with a task-specific head.

**Geometry recovers the third dimension.** <NoteLink to="camera-geometry" /> models the camera as a projection matrix
and relates several views through homographies and <NoteLink to="epipolar-geometry-and-stereo">epipolar
constraints</NoteLink>. From matched points it calibrates cameras, computes depth from stereo pairs and reconstructs
scenes and camera paths by <NoteLink to="structure-from-motion">structure from motion</NoteLink>
<Cite id="hartley2004" />.

## How the notes fit together

Image processing comes first: filtering underlies both of the other subtopics, and its local features are the input
to geometry. Recognition can then be read on its own, given convolutional networks from Part VI. Geometry needs the
feature-matching note and linear algebra but none of the recognition notes. The two later subtopics meet in systems
that both recognise and localise objects in 3D.
