<Definition>
For a signal made of $K$ complex exponentials in white noise, $x[n] = \sum_{k=1}^{K} A_k e^{i(\omega_k n + \phi_k)} +
e[n]$, the $M \times M$ correlation matrix of snapshots $\xvec_n = (x[n], \dots, x[n + M - 1])$ is

$$
R = \sum_{k=1}^{K} P_k\, \avec(\omega_k)\avec(\omega_k)^{H} + \sigma^2 \eye,
\qquad \avec(\omega) = \paren{1, e^{i\omega}, \dots, e^{i(M-1)\omega}},
$$

with $P_k = A_k^2$. Subspace methods estimate the $\omega_k$ from the eigenvectors of an estimate of $R$: MUSIC by
searching for steering vectors orthogonal to the noise eigenvectors <Cite id="schmidt1986" />, ESPRIT from the shift
structure of the signal eigenvectors <Cite id="roy1989" />.
</Definition>

## Signal and noise subspaces

The first term of $R$ has rank $K$ and its <NoteLink to="rank-and-null-space">column space</NoteLink> is spanned by the steering vectors
$\avec(\omega_1), \dots, \avec(\omega_K)$, which are linearly independent for distinct frequencies when $M > K$. Adding
$\sigma^2\eye$ shifts every eigenvalue by $\sigma^2$ and leaves the eigenvectors unchanged. So the eigenvalues of $R$
are $\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_K > \sigma^2$, followed by $M - K$ eigenvalues equal to $\sigma^2$. The first $K$
eigenvectors span the signal subspace, the span of the steering vectors. The remaining $M - K$ span its orthogonal
complement, the noise subspace, collected as columns of $E_n$. Every true steering vector is therefore orthogonal to
the noise subspace:

$$
E_n^{H}\avec(\omega_k) = \zeros, \qquad k = 1, \dots, K .
$$

A real sinusoid $\cos(\omega n + \phi)$ is the sum of two complex exponentials at $\pm\omega$, so $K$ real sinusoids
give a signal subspace of dimension $2K$.

## MUSIC

Multiple signal classification scans frequency and measures how nearly $\avec(\omega)$ lies in the signal subspace:

$$
P_{\mathrm{MUSIC}}(\omega) = \frac{1}{\norm{E_n^{H}\avec(\omega)}^2} .
$$

The denominator is zero at the true frequencies for the exact $R$, and small near them for an estimate, so the
pseudospectrum has sharp peaks there. It is not a <NoteLink to="parseval-theorem">power spectrum</NoteLink>: its peak heights say nothing about power. Its
resolution is not limited by the window length, because it uses the structure of $R$, not a <NoteLink to="fourier-transform">Fourier transform</NoteLink> of the
data. Root-MUSIC finds the peaks as roots of the polynomial $\avec(z)^{H} E_n E_n^{H}\avec(z)$ nearest the unit
circle, avoiding the grid search.

## ESPRIT

Estimation of signal parameters via rotational invariance uses the signal subspace directly. Let $A$ be the matrix of
steering vectors, and let $A_1$ and $A_2$ be $A$ without its last and without its first row. Shifting a steering
vector by one sample multiplies it by $e^{i\omega}$, so $A_2 = A_1\Phi$ with $\Phi = \diag(e^{i\omega_1}, \dots,
e^{i\omega_K})$. The signal eigenvectors $E_s$ span the same space, $E_s = AT$ for some invertible $T$, so

$$
E_{s,2} = E_{s,1}\,\Psi, \qquad \Psi = T^{-1}\Phi T .
$$

Solving this least-squares problem for $\Psi$ and taking its eigenvalues gives $e^{i\omega_k}$ directly: no search,
and no spurious peaks <Cite id="roy1989" />.

## Worked example

Take two unit-amplitude real sinusoids at $0.20\pi$ and $0.22\pi$ rad/sample with random phases, in white noise at 20
dB <Gloss name="snr" /> per sinusoid, and $N = 64$ samples. The separation, $0.02\pi = 0.063$ rad, is below the Fourier limit
$2\pi/64 = 0.098$ rad. Snapshots of length $M = 24$ with forward–backward averaging give a correlation matrix whose
eigenvalues start 3.44, 2.92, 1.00, 0.96, then drop to 0.011: four signal eigenvalues, one pair per real sinusoid,
above a noise floor near $\sigma^2 = 0.005$. MUSIC places its two peaks at $0.2018\pi$ and $0.2186\pi$, and ESPRIT
gives $0.2016\pi$ and $0.2191\pi$. The Hann-windowed <NoteLink to="periodogram">periodogram</NoteLink> of the same data shows two peaks at $0.181\pi$ and
$0.239\pi$: the two components interfere inside one main lobe, and the peaks it shows are displaced by about as much
as the separation itself.

## Pitfalls

- **The model order must be right.** Too small a signal subspace loses components; too large one lets noise
  eigenvectors into it and adds false peaks. Criteria based on the eigenvalue gap, such as MDL, estimate it.
- **Coloured noise.** The subspace split assumes white noise. Coloured noise must be whitened first.
- **Coherent sources.** Fully correlated components, such as a signal and its echo, reduce the rank of the signal
  term; forward–backward averaging and spatial smoothing restore it.
- **Low SNR.** Below a threshold SNR the noise and signal eigenvalues mix and the method breaks down abruptly.
