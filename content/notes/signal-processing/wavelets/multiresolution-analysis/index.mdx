<Definition>
A **multiresolution analysis** of $L^2(\reals)$ is a sequence of closed subspaces $V_j$ with

- nesting: $V_{j+1} \subset V_j$, where larger $j$ means coarser;
- scaling: $f(t) \in V_j$ exactly when $f(2t) \in V_{j-1}$;
- completeness: $\bigcap_j V_j = \set{0}$ and the union of the $V_j$ is dense in $L^2(\reals)$;
- a **scaling function** $\varphi$ whose integer shifts $\varphi(t - k)$ form an orthonormal basis of $V_0$.

The **detail space** $W_{j}$ is the orthogonal complement of $V_{j}$ in $V_{j-1}$, and it is spanned by shifts of a
**wavelet** $\psi$ <Cite id="mallat1989" />.
</Definition>

## The two-scale equation

$\varphi \in V_0 \subset V_{-1}$, and $V_{-1}$ has the orthonormal basis $\sqrt2\, \varphi(2t - n)$. Expanding
$\varphi$ in that basis gives the **two-scale equation**

$$
\varphi(t) = \sqrt2 \sum_n h[n]\, \varphi(2t - n), \qquad h[n] = \inner{\varphi(t)}{\sqrt2\, \varphi(2t - n)}.
$$

The coefficients $h[n]$ are a lowpass filter, and the whole structure of the analysis is encoded in them.

**Conditions on the filter.** Integrate both sides of the two-scale equation. With $\int\varphi = c \ne 0$ and the
substitution $u = 2t - n$, the right side gives $\sqrt2 \sum_n h[n]\, c/2$, so

$$
\sum_n h[n] = \sqrt2.
$$

Orthonormality of the shifts of $\varphi$, expanded through the two-scale equation, requires

$$
\inner{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(t - k)} = \sum_n \sum_m h[n]\, h[m]\, 2 \inner{\varphi(2t - n)}{\varphi(2t - 2k - m)} =
\sum_n h[n]\, h[n - 2k] = \delta[k],
$$

since $2\inner{\varphi(2t - n)}{\varphi(2t - p)} = \delta[n - p]$. The filter's even shifts must be orthonormal: this
is the orthogonal <NoteLink to="perfect-reconstruction-filter-banks">perfect-reconstruction</NoteLink> condition on
$h$.

## The wavelet and the detail spaces

The wavelet is built from $\varphi$ at the finer level with the highpass filter
$g[n] = (-1)^n h[L - 1 - n]$:

$$
\psi(t) = \sqrt2 \sum_n g[n]\, \varphi(2t - n).
$$

Its shifts are orthogonal to those of $\varphi$ and to each other, and they span $W_0$. Because
$V_{j-1} = V_j \oplus W_j$ at every level,

$$
V_{J} \oplus W_{J} \oplus W_{J-1} \oplus \dots \oplus W_1 = V_0.
$$

A signal in $V_0$ is its coarse approximation in $V_J$ plus details at each intermediate scale. The coefficients of
the projections onto these spaces are exactly the outputs of the
<NoteLink to="discrete-wavelet-transform">discrete wavelet transform</NoteLink>: one step of Mallat's algorithm
computes the projections of $V_{j-1}$ onto $V_j$ and $W_j$ from the coefficients at level $j - 1$. See
<NoteLink to="orthogonal-projection">orthogonal projection</NoteLink> for the geometry.

## Worked example

The Haar analysis has $V_0$ the piecewise-constant functions on unit intervals and $\varphi = \mathbb{1}_{[0,1)}$. A
unit box is the sum of two half-width boxes,

$$
\varphi(t) = \varphi(2t) + \varphi(2t - 1) = \sqrt2 \paren{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\, \varphi(2t) + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,
\varphi(2t - 1)},
$$

so $h = (1, 1)/\sqrt2$, which satisfies $\sum h = \sqrt2$ and $\sum_n h[n]^2 = 1$. The wavelet is
$\psi = \varphi(2t) - \varphi(2t - 1)$, $+1$ on the first half-interval and $-1$ on the second. Projecting onto $V_1$
averages over intervals of length 2; the detail in $W_1$ is what the averaging removed.

## Pitfalls

- **Index direction.** Some texts let $j$ increase toward finer scales, so $V_j \subset V_{j+1}$. The mathematics is
  the same with $j$ negated; check the convention before combining formulas from different sources.
- **Not every filter gives a function.** A filter satisfying the orthonormality condition defines $\varphi$ through
  the two-scale equation only if the iteration converges, which needs extra conditions, such as a zero of $H$ at
  $\omega = \pi$ and bounds on its size elsewhere.
- **Sampled signals are not in $V_0$ automatically.** Treating samples as the scaling coefficients of $V_0$ is a
  convention, often called the wavelet crime; it is harmless for most purposes but matters for exact approximation
  claims.
