<Definition>
A causal, stable filter $H(z)$ is minimum phase when all its zeros, as well as its poles, lie inside the unit circle.
Among all causal stable filters with the same <NoteLink to="frequency-response">magnitude response</NoteLink> $\abs{H(e^{i\omega})}$, it has the least phase lag,
the smallest <NoteLink to="linear-phase-and-group-delay">group delay</NoteLink> at every frequency, and the most energy concentrated at the start of its <NoteLink to="linear-time-invariant-systems">impulse response</NoteLink>
<Cite id="smith2007filters" />.
</Definition>

## Reflecting a zero keeps the magnitude

A zero at $z_0$ contributes the factor $1 - z_0 z^{-1}$. Replace it by the reflected zero at $1/z_0^*$, with the
factor $z_0^* - z^{-1}$. On the unit circle, $z^{-1} = e^{-i\omega}$ and

$$
\abs{z_0^* - e^{-i\omega}} = \abs{e^{-i\omega}}\,\abs{z_0^* e^{i\omega} - 1} = \abs{1 - z_0 e^{-i\omega}},
$$

taking the complex conjugate inside the modulus. The two factors have the same magnitude at every frequency, so a
zero can be moved to its reflection across the unit circle without changing $\abs{H}$. A filter with $k$ zeros off the
circle therefore has up to $2^k$ variants with identical magnitude response and different phase. The one with every
zero inside is minimum phase; the one with every zero outside is maximum phase.

## The minimum-phase–all-pass decomposition

The ratio of the two factors,

$$
A(z) = \frac{z_0^* - z^{-1}}{1 - z_0 z^{-1}},
$$

has $\abs{A(e^{i\omega})} = 1$ at every frequency: it is an all-pass filter, stable when $\abs{z_0} < 1$. Any rational
causal stable $H(z)$ can therefore be written

$$
H(z) = H_{\min}(z)\, H_{\mathrm{ap}}(z),
$$

where $H_{\min}$ takes every zero of $H$ outside the circle and reflects it inside, and $H_{\mathrm{ap}}$ collects
the all-pass factors that restore the original zeros. An all-pass factor with its pole inside the circle has positive
group delay at every frequency, so $H$ has at least as much group delay as $H_{\min}$: minimum phase is minimum delay.

## Consequences

- **Energy concentrates early.** Of all causal filters with the same magnitude response, the minimum-phase one
  maximises the partial energy $\sum_{n=0}^{m}\abs{h[n]}^2$ for every $m$.
- **Stable causal inverse.** $1/H(z)$ has poles at the zeros of $H$. Those are inside the circle only for a
  minimum-phase filter, so only a minimum-phase filter has a stable causal inverse. Equalisation and deconvolution
  depend on this.
- **Phase is determined by magnitude.** For a minimum-phase filter, $\log\abs{H}$ and the phase form a Hilbert-transform
  pair, so the phase can be computed from the magnitude alone, most easily through the <NoteLink
  to="cepstrum">cepstrum</NoteLink>: a minimum-phase sequence has a causal complex cepstrum.
- **Shorter delay than linear phase.** A linear-phase FIR filter can be converted to a minimum-phase filter with the
  same magnitude response by spectral factorisation, trading exact linear phase for much less delay.

## Worked example

Compare $h_1 = (1, 2)$ with $h_2 = (2, 1)$. Their <NoteLink to="z-transform">transfer functions</NoteLink> $1 + 2z^{-1}$ and $2 + z^{-1}$ have zeros at $-2$
and $-0.5$, reflections of each other across the unit circle. On the unit circle,

$$
\abs{1 + 2e^{-i\omega}}^2 = 5 + 4\cos\omega = \abs{2 + e^{-i\omega}}^2,
$$

so the magnitude responses are identical. $h_2$, with its zero inside, is minimum phase. Its first sample carries
energy 4 out of 5, against 1 out of 5 for $h_1$. At $\omega = 0$ the group delays are
$\sum_n n h[n] / \sum_n h[n]$: $1/3$ sample for $h_2$ and $2/3$ for $h_1$. Only $h_2$ has a stable causal inverse,
$1/(2 + z^{-1})$, with its pole at $-0.5$.

## Pitfalls

- **Zeros on the unit circle.** A filter with zeros exactly on the circle, such as most <Gloss name="fir" /> low-pass filters with
  stopband nulls, has no stable inverse at all, minimum phase or not.
- **Continuous-time terminology differs.** In continuous time the condition is zeros in the left half-plane; the
  discrete-time condition is inside the unit circle.
- **Minimum phase is not linear phase.** It minimises delay but distorts waveforms; choose it when latency matters more
  than waveform shape.
