<Definition>
Quantisation maps each sample of a continuous-amplitude signal to one of a finite set of levels. A uniform $b$-bit
quantiser on the range $[-1, 1]$ has $2^b$ levels spaced by the step $\Delta = 2/2^b$. The difference between the
quantised and the true sample is the quantisation error $e[n] = Q(x[n]) - x[n]$, which lies in
$[-\Delta/2, \Delta/2]$ when the signal stays inside the range <Cite id="widrow2008" />.
</Definition>

## The noise model

The standard model treats $e[n]$ as white noise, uniform on $[-\Delta/2, \Delta/2]$ and independent of the signal.
Its mean is zero and its power is the variance of that uniform distribution:

$$
\sigma_e^2 = \frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{-\Delta/2}^{\Delta/2} e^2\,de = \frac{\Delta^2}{12}.
$$

The model is good when the signal is busy compared with $\Delta$: it crosses many levels between samples and has no
simple relation to the grid. It fails for slowly varying or periodic signals at low bit depths, where the error is a
deterministic function of the signal and appears as harmonics rather than as noise.

## Six decibels per bit

A full-scale sine, $x[n] = \sin(\omega n)$ with amplitude 1, has power $1/2$. With $\Delta = 2/2^b$,

$$
\text{SNR} = \frac{1/2}{\Delta^2/12} = \frac{1/2}{4 \cdot 4^{-b}/12} = \frac{3}{2}\, 4^{b},
\qquad
10\log_{10}\text{SNR} = 10\log_{10}\tfrac32 + b \cdot 20\log_{10} 2 \approx 1.76 + 6.02\,b \text{ dB}.
$$

Each extra bit halves $\Delta$, which quarters the noise power: 6.02 dB per bit.

**Worked example.** At 16 bits the formula gives $1.76 + 96.3 = 98.1$ dB, the figure usually quoted for compact
discs. At 8 bits it gives 49.9 dB; the figure's simulated sine at 8 bits measures 49.8 dB.

## Dither

At low bit depths the error of a periodic signal is periodic too, and its power collects at harmonics of the signal,
where it is audible or visible as distortion. Dither adds a small random signal $d[n]$ before quantising. With
triangular (TPDF) dither, the sum of two independent uniforms on $[-\Delta/2, \Delta/2]$, the first two moments of the
total error become independent of the signal: the error is white, with power

$$
\frac{\Delta^2}{12} + 2 \cdot \frac{\Delta^2}{12} = \frac{\Delta^2}{4},
$$

three times the undithered noise, a loss of $10\log_{10} 3 = 4.77$ dB. Dither trades a little noise for the removal
of distortion <Cite id="widrow2008" />.

## Pitfalls

- **Clipping is not quantisation noise.** A signal that exceeds the range is clipped, which produces large errors
  outside $[-\Delta/2, \Delta/2]$. Headroom below full scale costs SNR at 6 dB per halving of amplitude, but avoids
  clipping.
- **The formula assumes a full-scale sine.** A signal that uses a tenth of the range loses 20 dB. Signals with a high
  crest factor, such as speech, sit well below their peak level.
- **Rounding before a nonlinearity.** Quantising early in a processing chain lets later gain amplify the error.
  Keep extra bits through intermediate steps and quantise once at the end.
