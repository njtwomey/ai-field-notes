<Definition>
The z-transform of a sequence $x[n]$ is

$$
X(z) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\,z^{-n},
$$

defined for the complex $z$ at which the sum converges, the region of convergence (ROC). The ROC is an annulus
$r_1 < \abs{z} < r_2$ centred at the origin, since convergence depends only on $\abs{z}$ <Cite id="oppenheim2010" />.
</Definition>

## The region of convergence matters

The same formula can come from different sequences. For $x[n] = a^n u[n]$, where $u$ is the unit step,

$$
X(z) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (a z^{-1})^n = \frac{1}{1 - a z^{-1}}, \qquad \abs{z} > \abs{a},
$$

a geometric series that converges when $\abs{a z^{-1}} < 1$. For the left-sided sequence $x[n] = -a^n u[-n-1]$,

$$
X(z) = -\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1} a^n z^{-n} = -\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} (a^{-1}z)^m = 1 - \frac{1}{1 - a^{-1}z} =
\frac{1}{1 - a z^{-1}}, \qquad \abs{z} < \abs{a}.
$$

The algebraic expression is the same; only the ROC tells the two apart. In general, a right-sided sequence has an ROC
outside its outermost pole, a left-sided one inside its innermost pole, and a two-sided one a ring between poles. The
ROC never contains a pole.

## Properties

Each follows from the definition by reindexing.

| Property | Sequence | Transform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Linearity | $a x_1[n] + b x_2[n]$ | $a X_1(z) + b X_2(z)$ |
| Delay | $x[n - n_0]$ | $z^{-n_0} X(z)$ |
| <NoteLink to="convolution">Convolution</NoteLink> | $(x * h)[n]$ | $X(z) H(z)$ |
| Exponential scaling | $a^n x[n]$ | $X(z/a)$ |
| Differentiation | $n\,x[n]$ | $-z\,\dfrac{dX}{dz}$ |
| Time reversal | $x[-n]$ | $X(1/z)$ |

The delay property is what makes the transform useful for recursions: a term $y[n - k]$ becomes $z^{-k}Y(z)$, so a
<NoteLink to="difference-equations">difference equation</NoteLink> becomes an algebraic equation, and an <NoteLink to="linear-time-invariant-systems">LTI system</NoteLink>
becomes a transfer function $H(z) = Y(z)/X(z)$.

## Relation to the DTFT

On the unit circle, $z = e^{i\omega}$, the transform is the <NoteLink to="discrete-time-fourier-transform">DTFT</NoteLink>:

$$
X(e^{i\omega}) = \sum_n x[n]\,e^{-i\omega n}.
$$

The DTFT exists, as an absolutely convergent sum, exactly when the ROC contains the unit circle. For a causal LTI
system, that is the condition that all poles lie inside the unit circle, which is BIBO stability.

## Inverting by partial fractions

A rational transform is inverted by splitting it into first-order terms, each of which is a known pair.

**Worked example.** Invert the causal

$$
H(z) = \frac{1}{(1 - 0.5 z^{-1})(1 - 0.25 z^{-1})}, \qquad \abs{z} > 0.5.
$$

Write $H(z) = \dfrac{A}{1 - 0.5 z^{-1}} + \dfrac{B}{1 - 0.25 z^{-1}}$. Multiplying through and setting $z^{-1} = 2$
(where the first factor vanishes) gives $A = 1/(1 - 0.25 \cdot 2) = 2$; setting $z^{-1} = 4$ gives
$B = 1/(1 - 0.5 \cdot 4) = -1$. Both terms are right-sided, since the ROC lies outside both poles, so

$$
h[n] = \paren{2 \cdot 0.5^n - 0.25^n}\,u[n].
$$

Check against the recursion: $H(z)$ corresponds to $y[n] = 0.75\,y[n-1] - 0.125\,y[n-2] + x[n]$, which gives
$h[0] = 1$, $h[1] = 0.75$ and $h[2] = 0.75^2 - 0.125 = 0.4375$. The formula gives $2 - 1 = 1$, $1 - 0.25 = 0.75$ and
$0.5 - 0.0625 = 0.4375$.

Repeated poles need terms $1/(1 - a z^{-1})^m$, which invert to polynomials in $n$ times $a^n$; the differentiation
property generates them.

## Pitfalls

- **An expression without an ROC is ambiguous.** $1/(1 - 2z^{-1})$ is the unstable causal sequence $2^n u[n]$ or the
  stable anti-causal sequence $-2^n u[-n-1]$.
- **Positive and negative powers.** Signal-processing texts write transforms in $z^{-1}$, the delay operator; control
  texts often use $z$. The poles and zeros are the same, but the polynomials look reversed.
