<Definition>
**Reassignment** moves the spectrogram value at $(t, \omega)$ to a new point $(\hat t, \hat \omega)$, the local centre
of gravity of the signal's energy seen through the window there, computed from the phase of the <Gloss name="stft">short-time Fourier
transform</Gloss> <Cite id="auger1995" />. **Synchrosqueezing** moves coefficients along frequency only, to the local
<NoteLink to="instantaneous-frequency">instantaneous frequency</NoteLink>, and sums them there <Cite id="daubechies2011" />.
</Definition>

## Where the energy really is

A spectrogram cell $(t, \omega)$ summarises everything within one window of $t$ and one main lobe of $\omega$. Its
value says how much energy is nearby, but not where. The phase of the STFT carries that information. For a tone,
the phase advances in time at the tone's frequency, whatever bin is observed; for an impulse, the phase varies across
frequency at a rate set by the impulse's time, whatever frame is observed. Reassignment reads these rates and moves
each value to where they point.

## The reassignment operators

Use the STFT with an absolute time reference, $X_w(t, \omega) = \int x(\tau)\, w(\tau - t)\, e^{-i\omega \tau}\, d\tau$,
and define two more transforms of the same signal with modified windows: $X_{w'}$ with the <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> $w'$, and
$X_{\mathcal{T}w}$ with $(\mathcal{T}w)(u) = u\, w(u)$. The reassigned coordinates are

$$
\hat \omega(t, \omega) = \omega - \operatorname{Im}\frac{X_{w'}(t, \omega)}{X_w(t, \omega)}, \qquad
\hat t(t, \omega) = t + \operatorname{Re}\frac{X_{\mathcal{T}w}(t, \omega)}{X_w(t, \omega)}.
$$

**Derivation of the frequency operator.** The local instantaneous frequency is the rate at which the phase of
$X_w$ advances in time, $\partial_t \arg X_w$, plus the carrier $\omega$ that the analysis removed. Differentiating
under the integral, $\partial_t X_w = -\int x(\tau)\, w'(\tau - t)\, e^{-i\omega\tau}\, d\tau = -X_{w'}$. For any
nonzero complex function, $\partial_t \arg X = \operatorname{Im}\paren{\partial_t X / X}$, so
$\partial_t \arg X_w = -\operatorname{Im}\paren{X_{w'}/X_w}$. For a tone $x = e^{i\omega_0\tau}$ the phase of $X_w$
is $(\omega_0 - \omega) t$ plus a constant, whose derivative is $\omega_0 - \omega$; adding the carrier gives
$\hat\omega = \omega_0$ in every bin where the tone is visible. The time operator follows in the same way from the
derivative of the phase with respect to $\omega$.

**Worked example.** A discrete cosine at $0.1234$ cycles per sample, analysed with a Gaussian window of <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard
deviation</NoteLink> 12 samples and a 256-point <Gloss name="dft" />, falls between bins 31 and 32 (at 0.1211 and 0.1250). The frequency
operator moves bins 30 to 33 all to $0.1234$, to within $5 \times 10^{-9}$. An impulse at sample 2007, analysed in
frames centred at samples 1995, 2000 and 2010, is moved to sample 2007 by the time operator in every case.

## What reassignment gives and costs

The reassigned spectrogram concentrates tones on their frequencies and impulses on their times, and a linear chirp on
its instantaneous-frequency line, like the <NoteLink to="wigner-ville-distribution">Wigner–Ville distribution</NoteLink> but without cross-terms between well-separated
components. It costs two extra STFTs. The result is no longer a bilinear distribution on a grid, and it cannot be
inverted: several cells may move to the same point, and the phase is discarded.

## Synchrosqueezing

Synchrosqueezing keeps only the frequency operator. Each coefficient $X(t, \omega)$ is added into the bin nearest
$\hat\omega(t, \omega)$ at the same time $t$:

$$
S(t, \xi) = \sum_{\omega \,:\, \hat\omega(t, \omega) \approx \xi} X(t, \omega).
$$

Because coefficients move only within one time slice, and a signal can be recovered from the sum over frequency of
its STFT or wavelet coefficients at each time, the squeezed transform remains invertible: summing $S(t, \xi)$ over
$\xi$ gives the same total. Components can then be extracted by summing along a ridge. Daubechies, Lu and Wu
introduced it for the <NoteLink to="continuous-wavelet-transform">continuous wavelet transform</NoteLink> as a better-founded alternative to <Gloss name="empirical-mode-decomposition">empirical mode decomposition</Gloss>
<Cite id="daubechies2011" />.

## Pitfalls

- **Low-energy cells move erratically.** The ratio $X_{w'}/X_w$ is unstable where $\abs{X_w}$ is small; threshold the
  spectrogram before reassigning.
- **Close components.** When two components fall inside one window, the centre of gravity lies between them, and
  both are moved to a spurious ridge.
- **The derivative window must be accurate.** The operators use the exact derivative $w'$ of the analysis window. A
  crude finite difference of a short discrete window biases the reassigned frequencies; compute $w'$ analytically.
  The ratios themselves do not depend on the phase convention, since both transforms carry the same phase factor.
