<Definition>
**Ensemble empirical mode decomposition** (EEMD) decomposes $I$ noisy copies $x + \varepsilon\sigma_x w_i$ of a signal
$x$ by empirical mode decomposition, where the $w_i$ are independent realisations of unit-variance white noise and
$\sigma_x$ is the <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink> of $x$, and averages the $j$-th IMF over the copies <Cite id="wu2009" />. The
**complete ensemble EMD with adaptive noise** (CEEMDAN) extracts one mode at a time, adding at each stage the
corresponding EMD mode of the noise to the current residue, so that the modes and the final residue add up to $x$
exactly <Cite id="torres2011" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

<NoteLink to="empirical-mode-decomposition">Empirical mode decomposition</NoteLink> suffers from **mode mixing**: an
intermittent component makes one IMF switch between unrelated oscillations. Take a slow sine with two short bursts of a
fast sine. Inside the bursts, the fastest oscillation is the burst, so the first IMF captures the burst. Outside them,
the fastest oscillation is the slow sine, so the first IMF captures the slow sine there. The first IMF then holds
pieces of both components, and every later IMF inherits the gaps. The cause is that sifting separates scales only
relative to the extrema that happen to be present at each time, and those change when a component switches on or off.

## Mechanism

### EMD of white noise is a dyadic filter bank

White noise has oscillations at every scale and at every time. Flandrin, Rilling and Gonçalvès, and independently Wu
and Huang, decomposed many realisations of noise and found that EMD then behaves like a fixed bank of band-pass
filters <Cite id="flandrin2004" /> <Cite id="wu2004" />. The first IMF is a high-pass filter. Each later IMF is a
band-pass filter of the same shape as the one before, shifted down by one octave, so its mean frequency is half the
previous one's. The energies of the IMFs after the first also halve from one to the next, so the product of an IMF's
energy and its mean period is roughly constant.

A heuristic accounts for the factor of two. Sifting subtracts the mean of envelopes whose knots are the extrema of the
current IMF, two per period. The local mean, which becomes the next residue, is therefore sampled at about twice the
IMF's frequency. It can hold oscillations up to about half that frequency, as a signal sampled at rate $f_s$ can hold
frequencies up to $f_s/2$. The next IMF is the fastest oscillation of that residue, so its frequency is about half the
previous one's. Nothing in the algorithm fixes the ratio exactly, and it drifts with the stopping rule.

**Worked example.** Decompose 200 realisations of 1,024 samples of unit-variance white Gaussian noise with 10 sifts
per IMF. The mean numbers of zero crossings of the first six IMFs are 714, 343, 169, 84, 42 and 21. The ratios between
neighbours are 2.08, 2.03, 2.01, 2.01 and 2.01. The mean energies (variances) are 0.64, 0.22, 0.11, 0.058, 0.031 and
0.016: after the first IMF, each is about half the one before. With PyEMD's default stopping rule instead of 10 sifts
the zero-crossing ratios rise to 2.1 to 2.5 at the lower modes.

### Why added noise cures mode mixing

When white noise is added to a signal, every scale is populated at every time, so the filter bank of the previous
section is in force everywhere. A signal component then lands in the IMF whose band contains its scale, in every noisy
copy and at every time, including the times when the component itself is absent: there, the same IMF holds only noise
of that band. For the slow sine with fast bursts, the first IMF holds the bursts where they occur and high-frequency
noise elsewhere, and the slow sine sits in a lower IMF throughout.

Each noisy copy still has its own IMFs contaminated by noise. Averaging over the ensemble removes most of the noise.
Each copy is decomposed exactly, so $\sum_j c_j^{(i)} + r^{(i)} = x + \varepsilon\sigma_x w_i$ for copy $i$. Averaging
over the $I$ copies gives

$$
\sum_j \bar c_j + \bar r = x + \varepsilon\sigma_x \bar w, \qquad \bar w = \frac{1}{I}\sum_{i=1}^{I} w_i .
$$

The $w_i$ are independent with unit variance, so at every sample $\bar w$ has variance $1/I$. The reconstruction error of
EEMD is noise with standard deviation $\varepsilon\sigma_x/\sqrt{I}$, which is the rule Wu and Huang give
<Cite id="wu2009" />. They recommend $\varepsilon \approx 0.2$ and ensembles of about 100 copies. The noise level $\varepsilon$
trades two effects. If it is too small, the noise adds no extrema and the decomposition is plain EMD. If it is too
large, each copy is mostly noise, and more copies are needed to average it away.

To average the $j$-th IMF across copies, every copy must be decomposed into the same number of IMFs by the same
operator. Implementations fix the number of IMFs and use a fixed number of sifts, typically 10, rather than a
stopping rule that could stop different copies at different points.

**Worked example.** Take the slow sine $\sin(2\pi \cdot 0.01\, n)$ with bursts $0.3\sin(2\pi \cdot 0.2\, n)$ on samples
151 to 249 and 381 to 419, for $n = 0, \ldots, 511$; the signal's standard deviation is $\sigma_x = 0.709$. Plain EMD
gives three IMFs. Its first IMF has a root-mean-square value of 0.46 outside the bursts, where it should be zero, and no
IMF comes within 100% relative error of the bursts. EEMD with $\varepsilon = 0.2$ and $I = 100$ copies puts the bursts
in the first IMF with a relative error of 21%, and that IMF has an RMS value of 0.016 outside the bursts. The slow sine
lands in the fourth IMF with an error of 12%. The reconstruction error has an RMS of 0.0137, against the predicted
$0.2 \times 0.709/\sqrt{100} = 0.0142$.

### CEEMDAN: noise added stage by stage

EEMD has two defects. Its reconstruction carries residual noise, and different copies can produce different numbers of
IMFs, so averaging slot by slot can mix unrelated modes. CEEMDAN fixes both by extracting one mode at a time and
averaging at every stage <Cite id="torres2011" />. Let $E_k(\cdot)$ denote the $k$-th IMF produced by EMD. Then:

1. average the first IMF of the noisy copies, $\tilde c_1 = \frac{1}{I}\sum_i E_1(x + \varepsilon_0 w_i)$, and set
   $r_1 = x - \tilde c_1$;
2. for $k = 1, 2, \ldots$, add the $k$-th EMD mode of each noise realisation to the residue, average the first IMFs,
   $\tilde c_{k+1} = \frac{1}{I}\sum_i E_1\paren{r_k + \varepsilon_k E_k(w_i)}$, and set $r_{k+1} = r_k - \tilde c_{k+1}$;
3. stop when the residue can no longer be decomposed, having at most two extrema.

The modes are defined as differences of residues, so the sum telescopes: $\sum_{k=1}^{K} \tilde c_k + r_K = x$ exactly,
with no residual noise. Each stage adds noise of the band that the next mode should occupy, $E_k(w_i)$, rather than
white noise, so the $k$-th stage sees noise only at the scale it is extracting. The weights $\varepsilon_k$ set the
<Gloss name="snr">signal-to-noise ratio</Gloss> at each stage.

Colominas, Schlotthauer and Torres improved the method by averaging local means instead of IMFs
<Cite id="colominas2014" />. With $M(y) = y - E_1(y)$ the local mean of $y$, the residues are
$r_k = \frac{1}{I}\sum_i M\paren{r_{k-1} + \beta_{k-1} E_k(w_i)}$ and the modes are $\tilde c_k = r_{k-1} - r_k$, which
still telescope to $x$. The noise enters each mode only through the local mean's estimate, which reduces residual noise
in the modes and the spurious modes that appear early in the original version. The widget uses this version, with
$\beta_k = \varepsilon\,\sigma(r_k)$ and each noise mode scaled by the standard deviation of the first mode of its
realisation. On the burst signal above, with $\varepsilon = 0.2$ and 50 realisations, it recovers the bursts with a
relative error of 29% and the slow sine with 4.9%, and reconstructs the signal to within $10^{-16}$.

## Cost

EEMD costs $I$ full EMDs, typically 100 to 500 times the cost of one. CEEMDAN decomposes each noise realisation once,
then runs $I$ single-mode extractions at each of its $K$ stages, so it costs roughly $2I$ EMDs. Both parallelise over
the ensemble.

## Trade-offs

- **The averages are not IMFs.** A mean of IMFs need not satisfy the IMF conditions. The averaged modes can be sifted
  once more to restore them.
- **Two parameters to choose.** The noise level and the ensemble size are chosen by the user, and the results depend on
  both. Too little noise leaves mode mixing in place; too much needs a larger ensemble.
- **Residual noise and splitting in EEMD.** The reconstruction error falls only as $1/\sqrt{I}$, and a component can be
  split across two neighbouring IMFs when copies disagree about its scale. CEEMDAN removes the first problem and
  reduces the second.
- **The filter bank is a property of noise, not of the signal.** Noise imposes octave bands, so two signal components
  within one octave still share an IMF. <NoteLink to="variational-mode-decomposition">Variational mode
  decomposition</NoteLink> places its bands where the signal's energy is instead.
