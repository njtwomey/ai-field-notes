<Definition>
A mixture $x(t) = \sum_{j=1}^{J} s_j(t)$, or with several microphones $\xvec(t) = \Amat\,\svec(t)$, is observed; source
separation estimates the sources $s_j$. Most single-channel methods work on the magnitude spectrogram, estimating each
source's share of every time-frequency cell and applying it as a mask to the mixture's short-time Fourier transform.
</Definition>

## Non-negative matrix factorisation

Arrange the mixture's magnitude spectrogram as a non-negative matrix $\Vmat \in \reals_{\ge0}^{F \times T}$, frequency
by time. NMF approximates it as

$$
\Vmat \approx \Wmat\Hmat, \qquad \Wmat \in \reals_{\ge0}^{F \times K},\ \Hmat \in \reals_{\ge0}^{K \times T}.
$$

Each column of $\Wmat$ is a spectral template, such as a note's harmonic pattern, and the matching row of $\Hmat$ says
how strongly it sounds in each frame. Non-negativity makes the model additive: parts combine without cancelling, so the
factors tend to be interpretable parts of the sound <Cite id="lee1999" />. For music, each template often corresponds
to one pitch of one instrument <Cite id="smaragdis2003" />.

**Multiplicative updates.** For the generalised Kullback–Leibler divergence
$D(\Vmat \,\|\, \Wmat\Hmat) = \sum_{ft}\brack{V_{ft}\log\frac{V_{ft}}{(\Wmat\Hmat)_{ft}} - V_{ft} + (\Wmat\Hmat)_{ft}}$,
the gradient with respect to $H_{kt}$ is $\sum_f W_{fk} - \sum_f W_{fk}V_{ft}/(\Wmat\Hmat)_{ft}$, a positive part minus
a negative part. Taking a gradient step with the element-wise step size $H_{kt}/\sum_f W_{fk}$ cancels the positive part
and leaves a multiplicative update:

$$
\Hmat \leftarrow \Hmat \odot \frac{\Wmat^\top\paren{\Vmat \oslash \Wmat\Hmat}}{\Wmat^\top\ones}, \qquad
\Wmat \leftarrow \Wmat \odot \frac{\paren{\Vmat \oslash \Wmat\Hmat}\Hmat^\top}{\ones\Hmat^\top},
$$

with $\odot$ and $\oslash$ element-wise. Products and ratios of non-negative numbers stay non-negative, so no
projection is needed, and an auxiliary-function argument, like the one behind EM, shows that each update never
increases the divergence <Cite id="lee2001" />. The squared-error version replaces the ratios by
$\Wmat^\top\Vmat / \Wmat^\top\Wmat\Hmat$.

## Masks

Given estimates of each source's magnitude, $\hat V_j = \Wmat_j\Hmat_j$ for the templates assigned to source $j$, the
soft or Wiener-like mask

$$
M_j(f, t) = \frac{\hat V_j(f, t)}{\sum_i \hat V_i(f, t)}
$$

splits every cell of the mixture's complex STFT in proportion, $\hat S_j = M_j \odot X$, keeping the mixture's phase,
and the inverse STFT gives the waveform. The masks sum to 1, so the separated sources sum to the mixture. With power
spectrograms in place of magnitudes it is exactly the <NoteLink to="wiener-filter">Wiener filter</NoteLink> gain. The
ideal binary mask, 1 where a source dominates and 0 elsewhere, is a common oracle target.

## Worked example

Mix a repeated low note at 196 Hz, sounding every half second, with a sustained note at 523 Hz, and factorise the
magnitude spectrogram (97 bins below 1.5 kHz, 92 frames) with rank $K = 2$ and 120 KL updates. One component learns the
low note's harmonic template and pulses every half second; the other learns the high note. Masking the mixture with the
first component's share gives a spectrogram whose cosine similarity with the low note's own spectrogram is 0.998, and the
remainder matches the high note equally well. With $K = 1$ the single template must average both notes and nothing is
separated.

## Several microphones

With $M$ microphones and instantaneous mixing $\xvec(t) = \Amat\svec(t)$, independent component analysis finds an
unmixing matrix $\Wmat \approx \Amat^{-1}$ by making the outputs as statistically independent as possible, for example
by maximising their non-Gaussianity <Cite id="hyvarinen2000" />. Real rooms mix by convolution, not by a matrix; applying
ICA in each frequency band of the STFT turns convolution into per-band multiplication, at the cost of aligning the
permutation and scaling of sources across bands.

## Pitfalls

- **Choosing $K$.** Too few components merge sources; too many split one source over several, which must then be
  grouped, by clustering the templates or by supervision.
- **Magnitude additivity is approximate.** Magnitudes of overlapping sources do not add exactly, because their phases
  interfere. Masks applied with the mixture phase leave artefacts where sources overlap.
- **Supervision helps a lot.** Learning templates from isolated examples of each source first, then fixing them while
  fitting the activations, separates far better than blind factorisation. Neural networks trained to predict masks
  now dominate practical systems.
