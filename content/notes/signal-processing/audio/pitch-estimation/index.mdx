<Definition>
A periodic signal satisfies $x[n] = x[n + P]$ for its period $P$, and its fundamental frequency is $f_0 = f_s/P$. A
harmonic sound has spectral peaks at $f_0, 2f_0, 3f_0, \dots$ Pitch estimation finds $P$ or $f_0$ frame by frame,
from the signal's self-similarity in time or the regular spacing of its harmonics in frequency. Perceived pitch
follows $f_0$ even when the component at $f_0$ itself is missing.
</Definition>

## Autocorrelation

The short-time autocorrelation $r(\tau) = \sum_{j=0}^{W-1} x[j]\,x[j + \tau]$ has peaks at $\tau = P, 2P, 3P, \dots$,
because the signal lines up with itself at every multiple of the period. The largest peak after $\tau = 0$, within
a search range, estimates $P$. The weakness is that the peaks at multiples of $P$ are nearly as high as the one at
$P$, so noise or a near-integer multiple can win: a sub-harmonic error, reporting $f_0/2$ or $f_0/3$. Peaks at
fractions of $P$, from strong harmonics, give octave errors upwards.

## YIN

YIN replaces the autocorrelation with a difference function and normalises it in two steps
<Cite id="decheveigne2002" />.

**Difference function.** For a periodic signal, $x[j] - x[j + P] = 0$, so

$$
d(\tau) = \sum_{j=0}^{W-1}\paren{x[j] - x[j + \tau]}^2 = r_0(0) + r_\tau(0) - 2r(\tau)
$$

is zero at the period, where $r_\tau(0)$ is the energy of the window starting at $\tau$. Unlike $r(\tau)$ it accounts
for changes in the window's energy with lag.

**Cumulative mean normalisation.** $d(\tau)$ is zero at $\tau = 0$ and tends to dip at every multiple of $P$, so its
global minimum is ambiguous. YIN divides by the running mean of $d$ up to $\tau$:

$$
d'(\tau) = \begin{cases} 1, & \tau = 0,\\[2pt] \dfrac{d(\tau)}{\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{\tau} d(j)}, & \tau > 0.\end{cases}
$$

This removes the dip at zero lag, starting $d'$ at 1, and makes $d'$ small only where $d$ is small relative to its
typical value so far. The first lag where $d'(\tau)$ falls below an absolute threshold, typically 0.1, is taken, then
followed down to the local minimum. Choosing the first qualifying dip, not the deepest, is what prevents sub-harmonic
errors: the true period comes before its multiples.

**Parabolic interpolation.** Fitting a parabola through $d'$ at $\tau - 1, \tau, \tau + 1$ refines the period below one
sample, to $\hat\tau = \tau + \frac{d'(\tau - 1) - d'(\tau + 1)}{2\paren{d'(\tau - 1) - 2d'(\tau) + d'(\tau + 1)}}$.
Without it, the resolution at high $f_0$ is poor: at 16 kHz, lags 72 and 73 are 222.2 and 219.2 Hz.

## Worked example

Take a 220 Hz tone with ten harmonics at amplitudes $1/h$, sampled at 16 kHz, and a 512-sample window. The true period
is $16000/220 = 72.73$ samples.

- **Autocorrelation.** The largest peak between lags 40 and 400 is at lag 218, three periods ($3 \times 72.73 = 218.2$,
  almost an integer), giving 73.4 Hz: a sub-harmonic error.
- **YIN.** $d'$ first falls below 0.1 at lag 73, where $d'(73) = 0.0018$. Parabolic interpolation gives
  $\hat\tau = 72.72$ and $f_0 = 220.01$ Hz.
- **Missing fundamental.** Removing the 220 Hz component, keeping harmonics 2 to 10, leaves the period unchanged, and
  YIN still returns 220.01 Hz.

## Frequency-domain methods

- **Cepstrum.** The log spectrum of a harmonic sound ripples with period $f_0$ in frequency; its inverse transform, the
  <NoteLink to="cepstrum">cepstrum</NoteLink>, peaks at quefrency $1/f_0$ <Cite id="noll1967" />.
- **Harmonic product spectrum.** Downsample the magnitude spectrum by factors $1, 2, \dots, R$ and multiply:
  $\mathrm{HPS}(f) = \prod_{r=1}^{R}\abs{X(rf)}$. Only at $f = f_0$ do all $R$ factors land on harmonics, so the product
  peaks there <Cite id="schroeder1968" />. It resists missing fundamentals but is prone to octave errors upward when
  odd harmonics are weak.

## Pitfalls

- **Octave errors are the main failure.** Every method has a characteristic direction of error. Tracking over time,
  with continuity constraints or a hidden Markov model over candidate pitches, removes most of them.
- **Voicing decisions.** Unvoiced speech and noise have no pitch. A confidence measure, such as the value of $d'$ at the
  chosen lag, decides whether a frame is voiced.
- **Window length.** The window must hold at least two periods of the lowest $f_0$ searched: 40 ms for 50 Hz. Longer
  windows blur fast pitch changes.
- **Polyphony.** These methods assume one periodic source. Several simultaneous notes need multi-pitch methods.
