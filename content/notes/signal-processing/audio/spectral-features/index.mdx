<Definition>
For a frame with <NoteLink to="parseval-theorem">power spectrum</NoteLink> $P_k = \abs{X[k]}^2$ at bin frequencies $f_k$, $k = 0, \dots, K$, the common spectral
features are statistics of $P$ treated as a distribution over frequency, and of its change over time. Together with the
time-domain zero-crossing rate and RMS energy, they are the low-level descriptors used in audio classification,
music information retrieval and sound-event detection <Cite id="muller2015" />.
</Definition>

## Definitions

Write $\bar P = \sum_k P_k$ for the total power.

- **Spectral centroid**, the power-weighted mean frequency: $\mu = \sum_k f_k P_k \big/ \bar P$. It correlates with
  perceived brightness.
- **Spectral spread** or bandwidth, the power-weighted <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink>: $\sigma = \sqrt{\sum_k (f_k - \mu)^2 P_k
  \big/ \bar P}$.
- **Spectral flatness**, the ratio of geometric to arithmetic mean power:

  $$
  \mathrm{SF} = \frac{\exp\paren{\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_k \log P_k}}{\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_k P_k} \in [0, 1].
  $$

  By the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means it is at most 1, with equality for a flat spectrum. A few strong
  peaks drive it towards 0. It is also called Wiener <NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink>.
- **Spectral roll-off**, the frequency below which a fixed fraction $\rho$ of the power lies, usually 85% or 95%: the
  smallest $f_R$ with $\sum_{f_k \le f_R} P_k \ge \rho\,\bar P$.
- **Spectral flux**, the change between consecutive frames' magnitudes, $\Phi_t = \sum_k \paren{\abs{X_t[k]} -
  \abs{X_{t-1}[k]}}^2$. The half-wave-rectified form, which counts only increases, is the standard <NoteLink to="onset-detection-and-beat-tracking">onset detection</NoteLink>
  function.
- **Zero-crossing rate**, the fraction of consecutive samples that change sign,
  $\mathrm{ZCR} = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1} \indicator\brack{\sgn x[n] \ne \sgn x[n-1]}$. A pure tone of frequency
  $f$ crosses zero twice per period, so its ZCR is $2f/f_s$ per sample.
- **RMS energy**, $\sqrt{\frac1N\sum_n x^2[n]}$, the frame's level, usually reported in dB.

## Worked example

Take four bins at 0, 100, 200 and 300 Hz with powers $(1, 4, 1, 0)$, so $\bar P = 6$.

- **Centroid.** $\mu = (0 + 400 + 200 + 0)/6 = 100$ Hz.
- **Spread.** $\sigma^2 = (100^2 \times 1 + 0 + 100^2 \times 1 + 200^2 \times 0)/6 = 3333$, so $\sigma = 57.7$ Hz.
- **Roll-off at 85%.** The cumulative powers are 1, 5, 6, 6; the first to reach $0.85 \times 6 = 5.1$ is the third bin,
  so $f_R = 200$ Hz.
- **Flatness.** With a zero bin the geometric mean is 0 and so is the flatness. For powers $(1, 4, 1, 1)$ instead, the
  geometric mean is $4^{1/4} = 1.414$ and the arithmetic mean $1.75$, so $\mathrm{SF} = 0.808$.

For white noise the flatness of a windowed <NoteLink to="periodogram">periodogram</NoteLink> is not 1 but about $e^{-\gamma} = 0.561$, with $\gamma$ the
Euler–Mascheroni constant. Each periodogram bin of white noise is exponentially distributed, and for an exponential
variable $\expect[\log P] = \log\expect[P] - \gamma$. Averaging several frames first, as in
<NoteLink to="welch-method">Welch's method</NoteLink>, brings it closer to 1.

## Pitfalls

- **Power or magnitude.** Some definitions weight by $\abs{X}$, some by $\abs{X}^2$. The centroid of a magnitude
  spectrum lies between that of the power spectrum and the band's midpoint.
- **Silence.** In silent frames all features are dominated by noise or undefined: centroid and flatness divide by a
  tiny total. Gate by RMS energy first.
- **Resolution dependence.** Flatness and flux depend on the window length and <Gloss name="fft" /> size. Compare features only across
  identical analysis settings.
