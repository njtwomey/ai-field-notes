<Definition>
The gammatone filter of order $n$, centre frequency $f_c$ and bandwidth parameter $b$ has <NoteLink to="linear-time-invariant-systems">impulse response</NoteLink>

$$
g(t) = a\, t^{n-1} e^{-2\pi b t} \cos\paren{2\pi f_c t + \phi}, \qquad t \ge 0.
$$

The envelope $t^{n-1}e^{-2\pi bt}$ is the shape of a gamma density, hence the name. Order $n = 4$ with
$b = 1.019\,\mathrm{ERB}(f_c)$, where the equivalent rectangular bandwidth is $\mathrm{ERB}(f) = 24.7(4.37f/1000 + 1)$ Hz, matches measured human auditory
filters <Cite id="glasberg1990" /> <Cite id="lyon2017" />.
</Definition>

## The frequency response

The impulse response is a gamma envelope times a carrier, so its transform is the envelope's transform
shifted to $\pm f_c$. The envelope $t^{n-1}e^{-2\pi bt}$ has <NoteLink to="fourier-transform">Fourier transform</NoteLink> $(n-1)!/(2\pi(b + if))^n$. Near
$f_c$, ignoring the image at $-f_c$,

$$
\abs{G(f)} \propto \brack{1 + \paren{\frac{f - f_c}{b}}^2}^{-n/2}.
$$

This is $n$ first-order resonances in cascade: a symmetric, rounded passband whose skirts fall at $6n$ dB per octave of
detuning far from the centre. Order 4 matches the shape of auditory filters measured with notched noise.

## Where 1.019 comes from

The <NoteLink to="auditory-scales">equivalent rectangular bandwidth</NoteLink> of a filter is the width of a rectangle with the same peak power gain and the same
area under $\abs{G}^2$. Integrating the response above,

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\brack{1 + \paren{\frac{f - f_c}{b}}^2}^{-n}\, df = b\,\frac{\pi\,(2n - 2)!}{2^{2n-2}\,\paren{(n-1)!}^2},
$$

which for $n = 4$ is $b \times \pi \times 720/(64 \times 36) = 0.982\,b$. Setting this equal to the measured
$\mathrm{ERB}(f_c)$ gives $b = \mathrm{ERB}(f_c)/0.982 = 1.019\,\mathrm{ERB}(f_c)$.

**Worked example.** At $f_c = 1$ kHz, $\mathrm{ERB} = 24.7 \times 5.37 = 132.6$ Hz and $b = 135.2$ Hz. The envelope
$t^3e^{-2\pi bt}$ peaks where its <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> vanishes, at $t = 3/(2\pi b) = 3.5$ ms. Integrating the squared magnitude
of a filter sampled at 48 kHz numerically gives an ERB of 132.7 Hz, matching the target.

## The bank

Centre frequencies are spaced equally in ERB number, typically one filter per ERB or finer, from about 50 Hz to the
<NoteLink to="sampling-theorem">Nyquist frequency</NoteLink>. The outputs are half-wave rectified and smoothed to model hair-cell transduction, or summed in
frames to give a cochleagram, the auditory analogue of a spectrogram. Cepstral coefficients of log gammatone energies
(GFCCs) are an alternative to <NoteLink to="mel-frequency-cepstral-coefficients">MFCCs</NoteLink> that is somewhat more robust to noise.

**Implementation.** The impulse response is not a rational function of time, but the all-pole approximation, a
cascade of four complex <NoteLink to="infinite-impulse-response-filters">one-pole filters</NoteLink> at $f_c$ with pole radius $e^{-2\pi b/f_s}$, reproduces the magnitude
response closely at a few multiplies per sample.

## Pitfalls

- **A linear model.** Real cochlear filters are nonlinear: they sharpen at low levels and broaden at high ones, and
  their gain is compressive. Gammatone banks ignore this; gammachirp and dynamic compressive models add it.
- **Phase alignment.** Filters with different $f_c$ have different <NoteLink to="linear-phase-and-group-delay">group delays</NoteLink>, the low ones longest. Summing their
  outputs without compensation smears transients.
- **Sampling at high frequencies.** When $f_c + 3b$ approaches the Nyquist frequency, the sampled impulse response
  aliases and the response is no longer symmetric.
