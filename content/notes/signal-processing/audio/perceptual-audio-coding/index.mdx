<Definition>
A perceptual audio coder transforms the signal into frequency components, quantises each component with as few bits as
possible while keeping the resulting <NoteLink to="quantisation">quantisation noise</NoteLink> below a masking threshold
computed from a model of human hearing, and entropy-codes the result <Cite id="painter2000" />. The transform in MP3's
hybrid filter bank, AAC, Vorbis and Opus's music mode is the modified discrete cosine transform.
</Definition>

## Masking

A loud sound makes quieter sounds at nearby frequencies inaudible. Three facts shape the threshold of audibility:

- **The threshold in quiet.** Even in silence, sounds below about $T(f) = 3.64 (f/\mathrm{kHz})^{-0.8} -
  6.5\,e^{-0.6(f/\mathrm{kHz} - 3.3)^2} + 10^{-3}(f/\mathrm{kHz})^4$ dB SPL are inaudible, with the ear most sensitive
  near 3 to 4 kHz <Cite id="terhardt1979" />.
- **Simultaneous masking.** A masker raises the threshold within its critical band and, more weakly, in neighbouring
  bands. On the <NoteLink to="auditory-scales">Bark scale</NoteLink> the spread has a roughly fixed shape; a common
  model is Schroeder's spreading function
  $\mathrm{SF}(\Delta z) = 15.81 + 7.5(\Delta z + 0.474) - 17.5\sqrt{1 + (\Delta z + 0.474)^2}$ dB. It is asymmetric:
  masking extends much further to higher frequencies than to lower.
- **Temporal masking.** Masking also extends a few milliseconds before a loud sound and tens of milliseconds after it.

The psychoacoustic model estimates, per frame and band, the global masking threshold: the thresholds of all maskers,
each spread across bands and lowered by an offset that depends on whether the masker is tonal or noise-like, summed in
power with the threshold in quiet.

## Bit allocation

Uniform quantisation with $b$ bits gives a <Gloss name="snr">signal-to-noise ratio</Gloss> of about $6.02\,b$ dB. The
signal-to-mask ratio $\mathrm{SMR} = L_{\text{signal}} - L_{\text{mask}}$ in a band is how far the signal sits above the
threshold, so noise stays masked with about $\mathrm{SMR}/6.02$ bits. Bands with negative SMR need no bits at all. The
encoder distributes the available bits to keep the noise-to-mask ratio at or below 0 dB everywhere, and shapes noise
into the bands where it is least audible when the bit budget is too small.

**Worked example.** A 1 kHz tone at 70 dB SPL lies at 8.5 Bark. With a masking offset of 10 dB, the threshold it creates
is 60 dB at 1 kHz, 45.6 dB at 800 Hz, 54.3 dB at 1.2 kHz and 23.4 dB at 2 kHz: higher above the masker than below it at
a similar Bark distance. The threshold in quiet at those frequencies is between $-0.3$ and 4 dB SPL. A band component 20
dB above its mask needs about $20/6.02 = 3.3$ bits, where 16-bit PCM spends 16.

## The MDCT and time-domain aliasing cancellation

Coding needs a transform that is critically sampled, producing as many coefficients as input samples, and uses
overlapping, smoothly tapered windows, which avoid block-edge artefacts. The MDCT has both. Each block of $2N$ samples,
overlapping its neighbours by half, gives $N$ coefficients:

$$
X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{2N-1} w[n]\, x[n] \cos\brack{\frac{\pi}{N}\paren{n + \frac12 + \frac{N}{2}}\paren{k + \frac12}},
\qquad k = 0, \dots, N - 1.
$$

With $N$ coefficients from $2N$ samples a single block cannot be inverted. Its inverse transform returns the windowed
block plus a time-reversed copy: in the first half, sample $n$ is corrupted by $-x[N - 1 - n]$; in the second half, by
$+x[3N - 1 - n]$. When consecutive blocks are windowed again and overlap-added, the alias in one block's second half and
the next block's first half have opposite signs and cancel, provided the window satisfies the Princen–Bradley condition

$$
w^2[n] + w^2[n + N] = 1, \qquad w[n] = w[2N - 1 - n].
$$

This is time-domain aliasing cancellation <Cite id="princen1987" />. The sine window $w[n] = \sin\paren{\pi(n +
\frac12)/(2N)}$ satisfies it, since $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$. With $N = 16$ and this window, a random signal is
reconstructed to within $10^{-14}$. Coders switch between long blocks, for
<NoteLink to="zero-padding-and-resolution">frequency resolution</NoteLink> on tonal music, and short blocks, to confine
pre-echo around transients.

## Pitfalls

- **Pre-echo.** Quantisation noise spreads over the whole block. Before a sharp attack it is not masked, since
  backward masking lasts only a few milliseconds, and is heard as a smear. Block switching and temporal noise shaping
  address it.
- **Tandem coding.** Decoding and re-encoding, or encoding with different coders in sequence, accumulates noise that
  each stage's model assumed was masked.
- **Models are averages.** Thresholds describe typical listeners and levels. Coders are tuned and validated by
  listening tests, not by objective error measures alone.
