<Definition>
A two-channel filter bank splits $x$ with analysis filters $H_0$ (lowpass) and $H_1$ (highpass), downsamples each
branch by 2, then upsamples, filters with synthesis filters $G_0$, $G_1$ and adds. It has **perfect reconstruction**
when the output is $\hat x[n] = c\, x[n - d]$ for a constant $c \ne 0$ and a delay $d$ <Cite id="vaidyanathan1993" />.
</Definition>

## The input–output relation

Downsampling by 2 then upsampling by 2 zeroes the odd samples, so it maps $V(z)$ to $\tfrac12 \brack{V(z) + V(-z)}$:
the term $V(-z)$ has coefficients $(-1)^n v[n]$, and averaging with $V(z)$ cancels the odd ones. Applying this to
both branches,

$$
\hat X(z) = \underbrace{\tfrac12 \brack{G_0(z) H_0(z) + G_1(z) H_1(z)}}_{T(z)} X(z)
+ \underbrace{\tfrac12 \brack{G_0(z) H_0(-z) + G_1(z) H_1(-z)}}_{A(z)} X(-z).
$$

$X(-z)$ is the input spectrum shifted by $\pi$: its appearance is aliasing, introduced by the downsamplers. $T(z)$ is
the transfer function of the alias-free part. Perfect reconstruction needs two conditions:

- **Alias cancellation:** $A(z) = 0$.
- **No distortion:** $T(z) = c\, z^{-d}$.

## Cancelling the aliasing

The choice

$$
G_0(z) = H_1(-z), \qquad G_1(z) = -H_0(-z)
$$

makes $A(z) = \tfrac12 \brack{H_1(-z) H_0(-z) - H_0(-z) H_1(-z)} = 0$ for any analysis filters. The aliasing that
leaks through each branch is cancelled exactly by the other branch, even though neither filter is ideal. The
remaining condition is then on the analysis filters alone:

$$
T(z) = \tfrac12 \brack{H_0(z) H_1(-z) - H_1(z) H_0(-z)} = c\, z^{-d}.
$$

## Three classic designs

- **Quadrature mirror filters (QMF).** Take $H_1(z) = H_0(-z)$, the lowpass mirrored about $\pi/2$. Then
  $T(z) = \tfrac12 \brack{H_0(z)^2 - H_0(-z)^2}$. With a linear-phase FIR $H_0$ this cannot be a pure delay except
  for the trivial two-tap case, so QMF banks cancel aliasing exactly but only approximate the magnitude condition.
- **Orthogonal (conjugate quadrature) filters.** Take an even-length $H_0$ of length $N + 1$ and
  $h_1[n] = (-1)^n h_0[N - n]$, that is $H_1(z) = -z^{-N} H_0(-z^{-1})$ <Cite id="smith1986" />. Substituting,
  $T(z) = \tfrac12 z^{-N} \brack{P(z) + P(-z)}$ with $P(z) = H_0(z) H_0(z^{-1})$. Perfect reconstruction therefore
  needs $P(z) + P(-z) = 2$: $P$ must be a **halfband filter**, whose even-indexed coefficients around the centre are
  zero except the centre itself. The synthesis filters are the time-reversed analysis filters, and the bank
  implements an orthogonal transform.
- **Biorthogonal filters.** Dropping orthogonality allows symmetric, linear-phase filters with perfect
  reconstruction, at the cost of an analysis and a synthesis basis that differ. The CDF 9/7 pair used in JPEG 2000 is
  an example <Cite id="vetterli1995" />.

## Worked examples

**Haar.** $H_0(z) = (1 + z^{-1})/\sqrt2$ and $H_1(z) = (1 - z^{-1})/\sqrt2$. Then $H_1(-z) = H_0(z)$ and
$H_0(-z) = H_1(z)$, so

$$
T(z) = \tfrac12 \brack{H_0(z)^2 - H_1(z)^2} = \tfrac14 \brack{(1 + z^{-1})^2 - (1 - z^{-1})^2} = z^{-1}.
$$

The Haar bank reconstructs exactly with a one-sample delay. It is the only case in which a QMF and an orthogonal
design coincide.

**Daubechies db2.** With $h_0 = (0.4830,\, 0.8365,\, 0.2241,\, -0.1294)$, the product filter is
$P(z) = \tfrac{1}{16}\paren{-z^{3} + 9z + 16 + 9z^{-1} - z^{-3}}$: its only nonzero even-offset coefficient is the
centre, so it is halfband, and the bank reconstructs with $T(z) = z^{-3}$ and $A(z) = 0$, as a direct numerical
check of both formulas confirms.

## Connection to wavelets

Iterating an orthogonal two-channel bank on its lowpass output gives the
<NoteLink to="discrete-wavelet-transform">discrete wavelet transform</NoteLink>. The halfband condition on
$P(z) = \abs{H_0(e^{i\omega})}^2$, together with a zero of order $N$ at $\omega = \pi$, is the design equation of the
Daubechies wavelets <Cite id="daubechies1992" />. See
<NoteLink to="wavelet-families">wavelet families</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="multiresolution-analysis">multiresolution analysis</NoteLink>.

## Pitfalls

- **Perfect reconstruction is fragile to processing.** Alias cancellation relies on both subbands arriving intact.
  Quantising or discarding a band breaks the cancellation, which is why coders shape quantisation noise to stay below
  masking thresholds.
- **Frequency selectivity is not guaranteed.** The PR conditions say nothing about how well $H_0$ separates the bands;
  short PR filters such as Haar overlap heavily.
