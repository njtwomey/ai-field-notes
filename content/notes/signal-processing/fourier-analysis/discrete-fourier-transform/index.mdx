<Definition>
The discrete Fourier transform of $N$ samples $x[0], \dots, x[N-1]$ and its inverse are

$$
X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\, e^{-i 2\pi k n / N}, \qquad
x[n] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} X[k]\, e^{i 2\pi k n / N},
$$

for $k, n = 0, \dots, N-1$. Coefficient $X[k]$ measures the component at frequency $\omega_k = 2\pi k/N$ radians per
sample, or $k f_s / N$ hertz for the sampling rate $f_s$ <Cite id="smith2007" />.
</Definition>

## Sampling the DTFT

For a sequence of length $N$, the <NoteLink to="discrete-time-fourier-transform">discrete-time Fourier transform (DTFT)</NoteLink> is
$X(e^{i\omega}) = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n] e^{-i\omega n}$. Setting $\omega = 2\pi k/N$ gives the DFT formula exactly:

$$
X[k] = X(e^{i\omega})\Big|_{\omega = 2\pi k / N}.
$$

The DFT is the DTFT sampled at $N$ equally spaced frequencies around the unit circle. Padding the sequence with zeros
to length $L > N$ samples the same curve at $L$ points; see
<NoteLink to="zero-padding-and-resolution">zero-padding and resolution</NoteLink>.

## The inverse, by orthogonality

The vectors $\evec_k = (e^{i 2\pi k n/N})_{n=0}^{N-1}$ are orthogonal:

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} e^{i 2\pi (k - m) n/N} = \begin{cases} N & k \equiv m \pmod N, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases}
$$

because for $k \ne m$ the terms are the $N$-th roots of unity raised to the power $k - m$, which sum to zero as a
geometric series: $\paren{1 - e^{i 2\pi(k-m)}}/\paren{1 - e^{i 2\pi (k-m)/N}} = 0$. Substituting the forward formula
into the inverse therefore returns $x[n]$.

## Matrix form

Write $W = e^{-i 2\pi / N}$. The DFT is multiplication by the $N \times N$ matrix $\Fmat$ with entries
$F_{kn} = W^{kn}$, taking the vector of samples $\xvec$ to the vector of coefficients $\hat\xvec$:

$$
\hat\xvec = \Fmat \xvec, \qquad \xvec = \frac{1}{N}\Fmat^{\mathsf H}\hat\xvec,
$$

where $^{\mathsf H}$ is the conjugate transpose. Orthogonality says $\Fmat^{\mathsf H}\Fmat = N\eye$, so
$\Fmat/\sqrt N$ is unitary. Computing $\Fmat\xvec$ directly
costs $N^2$ multiplications; the <NoteLink to="fast-fourier-transform">fast Fourier transform (FFT)</NoteLink> factors $\Fmat$ into sparse matrices and needs
$O(N\log N)$.

## Periodicity

Both formulas are periodic: $X[k + N] = X[k]$ and the inverse gives $x[n + N] = x[n]$. The DFT treats its input as one
period of an infinitely repeating sequence. Two consequences follow.

- **Shifts are circular.** Delaying by $m$ samples multiplies $X[k]$ by $W^{km}$ and rotates the sequence, with
  samples that fall off the end reappearing at the start.
- **Products give circular convolution.** $X[k] H[k]$ is the DFT of the circular convolution of $x$ and $h$, which
  wraps around unless the sequences are padded; see the <NoteLink to="convolution-theorem">convolution
  theorem</NoteLink>.

A signal whose first and last samples do not join smoothly has, in effect, a jump at the wrap point, which spreads
energy across all bins: <NoteLink to="spectral-leakage-and-windows">leakage</NoteLink>.

## Worked example

For $x = (1, 2, 3, 4)$, $W = e^{-i\pi/2} = -i$:

$$
X[0] = 10, \quad X[1] = 1 - 2i - 3 + 4i = -2 + 2i, \quad X[2] = 1 - 2 + 3 - 4 = -2, \quad X[3] = -2 - 2i.
$$

$X[0]$ is the sum, and $X[3] = \overline{X[1]}$ because $x$ is real. For real input, bins $k$ and $N - k$ are
conjugates, so only $N/2 + 1$ bins carry independent information.

## Pitfalls

- **Bin $k$ above $N/2$ is a negative frequency.** Bin $N - k$ represents $-2\pi k/N$. Plots of "frequency against
  bin" beyond $N/2$ show the negative half.
- **Scaling conventions.** The $1/N$ may sit in the inverse (as here and in NumPy), in the forward transform, or be
  split as $1/\sqrt N$ in both. Amplitudes read from a spectrum depend on which.
