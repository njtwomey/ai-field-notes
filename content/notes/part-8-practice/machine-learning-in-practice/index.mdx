<Definition>
**Machine learning in practice** covers what happens around a model once its method is chosen: whether its labels,
explanations, decisions and data handling can be trusted; how it is trained on many devices, served cheaply and watched
in production; and how specific published models and benchmark studies turned out. The Part has three chapters:
trustworthy machine learning, engineering and case studies.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Parts IV to VII explain how models learn and what they are used for. A model that scores well on a benchmark can still
fail in use. Its training labels may be wrong, its errors may fall on one group, small changes to its inputs may fool
it, its outputs may leak training records, it may be too large to train or too slow to serve, and its accuracy may
decay after release. This Part collects the methods that detect and reduce these failures, and the case studies that
show how real systems combined the ideas of the earlier Parts.

## Main ideas

**Trust needs more than accuracy.** <NoteLink to="trustworthy-machine-learning" /> covers five properties a deployed
model needs. <NoteLink to="label-noise" /> deals with wrong training labels, by modelling the noise, correcting the loss
or finding the errors. <NoteLink to="interpretability" /> explains predictions with
<NoteLink to="intrinsically-interpretable-models">interpretable models</NoteLink> or post-hoc attributions,
counterfactuals and concepts. <NoteLink to="fairness" /> defines group and <NoteLink to="individual-fairness">individual
fairness</NoteLink>, shows that the main group criteria conflict when base rates differ, and gives mitigation methods
<Cite id="barocas2023" />. <NoteLink to="robustness" /> covers distribution shift and
<NoteLink to="adversarial-examples">adversarial examples</NoteLink>. <NoteLink to="privacy-in-machine-learning" />
measures leakage with <NoteLink to="membership-inference">membership inference</NoteLink> and bounds it with
<Gloss name="differential-privacy">differential privacy</Gloss>.

**Scale is an engineering problem.** <NoteLink to="machine-learning-engineering" /> follows a model through its life.
<NoteLink to="training-at-scale" /> accounts for <NoteLink to="memory-footprint-of-training">training memory</NoteLink>
and splits it across devices with <NoteLink to="mixed-precision-training">mixed precision</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="data-parallelism">data parallelism</NoteLink>, <Gloss name="zero" /> and
<NoteLink to="model-parallelism">model parallelism</NoteLink> <Cite id="narayanan2021" />.
<NoteLink to="efficient-inference" /> reduces the cost of each prediction with quantisation, pruning, distillation,
<Gloss name="flashattention" /> and batching.

**Production changes the model's world.** <NoteLink to="deployment-and-monitoring" /> releases a new model in stages,
with shadow deployments and canary releases, and then monitors service health, inputs, predictions and accuracy.
<NoteLink to="drift-detection">Drift detection</NoteLink> tests whether recent inputs still resemble the data the model
was evaluated on. Machine learning systems tend to fail silently, so these checks replace the errors that ordinary
software would raise <Cite id="sculley2015" />.

**Evidence from real systems.** <NoteLink to="case-studies" /> compares published models on fixed axes:
<NoteLink to="language-model-case-studies" /> from <NoteLink to="bert-vs-gpt">BERT and GPT</NoteLink> to
<Gloss name="llama" /> and DeepSeek-V3, <Gloss name="resnet" /> against the <Gloss name="vit">vision
transformer</Gloss>, and four families of <Gloss name="imagenet" /> generators. Benchmark studies compare whole families
of methods, such as <NoteLink to="gradient-boosting">gradient boosting</NoteLink> against deep networks on tables and
the entries of <NoteLink to="m4-and-m5-competitions">the M4 and M5 forecasting competitions</NoteLink>. Each result
holds for its data, compute and protocol.

## How the notes fit together

The three chapters are independent, and each has its own overview:

- <NoteLink to="trustworthy-machine-learning" /> builds on the evaluation and decision theory of Part IV. Its five
  subtopics can be read in any order.
- <NoteLink to="machine-learning-engineering" /> assumes neural networks from Part VI. Training at
  scale, efficient inference and deployment are best read in that order.
- <NoteLink to="case-studies" /> assumes the techniques each study compares, so it is best read after the relevant
  chapters of Parts V and VI.
