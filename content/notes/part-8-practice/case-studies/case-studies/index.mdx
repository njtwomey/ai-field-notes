<Definition>
**Case studies** describe specific published models or benchmark studies and compare them on fixed axes. A model
comparison sets two or more models side by side on architecture, data, training and results. A benchmark study reports
how whole families of methods performed on shared data under a common protocol.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Technique notes explain one idea at a time. A working system combines many ideas with a dataset and a compute budget,
and its results depend on all of them. The case studies record what was combined, with the numbers reported by the
original authors. They also test general claims against evidence: whether attention beats convolution, whether deep
networks beat trees on tabular data, and whether machine learning beats statistics on forecasting. The answers depend
on data size, compute and evaluation protocol, and each case study states those conditions.

## Main ideas

**<Gloss name="language-model">Language models</Gloss>.** <NoteLink to="language-model-case-studies" /> compares encoder
and decoder pre-training in <NoteLink to="bert-vs-gpt" />, follows one dense design through three generations in
<NoteLink to="llama-family" />, and sets a 2019 model beside a 2024 <Gloss name="moe">mixture-of-experts</Gloss> model
in <NoteLink to="gpt-2-vs-deepseek-v3" />.

**Vision models.** <NoteLink to="resnet-vs-vision-transformer" /> compares a convolutional network with a transformer
over image patches. Pre-trained on 300 million images, the transformer matched the best convolutional network with far
less compute. Trained on <Gloss name="imagenet" /> alone, it fell behind <Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />.
<NoteLink to="image-generators-on-imagenet" /> compares one model from each family of image generators, a
<Gloss name="vq-vae" />, a <Gloss name="gan" />, a pixel-space <Gloss name="diffusion-model">diffusion model</Gloss> and
a <Gloss name="ldm">latent diffusion model</Gloss>, by <Gloss name="fid" />, recall and training compute.

**Benchmark studies.** <NoteLink to="gradient-boosting-vs-deep-learning-on-tabular-data" /> reports two 2022 benchmarks
in which tree ensembles beat deep networks on medium-sized tabular datasets <Cite id="grinsztajn2022" />.
<NoteLink to="m4-and-m5-competitions" /> reports two forecasting competitions. M4 was won by a hybrid of exponential
smoothing and a recurrent network, while pure machine-learning entries lost to simple statistical benchmarks. M5 was won
by gradient-boosted trees <Cite id="makridakis2022" />.

**The conditions decide the winner.** In the vision study the transformer, which has fewer built-in assumptions about
images, won only with very large pre-training data. In the tabular study trees beat deep networks on medium-sized data,
and in M4 pure machine-learning entries lost to simple statistical benchmarks. Each result holds for its data size,
compute and protocol, not in general.

## How the notes fit together

The notes are independent, and each follows a fixed set of sections so that comparisons line up. The language-model
notes have their own overview. The vision and benchmark notes, two each, are listed directly in their subtopics. A
reader can start with any study whose techniques are already familiar.
