<Definition>
**Language model case studies** describe particular published <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss> and
compare them in pairs or families on fixed axes: architecture, training data, training procedure, compute and reported
results. Each note is a record of what one group built and measured, rather than an explanation of a technique.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The technique notes of Parts VI and VIII explain attention,
<NoteLink to="positional-encoding">positional encodings</NoteLink>, normalisation and mixture of experts one at a time.
A real model combines a dozen such choices with a dataset and a compute budget. The case studies show which combinations
were used, what changed between generations, and how much of the improvement came from scale rather than design. They
also give concrete numbers, such as parameter counts, token counts and context lengths, that the technique notes refer
to.

## Main ideas

**Encoder or decoder.** <NoteLink to="bert-vs-gpt" /> compares two models of 2018 with the same width and depth.
<Gloss name="gpt" /> is a decoder trained to predict the next token from the left. <Gloss name="bert" /> is an encoder
trained to predict masked tokens from both sides, which suits classification and extraction <Cite id="devlin2019" />.
The comparison shows how the pre-training objective, more than the size, decided what each model was good at.

**A stable design scaled up.** <NoteLink to="llama-family" /> follows one dense decoder-only design, with
<Gloss name="rmsnorm" />, <Gloss name="swiglu" /> feed-forward layers and rotary positions, through three generations.
Training data grew from 1.4 trillion to 15.6 trillion tokens, the context from 2K to 128K tokens and the vocabulary from
32K to 128K. The first LLaMA models were trained on far more tokens than the compute-optimal count for their size, which
makes them cheaper to run for a given quality <Cite id="touvron2023" /> <Cite id="hoffmann2022" />.

**Five years of changes in one comparison.** <NoteLink to="gpt-2-vs-deepseek-v3" /> sets a 1.5-billion-parameter model
of 2019 beside a 671-billion-parameter model of 2024 that activates 37 billion parameters per token. The differences are
attention with a compressed latent <Gloss name="kv-cache">key-value cache</Gloss>, mixture-of-experts feed-forward
layers and low-precision training. Each is an engineering answer to the cost of training or serving at that size.

## How the notes fit together

Read <NoteLink to="bert-vs-gpt" /> first, since it sets up the decoder-only design that the other two notes assume.
The LLaMA note shows that design refined and scaled. The GPT-2 and DeepSeek-V3 note spans the whole period and
introduces the sparse architectures of current large models. Each note follows the same sections, so the three can be
compared section by section.
