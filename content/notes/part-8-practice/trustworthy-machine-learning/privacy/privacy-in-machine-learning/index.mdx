<Definition>
**Privacy in machine learning** concerns what a trained model, or the process of training it, reveals about the
individual records it was trained on. The notes in this topic measure that leakage by attacks, bound it with
differential privacy, and reduce the need to collect data centrally with federated learning.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Models are trained on medical records, messages, photographs and browsing histories. A model's parameters and outputs
are functions of those records, so they can leak them. An attacker with only query access can sometimes tell whether a
given person's record was used in training <Cite id="shokri2017" />. Regulation and user trust both require a bound on
such leakage, and a way to train when the data cannot leave its owners.

## Main ideas

**Leakage can be measured by attack.** <NoteLink to="membership-inference" /> decides from a trained model whether a
record was in its training set. Overfitting drives the attack: a simple attack that thresholds the loss gains exactly
the <NoteLink to="generalisation-gap">generalisation gap</NoteLink>. The success rate of such attacks gives an empirical
measure of leakage.

**Differential privacy bounds the leakage.** <NoteLink to="differential-privacy" /> calls a randomised algorithm
$(\varepsilon, \delta)$-differentially private when changing one record changes the probability of any output by at
most a factor $e^\varepsilon$, plus $\delta$ <Cite id="dwork2014" />. Adding Laplace or Gaussian noise scaled to a
function's sensitivity achieves it. Post-processing cannot weaken the guarantee, and composition rules add up the cost
of repeated releases. A differentially private model also caps the advantage of any membership inference attack.

**Private training.** <NoteLink to="differentially-private-stochastic-gradient-descent" /> makes gradient training
differentially private. It clips each example's gradient to a fixed norm, adds Gaussian noise to the sum and tracks the
total privacy loss over all steps. Sampling each example with a small probability amplifies the privacy of each step.
The price is lower accuracy for a given amount of data.

**Keep the data where it is.** <NoteLink to="federated-learning" /> trains one model on data held by many clients,
such as phones or hospitals. In FedAvg each selected client runs a few epochs of local training from the server's model,
and the server averages the returned models <Cite id="kairouz2021" />. The raw data never leave the clients. Updates can
still leak information, so federated learning is often combined with differential privacy or secure aggregation.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="differential-privacy" />, which defines the guarantee the other notes use. Membership inference
shows the threat the guarantee addresses, and its bound requires the definition. DP-SGD applies the guarantee to
training. Federated learning addresses the separate problem of training without central
<NoteLink to="data-collection">data collection</NoteLink>, and connects back to differential privacy for its updates.
