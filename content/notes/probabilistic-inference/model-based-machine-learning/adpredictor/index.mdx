<Definition>
An ad impression is described by a sparse binary feature vector $\xvec \in \{0, 1\}^D$ with one active entry per
feature (the ad, the query, the position, the user and so on). **AdPredictor** models the click $y \in \{+1, -1\}$ as
$\pr(y \mid \xvec, \wvec) = \Phi\paren{y\, \wvec\transpose\xvec / \beta}$ and keeps a factorised Gaussian belief
$w_i \sim \Gauss(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2)$ over the weights. After each impression it replaces the belief of every active
weight by the Gaussian that matches the mean and variance of its exact posterior <Cite id="graepel2010adpredictor" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

In sponsored search the engine ranks ads by bid times predicted click-through rate (CTR), and charges each advertiser
according to the CTRs of the ads below it. The CTR estimate therefore sets both what users see and what advertisers
pay. It must be calibrated, not merely ranked correctly. It must learn from billions of impressions a day, handle
features with a few values (position) and features with billions (user identifier), and track a web that changes.
AdPredictor replaced Bing's previous CTR predictor in 2009 <Cite id="graepel2010adpredictor" />.

## Mechanism

### The factor graph

The model has four layers, one factor each <Cite id="graepel2010adpredictor" />:

1. **Prior.** $w_i \sim \Gauss(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2)$, independently for every weight.
2. **Score.** $s = \wvec\transpose\xvec$, the sum of the active weights.
3. **Noise.** $t \sim \Gauss(s, \beta^2)$.
4. **Threshold.** $y = +1$ if $t > 0$ and $y = -1$ otherwise.

Integrating out $t$ gives $\pr(y = +1 \mid s) = \pr(s + \epsilon > 0) = \Phi(s / \beta)$, the probit link. The
graph is the one of <NoteLink to="trueskill">TrueSkill</NoteLink> for a team of active weights playing a team of fixed
skill zero, and the updates are TrueSkill's updates <Cite id="graepel2010adpredictor" /> <Cite id="herbrich2006" />.

### Deriving the update

Before seeing $y$, the weights and $t$ are jointly Gaussian. With $\mu_s = \sum_{i \in \Acal} \mu_i$ over the <NoteLink to="karush-kuhn-tucker-conditions">active
set</NoteLink> $\Acal$, the noisy score has

$$
t \sim \Gauss(\mu_s, \Sigma^2), \qquad \Sigma^2 = \beta^2 + \sum_{i \in \Acal} \sigma_i^2,
$$

and each active weight has $\cov(w_i, t) = \sigma_i^2$, because $t$ contains $w_i$ once and the other terms are
independent of it.

**Step 1: condition $t$ on the click.** Observing $y$ truncates $t$ to the half-line $y t > 0$. Define the surprise
$z = y \mu_s / \Sigma$, small when the observed outcome was unexpected. A Gaussian truncated to one side has closed-form
moments:

$$
\expect[t \mid y] = \mu_s + y\, \Sigma\, v(z), \qquad \var[t \mid y] = \Sigma^2\, \LB 1 - w(z) \RB,
$$

with $v(z) = \phi(z) / \Phi(z)$ and $w(z) = v(z)\,(v(z) + z)$, where $\phi$ is the standard normal density.

**Step 2: carry the change back to each weight.** Given $t$, the click carries no further information about the
weights, and $w_i$ given $t$ is the regression of a Gaussian on a Gaussian:
$\expect[w_i \mid t] = \mu_i + (\sigma_i^2 / \Sigma^2)(t - \mu_s)$ and $\var[w_i \mid t] = \sigma_i^2 -
\sigma_i^4 / \Sigma^2$. Averaging over $t \mid y$ with the laws of total expectation and total variance gives

$$
\tilde\mu_i = \mu_i + y\, \frac{\sigma_i^2}{\Sigma}\, v(z), \qquad
\tilde\sigma_i^2 = \sigma_i^2 \LB 1 - \frac{\sigma_i^2}{\Sigma^2}\, w(z) \RB.
$$

These are the exact posterior mean and variance of each weight. The exact joint posterior is not Gaussian and not
factorised; AdPredictor keeps only these two moments per weight. Keeping the moments and dropping the correlations is
<NoteLink to="assumed-density-filtering">assumed density filtering</NoteLink>, one pass of <Gloss name="ep">expectation propagation</Gloss>
<Cite id="minka2001" />. Inactive weights are unchanged.

The update reads as follows:

- **Surprise sets the size.** $v(z)$ is near 0 when the click was expected and grows almost linearly in $-z$ when it was
  not.
- **Variance sets the share.** Each active weight moves in proportion to $\sigma_i^2 / \Sigma$. A well-known feature,
  with small variance, anchors the prediction and barely moves; a new feature absorbs most of the change. This is a
  per-feature <Gloss name="learning-rate">learning rate</Gloss> derived from the model rather than tuned.
- **Every observation reduces variance.** $0 < w(z) < 1$, so each variance shrinks, by more when the outcome was
  surprising and the weight was uncertain.

<Derivation title="Moments of a Gaussian truncated to one side">
Let $t \sim \Gauss(m, S^2)$ and condition on $t > 0$ (the case $y = +1$; $y = -1$ follows by $t \to -t$). Write
$t = m + S u$ with $u \sim \Gauss(0, 1)$, so the condition is $u > -m/S = -z$. The mass of the region is $\Phi(z)$. The
standard normal density satisfies $\phi'(u) = -u\,\phi(u)$, so

$$
\expect[u \mid u > -z] = \frac{1}{\Phi(z)}\int_{-z}^{\infty} u\,\phi(u)\,du = \frac{\phi(-z)}{\Phi(z)} = v(z).
$$

Integrating by parts, $\int_{-z}^\infty u^2 \phi(u)\,du = \brack{-u\,\phi(u)}_{-z}^{\infty} + \int_{-z}^\infty \phi(u)\,du =
-z\,\phi(z) + \Phi(z)$, so $\expect[u^2 \mid u > -z] = 1 - z\,v(z)$ and

$$
\var[u \mid u > -z] = 1 - z\,v(z) - v(z)^2 = 1 - w(z).
$$

Scaling back, $\expect[t \mid t > 0] = m + S\,v(z)$ and $\var[t \mid t > 0] = S^2\LB 1 - w(z) \RB$. The
<NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-truncated-gaussian">truncated Gaussian</NoteLink> note works the same update as
an EP site.
</Derivation>

### Prediction

The predictive probability integrates the probit over the Gaussian beliefs. The noisy score is Gaussian with mean
$\mu_s$ and variance $\Sigma^2$, so

$$
\pr(y = +1 \mid \xvec) = \Phi\paren{\frac{\mu_s}{\Sigma}} = \Phi\paren{\frac{\sum_{i \in \Acal}\mu_i}{\sqrt{\beta^2 + \sum_{i \in \Acal}\sigma_i^2}}}.
$$

Weight uncertainty enlarges the denominator and pulls the prediction towards 0.5. With zero variances the probit of the
data model returns.

### Dynamics

With a fixed model every variance shrinks towards zero and learning stops. The paper's first remedy, adding a small
variance to every weight per unit time as in a <NoteLink to="kalman-filter">Kalman filter</NoteLink>, lets a rarely seen weight grow wider than its prior; a
single surprising impression then moves it too far. The adopted remedy instead forgets old data gradually, so that a
weight with no new data returns to its prior <Cite id="graepel2010adpredictor" />. One way to write this is to
discount the evidence: raise the accumulated likelihood to the power $1 - \varepsilon$ per unit time. In <NoteLink to="exponential-family">natural
parameters</NoteLink> the posterior is then pulled towards the prior $\Gauss(\mu_{0}, \sigma_{0}^2)$:

$$
\frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{new}}^2} = \frac{1 - \varepsilon}{\sigma^2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma_0^2}, \qquad
\frac{\mu_{\text{new}}}{\sigma_{\text{new}}^2} = (1 - \varepsilon)\frac{\mu}{\sigma^2} + \varepsilon\frac{\mu_0}{\sigma_0^2}.
$$

The correction can be deferred and applied in one step the next time the weight is active.

### Web-scale engineering

- **Sparse storage.** Only weights whose belief differs from the prior are stored. A user identifier with billions of
  values costs memory only for users actually seen.
- **Pruning.** A weight is reset to its prior when predicting with the prior instead of its posterior would change the
  CTR of an impression activating it very little, measured by a KL divergence between the two Bernoulli predictions
  <Cite id="graepel2010adpredictor" />.
- **Parallel training.** Each machine runs the online update on its shard, starting from the shared belief, and sends
  back the message its data sent to each weight. The master multiplies the messages in. This is a message-passing
  schedule on a <NoteLink to="factor-graph">factor graph</NoteLink> with replicated weight variables.
- **Exploration.** The CTR estimate decides which ads are shown, so it decides its own future training data. Sampling
  weights from the posterior, instead of using the mean, raises uncertain ads in the ranking now and then. This is
  <NoteLink to="thompson-sampling">Thompson sampling</NoteLink> <Cite id="thompson1933" />.

## Cost

One update touches only the active weights: $O(|\Acal|)$ arithmetic, a handful of features per impression. Memory is
two numbers per stored weight. There is no learning rate to tune; the prior variance, $\beta$ and $\varepsilon$ play its
role.

## Trade-offs

- **Calibration by construction.** Before any recalibration, a <NoteLink to="naive-bayes">naive Bayes</NoteLink> baseline was off by orders of magnitude at
  small CTRs, while AdPredictor's predictions lay close to the diagonal of a calibration plot
  <Cite id="graepel2010adpredictor" />.
- **Factorised posterior.** Correlations between weights are dropped after every update. Two features that always appear
  together share the credit arbitrarily, and the order of the data matters. Several EP passes remove the order
  dependence but need the messages to be stored.
- **Linear in the features.** Interactions must be built as crossed features, such as ad × position. Later click models
  learn such interactions; see <NoteLink to="click-models">click models</NoteLink> for position effects.
- **Weights are not identifiable.** Adding a constant to every ad weight and subtracting it from a bias weight leaves all
  predictions unchanged. Only sums of weights along active sets are learned; the individual weights are set by the prior.
