<Definition>
Binary labels $y_i \in \set{-1, +1}$ with inputs $\xvec_i \in \reals^d$ follow the probit model
$p(y_i \mid \xvec_i, \wvec) = \Phi(y_i\,\xvec_i\transpose\wvec)$, where $\Phi$ is the standard normal cdf. The weights
have prior $\wvec \sim \Gauss(\zeros, s_0\eye)$. Approximate the posterior
$p(\wvec \mid \Dcal) \propto \Gauss(\wvec \mid \zeros, s_0\eye)\prod_{i=1}^n \Phi(y_i\,\xvec_i\transpose\wvec)$ by a
Gaussian with expectation propagation (EP): first for $d = 1$, $s_0 = 1$ and three points, then for $d = 2$, $s_0 = 4$
and seven points.
</Definition>

## Setup

The probit likelihood says that $y_i = +1$ when $\xvec_i\transpose\wvec + n_i > 0$, with latent noise
$n_i \sim \Gauss(0, 1)$. There is no bias term; the decision boundary $\xvec\transpose\wvec = 0$ passes through the
origin, which keeps the weight space two-dimensional in the second example.

- **Factors.** Each likelihood term $t_i(\wvec) = \Phi(y_i\,\xvec_i\transpose\wvec)$ depends on $\wvec$ only through the
  scalar projection $a_i = \xvec_i\transpose\wvec$.
- **Sites.** EP replaces $t_i$ by a Gaussian function of the same projection,
  $\tilde t_i(\wvec) = \exp\paren{-\tfrac12\tau_i a_i^2 + \nu_i a_i}$, with site precision $\tau_i$ and
  precision-weighted mean $\nu_i$.
- **Approximation.** The product of the prior and the sites is Gaussian, $q(\wvec) = \Gauss(\mvec, \Smat)$ with

$$
\Smat = \paren{s_0^{-1}\eye + \sum_{i=1}^n \tau_i\,\xvec_i\xvec_i\transpose}^{-1}, \qquad
\mvec = \Smat\sum_{i=1}^n \nu_i\,\xvec_i.
$$

## Solution

### Why each update is one-dimensional

Under $q$, the projection $a_i$ is Gaussian with mean $m_i = \xvec_i\transpose\mvec$ and variance
$s_i^2 = \xvec_i\transpose\Smat\xvec_i$. The site multiplies the density of $a_i$ by
$\exp(-\tfrac12\tau_i a_i^2 + \nu_i a_i)$ and leaves the <NoteLink to="conditional-probability">conditional distribution</NoteLink> of $\wvec$ given $a_i$ unchanged. So
the cavity $q^{\setminus i} \propto q/\tilde t_i$ has, for $a_i$, precision and precision-weighted mean

$$
\tau_{\setminus i} = \frac{1}{s_i^2} - \tau_i, \qquad \nu_{\setminus i} = \frac{m_i}{s_i^2} - \nu_i.
$$

The tilted distribution $q^{\setminus i}(\wvec)\,\Phi(y_i a_i)$ also changes only the marginal of $a_i$. Under the
cavity, $\wvec$ given $a_i$ is Gaussian with a mean linear in $a_i$ and a covariance that does not depend on $a_i$. The
mean and covariance of $\wvec$ under the tilted distribution are therefore determined by the mean and variance of
$a_i$ under it, and the Gaussian that matches them is the cavity times a Gaussian function of $a_i$. The full
$d$-dimensional moment match reduces to a one-dimensional one:

1. Cavity on $a_i$: $\Gauss(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with $\mu = \nu_{\setminus i}/\tau_{\setminus i}$ and
   $\sigma^2 = 1/\tau_{\setminus i}$.
2. Tilted moments of $\Gauss(a \mid \mu, \sigma^2)\,\Phi(y_i a)$, with $z = y_i\mu/\sqrt{1 + \sigma^2}$,
   $v = \phi(z)/\Phi(z)$ and $w = v(v + z)$:
   $\hat\mu = \mu + y_i\sigma^2 v/\sqrt{1 + \sigma^2}$ and $\hat\sigma^2 = \sigma^2 - \sigma^4 w/(1 + \sigma^2)$. These are
   derived in <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-truncated-gaussian">a Gaussian times a step function</NoteLink>.
3. New site: $\tau_i = 1/\hat\sigma^2 - \tau_{\setminus i}$ and $\nu_i = \hat\mu/\hat\sigma^2 - \nu_{\setminus i}$.
4. Recompute $\Smat$ and $\mvec$. A change $\delta\tau$ in one site is a rank-one update,
   $\Smat \leftarrow \Smat - \frac{\delta\tau\,\Smat\xvec_i\xvec_i\transpose\Smat}{1 + \delta\tau\,s_i^2}$, costing $O(d^2)$.

### One dimension, by hand

Take $d = 1$, prior $w \sim \Gauss(0, 1)$, inputs $x = (1, 2, -0.5)$ and all labels $+1$. The third point disagrees
with the first two: it favours negative $w$. In one dimension the site can be written on $w$ itself; a factor
$\Phi(y\,x\,w)$ with cavity $\Gauss(w \mid \mu, \sigma^2)$ has $z = y x\mu/\sqrt{1 + x^2\sigma^2}$, tilted mean
$\mu + y x\sigma^2 v/\sqrt{1 + x^2\sigma^2}$ and tilted variance $\sigma^2 - x^2\sigma^4 w/(1 + x^2\sigma^2)$.

| sweep | point | cavity mean | cavity var | $z$     | tilted mean | tilted var | site precision | site mean |
| ----- | ----- | ----------- | ---------- | ------- | ----------- | ---------- | -------------- | --------- |
| 1     | 1     | 0           | 1          | 0       | 0.5642      | 0.6817     | 0.4669         | 1.773     |
| 1     | 2     | 0.5642      | 0.6817     | 0.5845  | 0.8938      | 0.4370     | 0.8215         | 1.482     |
| 1     | 3     | 0.8938      | 0.4370     | −0.4243 | 0.6685      | 0.4060     | 0.1744         | −2.289    |
| 2     | 1     | 0.4102      | 0.5010     | 0.3348  | 0.6546      | 0.4078     | 0.4561         | 1.724     |
| 2     | 2     | 0.2375      | 0.6133     | 0.2557  | 0.6617      | 0.3618     | 1.1332         | 1.272     |
| 2     | 3     | 0.8604      | 0.3862     | −0.4108 | 0.6619      | 0.3618     | 0.1744         | −2.286    |

The first row is the single-factor update of <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation">expectation propagation</NoteLink>:
$\Gauss(1/\sqrt\pi, 1 - 1/\pi)$. The first sweep is <NoteLink to="assumed-density-filtering">assumed density
filtering</NoteLink>. In sweep 2 the cavity for point 2 is the prior times the sites of points 1 and 3,
$\Gauss(0.2375, 0.6133)$, much lower than the $\Gauss(0.5642, 0.6817)$ it met in sweep 1, because point 3 has pulled
$w$ down; point 2's site is refitted with precision $1.13$ instead of $0.82$. The site of point 3 has a mean of $-2.29$
and a small precision: a weak pull towards negative $w$.

EP converges to $\Gauss(0.6597, 0.3614)$ after a few sweeps. The exact posterior, integrated on a grid, has mean
$0.6608$ and variance $0.3674$.

The <NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">Laplace approximation</NoteLink> centres on the mode, where
$w = \sum_i y_i x_i\,v(y_i x_i w)$, and takes the variance from the curvature
$1 + \sum_i x_i^2\,w(y_i x_i w)$ there, using $\frac{d^2}{da^2}\log\Phi(a) = -w(a)$. It gives $\Gauss(0.5328, 0.3246)$.
The posterior is skewed towards large $w$: the likelihood $\Phi(x w)$ is flat for large positive $x w$ and falls off
steeply for negative values. The mode sits on the steep side, 0.13 below the mean, and Laplace inherits that error. EP
matches the mean to within 0.001.

### Two dimensions

Seven points with $s_0 = 4$: $(1, 0.5)$, $(2, 1.5)$ and $(0.5, 2)$ labelled $+1$; $(-1, -0.5)$, $(-1.5, 0.5)$,
$(-0.5, -2)$ and $(0.8, -0.3)$ labelled $-1$. The last point lies on the positive side of the boundary that the other six suggest. Computed
with numpy (the exact posterior on a $1201 \times 1201$ grid):

| method  | mean             | $\var(w_1)$ | $\cov(w_1, w_2)$ | $\var(w_2)$ | $p(y = +1 \mid (1, 1))$ |
| ------- | ---------------- | ----------- | ---------------- | ----------- | ----------------------- |
| exact   | $(1.140, 1.811)$ | 0.551       | 0.198            | 1.396       | 0.964                   |
| EP      | $(1.141, 1.819)$ | 0.510       | 0.084            | 1.147       | 0.961                   |
| Laplace | $(0.812, 1.154)$ | 0.442       | 0.110            | 1.293       | 0.874                   |

EP's mean is within 0.01 of the exact mean after three sweeps. Its covariance is too small along $w_2$: the posterior
has a long tail towards large $\wvec$, because the data are nearly separable and nothing stops the weights growing in
that direction except the prior. EP matches the moments of each tilted distribution, not of the posterior itself; for a
strongly skewed posterior the two differ, and here EP's variance along $w_2$ is 18 per cent too small.

The predictive probability averages the likelihood over the posterior. Under a Gaussian $\Gauss(\mvec, \Smat)$,
$\xvec\transpose\wvec + n$ is Gaussian with mean $\mvec\transpose\xvec$ and variance $1 + \xvec\transpose\Smat\xvec$, so

$$
p(y = +1 \mid \xvec, \Dcal) \approx \Phi\paren{\frac{\mvec\transpose\xvec}{\sqrt{1 + \xvec\transpose\Smat\xvec}}}.
$$

At $\xvec = (1, 1)$ EP gives $0.961$ against the exact $0.964$. Laplace gives $0.874$: its mean is too close to the
origin, so $\mvec\transpose\xvec = 1.97$ against EP's $2.96$. Plugging the Laplace mode into
the likelihood, $\Phi(\hat\wvec\transpose\xvec) = 0.975$, ignores the uncertainty altogether.

## What it illustrates

- **Sites on projections.** Whenever a factor depends on $\wvec$ only through $\xvec_i\transpose\wvec$, EP's update is a
  one-dimensional moment match plus a rank-one covariance update. The same structure gives
  <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-gaussian-process-classification">EP for Gaussian process classification</NoteLink>, where $\xvec_i$
  becomes a row of a kernel matrix.
- **Mean against mode.** For skewed posteriors, EP's mean is close to the posterior mean; the Laplace approximation is
  anchored at the mode. See <NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">the Laplace approximation</NoteLink>.
- **Predictive uncertainty.** The predictive probability shrinks towards $0.5$ away from the data, where
  $\xvec\transpose\Smat\xvec$ is large. Compare the plug-in prediction of
  <NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink> at the maximum-likelihood weights.

## Variations

- **Noise-free labels.** Replacing $\Phi(y_i\xvec_i\transpose\wvec)$ by the step $\indicator(y_i\xvec_i\transpose\wvec > 0)$
  makes the posterior uniform on the <NoteLink to="bayes-point-machine">version space</NoteLink> of separating weight vectors (times the prior). Its mean is the
  Bayes point, and EP with step sites is Minka's Bayes point machine <Cite id="minka2001thesis" />.
- **Logistic likelihood.** For $\sigma(y_i\xvec_i\transpose\wvec)$ the tilted moments have no closed form; each update
  needs a one-dimensional quadrature. Approximating $\sigma(a) \approx \Phi(\sqrt{\pi/8}\,a)$ gives closed-form
  moments at a small cost in accuracy <Cite id="bishop2006" />.
- **Separable data.** When the data are separable and the prior is broad, the posterior stretches far along the
  separating directions and the tilted variances of well-classified points become small. Updates can then overshoot,
  and damping the site parameters is the usual remedy; see
  <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-failure-modes">EP failure modes and fixes</NoteLink>.
