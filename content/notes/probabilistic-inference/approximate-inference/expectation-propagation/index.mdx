<Definition>
Let a posterior factorise as $p(\thetavec) \propto p_0(\thetavec)\prod_{i=1}^{n} t_i(\thetavec)$, with a prior $p_0$ in
an <NoteLink to="exponential-family">exponential family</NoteLink> $\Fcal$ and factors $t_i$, for example the likelihoods of single data points. Expectation
propagation (EP) approximates it by

$$
q(\thetavec) \propto p_0(\thetavec)\prod_{i=1}^{n} \tilde t_i(\thetavec),
$$

where each **site** $\tilde t_i$ is an unnormalised member of $\Fcal$. It refines one site at a time: remove it from
$q$, multiply in the true factor $t_i$, and project the result back onto $\Fcal$ by matching moments
<Cite id="minka2001" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

A posterior with a Gaussian prior and non-Gaussian likelihoods, such as a
<NoteLink to="gaussian-process-classification">Gaussian process classifier</NoteLink> or a probit regression, is not Gaussian, and
its moments are integrals with no closed form. Each single factor is tractable, however: the product of a
Gaussian and one probit factor has closed-form moments. EP builds a global Gaussian approximation from these local,
tractable computations.

## Moment matching

The projection step solves, for a target density $\hat p$,

$$
q^{\mathrm{new}} = \argmin_{q \in \Fcal} \KL\paren{\hat p \,\|\, q}.
$$

Write $q(\thetavec) = h(\thetavec)\exp\paren{\etavec^\top\tvec(\thetavec) - A(\etavec)}$ with <NoteLink to="sufficient-statistic">sufficient statistics</NoteLink>
$\tvec$ and log-normaliser $A$. As a function of the natural parameter $\etavec$,

$$
\KL(\hat p \,\|\, q) = \expect_{\hat p}[\log\hat p - \log h] - \etavec^\top\expect_{\hat p}[\tvec(\thetavec)] + A(\etavec).
$$

The <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> of the log-normaliser is the mean of the sufficient statistics, $\nabla A(\etavec) = \expect_q[\tvec]$.
Setting the gradient of the divergence to zero gives

$$
\expect_{q^{\mathrm{new}}}\brack{\tvec(\thetavec)} = \expect_{\hat p}\brack{\tvec(\thetavec)}.
$$

$A$ is convex, so this stationary point is the minimum. For a Gaussian family, $\tvec(\thetavec)$ collects $\thetavec$
and $\thetavec\thetavec^\top$, and the projection matches the mean and covariance. This is the forward <Gloss name="kl">KL divergence</Gloss>,
so the projection is mass-covering; see <NoteLink to="variational-inference">variational inference</NoteLink> for the
contrast with the reverse direction.

## Cavity and tilted distributions

The ideal site would make $q$ close to the true posterior $p$, for example by minimising $\KL(p \,\|\, q)$. That needs
the moments of $p$, which are the intractable integrals the approximation was meant to avoid. EP makes the problem
local. To refine site $i$, it keeps factor $t_i$ exact and replaces every other factor by its current site. The product
of the prior and the other sites is the **cavity** $q^{\setminus i}$, and the stand-in for the posterior is the
**tilted** distribution $\hat p_i \propto q^{\setminus i}\,t_i$. EP chooses the new site to minimise

$$
\KL\paren{q^{\setminus i}\,t_i \,\big\|\, q^{\setminus i}\,\tilde t_i}
$$

over sites $\tilde t_i$ in $\Fcal$. The cavity is a member of $\Fcal$, possibly unnormalised, and multiplying it by every
member of $\Fcal$ gives every member of $\Fcal$. So minimising over the site is the same as minimising
$\KL(\hat p_i \,\|\, q)$ over all $q \in \Fcal$, which is the moment match above. The site is then whatever turns the
cavity into the projection, $\tilde t_i = q^{\mathrm{new}}/q^{\setminus i}$.

Only the tilted moments are needed, and $\hat p_i$ differs from a member of $\Fcal$ by one factor. When $t_i$ depends on
$\thetavec$ through a scalar, such as $\xvec_i\transpose\thetavec$, those moments are one-dimensional integrals.

## Mechanism

<EpLoop />

Initialise every site to 1 (so $q = p_0$), or to a rough guess. Then repeat until the sites stop changing, visiting
each $i$ in turn:

1. **Cavity.** Remove site $i$: $q^{\setminus i}(\thetavec) \propto q(\thetavec)/\tilde t_i(\thetavec)$. In natural
   parameters this is a subtraction; for a Gaussian, precisions and precision-weighted means subtract.
2. **Tilted distribution.** Multiply in the true factor: $\hat p_i(\thetavec) \propto q^{\setminus i}(\thetavec)\, t_i(\thetavec)$.
3. **Project.** Set $q^{\mathrm{new}}$ to the member of $\Fcal$ with the moments of $\hat p_i$.
4. **Update the site.** $\tilde t_i \propto q^{\mathrm{new}}/q^{\setminus i}$, again a subtraction of natural
   parameters, so that $q = q^{\mathrm{new}}$.

For a Gaussian $\Fcal$ in natural parameters, with precision $\tau$ and precision-weighted mean $\nu = \tau\mu$, the
whole update is four subtractions and one moment computation:

$$
\tau_{\setminus i} = \tau - \tilde\tau_i, \quad \nu_{\setminus i} = \nu - \tilde\nu_i; \qquad
(\hat\mu, \hat\sigma^2) = \text{moments of } \hat p_i; \qquad
\tilde\tau_i = \frac{1}{\hat\sigma^2} - \tau_{\setminus i}, \quad \tilde\nu_i = \frac{\hat\mu}{\hat\sigma^2} - \nu_{\setminus i}.
$$

A site's precision $\tilde\tau_i$ can be negative. That is allowed: sites are only multiplied together, and only $q$ and
the cavities must be proper distributions.

### Fixed points

A fixed point is a set of sites that no update changes. At a fixed point, $q^{\mathrm{new}} = q$ for every $i$, so

$$
\expect_q\brack{\tvec(\thetavec)} = \expect_{\hat p_i}\brack{\tvec(\thetavec)} \quad \text{for every } i = 1, \dots, n.
$$

The approximation agrees on the expected sufficient statistics with each of the $n$ tilted distributions, each of which
treats one factor exactly. It is not required to agree with the true posterior, which treats all factors exactly at
once; with a single factor the two coincide, and EP matches the posterior's moments exactly. The order of the updates
does not appear in the condition, so the fixed points do not depend on it.

EP never minimises a global divergence. Its fixed points are stationary points of a constrained free energy that
approximates the log evidence and generalises the Bethe free energy of
<NoteLink to="loopy-belief-propagation">loopy belief propagation</NoteLink> <Cite id="minka2005" />. EP with a fully
factorised discrete $q$ on a graphical model is loopy belief propagation <Cite id="minka2001" />.

Plain iteration is not guaranteed to reach a fixed point; damping, power EP and double-loop algorithms are the
remedies, described in <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-failure-modes">EP failure modes and fixes</NoteLink>.

### Evidence

Give each site a scale chosen so that $\int q^{\setminus i}\,\tilde t_i\,d\thetavec$ equals the tilted normaliser
$Z_i = \int q^{\setminus i}\,t_i\,d\thetavec$: the site then reproduces the factor's effect on the normaliser as well as
on the moments. The integral of the prior times all scaled sites is a <NoteLink to="gaussian-integral">Gaussian integral</NoteLink> and approximates the evidence
$p(\text{data})$. It is used for hyperparameter learning, for example in Gaussian process classification
<Cite id="rasmussen2006" />.

### Assumed density filtering

<NoteLink to="assumed-density-filtering">Assumed density filtering</NoteLink> (ADF) is a single pass of the update with
every site starting at 1, so each factor is included once and never revisited <Cite id="minka2001thesis" />. Its result
depends on the order of the factors. EP's iteration refines early sites in the light of later ones and removes the
order dependence. The <NoteLink to="trueskill">TrueSkill</NoteLink> rating system runs ADF over a stream of games, and
<NoteLink to="trueskill-through-time">TrueSkill Through Time</NoteLink> runs full EP over the same history.

## Worked example: one probit factor

Take a prior $\theta \sim \Gauss(0, 1)$ and one factor $t(\theta) = \Phi(\theta)$, the probit likelihood of a positive
label, where $\Phi$ is the standard normal cdf. With one site, the cavity is the prior, $\Gauss(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with
$\mu = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = 1$.

The tilted distribution $\hat p(\theta) \propto \Gauss(\theta \mid \mu, \sigma^2)\,\Phi(\theta)$ has closed-form
moments. With $\kappa = \mu/\sqrt{1 + \sigma^2}$, its normaliser is $\Phi(\kappa)$, and

$$
\expect[\theta] = \mu + \frac{\sigma^2\, \phi(\kappa)}{\Phi(\kappa)\sqrt{1 + \sigma^2}}, \qquad
\var[\theta] = \sigma^2 - \frac{\sigma^4\, \phi(\kappa)}{(1 + \sigma^2)\,\Phi(\kappa)}\paren{\kappa + \frac{\phi(\kappa)}{\Phi(\kappa)}},
$$

where $\phi$ is the standard normal density <Cite id="rasmussen2006" />. Here $\kappa = 0$, $\Phi(0) = 1/2$ and
$\phi(0) = 1/\sqrt{2\pi}$:

- **Normaliser.** $\Phi(0) = 0.5$.
- **Mean.** $\frac{1/\sqrt{2\pi}}{0.5 \times \sqrt 2} = 1/\sqrt\pi = 0.564$.
- **Variance.** $1 - \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1/\sqrt{2\pi}}{0.5} \times \frac{1/\sqrt{2\pi}}{0.5} = 1 - 1/\pi = 0.682$.

So $q^{\mathrm{new}} = \Gauss(0.564, 0.682)$. The site is the ratio of $q^{\mathrm{new}}$ to the cavity. Its precision
is $1/0.682 - 1/1 = 0.467$, so its variance is $\pi - 1 = 2.14$. Its precision-weighted mean is $0.564/0.682 - 0 = 0.828$,
so its mean is $0.828/0.467 = \sqrt\pi = 1.77$. The probit factor is replaced by the Gaussian site
$\tilde t(\theta) \propto \Gauss(\theta \mid 1.77, 2.14)$. With several factors, each site is refined in turn against a
cavity that contains the other sites.

## Cost

For a Gaussian $q$ over $d$ dimensions, each site update costs a rank-one update of the covariance for scalar factors,
$O(d^2)$, and a full pass over $n$ factors $O(n d^2)$. For Gaussian process classification with $n$ training points
this gives $O(n^3)$ per pass, like the Laplace approximation, with a few passes needed.

## Trade-offs

- **Accuracy.** For Gaussian process classification EP gives more accurate marginal posteriors and evidence estimates
  than the <NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">Laplace approximation</NoteLink>, which fits the curvature at the mode
  <Cite id="rasmussen2006" />. Matching moments captures the skew of the posterior in the mean, where Laplace ignores it.
- **No convergence guarantee.** EP is not the optimisation of a single objective, and it can oscillate or diverge,
  typically when the posterior is multimodal. Damping, replacing each site by a weighted geometric average of its old
  and new values, usually fixes this; see <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-failure-modes">EP failure modes and
  fixes</NoteLink>.
- **Invalid sites.** A site can have negative precision. The approximation stays valid as long as $q$ and every cavity
  have positive precision; if a cavity does not, the update must be skipped or damped.
- **Tilted moments must be computable.** Each factor must allow the moments of $q^{\setminus i}\, t_i$, in closed form or
  by low-dimensional quadrature. This suits factors that depend on $\thetavec$ through a scalar, such as
  $t_i(\thetavec) = f(\xvec_i^\top\thetavec)$.
- **Distributed inference.** Sites are local, so data can be split across machines, each refining its own site against
  a shared global approximation <Cite id="vehtari2020" />.
