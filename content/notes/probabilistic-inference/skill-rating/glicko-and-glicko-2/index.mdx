<Definition>
**Glicko** gives each player a rating $r$ and a **rating deviation** RD, the mean and <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink> of a Gaussian
belief about the player's strength <Cite id="glickman1999" />. Games are grouped into rating periods. At the start of a
period RD grows to reflect possible change in strength. At the end, each player's $r$ and RD are updated from the
period's results by an approximate Bayesian update, in which opponents with uncertain ratings count for less.
**Glicko-2** adds a volatility $\sigma$, the standard deviation of the per-period change in strength, and updates it
from the results as well <Cite id="glickman2001" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

<NoteLink to="elo-rating">Elo</NoteLink> moves every rating by the same factor $K$. A fixed step is too small for a player whose rating is unreliable and too large for a reliable player who loses to an opponent whose rating is unreliable.
Glickman's example: two players rated 1700 meet, one returning after years away and one who plays every weekend. The
returner's rating is unreliable, so a win should raise it by more than Elo's step. The regular's rating is reliable, and
losing to someone whose rating means little should lower it by less <Cite id="glickman1999" />. Both effects need a
measure of how well each rating is known.

## Mechanism

Glicko uses the logistic model of Elo. Write $q = \ln 10/400 = 0.005756$, so that the probability that a player of
strength $\theta$ beats an opponent of strength $\theta_j$ is $\sigma\paren{q(\theta - \theta_j)}$, with
$\sigma(z) = 1/(1 + e^{-z})$.

### Uncertain opponents

The opponent's strength is known only as $\theta_j \sim \Gauss(r_j, \mathrm{RD}_j^2)$. The expected score must average
over it. Approximate the logistic curve by the Gaussian cdf with the same variance: the standard logistic distribution
has variance $\pi^2/3$, so $\sigma(z) \approx \Phi\paren{z\sqrt 3/\pi}$. For a Gaussian argument
$z = q(\theta - \theta_j)$ with variance $q^2\mathrm{RD}_j^2$, the average of $\Phi$ is again a $\Phi$ with the variance
added to the denominator. Converting back to the logistic form gives

$$
E_j = \sigma\paren{g(\mathrm{RD}_j)\, q\,(r - r_j)}, \qquad g(\mathrm{RD}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 3q^2\mathrm{RD}^2/\pi^2}} .
$$

The factor $g \le 1$ flattens the expected score towards $\tfrac12$ against an opponent whose rating is uncertain. Such
games carry less information and move ratings less.

### The update as one Newton step

Treat the player's strength $\theta$ as uncertain too, with prior $\Gauss(r, \mathrm{RD}^2)$. After games against
opponents $j = 1, \dots, m$ with scores $s_j \in \set{0, \tfrac12, 1}$, the log-posterior is

$$
\log p(\theta \mid s) = -\frac{(\theta - r)^2}{2\,\mathrm{RD}^2} + \sum_j \brack{s_j \log E_j(\theta) + (1 - s_j)\log\paren{1 - E_j(\theta)}} + \text{const},
$$

with $E_j(\theta) = \sigma\paren{g_j q(\theta - r_j)}$ and $g_j = g(\mathrm{RD}_j)$. Replace the log-likelihood by its
<NoteLink to="hessian">second-order Taylor expansion</NoteLink> at $\theta = r$. Its <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> there is $\sum_j q g_j (s_j - E_j)$, and its negative second
<NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> is

$$
\frac{1}{d^2} = q^2 \sum_j g_j^2 E_j (1 - E_j).
$$

The posterior is then Gaussian, with precision equal to the prior precision plus $1/d^2$, and mean one Newton step from
$r$:

$$
\mathrm{RD}' = \paren{\frac{1}{\mathrm{RD}^2} + \frac{1}{d^2}}^{-1/2}, \qquad
r' = r + \frac{q}{1/\mathrm{RD}^2 + 1/d^2} \sum_j g_j (s_j - E_j).
$$

These are Glicko's update equations <Cite id="glickman1999" />. The quantity $d^2$ is the variance of the rating implied
by the period's games alone. The step size $q\,\mathrm{RD}'^2$ replaces Elo's $K$: it is large for an uncertain player and
small for a well-measured one. With RD held fixed and $g_j = 1$, the update is Elo's with
$K = q\,\mathrm{RD}'^2$. The <NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">Laplace approximation</NoteLink> expands at the
posterior mode; Glicko expands once at the prior mean and stops after one Newton step.

### Growth between periods

Strength drifts between periods. Glicko models this as a Gaussian <NoteLink to="random-walk">random walk</NoteLink>, so the variance grows linearly with time:
before a period, $\mathrm{RD} \leftarrow \min\paren{\sqrt{\mathrm{RD}^2 + c^2 t}, 350}$, where $t$ is the number of
periods since the player's last game and 350 is the deviation of a new player <Cite id="glickman1999" />. The constant
$c$ sets how fast a returning player's rating becomes uncertain again. The predict-then-update cycle is the same as in
the <NoteLink to="kalman-filter">Kalman filter</NoteLink>.

A player's strength is often reported as the interval $r \pm 2\,\mathrm{RD}$, about a 95% <NoteLink to="credible-intervals-and-highest-posterior-density">credible interval</NoteLink>.

## Glicko-2: volatility

A fixed $c$ assumes every player's strength drifts at the same rate. Glicko-2 gives each player a volatility $\sigma$,
the standard deviation of the change in strength per period, and lets the volatility itself follow a random walk on the
log scale with step standard deviation $\tau$ <Cite id="glickman2001" />. The system constant $\tau$, typically between
0.3 and 1.2, limits how fast volatility can change <Cite id="glickman2022" />.

Glicko-2 works on a rescaled axis, $\mu = (r - 1500)/173.7178$ and $\phi = \mathrm{RD}/173.7178$, so that the model is
$\sigma(\mu - \mu_j)$ with natural logarithms. The update has four steps.

1. **Evidence from the period.** As in Glicko, compute $E_j$ with $g(\phi_j) = 1/\sqrt{1 + 3\phi_j^2/\pi^2}$, the
   variance from the games alone $v = \paren{\sum_j g(\phi_j)^2 E_j(1 - E_j)}^{-1}$, and the estimated improvement
   $\Delta = v\sum_j g(\phi_j)(s_j - E_j)$.
2. **Volatility.** Choose the new volatility as the mode of an approximate posterior. Let $x = \log\sigma'^2$, with a
   Gaussian prior centred at $a = \log\sigma^2$ with variance $\tau^2$. Given $x$, the observed improvement $\Delta$ is
   treated as Gaussian with mean 0 and variance $S(x) = \phi^2 + v + e^x$: the prior deviation, the noise of the
   period's estimate, and the drift. The log-posterior of $x$ is
   $-\tfrac12\log S(x) - \Delta^2/\paren{2S(x)} - (x - a)^2/(2\tau^2)$. Setting its derivative to zero gives
   $$
   f(x) = \frac{e^x\paren{\Delta^2 - \phi^2 - v - e^x}}{2\paren{\phi^2 + v + e^x}^2} - \frac{x - a}{\tau^2} = 0 ,
   $$
   which is solved by a one-dimensional root finder <Cite id="glickman2022" />. Volatility rises when $\Delta^2$ exceeds
   the variance the model expected, that is, when results surprise.
3. **Drift.** Widen the belief by the new volatility: $\phi^* = \sqrt{\phi^2 + \sigma'^2}$.
4. **Update.** $\phi' = \paren{1/\phi^{*2} + 1/v}^{-1/2}$ and $\mu' = \mu + \phi'^2\sum_j g(\phi_j)(s_j - E_j)$.

Convert back with $r' = 173.7178\,\mu' + 1500$ and $\mathrm{RD}' = 173.7178\,\phi'$.

## Worked example

This is Glickman's own example <Cite id="glickman2022" />. A player rated $r = 1500$ with $\mathrm{RD} = 200$ plays three
games in one period:

| opponent | $r_j$ | $\mathrm{RD}_j$ | result | $g(\mathrm{RD}_j)$ | $E_j$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1400 | 30 | win | 0.9955 | 0.639 |
| 2 | 1550 | 100 | loss | 0.9531 | 0.432 |
| 3 | 1700 | 300 | loss | 0.7242 | 0.303 |

**Glicko.** $1/d^2 = q^2 \sum_j g_j^2 E_j(1 - E_j)$ gives $d = 231.7$. The new deviation is
$(1/200^2 + 1/231.7^2)^{-1/2} = 151.4$, and the new rating is $r' = 1464.1$. The win over a well-measured weaker player
counts almost fully; the loss to the very uncertain 1700 player counts for less, because $g_3 = 0.72$.

**Glicko-2**, with volatility $\sigma = 0.06$ and $\tau = 0.5$. On the rescaled axis, $v = 1.779$ and
$\Delta = -0.484$. The root of $f$ gives $\sigma' = 0.05999$, almost unchanged, because $\Delta^2 = 0.234$ is well below
$\phi^2 + v = 3.11$: the results were not surprising. Then $r' = 1464.05$ and $\mathrm{RD}' = 151.52$.

All values were recomputed with numpy and scipy and agree with Glickman's to the digits shown.

## Cost

An update costs $O(m)$ for $m$ games in the period, plus a short <NoteLink to="root-finding">root-finding</NoteLink> loop for Glicko-2. The state is two numbers
per player for Glicko and three for Glicko-2.

## Trade-offs

- **Rating periods.** Games within a period are treated as simultaneous. Glickman recommends periods with at least
  10 to 15 games per player for Glicko-2 <Cite id="glickman2022" />. Online games, with updates after every game, fit the
  design poorly.
- **Two players only.** Like Elo, Glicko has no model of teams or of rankings of more than two players.
  <NoteLink to="trueskill">TrueSkill</NoteLink> adds both, with a Gaussian model in place of the logistic one.
- **Approximations.** The Gaussian approximation to the logistic-normal average, and the single Newton step, are
  accurate when the period's games are not too surprising. A very unlikely set of results can move the rating by an
  unreasonable amount; a small $\tau$ limits the damage in Glicko-2.
- **Not zero-sum.** Each player's change is scaled by their own deviation, so points are not conserved. This removes the
  rating-pool effects of Elo but lets the average rating drift.
