<Definition>
Let $\Smat \in \reals^{m \times d}$ hold the $m$ decoder states entering a cross-attention sublayer and
$\Hmat \in \reals^{n \times d}$ the $n$ vectors output by the encoder. Cross-attention computes
$$
\operatorname{CrossAttn}(\Smat, \Hmat) =
\operatorname{softmax}\paren{\frac{\Smat \Wmat_Q (\Hmat \Wmat_K)^{\tr}}{\sqrt{d_k}}} \Hmat \Wmat_V \in \reals^{m \times d_v},
$$
with learned $\Wmat_Q, \Wmat_K \in \reals^{d \times d_k}$ and $\Wmat_V \in \reals^{d \times d_v}$. The transformer uses a
multi-head version in the middle sublayer of every decoder block <Cite id="vaswani2017" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A translation decoder writing the $t$-th target word needs to know which source words it corresponds to. Cross-attention
answers this with a soft lookup. The decoder state at position $t$ forms a query. Each source position offers a key
describing it and a value carrying its content. The output is the average of the source values, weighted by how well
each key matches the query.

The same idea drives <NoteLink to="bahdanau-attention">additive attention</NoteLink> in recurrent encoder–decoders, where
the weights act as a soft alignment between target and source words <Cite id="bahdanau2015" />. The transformer
replaces the scoring network with a scaled dot product and applies the lookup in every decoder layer.

## Details

**Shapes.** The score matrix $\Smat \Wmat_Q (\Hmat \Wmat_K)^{\tr}$ is $m \times n$: one row per target position, one
column per source position. Self-attention always has a square score matrix; cross-attention has a rectangular one,
since source and target lengths differ.

**No causal mask.** The whole source sentence is known before decoding starts, so every target position may read every
source position. Only padding in the source batch is masked. Causality of the decoder is enforced by the
<NoteLink to="causal-masking">causal mask</NoteLink> in the decoder's self-attention sublayer, which comes first in each
block.

**Every layer reads the same encoder output.** All $N$ decoder blocks attend to the final encoder output $\Hmat$, each
with its own projections $\Wmat_K^{(l)}, \Wmat_V^{(l)}$. The encoder runs once per source sentence.

**Order of the source.** Permuting the rows of $\Hmat$ permutes the columns of the score matrix and the rows of
$\Hmat \Wmat_V$ in the same way, so the output is unchanged. Cross-attention treats the source as a set. Source order
reaches the decoder only through the positional encodings added before the encoder, which make the rows of $\Hmat$
differ by position.

**Caching during generation.** The source keys $\Hmat \Wmat_K^{(l)}$ and values $\Hmat \Wmat_V^{(l)}$ do not change
while the target grows. They are computed once and reused for every generated token. The cache holds $2 N n d$ numbers,
with $d = h\, d_k$ summed over heads. Each new token then costs $O(n d)$ per layer for its cross-attention. This cache is
separate from the growing self-attention <NoteLink to="key-value-cache">KV cache</NoteLink>.

## Worked example

Take $d_k = d_v = 2$, identity projections, two decoder queries and three source positions:

$$
\Qmat = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad
\Kmat = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad
\Vmat = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

The scaled scores $\Qmat \Kmat^{\tr} / \sqrt{2}$ form a $2 \times 3$ matrix:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} 1.414 & 0 & 0.707 \\ 0 & 1.414 & 0.707 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

Row 1 has softmax $(0.576, 0.140, 0.284)$. The first query matches source position 1 best, position 3 partly, and
position 2 least. Row 2 is the mirror image, $(0.140, 0.576, 0.284)$. The outputs are
$0.576\,(1, 0) + 0.140\,(0, 1) + 0.284\,(1, 1) = (0.860, 0.424)$ and, by symmetry, $(0.424, 0.860)$.

For the base transformer ($N = 6$, $d = 512$), the cross-attention cache holds $2 \times 6 \times 512 = 6144$ numbers per
source token. A 50-token source sentence needs $307{,}200$ numbers, $0.61$ MB in 16-bit floats.

## Uses

- **Translation and summarisation.** The original transformer and later encoder–decoder models such as T5 use
  cross-attention in every decoder block; see <NoteLink to="encoder-decoder-transformers">encoder–decoder
  transformers</NoteLink>.
- **Conditioning on another modality.** Text-to-image diffusion models let image features attend to a text encoding
  through cross-attention; see <NoteLink to="latent-diffusion">latent diffusion</NoteLink>.

## Assumptions

- The source is fully available before generation. Streaming translation, where the source arrives while the target is
  written, needs extra masking of source positions not yet seen.
- One encoder output is rich enough for all decoder layers. Each layer can only re-project $\Hmat$, not ask the encoder
  for different features.

## Pitfalls

- **Source padding.** Padding positions in a batch must be masked in the cross-attention scores. Otherwise the decoder
  attends to padding vectors.
- **Attention weights as alignments.** Cross-attention weights often look like word alignments, but a layer can route
  information through positions with low weight and high-norm values. The weights alone do not prove which source word
  caused an output.
- **Decoder-only models have none.** A decoder-only model places the source in the prompt and reads it with causal
  self-attention. There is no separate cross-attention sublayer and no separate source cache.
