<Definition>
In a causal attention layer with $H$ heads, ALiBi computes the logit of query position $i$ and key position $j \le i$ in
head $h \in \set{1, \dots, H}$ as
$$
e_{ij} = \frac{\qvec_i^{\tr} \kvec_j}{\sqrt{d_k}} - m_h\, (i - j), \qquad m_h = 2^{-8h/H}.
$$
The slopes are fixed before training and never learned. <Gloss name="nope">No positional encoding</Gloss> is added
anywhere else in the network <Cite id="press2022" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

A <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss> trained on sequences of length $L$ should ideally work on longer
inputs at inference. Press et al. found that sinusoidal models have very limited ability to do so, that
<NoteLink to="rotary-position-embedding">rotary embeddings</NoteLink> do somewhat better, and that the
<NoteLink to="t5-relative-position-bias">T5 bias</NoteLink> does better still but is considerably slower
<Cite id="press2022" />. Training on longer sequences instead is expensive, because attention cost grows with the square
of the length.

## Mechanism

**A penalty proportional to distance.** Each head lowers the logit of a key by $m_h$ for every position it lies behind
the query. The bias for query $i$ is the row $m_h \cdot (-(i-1), \dots, -2, -1, 0)$ over keys $1, \dots, i$.

**Multiplicative decay.** Exponentiating shows what the bias does to the weights:

$$
\alpha_{ij} \propto \exp\paren{\frac{\qvec_i^{\tr} \kvec_j}{\sqrt{d_k}}} \cdot e^{-m_h (i - j)}.
$$

Each head multiplies the content weight by an <NoteLink to="first-order-linear-odes">exponential decay</NoteLink> in
distance, with half-life $\ln 2 / m_h$ tokens. A key $\ln 2 / m_h$ positions back needs a content score higher by
$\ln 2$ to receive the same weight as the adjacent key.

**Slopes.** For $H$ heads the slopes are the geometric sequence that starts at $2^{-8/H}$ with ratio $2^{-8/H}$. With 8
heads they are $\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{4}, \dots, \tfrac{1}{256}$. With 16 heads they are
$2^{-0.5}, 2^{-1}, \dots, 2^{-8}$. The half-lives for 8 heads run from $2 \ln 2 = 1.4$ tokens to $256 \ln 2 = 177$
tokens, so some heads are local and others look far back. Press et al. tried learned slopes; they extrapolated worse and
slowed training by 3% <Cite id="press2022" />.

**Why it extrapolates.** A <NoteLink to="positional-encoding">position embedding</NoteLink> at an unseen position is an
input the model has never seen. An ALiBi bias at an unseen distance is just a larger penalty of the same form, and it
drives the weight of far keys towards 0. Beyond a head's effective range, extra keys contribute almost nothing, so the
model behaves much as it did on training lengths.

**Relative by construction.** The bias depends only on $i - j$, so ALiBi is a relative scheme. The bias matrix is a
Toeplitz matrix: constant along each diagonal.

## Evidence

A 1.3-billion-parameter model trained with ALiBi on 1024-token inputs matched, on 2048-token inputs, the perplexity of
a sinusoidal model trained on 2048 tokens. It trained 11% faster and used 11% less memory <Cite id="press2022" />. On
WikiText-103, ALiBi models kept improving perplexity for up to 200 tokens beyond a training length of 512, and did not
degrade beyond that.

At fixed length, Haviv et al. measured validation perplexity on the Pile for 1.3-billion-parameter models with
1024-token inputs: 12.51 for ALiBi, 12.93 for sinusoids, 13.05 for learned embeddings and 13.10 for no position
information <Cite id="haviv2022" />.

## Worked example

Take $H = 8$ heads. Head 1 has $m_1 = 2^{-1} = 0.5$ and head 8 has $m_8 = 2^{-8} = 0.0039$.

A query at position $i = 4$ attends to keys $j = 4, 3, 2, 1$, at distances $0, 1, 2, 3$. Suppose all four content
scores are equal. In head 1 the logits are $0, -0.5, -1.0, -1.5$ relative to the nearest key, and the softmax gives
weights $0.455, 0.276, 0.167, 0.102$. In head 8 the logits differ by at most $3 \times 0.0039 = 0.012$, so the weights
are almost uniform, about $0.25$ each.

At distance 1000, head 1 multiplies the content weight by $e^{-500}$, effectively zero, while head 8 multiplies it by
$e^{-3.9} = 0.02$. Head 8 can still reach a key that far back if its content score is about 4 higher than that of nearby
keys.

## Cost

The bias is a closed-form function of $i - j$ and the head, so it adds one subtraction per logit and no parameters. It
can be generated inside an attention kernel instead of being stored. Press et al. measured a memory increase of 0 to
0.7% over the sinusoidal model at the same length <Cite id="press2022" />.

## Trade-offs

- **Recency bias.** Every head prefers nearby tokens. Tasks that need a distant token to dominate must overcome the
  penalty with content. Kazemnejad et al. found ALiBi generalised poorly to longer inputs on synthetic reasoning tasks,
  behind the T5 bias and no encoding at all <Cite id="kazemnejad2023" />.
- **Extrapolation without use.** Perplexity stays flat beyond the training length partly because far tokens are ignored.
  A flat perplexity curve does not show that the model uses the extra context.
- **Causal form.** The published form is defined for causal attention. Bidirectional variants must choose how to treat
  keys after the query, for example with $\abs{i - j}$.
