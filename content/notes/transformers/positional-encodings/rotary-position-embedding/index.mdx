<Definition>
Split a query or key $\xvec \in \reals^{d}$ into $d/2$ pairs $(x_{2i}, x_{2i+1})$. Rotary position embedding at position
$m$ rotates pair $i$ by the angle $m\theta_i$:
$$
\begin{bmatrix} \tilde x_{2i} \\ \tilde x_{2i+1} \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos m\theta_i & -\sin m\theta_i \\ \sin m\theta_i & \cos m\theta_i \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} x_{2i} \\ x_{2i+1} \end{bmatrix},
\qquad \theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d}.
$$
It is applied to the queries and keys of every attention layer, after their projections and before the dot product.
Values are not rotated <Cite id="su2021" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A <NoteLink to="sinusoidal-positional-encoding">sinusoidal encoding</NoteLink> places a clock on every position: pair
$i$ of $\pvec_t$ points at angle $\omega_i t$. RoPE uses the same clock but, instead of adding its hands to the token
vector, turns the query and the key themselves. The dot product of two vectors depends only on their lengths and the
angle between them. Turning the query by $m\theta_i$ and the key by $n\theta_i$ changes the angle between them by
$(n - m)\theta_i$, whatever $m$ and $n$ are. The score therefore sees how far apart the two tokens are, not where they
are.

## Derivation

Su et al. ask for position-dependent maps $f_q, f_k$ such that the score depends on the positions only through their
difference: $f_q(\qvec, m)^{\tr} f_k(\kvec, n) = g(\qvec, \kvec, n - m)$ for some function $g$ <Cite id="su2021" />.

**Two dimensions.** Identify $(a, b) \in \reals^2$ with the complex number $a + \mathrm{i} b$. Then the dot product of
two vectors $u, v \in \reals^2$ is $\operatorname{Re}(u \bar v)$: $(a + \mathrm{i}b)(c - \mathrm{i}d)$ has real part
$ac + bd$. Rotation by an angle $\phi$ is multiplication by $e^{\mathrm{i}\phi}$. Set
$f_q(q, m) = q\, e^{\mathrm{i} m\theta}$ and $f_k(k, n) = k\, e^{\mathrm{i} n\theta}$. Then

$$
f_q(q, m)^{\tr} f_k(k, n)
= \operatorname{Re}\brack{q\, e^{\mathrm{i} m\theta}\ \overline{k\, e^{\mathrm{i} n\theta}}}
= \operatorname{Re}\brack{q \bar k\, e^{\mathrm{i}(m - n)\theta}}.
$$

The positions appear only through $m - n$. In matrix form, with $\Rmat_\phi$ the rotation by $\phi$,
$(\Rmat_{m\theta} \qvec)^{\tr} (\Rmat_{n\theta} \kvec) = \qvec^{\tr} \Rmat_{m\theta}^{\tr} \Rmat_{n\theta} \kvec =
\qvec^{\tr} \Rmat_{(n-m)\theta} \kvec$, because $\Rmat_\phi^{\tr} = \Rmat_{-\phi}$ and rotations of the plane add their
angles.

**Any even width.** Apply a different frequency $\theta_i$ to each pair. The full rotation
$\Rmat_m = \diag(\Rmat_{m\theta_0}, \dots, \Rmat_{m\theta_{d/2-1}})$ is block-diagonal and orthogonal, and the argument
holds block by block:

$$
(\Rmat_m \qvec)^{\tr} (\Rmat_n \kvec) = \qvec^{\tr} \Rmat_{n-m} \kvec
= \sum_{i=0}^{d/2-1} \operatorname{Re}\brack{q^{(i)} \overline{k^{(i)}}\, e^{\mathrm{i}(m-n)\theta_i}},
$$

where $q^{(i)} = q_{2i} + \mathrm{i} q_{2i+1}$ and likewise for $k^{(i)}$. With a projected query
$\qvec_m = \Wmat_Q^{\tr} \xvec_m$, the score becomes $\xvec_m^{\tr} \Wmat_Q \Rmat_{n-m} \Wmat_K^{\tr} \xvec_n$: content
through $\Wmat_Q, \Wmat_K$, position through $\Rmat_{n-m}$ alone.

**What RoPE does not change.** Rotations preserve length, so $\norm{\Rmat_m \qvec} = \norm{\qvec}$. The values are
untouched, so position shapes the attention weights but not the vectors being averaged. There are no learned
parameters.

## Implementation

The rotation needs no matrix. For each pair, $\tilde x_{2i} = x_{2i} \cos m\theta_i - x_{2i+1} \sin m\theta_i$ and
$\tilde x_{2i+1} = x_{2i+1} \cos m\theta_i + x_{2i} \sin m\theta_i$. Written for the whole vector,

$$
\tilde\xvec = \xvec \odot \cvec_m + \xvec' \odot \svec_m, \qquad
\xvec' = (-x_1, x_0, -x_3, x_2, \dots),
$$

where $\cvec_m$ and $\svec_m$ hold $\cos m\theta_i$ and $\sin m\theta_i$, each repeated for both members of pair $i$. The
cost is $O(d)$ per query or key, negligible beside the $O(d^2)$ projections. The tables of $\cos$ and $\sin$ are
computed once per position.

**Partial rotary.** Some models rotate only a fraction of each head's dimensions and leave the rest unrotated.
GPT-NeoX-20B rotates the first 25% <Cite id="black2022" />. The unrotated dimensions give a head a content-only score
component that does not change with distance.

## Long-term decay

Su et al. show that the rotary score tends to shrink as the distance grows <Cite id="su2021" />. Write
$h_i = q^{(i)} \overline{k^{(i)}}$ and $S_j = \sum_{i=0}^{j-1} e^{\mathrm{i}(m-n)\theta_i}$, with $S_0 = 0$ and
$h_{d/2} = 0$. Since $e^{\mathrm{i}(m-n)\theta_i} = S_{i+1} - S_i$, summation by parts (Abel summation) gives

$$
\sum_{i=0}^{d/2-1} h_i\, e^{\mathrm{i}(m-n)\theta_i} = \sum_{i=0}^{d/2-1} h_i (S_{i+1} - S_i)
= -\sum_{i=0}^{d/2-1} S_{i+1} (h_{i+1} - h_i).
$$

Taking absolute values,

$$
\abs{\sum_{i} h_i\, e^{\mathrm{i}(m-n)\theta_i}} \le \paren{\max_i \abs{h_{i+1} - h_i}} \sum_{j=1}^{d/2} \abs{S_j}.
$$

The first factor depends on the content. The second depends only on the distance $m - n$. At distance 0 every term of
$S_j$ is 1, so $\abs{S_j} = j$. At large distances the phases $(m - n)\theta_i$ spread around the circle and partly
cancel, so $\abs{S_j}$ is smaller. For $d = 128$ and base 10,000, the mean of $\abs{S_j}$ is $32.5$ at distance 0,
$17.9$ at 10, $10.2$ at 100 and $4.5$ at 1000. The decay is a bound, not a law: particular queries and keys can still
score highly at long range.

## The base

The frequencies $\theta_i = b^{-2i/d}$ run from 1 radian per token down to $b^{-(d-2)/d}$. The slowest pair has wavelength
$2\pi b^{(d-2)/d}$: about 54,000 tokens for $b = 10{,}000$ and $d = 128$. RoFormer and Llama 2 use $b = 10{,}000$
<Cite id="su2021" /> <Cite id="touvron2023" />. Llama 3 uses $b = 500{,}000$ to support longer contexts
<Cite id="grattafiori2024" />.

A trained model has seen only the angles $m\theta_i$ for $m$ below its training length. Beyond that, the slow pairs reach
angles never seen in training, and quality falls. Rescaling positions or frequencies so that no angle leaves the
trained range is the basis of position interpolation, NTK-aware scaling and YaRN; see
<NoteLink to="long-context-methods">long-context methods</NoteLink>.

## Worked example

Take $d = 4$, so $\theta_0 = 1$ and $\theta_1 = 10000^{-1/2} = 0.01$. Let $\qvec = (1, 0, 1, 0)$ and $\kvec = (1, 0, 0, 1)$.

Place the query at $m = 2$ and the key at $n = 5$. Rotating gives $\tilde\qvec = (-0.416, 0.909, 1.000, 0.020)$ and
$\tilde\kvec = (0.284, -0.959, -0.050, 0.999)$, and $\tilde\qvec^{\tr}\tilde\kvec = -1.020$.

Move both by 8, to $m = 10$ and $n = 13$. The rotated vectors are different:
$\tilde\qvec = (-0.839, -0.544, 0.995, 0.100)$ and $\tilde\kvec = (0.907, 0.420, -0.130, 0.992)$. The score is again
$-1.020$.

The formula $\qvec^{\tr} \Rmat_{n-m} \kvec$ predicts it. Pair 0 contributes $(1, 0) \cdot \Rmat_{3}(1, 0) = \cos 3 =
-0.990$. Pair 1 contributes $(1, 0) \cdot \Rmat_{0.03}(0, 1) = -\sin 0.03 = -0.030$. The sum is $-1.020$.

Swapping the roles, query at 5 and key at 2, gives $\cos(-3) + \sin 0.03 = -0.960$. The score depends on the signed
offset, so RoPE distinguishes earlier from later tokens.

## Assumptions

- Positions are integers on a line. Images and video need several position coordinates; see
  <NoteLink to="rotary-position-embedding-variants">xPos and RoPE variants</NoteLink>.
- The relevant relation between two tokens depends on their offset.

## Pitfalls

- **Pairing convention.** Implementations pair dimensions either as $(2i, 2i+1)$ or as $(i, i + d/2)$. Both are valid
  rotary embeddings, but weights trained under one convention give wrong outputs under the other.
- **Changing the base changes the model.** Every score depends on $\theta_i$. Changing $b$ in a trained model needs
  fine-tuning.
- **Length generalisation is limited.** Press et al. found rotary models degrade beyond their training length, and
  Kazemnejad et al. found them to generalise to longer inputs little better than absolute embeddings
  <Cite id="press2022" /> <Cite id="kazemnejad2023" />.
- **Rotate before caching.** In a <NoteLink to="key-value-cache">KV cache</NoteLink>, each key is stored after rotation by its
  own position. It stays valid as new tokens arrive, because its angle depends only on its own position.
- **Low-rank key compression.** A rotation depends on position, so it cannot be absorbed into a fixed <NoteLink to="orthogonal-projection">projection matrix</NoteLink>.
  <NoteLink to="multi-head-latent-attention">Multi-head latent attention</NoteLink> works around this by carrying RoPE on
  a separate small key.
