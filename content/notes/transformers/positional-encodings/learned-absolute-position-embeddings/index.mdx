<Definition>
A learned absolute <NoteLink to="positional-encoding">position embedding</NoteLink> is a matrix $\Pmat \in \reals^{T_{\max} \times d}$ trained with the rest of the
model. The input to the first layer at position $t \in \set{0, \dots, T_{\max} - 1}$ is
$$
\xvec_t = \evec_{w_t} + \pvec_t,
$$
where $\evec_{w_t}$ is the embedding of the token $w_t$ and $\pvec_t$ is row $t$ of $\Pmat$. Positions $t \ge T_{\max}$
have no embedding.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Position is treated like a vocabulary of $T_{\max}$ symbols. Position 17 has its own vector, just as the word "cat"
has its own vector, and <NoteLink to="gradient-descent">gradient descent</NoteLink> decides what each vector should contain. Nothing ties $\pvec_{17}$ to
$\pvec_{18}$ except what the data teaches. The scheme makes no assumption about how positions relate, so it can learn any
position pattern the training data supports, and none that it does not.

## Details

**Where it is used.** <Gloss name="bert" /> adds learned embeddings for 512 positions <Cite id="devlin2019" />. <Gloss name="gpt-2" /> uses a context of
1024 tokens and learned embeddings for each <Cite id="radford2019" />. The <NoteLink to="transformer">original transformer</NoteLink> tried them as an
ablation and found them almost identical to sinusoids: 25.7 <Gloss name="bleu" /> against 25.8 on newstest2013
<Cite id="vaswani2017" />.

**Parameters.** The table has $T_{\max} d$ entries. For BERT-base, $512 \times 768 = 393{,}216$, about 0.4% of its 110
million parameters. For GPT-2's largest model, $1024 \times 1600 = 1{,}638{,}400$.

**Which rows get trained.** The <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> of the loss with respect to $\pvec_t$ is the sum of the gradients with respect
to the inputs at position $t$, over every training sequence that reaches position $t$. A sequence of length $n$ updates
rows $0, \dots, n - 1$ only. If most training sequences are short, the late rows receive few updates. BERT trained on
128-token sequences for 90% of its steps and on 512-token sequences for the remaining 10%, in part to learn the
embeddings of the later positions <Cite id="devlin2019" />.

**Position and content mix in the scores.** The first layer's query–key score between positions $m$ and $n$ expands
into four terms,

$$
(\evec_m + \pvec_m)^{\tr} \Wmat_Q \Wmat_K^{\tr} (\evec_n + \pvec_n)
= \underbrace{\evec_m^{\tr} \Mmat \evec_n}_{\text{content}}
+ \underbrace{\evec_m^{\tr} \Mmat \pvec_n + \pvec_m^{\tr} \Mmat \evec_n}_{\text{content} \times \text{position}}
+ \underbrace{\pvec_m^{\tr} \Mmat \pvec_n}_{\text{position}},
$$

with $\Mmat = \Wmat_Q \Wmat_K^{\tr}$ and token subscripts shortened to positions. The position term can depend on $m$
and $n$ in any way, not only on $n - m$. Ke et al. argued that the mixed terms add noise and proposed computing the
content and position terms with separate projections <Cite id="ke2021" />.

**Comparison at scale.** For 1.3-billion-parameter <NoteLink to="autoregressive-transformer-language-model">causal language models</NoteLink> on the Pile with 1024-token contexts, Haviv et
al. measured validation perplexities of 13.05 with learned embeddings, 12.93 with sinusoids, 12.51 with <Gloss name="alibi" /> and 13.10
with no positional information at all <Cite id="haviv2022" />.

## Worked example

A model with $T_{\max} = 4$ and $d = 2$ has a $4 \times 2$ table. Suppose training has produced

$$
\Pmat = \begin{bmatrix} 0.3 & -0.1 \\ 0.2 & 0.4 \\ -0.5 & 0.1 \\ 0.0 & -0.3 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

The sentence "dog bites" enters as $(\evec_{\text{dog}} + (0.3, -0.1),\ \evec_{\text{bites}} + (0.2, 0.4))$. The
sentence "bites dog" enters as $(\evec_{\text{bites}} + (0.3, -0.1),\ \evec_{\text{dog}} + (0.2, 0.4))$. The two inputs
now differ as multisets of vectors, so attention can tell them apart. A five-token input has no row for $t = 4$ and
cannot be processed at all.

## Assumptions

- Every input is at most $T_{\max}$ tokens long.
- The training data covers each position often enough to train its row.
- Absolute position carries the useful signal. Relative structure, such as "the previous token", must be learned
  separately at every position.

## Pitfalls

- **No extrapolation.** Rows beyond $T_{\max}$ do not exist. The table can be extended and the model fine-tuned, but the
  new rows start untrained. Schemes defined at every position, such as ALiBi, were designed to avoid this
  <Cite id="press2022" />.
- **Undertrained tail.** Late rows are trained on fewer examples than early ones. A model can behave worse near the end
  of its context for this reason alone.
- **Shifted inputs.** The same phrase at position 3 and at position 300 is represented differently in the first layer.
  The model must learn translation invariance from data, position by position.
