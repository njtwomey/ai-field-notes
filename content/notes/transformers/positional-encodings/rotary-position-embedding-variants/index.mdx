<Definition>
A rotary variant keeps the defining property of <NoteLink to="rotary-position-embedding">RoPE</NoteLink>, that queries
and keys are transformed by position before the dot product so that the score depends on relative position, and
changes either the transformation or the positions it uses. **xPos** multiplies each rotated query pair at position $m$
by $\zeta_i^{m}$ and each rotated key pair at position $n$ by $\zeta_i^{-n}$, with $0 < \zeta_i \le 1$
<Cite id="sun2023xpos" />. **2D and multimodal RoPE** assign different pairs to different position coordinates
<Cite id="heo2024" /> <Cite id="wang2024qwen2vl" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

RoPE turns queries and keys by angles proportional to their positions. There are two refinements. The first makes
the rotation also shrink the score with distance, so that far tokens count less and the score does not oscillate
unpredictably at long range. The second starts from the fact that an image patch has a row and a column, and a video
frame a time, so one scalar position is not enough.

## xPos: rotation with decay

**Construction.** In complex form, RoPE sends pair $i$ of a query at position $m$ to $q^{(i)} e^{\mathrm{i} m\theta_i}$.
xPos sends it to $q^{(i)} \zeta_i^{m} e^{\mathrm{i} m\theta_i}$, and pair $i$ of a key at position $n$ to
$k^{(i)} \zeta_i^{-n} e^{\mathrm{i} n\theta_i}$ <Cite id="sun2023xpos" />. The contribution of pair $i$ to the score is

$$
\operatorname{Re}\brack{q^{(i)} \zeta_i^{m} e^{\mathrm{i} m\theta_i}\ \overline{k^{(i)} \zeta_i^{-n} e^{\mathrm{i} n\theta_i}}}
= \zeta_i^{m-n} \operatorname{Re}\brack{q^{(i)} \overline{k^{(i)}}\, e^{\mathrm{i}(m-n)\theta_i}},
$$

because $\zeta_i$ is real. The score still depends only on $m - n$. In a causal model $m \ge n$, so the factor
$\zeta_i^{m-n} \le 1$ decays exponentially with distance, pair by pair.

**Which pairs decay.** Sun et al. set $\hat\zeta_i = (i/(d/2) + \gamma)/(1 + \gamma)$ for pair $i = 0, \dots, d/2 - 1$,
with a constant $\gamma > 0$ <Cite id="sun2023xpos" />. The fastest-rotating pairs, small $i$, get the smallest
$\hat\zeta_i$ and decay fastest. The slowest pairs have $\hat\zeta_i$ close to 1 and barely decay. The fast pairs are
the ones whose cosines oscillate most at long distances, so damping them stabilises the expected score as a function of
distance. The reference implementation uses $\gamma = 0.4$ and raises $\hat\zeta_i$ to the power of the position divided
by 512, which slows the decay <Cite id="torchscalecode" />.

**Blockwise causal attention.** At inference on sequences longer than the training length, Sun et al. also restrict each
query to a window of keys, processed block by block, so that no query sees an offset larger than those met in training.

**Results.** On arXiv documents, a model trained on 1024 tokens gave perplexity 294.45 at 4096 tokens with RoPE, 24.26
with ALiBi, 28.43 with xPos alone and 20.73 with xPos plus blockwise causal attention <Cite id="sun2023xpos" />.

**Precision.** $\zeta_i^{m}$ and $\zeta_i^{-n}$ are computed separately and only their product is well scaled. For large
positions, $\zeta_i^{-n}$ overflows and $\zeta_i^{m}$ underflows in 16-bit floats. Sun et al. chose $\gamma$ so the
factors stay representable at positions up to 8192. The reference implementation also centres the positions of each
sequence around 0 before exponentiating, which halves the largest exponent <Cite id="torchscalecode" />.

**Only causal.** For keys after the query, $m - n < 0$ and $\zeta_i^{m-n} > 1$: the factor grows. xPos is designed for
<NoteLink to="decoder-only-transformers">causal decoders</NoteLink>.

## Two-dimensional RoPE

An image patch at column $x$ and row $y$ has a position $\pvec = (x, y)$. The relative property of RoPE extends to
any position space in which rotation angles add.

**Axial.** Split the pairs into two halves. Rotate the first half by $x\theta_i$ and the second half by $y\theta_i$.
Each half obeys the one-dimensional argument, so the score depends on $(\Delta x, \Delta y)$ <Cite id="heo2024" />. A
pair only ever sees one axis.

**Mixed.** Rotate pair $i$ by $\theta^x_i x + \theta^y_i y$, with learnable frequencies for both axes. The angle
difference between two patches is $\theta^x_i \Delta x + \theta^y_i \Delta y$, again a function of the relative
position alone. Heo et al. call this RoPE-Mixed and argue that mixing lets a pair respond to diagonal directions, which
axial frequencies cannot <Cite id="heo2024" />.

## Multimodal RoPE

Qwen2-VL gives every token three position coordinates: time, height and width. M-RoPE splits the rotary pairs into
three groups and rotates each group by one coordinate. Text tokens get the same value in all three coordinates, so for
text M-RoPE reduces to ordinary one-dimensional RoPE. Image tokens share a time coordinate and take their height and
width from their place in the image. Video frames increment the time coordinate <Cite id="wang2024qwen2vl" />.

## Longer contexts

Changing the rotation frequencies after training, so that angles at long positions stay within the range seen in
training, is the subject of position interpolation, NTK-aware scaling and YaRN; see
<NoteLink to="long-context-methods">long-context methods</NoteLink>. Raising the base before training, as Llama 3 does,
is covered in <NoteLink to="rotary-position-embedding">rotary position embedding</NoteLink>.

## Worked example

**Decay.** Take a pair with $\zeta_i = 0.98$. At distance 10 its contribution is scaled by $0.98^{10} = 0.817$; at
distance 50 by $0.364$; at 200 by $0.018$. A slow pair with $\zeta_i = 0.999$ keeps $0.999^{200} = 0.819$ of its
contribution at distance 200. Short-range information is carried by the fast pairs and long-range information by the
slow ones.

**Two dimensions.** With axial 2D RoPE, take one $x$-pair and one $y$-pair with $\theta = 1$. A query patch at
$(x, y) = (3, 5)$ and a key patch at $(1, 4)$ differ by $(\Delta x, \Delta y) = (2, 1)$, query minus key. The $x$-pair's
score is rotated by $2$ radians and the $y$-pair's by $1$ radian. The patch pair $(10, 7)$ and $(8, 6)$ has the same
difference and gets the same rotations.

## Assumptions

- xPos assumes that attention should decay with distance. Tasks that need exact retrieval from far back work against
  this bias, as with <NoteLink to="alibi">ALiBi</NoteLink>.
- 2D and multimodal variants assume positions on a grid. Irregular point sets need other schemes.

## Pitfalls

- **Numerical range.** Computing $\zeta_i^{-n}$ at large $n$ in low precision overflows. Either work in 32-bit floats or
  rescale the exponent.
- **Grid resolution.** A vision model trained on $14 \times 14$ patches meets unseen coordinates at higher resolution,
  just as a <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss> meets unseen positions at longer lengths.
- **Frequency budget.** Splitting pairs among two or three coordinates leaves fewer frequencies per coordinate, so each
  axis is resolved more coarsely than a one-dimensional sequence of the same width.
