<Definition>
A gated feed-forward layer maps a token vector $\xvec \in \reals^{d}$ to

$$
\operatorname{FFN}_{\phi\text{GLU}}(\xvec) = \paren{\phi(\xvec\Wmat) \odot \xvec\Vmat}\Wmat_2,
\qquad \Wmat, \Vmat \in \reals^{d \times d_{ff}},\ \Wmat_2 \in \reals^{d_{ff} \times d},
$$

where $\phi$ is applied elementwise and $\odot$ is the elementwise product <Cite id="shazeer2020" />. The variants are
named by $\phi$: sigmoid gives GLU, <Gloss name="relu" /> gives ReGLU, <Gloss name="gelu" /> gives GEGLU, $\operatorname{Swish}_1(a) = a\,\sigma(a)$
(<Gloss name="silu" />) gives SwiGLU, and the identity gives the bilinear layer.

</Definition>

## Problem

The feed-forward sublayer of the original <NoteLink to="transformer">transformer</NoteLink> is
$\max(0, \xvec\Wmat_1)\Wmat_2$ with $d_{ff} = 4d$ <Cite id="vaswani2017" />. It holds two thirds of a block's
parameters: $8d^2$ against $4d^2$ for attention. Replacing ReLU by GELU or Swish changes quality little <Cite id="shazeer2020" />. The question is whether a different form of layer, at the same number of parameters and the
same compute, is better.

## Mechanism

### The gate

Dauphin et al. introduced the gated linear unit for convolutional <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss>: the product of two <NoteLink to="matrix-vector-product">linear maps</NoteLink>, one
passed through a sigmoid <Cite id="dauphin2017" />. Shazeer placed it inside the transformer's feed-forward layer, in
place of the first linear map and its activation, and dropped the biases <Cite id="shazeer2020" />.

Each hidden unit $i$ computes $\phi(a_i)\,b_i$ with $a_i = \xvec\wvec_i$ and $b_i = \xvec\vvec_i$, where $\wvec_i$ and
$\vvec_i$ are the $i$-th columns of $\Wmat$ and $\Vmat$. For SwiGLU:

- when $a_i \gg 0$, $\sigma(a_i) \approx 1$ and the unit outputs $a_i b_i$;
- when $a_i \ll 0$, $\operatorname{Swish}_1(a_i) \approx 0$ and the unit is shut off.

In the open state the unit computes

$$
a_i b_i = (\xvec\wvec_i)(\xvec\vvec_i) = \xvec \paren{\wvec_i \vvec_i^\tr} \xvec^\tr ,
$$

a <NoteLink to="positive-definite-matrices">quadratic form</NoteLink> in $\xvec$ with the rank-one matrix $\wvec_i\vvec_i^\tr$. The bilinear variant is exactly this at
every input. A ReLU unit $\max(0, a_i)$ is piecewise linear in $\xvec$, so a product of two input features must be
approximated by many units. A gated unit represents such a product with one unit, and the gate decides where it
applies.

### Keeping parameters and compute equal

The standard layer has two matrices of $d \times d_{ff}$ entries; the gated layer has three of $d \times d'_{ff}$.
Equal parameter counts require

$$
2\,d\,d_{ff} = 3\,d\,d'_{ff} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d'_{ff} = \tfrac23 d_{ff}.
$$

With $d_{ff} = 4d$ this gives $d'_{ff} = 8d/3$. The forward cost per token is two <Gloss name="flop" plural /> per weight in each <NoteLink to="matrix-multiplication">matrix
product</NoteLink>, $2 \cdot 2 d d_{ff}$ for the standard layer and $2 \cdot 3 d d'_{ff}$ for the gated one, so the same condition
also equalises FLOPs. The activation $\phi$ and the elementwise product cost $O(d'_{ff})$ per token, which is small next
to $O(d\,d'_{ff})$.

Shazeer used $d = 768$ and reduced $d_{ff}$ from 3072 to 2048 for the three-matrix variants <Cite id="shazeer2020" />. <Gloss name="llama" /> uses a hidden width of $\frac23 \cdot 4d$ <Cite id="touvron2023b" />. For $d = 4096$
that is $10922.7$, which the released code rounds up to a multiple of 256, giving $d'_{ff} = 11008$.

### Evidence

Shazeer trained T5-base encoder–decoders (12 + 12 layers, $d = 768$) on span corruption, matched for parameters and
compute, and reported held-out log-perplexity <Cite id="shazeer2020" />:

| Feed-forward layer | 65,536 steps | 524,288 steps |
| ------------------ | ------------ | ------------- |
| ReLU (baseline)    | 1.997        | 1.677         |
| GELU               | 1.983        | 1.679         |
| Swish              | 1.994        | 1.683         |
| GLU                | 1.982        | 1.663         |
| Bilinear           | 1.960        | 1.648         |
| GEGLU              | 1.942        | 1.633         |
| SwiGLU             | 1.944        | 1.636         |
| ReGLU              | 1.953        | 1.645         |

The <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink> across four short runs was about 0.005. Every gated variant beat every ungated one at the
end of training, and GEGLU and SwiGLU were best. The paper gives no reason: "we attribute their success, as all else,
to divine benevolence" <Cite id="shazeer2020" />. <Gloss name="palm" /> adopted SwiGLU, noting that it needs three matrix products but
improved quality in compute-matched comparisons <Cite id="chowdhery2022" />.

## Worked example

**Parameter counts at $d = 4096$.** The ReLU layer with $d_{ff} = 16384$ has $2 \cdot 4096 \cdot 16384 = 134{,}217{,}728$
weights. The SwiGLU layer with $d'_{ff} = 11008$ has $3 \cdot 4096 \cdot 11008 = 135{,}266{,}304$ weights, 0.8% more
because of the rounding.

**One hidden pair.** Suppose two hidden units receive gate pre-activations $\avec = (2, -2)$ and linear values
$\bvec = (1.5, 3)$. Then $\operatorname{Swish}_1(2) = 2\sigma(2) = 1.762$ and
$\operatorname{Swish}_1(-2) = -2\sigma(-2) = -0.238$. The hidden outputs are $1.762 \times 1.5 = 2.642$ and
$-0.238 \times 3 = -0.715$. The second unit has the larger linear value but a mostly closed gate, so it contributes less.
Swish is not zero for negative inputs, so the closed gate still passes a small signal of opposite sign.

## Cost

Three matrix products instead of two. $\Wmat$ and $\Vmat$ read the same input and are usually concatenated into one
$d \times 2d'_{ff}$ product. The backward pass stores both $\xvec\Wmat$ and $\xvec\Vmat$: $2d'_{ff} = 16d/3$ values
per token against $4d$ for the ReLU layer, a third more activation memory in the feed-forward layer.

## Trade-offs

- **Hidden width.** $8d/3$ is not an integer multiple of the sizes that matrix hardware prefers. Models round it, and
  some choose other widths entirely; the parameter match is a convention, not a requirement.
- **Sparsity.** A ReLU layer outputs exact zeros for every unit with a negative pre-activation, about half of them at initialisation. SwiGLU and GEGLU outputs are rarely zero,
  which removes the activation sparsity that some inference methods exploit.
- **Mixture of experts.** Each expert in a <NoteLink to="mixture-of-experts">mixture-of-experts</NoteLink> layer is
  usually a gated feed-forward layer, so the $8d/3$ convention carries over to expert widths.
