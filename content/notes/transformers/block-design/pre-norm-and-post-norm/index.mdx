<Definition>
A transformer block wraps each sublayer $F$ (attention or the feed-forward network) in a residual connection and a
normalisation $\operatorname{LN}$. **Post-norm** applies the normalisation after the addition:
$\xvec_{\ell+1} = \operatorname{LN}(\xvec_\ell + F(\xvec_\ell))$. **Pre-norm** applies it to the branch input only:
$\xvec_{\ell+1} = \xvec_\ell + F(\operatorname{LN}(\xvec_\ell))$, with one more normalisation before the output head.
**Peri-norm** (sandwich) normalises both the input and the output of the branch:
$\xvec_{\ell+1} = \xvec_\ell + \operatorname{LN}(F(\operatorname{LN}(\xvec_\ell)))$.
</Definition>

## Problem

The original <NoteLink to="transformer">transformer</NoteLink> is post-norm <Cite id="vaswani2017" />. Post-norm
transformers train only with a <NoteLink to="learning-rate-schedules">learning-rate warmup</NoteLink>: the <Gloss name="learning-rate">learning rate</Gloss> starts near zero and rises over the first few
thousand steps. Without warmup, training diverges or settles at a poor solution. Xiong et al. trained a post-norm
transformer on IWSLT14 German–English with Adam. Without warmup it reached a BLEU score of 8.45; with warmup it reached
about 34 <Cite id="xiong2020" />. The result was also sensitive to the warmup length: 500 warmup steps gave <Gloss name="bleu" /> 31.16
at peak learning rate $5 \times 10^{-4}$ and 2.77 at $10^{-3}$. The question is why the placement of one normalisation
decides whether warmup is needed.

## Mechanism

Two facts decide the answer: how large the residual stream becomes, and how a normalisation scales the <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> that
passes back through it.

### The Jacobian of a normalisation

Take <NoteLink to="layer-normalisation">layer normalisation</NoteLink> with unit gain, zero shift and $\epsilon = 0$. Write $\Pmat = \I - \frac1d \ones\ones^\tr$
for the $d \times d$ matrix that subtracts the mean. The <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink> of $\xvec$ is
$\norm{\Pmat\xvec}/\sqrt d$, so

$$
\operatorname{LN}(\xvec) = \sqrt d \, \frac{\Pmat\xvec}{\norm{\Pmat\xvec}}.
$$

For $\uvec = \Pmat\xvec$, the <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> of $\uvec/\norm{\uvec}$ is $(\I - \hat\uvec\hat\uvec^\tr)/\norm{\uvec}$ with
$\hat\uvec = \uvec/\norm{\uvec}$. By the chain rule

$$
\frac{\partial \operatorname{LN}(\xvec)}{\partial \xvec} = \frac{\sqrt d}{\norm{\Pmat\xvec}}\paren{\I -
\hat\uvec\hat\uvec^\tr}\Pmat .
$$

The two matrices on the right are projections, with <NoteLink to="matrix-norms">spectral norm</NoteLink> 1. The <NoteLink to="jacobian">Jacobian</NoteLink> therefore has spectral norm
$\sqrt d/\norm{\Pmat\xvec}$. **A normalisation divides the gradient passing through it by the size of its input**, in
units where a standard vector has norm $\sqrt d$.

### How large the residual stream becomes

In a post-norm stack every block ends with a normalisation, so every $\xvec_\ell$ has norm $\sqrt d$, whatever the depth.

In a pre-norm stack nothing normalises the stream itself. Unrolling the recursion gives

$$
\xvec_L = \xvec_0 + \sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1} F_\ell(\operatorname{LN}(\xvec_\ell)).
$$

Each branch reads a normalised input, so its output has the same scale at every depth. At initialisation the branch
outputs are nearly uncorrelated with each other and with $\xvec_0$. If each adds variance $c$ per coordinate, then
$\expect\norm{\xvec_\ell}^2 \approx (1 + c\ell)\, d$. Xiong et al. prove, for a transformer with <Gloss name="relu" /> feed-forward
layers at initialisation, that $(1 + \ell/2)\,d \le \expect\norm{\xvec_\ell}^2 \le (1 + 3\ell/2)\,d$ <Cite id="xiong2020" />. The pre-norm residual stream grows as $\sqrt\ell$.

### Gradients of the last layer

The gradient of the loss with respect to the parameters of the last block reaches them through the normalisation
that follows the block.

- **Post-norm.** The input to the last normalisation is $\xvec_{L-1} + F(\xvec_{L-1})$. Its norm is a constant multiple
  of $\sqrt d$ that does not depend on $L$. The last block's gradient therefore does not shrink with depth.
- **Pre-norm.** The input to the final normalisation is $\xvec_L$, with norm about $\sqrt{(1 + cL)d}$. Its Jacobian
  has norm about $1/\sqrt{1 + cL}$, and every gradient that reaches the blocks passes through it.

Xiong et al. make this precise: with probability close to 1 at initialisation, the gradient of the last feed-forward
weight matrix $\Wmat^{2,L}$ satisfies

$$
\norm{\frac{\partial \Lcal}{\partial \Wmat^{2,L}}}_F \le O\paren{d\sqrt{\ln d}} \ \text{(post-norm)}, \qquad
\norm{\frac{\partial \Lcal}{\partial \Wmat^{2,L}}}_F \le O\paren{d\sqrt{\frac{\ln d}{L}}} \ \text{(pre-norm)}
$$

This is their Theorem 1 <Cite id="xiong2020" />. Near the output, post-norm gradients are larger than pre-norm ones by a factor that grows as
$\sqrt L$. With the learning rate that suits the rest of the network, the first updates to the top layers of a post-norm
model are too large. Warmup keeps those first updates small until the model leaves the region around initialisation. A
pre-norm model does not have the large top-layer gradients, and Xiong et al. trained pre-norm transformers without
warmup on translation and on <Gloss name="bert" /> pre-training. They report that pre-norm BERT reached a given validation loss faster
than post-norm BERT, a speed-up they put at 40% <Cite id="xiong2020" />.

### The identity path

The <NoteLink to="residual-connections">residual connection</NoteLink> exists to give the gradient an identity path.
In a pre-norm block the Jacobian is $\I + \partial F_\ell(\operatorname{LN}(\xvec_\ell))/\partial\xvec_\ell$, and the
identity term reaches every layer unchanged. In a post-norm block the identity term is multiplied by the normalisation's
Jacobian. If the branch adds variance $c$, the input to the normalisation has norm about $\sqrt{(1 + c)d}$, and the
identity path is scaled by about $1/\sqrt{1 + c}$ per block. Over $L$ blocks the pure identity path is scaled by
$(1 + c)^{-L/2}$. The gradient still reaches the lower layers through the branches, but the exact identity path that makes deep
<Gloss name="resnet">residual networks</Gloss> trainable is lost.

## Worked example

Take $L = 24$ blocks whose branches each add variance $c = 1$ per coordinate at initialisation.

- **Pre-norm.** $\expect\norm{\xvec_{24}}^2 \approx (1 + 24)\,d = 25d$, so $\norm{\xvec_{24}} \approx 5\sqrt d$. The
  final normalisation divides every gradient by about 5. The last block's own branch output has norm about $\sqrt d$,
  one fifth of the stream it is added to.
- **Post-norm.** Each block normalises a sum of norm about $\sqrt{2d}$, so the identity path is scaled by
  $1/\sqrt 2$ per block. Across 24 blocks the pure identity path is scaled by $2^{-12} = 1/4096$.

## Remedies and variants

**Pre-norm with a final normalisation.** <Gloss name="gpt-2" /> moved layer normalisation to the input of each sub-block and added one
after the final block <Cite id="radford2019" />. Most later <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss> use pre-norm, usually with <NoteLink to="rmsnorm-in-transformers">RMSNorm</NoteLink>.

**DeepNorm.** Wang et al. keep post-norm and change the residual weighting to
$\xvec_{\ell+1} = \operatorname{LN}(\alpha\xvec_\ell + F(\xvec_\ell))$ with $\alpha > 1$. They also scale the
initialisation of the feed-forward, value and output projections by $\beta < 1$ <Cite id="wang2022deepnet" />. Both
changes make the identity term dominate the sum, so the normalisation's Jacobian is closer to a projection that passes
$\xvec_\ell$ through. For a <NoteLink to="decoder-only-transformers">decoder-only model</NoteLink> with $M$ layers they use $\alpha = (2M)^{1/4}$ and
$\beta = (8M)^{-1/4}$; for an <NoteLink to="encoder-only-transformers">encoder-only model</NoteLink> with $N$ layers, $\alpha = (2N)^{1/4}$ and $\beta = (8N)^{-1/4}$.
With these settings they trained a transformer with 1,000 layers (2,500 attention and feed-forward sublayers) <Cite id="wang2022deepnet" />.

**Sandwich and peri-norm.** CogView added a normalisation to the output of each sublayer, which it called
Sandwich-LN, to stop values in the residual stream overflowing in 16-bit training <Cite id="ding2021cogview" />.
NormFormer adds a normalisation after attention, a learned per-head scale on attention outputs and a normalisation
after the first feed-forward layer <Cite id="shleifer2021normformer" />. Gemma 2 applies RMSNorm to both the input and
the output of every attention and feed-forward sublayer <Cite id="gemmateam2024" />. Kim et al. call this arrangement
peri-norm. Because each branch output has a fixed scale, the stream variance grows by a constant per layer,
$\var(\xvec_{\ell+1}) \approx \var(\xvec_\ell) + \beta_0$. Unlike pre-norm, a single very large activation inside a
branch cannot produce an equally large update to the stream <Cite id="kim2025periln" />.

| Placement | Stream norm at depth $\ell$ | Identity path           | Warmup needed | Used in                           |
| --------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Post-norm | $\sqrt d$                   | scaled at every block   | yes           | original transformer, BERT        |
| Pre-norm  | grows as $\sqrt{1 + c\ell}$ | exact                   | no            | GPT-2, <Gloss name="gpt-3" />, Llama and most <Gloss name="llm" plural /> |
| DeepNorm  | $\sqrt d$                   | up-weighted by $\alpha$ | reduced       | DeepNet                           |
| Peri-norm | grows, bounded per layer    | exact                   | no            | CogView, Gemma 2                  |

## Cost

Pre-norm and post-norm use the same number of normalisations, plus one final normalisation in pre-norm. Peri-norm adds
one normalisation per sublayer: $2d$ extra parameters per block for RMSNorm and one more memory-bound pass over the
activations.

## Trade-offs

- **Depth utilisation in pre-norm.** The stream at layer $\ell$ has norm about $\sqrt{(1 + c\ell)d}$ and each branch
  adds a vector of norm about $\sqrt{cd}$. The relative change a deep layer can make shrinks as $1/\sqrt\ell$, so late
  layers change the representation less than early ones.
- **Stability against final quality.** Post-norm keeps every layer's contribution on the same scale but needs warmup
  and careful tuning. DeepNorm keeps post-norm's normalised stream
  and up-weights the identity term to stabilise the start. Peri-norm keeps pre-norm's exact identity path and fixes
  the scale of each branch's update to the stream.
- **Final normalisation.** A pre-norm or peri-norm stack must normalise before the output head. Omitting it feeds the
  head a vector whose scale depends on depth.
