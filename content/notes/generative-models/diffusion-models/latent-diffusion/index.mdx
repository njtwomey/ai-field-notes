<Definition>
A **latent diffusion model** (LDM) is a two-stage generative model <Cite id="rombach2022" />. An encoder $\Ecal$ maps
an image $\xvec \in \reals^{H \times W \times 3}$ to a latent $\zvec = \Ecal(\xvec) \in \reals^{h \times w \times c}$
with $h = H/f$ and $w = W/f$ for a downsampling factor $f$, and a decoder $\Dcal$ maps latents back to images. A
<NoteLink to="denoising-diffusion-probabilistic-models">diffusion model</NoteLink> is then trained on the latents with
the noise-prediction loss

$$
L = \expect_{\zvec = \Ecal(\xvec),\, \yvec,\, \epsilonvec,\, t}\brack{\norm{\epsilonvec -
\epsilonvec_{\thetavec}\paren{\zvec_t, t, \tau_{\thetavec}(\yvec)}}^2},
$$

where $\zvec_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\zvec + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilonvec$ and $\tau_{\thetavec}$ encodes an
optional condition $\yvec$, such as a text prompt. A sample is $\Dcal(\zvec_0)$, where $\zvec_0$ comes from the <NoteLink to="reverse-time-stochastic-differential-equations">reverse
diffusion</NoteLink> started at $\zvec_T \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$.
</Definition>

## Problem

A pixel-space <Gloss name="diffusion-model">diffusion model</Gloss> evaluates a large <Gloss name="u-net" /> on the full-resolution image at every training step and every
sampling step. Most of the bits in a natural image describe fine texture that is hard to perceive. A likelihood-based
model spends much of its capacity on those bits. Rombach et al. separate the two tasks: perceptual compression, which
removes imperceptible detail, and semantic compression, which models the content <Cite id="rombach2022" />.

## Mechanism

<LatentDiffusionDiagram />

**Stage 1: the autoencoder.** The <NoteLink to="autoencoder">autoencoder</NoteLink> is trained once and then frozen.
Its loss combines pixel reconstruction, a perceptual loss and a patch-based adversarial loss, as in VQGAN
<Cite id="esser2021" />. The latent space is regularised lightly, either by a small <Gloss name="kl" /> penalty towards
$\Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$ as in a <NoteLink to="variational-autoencoder">VAE</NoteLink> or by a vector-quantisation layer
as in a <NoteLink to="vector-quantised-variational-autoencoder">VQ-VAE</NoteLink>. The regularisation keeps the latents at a bounded scale; it is too weak
for the autoencoder to be a good generative model by itself. The latent keeps a two-dimensional grid structure, unlike
the one-dimensional token sequence of earlier two-stage models, so a convolutional U-Net can model it
<Cite id="rombach2022" />.

**Stage 2: diffusion on the latents.** The diffusion model is an ordinary DDPM whose data are the latents
$\zvec = \Ecal(\xvec)$. Every result about DDPMs, fast samplers such as <NoteLink to="denoising-diffusion-implicit-models">DDIM</NoteLink>, and
<NoteLink to="diffusion-guidance">classifier-free guidance</NoteLink> applies unchanged.

**Conditioning by cross-attention.** A domain-specific encoder $\tau_{\thetavec}$, such as a transformer over text
tokens, turns the condition into a sequence of vectors. Cross-attention layers inside the U-Net let each spatial
position attend to them: queries come from the U-Net's intermediate features $\varphi(\zvec_t)$, and keys and values
from $\tau_{\thetavec}(\yvec)$,

$$
\mathrm{Attention}(\Qmat, \Kmat, \Vmat) = \mathrm{softmax}\paren{\frac{\Qmat\Kmat^\top}{\sqrt{d}}}\Vmat, \qquad
\Qmat = \varphi(\zvec_t)\Wmat_Q,\; \Kmat = \tau_{\thetavec}(\yvec)\Wmat_K,\; \Vmat = \tau_{\thetavec}(\yvec)\Wmat_V.
$$

The same mechanism handles text, semantic layouts and class labels <Cite id="rombach2022" />. Conditions that are
images of the same size, such as a low-resolution input for super-resolution, are instead concatenated with
$\zvec_t$ along the channel axis.

**Sampling.** Draw $\zvec_T \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$, run the reverse diffusion with guidance to obtain $\zvec_0$,
and decode once: $\hat{\xvec} = \Dcal(\zvec_0)$.

## Worked example

A $512 \times 512$ RGB image has $512 \times 512 \times 3 = 786{,}432$ values. With $f = 8$ and $c = 4$ latent
channels the latent has $64 \times 64 \times 4 = 16{,}384$ values, 48 times fewer. The cost of a <NoteLink to="convolutional-layer">convolutional layer</NoteLink>
grows linearly with the number of spatial positions, which fall by a factor $f^2 = 64$. The cost of self-attention
grows with the square of the number of positions, which falls by a factor $64^2 = 4096$. A 50-step sampler therefore
runs its 50 U-Net evaluations on $64 \times 64$ grids and one decoder pass at full resolution.

## Cost

Training the autoencoder is a one-time cost, and its latents can be reused for many diffusion models. Rombach et al.
found that downsampling factors from 4 to 16 balanced efficiency and fidelity. $f = 1$ or 2 trained slowly, and
$f = 32$ lost too much detail for the diffusion model to recover <Cite id="rombach2022" />.

## Trade-offs

- **Fidelity is capped by the decoder.** Details the autoencoder cannot reconstruct, such as small text or fine facial
  features, are missing from every sample, however good the diffusion model is.
- **The latent is not perceptually uniform.** Squared error in latent space does not equal perceptual error in pixel
  space; the autoencoder's perceptual and adversarial losses are what make the latent meaningful.
- **Latent scale.** The latents must have roughly unit variance to match the noise schedule. Implementations rescale
  them by a constant estimated from the training data.
- **Widely used.** Stable Diffusion is a latent diffusion model with $f = 8$, a text encoder for $\tau_{\thetavec}$ and
  classifier-free guidance <Cite id="ho2022" />.
