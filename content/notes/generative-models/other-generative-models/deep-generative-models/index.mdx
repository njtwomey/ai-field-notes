<Definition>
A **deep generative model** is a probability distribution $p_{\thetavec}(\xvec)$ over high-dimensional data, such as
images, audio or text, whose parameters $\thetavec$ are those of one or more neural networks. It is trained on samples
from the data distribution $p_{\text{data}}$ so that $p_{\thetavec} \approx p_{\text{data}}$. It must support drawing
new samples, and some families also evaluate the density $p_{\thetavec}(\xvec)$ of a given point.
</Definition>

## Components

Every family turns simple randomness into structured data, and each chooses a different way to make training
tractable.

- **<NoteLink to="autoregressive-generative-models">Autoregressive models</NoteLink>** factorise $p(\xvec) = \prod_i
  p(x_i \mid x_{<i})$ and predict each factor with a masked network.
- **<NoteLink to="variational-autoencoder">Variational autoencoders</NoteLink>** introduce a latent $\zvec$, decode it
  with $p_{\thetavec}(\xvec \mid \zvec)$, and maximise the <Gloss name="elbo">evidence lower bound</Gloss> (ELBO) with an encoder
  $q_{\phivec}(\zvec \mid \xvec)$. The <NoteLink to="vector-quantised-variational-autoencoder">vector-quantised variational autoencoder (VQ-VAE)</NoteLink> uses a discrete latent and a separately
  fitted prior.
- **<NoteLink to="normalising-flow">Normalising flows</NoteLink>** map noise to data through an invertible network and
  compute the likelihood by <NoteLink to="change-of-variables">change of variables</NoteLink>.
- **<NoteLink to="generative-adversarial-network">Generative adversarial networks</NoteLink>** train a sampler against
  a discriminator and never evaluate a density.
- **<NoteLink to="denoising-diffusion-probabilistic-models">Diffusion models</NoteLink>** add noise to data over many
  steps and learn to reverse it; <NoteLink to="score-based-generative-models">score-based models</NoteLink> describe
  the same process through the score $\nabla_{\xvec} \log p_t(\xvec)$.

The comparison below states the classical form of each family; many
current models combine several.

| Family            | Likelihood                                   | Sampling                                 | Latent space                                        | Training objective                                |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Autoregressive    | Exact                                        | Slow: one network pass per element       | None                                                | Maximum likelihood (sum of cross-entropies)       |
| VAE               | Lower bound (ELBO); estimate by sampling     | Fast: one decoder pass                   | Low-dimensional, continuous, with a Gaussian prior  | Maximise the ELBO                                 |
| VQ-VAE            | Bound, via the prior over codes              | Slow if the prior is autoregressive      | Discrete grid of codebook indices                   | Reconstruction plus codebook and commitment losses, then prior likelihood |
| Normalising flow  | Exact                                        | Fast: one inverse pass                   | Same dimension as the data, invertible              | Maximum likelihood                                |
| GAN               | None                                         | Fast: one generator pass                 | Low-dimensional noise, no encoder                   | Minimax game against a discriminator              |
| Diffusion / score | Lower bound, or exact via the <NoteLink to="probability-flow-ode">probability flow ODE</NoteLink> | Slow: tens to a thousand network passes | Noise of the same dimension as the data             | Denoising (noise-prediction) regression           |



- **Likelihood.** Exact likelihood allows model comparison by held-out log-likelihood, compression and anomaly
  scoring. A GAN supports none of these, and its quality is judged by sample metrics such as the
  <NoteLink to="frechet-inception-distance">Fréchet inception distance (FID)</NoteLink>.
- **Sampling speed.** Single-pass samplers (VAE, flow, GAN) are fast. Autoregressive and <Gloss name="diffusion-model">diffusion models</Gloss> trade
  sequential computation for sample quality. <NoteLink to="denoising-diffusion-implicit-models">Denoising diffusion implicit models (DDIM)</NoteLink> and <Gloss name="ode" /> solvers reduce diffusion
  sampling to tens of steps.
- **Latent space.** Only VAEs, and to a degree GANs, provide a compact latent in which interpolation and editing are
  meaningful. Flows and diffusion models have latents as large as the data; the deterministic DDIM map still makes
  their latents usable for inversion and interpolation.
- **Objective.** Likelihood-based objectives cover all modes of the data but can spend capacity on imperceptible
  detail. The adversarial objective produces sharp samples but can drop modes.

## Data flow

Sampling in every family follows one pattern: draw simple noise, then apply a learned transformation.

1. **Noise.** A standard Gaussian vector (VAE, flow, GAN, diffusion), uniform random numbers for sampling categorical
   conditionals (autoregressive), or both.
2. **Transformation.** One network pass (VAE decoder, flow, GAN generator), a sequential loop over elements
   (autoregressive), or an iterative denoising loop (diffusion).
3. **Output.** A sample in data space, optionally decoded from a latent space (VQ-VAE,
   <NoteLink to="latent-diffusion">latent diffusion</NoteLink>).

Training differs more than sampling. Likelihood-based families maximise $\log p_{\thetavec}(\xvec)$ or a bound on it;
GANs replace the likelihood by a learned discriminator; diffusion models reduce training to regression on denoising
targets.

## Variants

- **Two-stage models** compress data with an <NoteLink to="autoencoder">autoencoder</NoteLink> and fit a second generative model to the codes: VQ-VAE with
  an autoregressive prior, and <NoteLink to="latent-diffusion">latent diffusion</NoteLink>.
- **Conditional models** learn $p(\xvec \mid \cvec)$ for a label, text or image $\cvec$:
  <NoteLink to="conditional-variational-autoencoder">conditional VAEs</NoteLink>, conditional GANs, and diffusion
  models with <NoteLink to="diffusion-guidance">guidance</NoteLink>.
- **Hybrids** combine families: VAEs with flow posteriors or autoregressive decoders, and autoencoders trained with
  adversarial losses.
- **Links between families.** A diffusion model's probability flow ODE is a <NoteLink to="neural-ordinary-differential-equations">continuous normalising flow</NoteLink>
  <Cite id="song2021" />. An <Gloss name="ar">autoregressive model</Gloss> with Gaussian conditionals is a flow
  <Cite id="papamakarios2021" />. A diffusion model is a hierarchical VAE with a fixed encoder.
