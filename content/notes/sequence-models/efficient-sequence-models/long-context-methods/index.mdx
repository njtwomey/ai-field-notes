<Definition>
A **long-context method** makes a transformer trained on sequences of at most $L$ tokens work on sequences of length
$L' > L$. **Context extension** methods for rotary position embedding (RoPE) rescale the rotation angles so that
positions beyond $L$ produce angles the model has seen: position interpolation divides every position by
$s = L'/L$ <Cite id="chen2023" />, and NTK-aware scaling and YaRN rescale each frequency by a different amount
<Cite id="peng2024yarn" />. **Systems** methods such as ring attention distribute exact attention over several devices
so that the longer sequence fits in memory <Cite id="liu2024ring" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

Two things stop a model at its training length. Attention's cost grows as $n^2$, which
<NoteLink to="flash-attention">memory-efficient kernels</NoteLink> and distribution address. And the model has never seen
position values beyond $L$.

<NoteLink to="rotary-position-embedding">Rotary position embedding</NoteLink> splits each query and key of width $d$ into
$d/2$ pairs and rotates pair $i$ at position $m$ by the angle $m\theta_i$, with

$$
\theta_i = b^{-2i/d}, \qquad i = 0, 1, \dots, d/2 - 1,
$$

and base $b = 10{,}000$ <Cite id="su2021" />. The score between positions $m$ and $n$ then depends on the angles
$(m - n)\theta_i$. The wavelength of pair $i$ is $\lambda_i = 2\pi/\theta_i = 2\pi b^{2i/d}$ tokens. High-frequency pairs
(small $i$) complete many rotations within $L$ tokens and see every angle during training. Low-frequency pairs (large
$i$) have wavelengths longer than $L$; during training they see only a fraction of a turn. At positions beyond $L$
those pairs take angles never seen in training, and attention scores become erratic. Perplexity rises sharply just
past $L$ <Cite id="chen2023" />.

## Mechanism

### Position interpolation

Position interpolation replaces position $m$ by $m/s$, with $s = L'/L$ <Cite id="chen2023" />. Every rotation angle
becomes $m\theta_i/s$. For $m < L'$ the angle is below $L\theta_i$, so it lies in the range seen in training for every
pair. The model is interpolating between trained positions instead of extrapolating past them.

Chen et al. bound the attention score under interpolation and show the bound is about 600 times smaller than under
extrapolation. With a short fine-tuning, position interpolation extended LLaMA models to 32,768 tokens
<Cite id="chen2023" />.

The cost is resolution. Adjacent tokens now differ in angle by $\theta_i/s$ instead of $\theta_i$. For the
high-frequency pairs, which encode fine local order, the model must tell apart angles $s$ times closer than in training.

### NTK-aware scaling

NTK-aware scaling changes the base instead of the positions: $b' = b\,s^{d/(d-2)}$ <Cite id="peng2024yarn" />. The new
frequencies are $\theta_i' = b'^{-2i/d} = \theta_i\,s^{-2i/(d-2)}$.

- For $i = 0$, $s^0 = 1$: the highest frequency is unchanged.
- For the last pair, $i = d/2 - 1$, the exponent is $-(d-2)/(d-2) = -1$: the lowest frequency is divided by exactly
  $s$, as in position interpolation.

Between the two, the scaling factor changes geometrically. High frequencies are extrapolated, which is harmless because
they already covered every angle in training. Low frequencies are interpolated. The method was proposed in an online
post and adopted by Code Llama as "adjusted base frequency"; YaRN documents and extends it <Cite id="peng2024yarn" />.

### YaRN

YaRN chooses the treatment of each pair from how many full rotations it makes within the original context,
$r_i = L/\lambda_i$ <Cite id="peng2024yarn" />. With thresholds $\alpha < \beta$, define the ramp
$\gamma(r) = 0$ for $r < \alpha$, $1$ for $r > \beta$, and $(r - \alpha)/(\beta - \alpha)$ in between, and set

$$
\theta_i' = \paren{1 - \gamma(r_i)}\frac{\theta_i}{s} + \gamma(r_i)\,\theta_i.
$$

Pairs that rotate fewer than $\alpha$ times are fully interpolated. Pairs that rotate more than $\beta$ times are left
alone. For LLaMA models, $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = 32$.

YaRN also sharpens attention. Interpolation makes attention distributions flatter at long range, so YaRN divides the
logits by a temperature $t$ with $\sqrt{1/t} = 0.1\ln s + 1$. The temperature folds into the rotary embedding, so it
costs nothing at inference. YaRN needs 10 times fewer tokens and 2.5 times fewer training steps than earlier methods,
and extended Llama 2 to 128K tokens <Cite id="peng2024yarn" />.

### Worked example

Take head width $d = 128$, base $b = 10{,}000$, training length $L = 4096$ and target $L' = 16{,}384$, so $s = 4$.

- Wavelengths run from $\lambda_0 = 2\pi \approx 6.28$ tokens to $\lambda_{63} = 2\pi \times 10{,}000^{126/128} \approx
  54{,}410$ tokens. The rotation counts $r_i = 4096/\lambda_i$ run from about 652 down to 0.075.
- Under YaRN with $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = 32$, the 21 pairs with $r_i > 32$ keep their frequency, the 18 pairs with
  $r_i < 1$ are divided by 4, and the 25 in between are blended.
- NTK-aware scaling instead sets $b' = 10{,}000 \times 4^{128/126} \approx 40{,}890$.
- YaRN's temperature satisfies $\sqrt{1/t} = 0.1\ln 4 + 1 = 1.139$, so logits are multiplied by $1/t = 1.296$.

### Ring attention

Extending the positions does not make a million-token sequence fit on one accelerator. Even with
<NoteLink to="flash-attention">FlashAttention</NoteLink>, the activations of every layer grow with $n$. Ring attention
distributes one sequence over $P$ devices arranged in a ring <Cite id="liu2024ring" />:

1. Split the sequence into $P$ blocks. Device $p$ holds query block $p$ and, at the start, key–value block $p$.
2. At each of $P$ steps, each device computes attention between its query block and the key–value block it holds, and
   accumulates the result with the online softmax that FlashAttention uses to merge partial results.
3. At the same time, each device sends its key–value block to the next device in the ring and receives one from the
   previous device.

After $P$ steps every query block has seen every key–value block, so the result is exact attention. When computing one
block takes longer than sending one, the communication hides behind the computation. The feasible context length then
grows linearly with the number of devices, with no approximation <Cite id="liu2024ring" />. Ring attention is a form of
sequence parallelism; see <NoteLink to="model-parallelism">model parallelism</NoteLink> for the other ways to split a
model.

## Cost

Position interpolation, NTK-aware scaling and YaRN change only the rotation frequencies, so inference costs nothing
extra. They work best with a short fine-tuning at the new length; NTK-aware scaling also works to a degree without
any <Cite id="peng2024yarn" />. Attention cost at the new length is unchanged: $O(L'^2)$ time per layer and a
<NoteLink to="kv-cache">KV cache</NoteLink> of $L'$ entries. Ring attention adds communication of $O(nd)$ per layer per
device and keeps each device's memory proportional to $n/P$.

## Trade-offs

- **Interpolation versus extrapolation.** Interpolation keeps angles in range but compresses local order. Leaving high
  frequencies alone, as NTK-aware scaling and YaRN do, keeps local order and interpolates only where it is needed.
- **Nominal versus effective context.** A model that runs at length $L'$ does not necessarily use information from all
  $L'$ tokens. Check retrieval at every depth, for example with a passkey hidden at a random position, as Chen et al. do.
- **Other encodings.** ALiBi adds a linear distance penalty to scores and extrapolates somewhat without any change
  <Cite id="press2022" />. Interpolation methods are specific to RoPE.
- **Short-context quality.** Changing the frequencies alters the model at every length. YaRN and fine-tuned
  interpolation keep most short-context quality; untuned scaling can lose some.
