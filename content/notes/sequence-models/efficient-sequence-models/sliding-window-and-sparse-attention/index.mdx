<Definition>
**Sparse attention** restricts each query $i$ to a set of key positions $\Scal(i) \subset \set{1, \dots, n}$ and computes
the softmax only over that set:

$$
\ovec_i = \sum_{j \in \Scal(i)} \frac{\exp(\qvec_i^\tr\kvec_j/\sqrt d)}{\sum_{l \in \Scal(i)} \exp(\qvec_i^\tr\kvec_l/\sqrt d)}\,\vvec_j.
$$

In **sliding-window attention**, $\Scal(i) = \set{j : \abs{i - j} \le w}$, or $\set{j : i - w \le j \le i}$ with a <NoteLink to="causal-masking">causal
mask</NoteLink>. Other patterns add dilated or strided positions, **global tokens** that attend to and are attended by every
position, or random keys. The cost is proportional to $\sum_i \abs{\Scal(i)}$ instead of $n^2$
<Cite id="beltagy2020" /> <Cite id="zaheer2020" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

Full <NoteLink to="scaled-dot-product-attention">attention</NoteLink> scores all $n^2$ query–key pairs. In text, most of
a token's attention weight goes to nearby tokens, and long-range links are few. Computing all $n^2$ scores spends most of
the work on weights that end up near zero. Sparse attention decides in advance which pairs to score, so the cost follows
the pattern, not the square of the length.

## Mechanism

<AttentionPatterns />

### Sliding window

Each query attends to the $w$ keys on each side of it (Longformer) or the $w$ preceding keys (causal models). A layer
scores about $n(2w + 1)$ pairs, $O(nw)$, and never forms the $n \times n$ matrix <Cite id="beltagy2020" />. This is
the attention analogue of a <NoteLink to="convolutional-layer">convolution</NoteLink>: a fixed-width local receptive
field, but with weights computed from content.

**Receptive field across layers.** Consider a bidirectional window of half-width $w$. The output of layer 1 at position
$i$ depends on inputs at positions $i - w$ to $i + w$. Suppose the output of layer $\ell - 1$ at every position $j$
depends on inputs within $(\ell - 1)w$ of $j$. Layer $\ell$ at position $i$ reads layer $\ell - 1$ at positions
$j \in [i - w, i + w]$, and each of those depends on inputs within $(\ell - 1)w$ of $j$. So layer $\ell$ at $i$ depends
on inputs within $\ell w$ of $i$. By induction, $L$ layers give a receptive field of $2Lw + 1$ positions, or $Lw + 1$
with a causal window. Longformer uses small windows in lower layers and larger ones in upper layers, so that lower layers
model local detail cheaply <Cite id="beltagy2020" />.

The receptive field is an upper bound. Information that must cross $Lw$ positions passes through $L$ successive
weighted averages and is diluted at each step.

### Dilated and strided patterns

A **dilated** window with dilation $\delta$ attends to keys $i \pm \delta, i \pm 2\delta, \dots, i \pm w\delta$. It has the
same cost as a window of width $w$ and $\delta$ times the reach, at the price of gaps. Longformer uses dilation on some
heads of upper layers, and none in lower layers <Cite id="beltagy2020" />.

The **Sparse Transformer** factorises attention into two patterns on different heads or layers
<Cite id="child2019" />. With stride $l$, one head attends to the previous $l$ positions and another to every $l$-th
position. Any earlier position $j$ is reachable from $i$ in two hops: the strided head moves from $i$ to a position $k$
with $i - k$ a multiple of $l$ and $0 \le k - j < l$, and the local head moves from $k$ to $j$. Choosing $l \approx \sqrt n$ gives $O(\sqrt n)$ keys per query and $O(n\sqrt n)$
cost.

### Global tokens

A few positions, such as a classification token or the tokens of a question, get **global attention**: they attend to
every position, and every position attends to them <Cite id="beltagy2020" />. With $g$ global tokens the extra cost is
$O(gn)$. Any two positions are then connected in two hops through a global token, in a single layer. Longformer gives
global attention its own query, key and value projections, and chooses the global tokens per task.

### BigBird: window, global and random

BigBird combines a sliding window, $g$ global tokens and $r$ random keys per query <Cite id="zaheer2020" />. Random
edges make the attention graph an expander-like graph with short paths between all pairs. Zaheer et al. prove that this
sparse attention, with its $O(1)$ global tokens, keeps two properties of full attention: it is a universal approximator
of sequence-to-sequence functions and it is Turing complete. BigBird handled sequences up to 8 times longer than
previously possible on the same hardware.

<Gloss name="gpu" plural /> are inefficient at gathering scattered single keys. BigBird therefore applies every pattern to blocks of
consecutive tokens, so each query block attends to whole key blocks and the work stays in dense <NoteLink to="matrix-multiplication">matrix products</NoteLink>.

### Generation with a window

With a causal window of $W$ keys, a decoder needs only the last $W$ keys and values. A rolling buffer stores the key and
value of position $i$ in slot $i \bmod W$, so the <NoteLink to="key-value-cache">KV cache</NoteLink> has a fixed size.
Mistral 7B uses $W = 4096$ over 32 layers. Its theoretical attention span is $32 \times 4096 \approx 131{,}000$ tokens,
and the rolling buffer reduces cache memory 8 times at sequence length 32,768 <Cite id="jiang2023mistral" />.

### Worked example

Take a causal window covering the current token and the $w = 2$ before it, in a stack of $L = 3$ layers.

- Layer 1 at position 10 reads positions 8, 9 and 10.
- Layer 2 at position 10 reads layer-1 outputs at 8, 9, 10, which read inputs from 6 to 10.
- Layer 3 at position 10 reads inputs from $10 - 3 \times 2 = 4$ to 10: $Lw + 1 = 7$ positions.

For a length of $n = 16{,}384$ and a bidirectional window of half-width $w = 256$, each query scores at most
$2w + 1 = 513$ keys, $513 / 16{,}384 = 3.1\%$ of a full row. Twelve layers reach $12 \times 256 = 3072$ positions in
each direction, well short of the whole sequence, so global tokens are needed for document-level tasks.

## Cost

A pattern with $s$ keys per query costs $O(nsd)$ time and $O(ns)$ memory for the scores, against $O(n^2 d)$ and
$O(n^2)$. Longformer and BigBird have $s = O(w + g + r)$, constant in $n$. The Sparse Transformer has $s = O(\sqrt n)$.

The saving in arithmetic becomes a saving in time only with a kernel that skips the empty blocks. Banded and
block-sparse kernels do this. A dense kernel with a mask computes every score and discards most of them.

## Trade-offs

- **Recall at a distance.** A sliding window alone cannot attend to a token beyond its receptive field. A question
  about the first page of a book is out of reach at the last page unless global tokens, a large window or
  <NoteLink to="hybrid-sequence-models">interleaved full-attention layers</NoteLink> provide a path.
- **Fixed patterns.** The pattern does not depend on content. A long-range dependency that the pattern does not cover
  is simply lost, whereas full attention can find it.
- **Task-specific global tokens.** Longformer's global tokens are chosen by hand for each task, which a general
  pretrained decoder cannot do.
- **Exactness.** Within the pattern the softmax is exact. Combined with
  <NoteLink to="flash-attention">FlashAttention</NoteLink>-style tiling, a banded pattern costs time and memory linear in
  $n$.
