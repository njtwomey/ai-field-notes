<Definition>
A **hybrid sequence model** is a deep network whose token-mixing layers are of two kinds: self-attention layers, which
compare every token with every earlier token, and recurrent layers with a fixed-size state, such as
<NoteLink to="mamba">Mamba</NoteLink>. The two kinds are interleaved in a fixed ratio. Each layer is followed by a
feed-forward or mixture-of-experts sublayer, as in a <NoteLink to="transformer">transformer</NoteLink>
<Cite id="lieber2024" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Attention and recurrent layers fail in opposite ways. Attention keeps every earlier token in its
<NoteLink to="key-value-cache">KV cache</NoteLink>, so it can retrieve any of them exactly, but the cache and the cost grow with
the context. A state-space layer compresses the whole past into a fixed-size state, so each token costs the same
whatever the context, but details that were not kept in the state are gone.

Most token mixing in a <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss> is local or summarising, which a recurrent layer does well. A minority of
operations need exact lookup: copying a name from far back, or matching the pattern of a few-shot prompt. A few
attention layers are enough to supply these, and the rest of the network can be cheaper.

## Details

### Jamba

Jamba groups layers into blocks of 8, with one attention layer and seven Mamba layers per block, a ratio of 1:7
<Cite id="lieber2024" />. Every second layer replaces its <Gloss name="mlp" /> with a <NoteLink to="mixture-of-experts">mixture of
experts</NoteLink> of 16 experts, of which the top 2 are used per token. The released model has 52B parameters, 12B of
them active per token, supports a context of 256K tokens and fits on one 80 GB <Gloss name="gpu" />.

**KV cache.** Only attention layers keep a KV cache. With one attention layer in eight, Jamba's cache is one eighth of
that of a transformer with the same number of layers and heads. At 256K tokens in 16-bit precision, Jamba's cache takes
4 GB, against 32 GB for Mixtral 8x7B and 128 GB for Llama-2 70B <Cite id="lieber2024" />.

**Throughput.** At a context of 128K tokens Jamba processes 3 times as many tokens per second as Mixtral 8x7B
<Cite id="lieber2024" />. The Mamba layers do the bulk of the mixing at linear cost.

**Positions.** Jamba's attention layers use no explicit <NoteLink to="positional-encoding">positional encoding</NoteLink>. The Mamba layers see tokens in order, so
they supply position information to the layers above them. Results with and without rotary embeddings were similar
<Cite id="lieber2024" />.

### What the attention layers provide

Lieber et al. compared pure attention, pure Mamba and hybrid models of the same size. At 7B parameters the hybrid
reached a lower training loss than either pure model. The pure Mamba model did worse on tasks that depend on <Gloss name="icl">in-context
learning</Gloss>, such as following the output format shown in a few-shot prompt: on IMDB sentiment classification it often
answered "Very Good" instead of the required "Positive" or "Negative", and scored 48.8 against 84.1 for attention and
90.9 for the hybrid. The authors attribute the gap to induction heads, attention patterns that find an earlier
occurrence of the current token and copy what followed it, and show one in the hybrid's attention layers
<Cite id="lieber2024" />.

### Design choices

- **Ratio.** Fewer attention layers give a smaller cache and higher throughput; too few lose recall. Jamba found 1:3
  and 1:7 similar in quality and chose 1:7 for efficiency <Cite id="lieber2024" />.
- **Placement.** Attention layers are spread through the depth, not grouped, so that every stage of processing can
  reach any earlier token.
- **Kind of attention layer.** The attention layers can themselves be
  <NoteLink to="sliding-window-and-sparse-attention">sliding-window</NoteLink> layers, which bounds their cache too,
  at the cost of exact recall beyond the window.
- **Kind of recurrent layer.** Any linear recurrence can take the place of Mamba, including
  <NoteLink to="rwkv-and-retentive-networks">retention or RWKV</NoteLink> layers. Mamba-2's duality with masked
  attention makes the two kinds of layer share much of their implementation <Cite id="dao2024" />.

## Assumptions

- **Most mixing is compressible.** The design assumes that a few exact-recall layers suffice and the rest of the
  network can work from a compressed state.
- **Memory is the bottleneck.** The savings matter when the KV cache or long-context attention limits <NoteLink to="minibatching-and-batch-size">batch size</NoteLink> or
  context length. At short contexts a hybrid runs at about the speed of a transformer.

## Pitfalls

- **Cache still grows.** The attention layers' cache is smaller but still linear in context length. At very long
  contexts it dominates again.
- **Two kinds of kernels.** Training and serving need efficient implementations of both layer types, and the two have
  different parallelisation and caching schemes.
