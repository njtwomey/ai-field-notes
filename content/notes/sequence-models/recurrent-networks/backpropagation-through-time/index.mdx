<Definition>
Backpropagation through time (BPTT) computes the <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> of a sequence loss $\Lcal = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \Lcal_t$ with
respect to the shared parameters of a recurrent network. It unrolls the recurrence into $T$ copies of one layer,
runs <NoteLink to="backpropagation">backpropagation</NoteLink> through the unrolled graph, and sums the gradients of the
$T$ copies of each parameter <Cite id="werbos1990" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

A <NoteLink to="recurrent-neural-network">recurrent neural network</NoteLink> updates its state by
$\hvec_t = \phi(\avec_t)$ with pre-activation $\avec_t = \Wmat \hvec_{t-1} + \Umat \xvec_t + \bvec$. The loss at step $t$
depends on $\Wmat$ directly through $\avec_t$ and indirectly through every earlier state. The gradient must account for
all of these paths.

## Mechanism

**Forward pass.** Run the recurrence for $t = 1, \dots, T$, storing every $\hvec_t$ and $\avec_t$, and compute the
losses $\Lcal_t$.

**Backward pass.** Define the adjoint $\bar\hvec_t = \partial \Lcal / \partial \hvec_t$, a row vector. The state
$\hvec_t$ affects the loss through $\Lcal_t$ and through $\hvec_{t+1}$, so by the <NoteLink to="multivariable-chain-rule">multivariable chain rule</NoteLink>

$$
\bar\hvec_t = \frac{\partial \Lcal_t}{\partial \hvec_t} + \bar\hvec_{t+1}\, \frac{\partial \hvec_{t+1}}{\partial \hvec_t},
\qquad \bar\hvec_{T+1} = \zeros.
$$

The <NoteLink to="jacobian">Jacobian</NoteLink> of one step follows from $\hvec_{t+1} = \phi(\Wmat \hvec_t + \Umat \xvec_{t+1} + \bvec)$:

$$
\Jmat_{t+1} \eqdef \frac{\partial \hvec_{t+1}}{\partial \hvec_t} = \diag\paren{\phi'(\avec_{t+1})}\, \Wmat.
$$

**Accumulate.** Each copy of $\Wmat$ receives the adjoint of its pre-activation,
$\bar\avec_t = \bar\hvec_t \diag(\phi'(\avec_t))$, and the total gradient sums over copies:

$$
\frac{\partial \Lcal}{\partial \Wmat} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \bar\avec_t^{\tr}\, \hvec_{t-1}^{\tr}, \qquad
\frac{\partial \Lcal}{\partial \Umat} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \bar\avec_t^{\tr}\, \xvec_t^{\tr}.
$$

Unrolling the recursion for $\bar\hvec_k$ shows what each term carries. The contribution of the loss at step $t$ to
the adjoint at an earlier step $k$ is

$$
\frac{\partial \Lcal_t}{\partial \hvec_k} = \frac{\partial \Lcal_t}{\partial \hvec_t} \prod_{i = k+1}^{t} \Jmat_i
= \frac{\partial \Lcal_t}{\partial \hvec_t}\, \Jmat_t \Jmat_{t-1} \cdots \Jmat_{k+1}.
$$

## Vanishing and exploding gradients

The product of $t - k$ Jacobians scales the contribution of the loss at step $t$ to the adjoint at step $k$.
Bound its norm with the submultiplicativity of the <NoteLink to="matrix-norms">spectral norm</NoteLink>, $\norm{\Amat\Bmat} \le \norm{\Amat}\norm{\Bmat}$.
Suppose $\abs{\phi'(z)} \le \gamma$ for all $z$; for $\tanh$, $\gamma = 1$. Then
$\norm{\diag(\phi'(\avec_i))} \le \gamma$, and with $\sigma_1$ the largest singular value of $\Wmat$,

$$
\norm{\prod_{i=k+1}^{t} \Jmat_i} \le \prod_{i=k+1}^{t} \norm{\diag(\phi'(\avec_i))}\,\norm{\Wmat} \le (\gamma \sigma_1)^{t-k}.
$$

- **Vanishing.** If $\gamma \sigma_1 < 1$, the bound falls geometrically in the gap $t - k$. Long-range terms are
  exponentially smaller than short-range ones, so the summed gradient is dominated by nearby steps. The network cannot
  learn that an input 50 steps back matters <Cite id="bengio1994" />.
- **Exploding.** The bound is only an upper limit, so $\gamma \sigma_1 > 1$ is necessary for explosion, not
  sufficient <Cite id="pascanu2013" />. In the linear case $\phi(z) = z$, the product is $\Wmat^{t-k}$. Along an
  eigenvector with eigenvalue $\lambda$, $\Wmat^{t-k}\vvec = \lambda^{t-k}\vvec$, so any eigenvalue with
  $\abs{\lambda} > 1$ makes some component grow exponentially.

A single long-range term can then dominate the sum, and one gradient step can throw the parameters far away. Both
failures come from one fact: backpropagation through $t - k$ steps multiplies by the same matrix $t - k$ times.

## Worked example

For the scalar recurrent neural network $h_t = \tanh(w h_{t-1} + u x_t)$, each Jacobian is the number
$J_t = w\,(1 - h_t^2)$. With $w = 0.5$ and states near zero, $J_t \approx 0.5$. After 50 steps the factor is at most
$0.5^{50} \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}$, below the resolution of a 32-bit float relative to 1. With $w = 1.5$ and
states near zero, a linear network would multiply by $1.5^{50} \approx 6.4 \times 10^{8}$. A $\tanh$ unit in
saturation, $h_t$ near $\pm 1$, has $1 - h_t^2$ near zero, which cuts the chain even when $\abs{w} > 1$.

## Cost

The forward pass stores $T$ states of size $n$, so memory grows linearly with the sequence length. Time is
$O(T n^2)$ per sequence, the same order as the forward pass.

**Truncated BPTT** splits a long sequence into chunks of $K$ steps. It carries the hidden state forward across chunks
but stops the backward pass at each chunk boundary. Memory is $O(K n)$. Dependencies longer than $K$ steps receive no
gradient.

## Trade-offs

- **Gradient clipping** handles explosion. If the gradient $\gvec$ has norm above a threshold $\tau$, rescale it to
  $\tau\, \gvec / \norm{\gvec}$ <Cite id="pascanu2013" />. The direction is kept and the step length is bounded.
  Clipping does not help vanishing.
- **Gated architectures** handle vanishing. The <NoteLink to="long-short-term-memory">long short-term memory (LSTM)</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="gated-recurrent-unit">gated recurrent unit (GRU)</NoteLink>
  add a path whose Jacobian is a diagonal of gates that can stay close to 1.
- **Attention** gives each step a direct connection to every earlier step, so the gradient path length is 1 regardless of
  the gap.
- **Initialisation** of $\Wmat$ as an orthogonal or identity matrix puts every singular value at 1, which delays
  both failures early in training.

## Pitfalls

- **Small gradients look like convergence.** A vanishing gradient gives a small loss change and a small gradient norm.
  The model has not learned the long dependency; it has stopped receiving a signal about it.
- **Truncation bias.** Truncated BPTT gives a biased gradient. The chunk length is an upper limit on what the model can
  learn to remember.
