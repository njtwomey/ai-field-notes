<Definition>
For one class, sort all detections over the test set by decreasing confidence. A detection is a <Gloss name="tp">true positive</Gloss> if its
<NoteLink to="intersection-over-union">IoU</NoteLink> with a ground-truth object of that class is at least a threshold
$t$ and that object has not already been matched; otherwise it is a <Gloss name="fp">false positive</Gloss>. With $G$ ground-truth objects, the
precision and recall after the first $j$ detections are $P_j = \text{TP}_j / j$ and $R_j = \text{TP}_j / G$. The
<Gloss name="ap">average precision</Gloss> is the area under the interpolated precision

$$
P_{\text{interp}}(r) = \max_{j \,:\, R_j \ge r} P_j ,
$$

and mAP averages AP over classes <Cite id="everingham2010" />. <Gloss name="coco" />'s headline number also averages over
$t \in \set{0.50, 0.55, \dots, 0.95}$ <Cite id="lin2014" />.
</Definition>

## Matching

Detection is a set prediction: a detector outputs boxes with confidences, and each ground-truth object should be found
once. Matching in order of decreasing confidence lets the most confident detection claim an object. A second detection
of the same object is a false positive, so a detector cannot gain by repeating itself, and <Gloss name="nms">non-maximum suppression</Gloss>
matters. Detections of objects marked "difficult" (VOC) or "crowd" (COCO) are ignored rather than counted either way.

## Interpolation

The raw precision–recall sequence zigzags: precision drops at each false positive and recovers at the next true one.
Interpolation replaces each precision by the best precision at any equal or higher recall, which makes the curve
monotone. Its justification is that a user wanting recall $r$ would pick the <NoteLink to="operating-points-and-equal-error-rate">operating point</NoteLink> with the best precision
at recall at least $r$.

The benchmarks integrate this envelope differently:

| Protocol | Recall points | IoU thresholds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PASCAL VOC 2007 | 11 ($0, 0.1, \dots, 1$) | 0.5 |
| PASCAL VOC 2010–2012 | every distinct recall (exact area) | 0.5 |
| COCO | 101 ($0, 0.01, \dots, 1$) | 0.50 to 0.95 in steps of 0.05 |

COCO calls the averaged number AP, and reports AP50 and AP75 at single thresholds. Averaging over thresholds rewards
precise localisation: a box with IoU 0.6 counts at 0.5 and 0.55 but fails the eight stricter thresholds.

**Worked example.** One class has 4 ground-truth objects. Five detections, in confidence order, are TP, FP, TP, TP,
FP. Precision after each is $1, 0.5, 0.667, 0.75, 0.6$ and recall $0.25, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.75$. The interpolated
envelope is 1 up to recall 0.25 and 0.75 up to recall 0.75, then 0. The exact area is
$0.25 \times 1 + 0.25 \times 0.75 + 0.25 \times 0.75 = 0.625$. The 11-point and 101-point versions give $0.614$ and
$0.629$, because they sample the step function at slightly different places. Recall never reaches 1, since one object
was never detected.

## Pitfalls

- **Protocols are not interchangeable.** VOC AP50 and COCO AP often differ by around 20 points for the same detector.
  Always state the protocol.
- **mAP ignores <NoteLink to="calibration-error">confidence calibration</NoteLink>.** Only the ranking of confidences matters, so a detector can have high mAP with
  meaningless confidence values.
- **Per-image budgets.** COCO caps detections at 100 per image. A detector producing more has its lowest-confidence
  detections dropped, which caps its recall.
- **Size matters.** COCO also reports AP for small, medium and large objects, because the overall number hides that
  small objects are much harder.
