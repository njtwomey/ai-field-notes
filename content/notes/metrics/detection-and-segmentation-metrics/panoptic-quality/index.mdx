<Definition>
For one class, match predicted segments $p$ to ground-truth segments $g$ with intersection over union $\text{IoU}(p, g) > 0.5$. The matches are
<Gloss name="tp">true positives (TP)</Gloss>, unmatched predictions are <Gloss name="fp">false positives (FP)</Gloss>, and unmatched ground-truth segments are <Gloss name="fn">false
negatives (FN)</Gloss>. Then <Cite id="kirillov2019" />

$$
\text{PQ} = \frac{\sum_{(p, g) \in \text{TP}} \text{IoU}(p, g)}{\abs{\text{TP}} + \tfrac12\abs{\text{FP}} + \tfrac12\abs{\text{FN}}}
= \underbrace{\frac{\sum_{\text{TP}} \text{IoU}(p, g)}{\abs{\text{TP}}}}_{\text{SQ}} \times
\underbrace{\frac{\abs{\text{TP}}}{\abs{\text{TP}} + \tfrac12\abs{\text{FP}} + \tfrac12\abs{\text{FN}}}}_{\text{RQ}},
$$

averaged over classes.
</Definition>

## The task

Panoptic segmentation unifies two tasks: <NoteLink to="semantic-segmentation">semantic segmentation</NoteLink> labels every pixel with a class, including amorphous
"stuff" such as sky and road; instance segmentation separates countable "things" such as people and cars. A panoptic
prediction assigns every pixel a class and, for things, an instance identity, with no overlaps. PQ scores both parts
with one number.

## Why the 0.5 threshold makes matching unique

Within one image, predicted segments do not overlap, and neither do ground-truth segments. Suppose $\text{IoU}(p, g) >
0.5$. Since $\abs{p \cup g} \ge \abs{g}$,

$$
\abs{p \cap g} > \tfrac12\abs{p \cup g} \ge \tfrac12\abs{g} ,
$$

so $p$ covers more than half of $g$. Two disjoint predicted segments cannot both cover more than half of $g$, so at
most one prediction matches each ground-truth segment, and by the same argument at most one ground truth matches each
prediction. The matching is therefore unique and needs no assignment algorithm or confidence ordering, unlike
<NoteLink to="mean-average-precision-for-detection">detection mAP</NoteLink>.

## Two factors

**Segmentation quality** SQ is the <NoteLink to="dice-and-segmentation-overlap">mean IoU</NoteLink> of the matched pairs: how well the found segments are delineated.
**Recognition quality** RQ is the <NoteLink to="precision-recall-and-f-score">F1 score</NoteLink> of the matching,
$2\text{TP}/(2\text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN})$: how many segments were found without spurious ones. Reporting SQ and RQ
separately shows whether delineation or recognition is weak.

**Worked example.** One class has three matches with <Gloss name="iou" /> 0.9, 0.7 and 0.6, one unmatched prediction and two unmatched
ground-truth segments. Then $\text{SQ} = 2.2/3 = 0.733$, $\text{RQ} = 3/(3 + 0.5 + 1) = 0.667$, and
$\text{PQ} = 0.489$.

## Pitfalls

- **Stuff and things.** PQ is often reported separately for stuff and thing classes, because stuff classes are easier
  to match. The overall number can hide weak instance separation.
- **Small segments.** A segment that is small relative to its annotation error rarely reaches IoU 0.5, and counts as
  both a false positive and a false negative.
- **Void regions.** Unlabelled pixels are removed from predicted segments before matching, and predictions mostly in
  void regions are ignored. Implementations must follow this rule to reproduce benchmark numbers.
