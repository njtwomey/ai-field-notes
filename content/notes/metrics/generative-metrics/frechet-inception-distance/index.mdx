<Definition>
Let $(\muvec_1, \Sigmamat_1)$ and $(\muvec_2, \Sigmamat_2)$ be the mean and covariance of Inception-network features of
real and generated images. The Fréchet inception distance is <Cite id="heusel2017" />

$$
\text{FID} = \norm{\muvec_1 - \muvec_2}^2 + \trace\paren{\Sigmamat_1 + \Sigmamat_2 - 2\paren{\Sigmamat_1\Sigmamat_2}^{1/2}} .
$$
</Definition>

## Where the formula comes from

The Fréchet distance, also called the Wasserstein-2 distance, between two distributions is the smallest root-mean-square
distance between paired draws, minimised over all ways of pairing them. For two Gaussians it has the closed form above
<Cite id="dowson1982" />, and FID is its square; the derivation is in <NoteLink to="wasserstein-distances">Wasserstein
distances</NoteLink>. The first term is the squared distance between the means. The second
is zero exactly when $\Sigmamat_1 = \Sigmamat_2$: for equal covariances $\paren{\Sigmamat^2}^{1/2} = \Sigmamat$ and the
trace cancels. So FID penalises a generator both for shifting the feature distribution and for changing its spread,
including shrinking it, which is what mode collapse does.

## The matrix square root

$\Sigmamat_1\Sigmamat_2$ is not symmetric, but it is similar to the symmetric <Gloss name="positive-semi-definite">positive semi-definite</Gloss> matrix
$\Sigmamat_1^{1/2}\Sigmamat_2\Sigmamat_1^{1/2}$, so its eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ are real and non-negative, and
$\trace\paren{\Sigmamat_1\Sigmamat_2}^{1/2} = \sum_i \sqrt{\lambda_i}$. In two dimensions this has a closed form. With
$\Mmat = \Sigmamat_1\Sigmamat_2$,

$$
\paren{\sqrt{\lambda_1} + \sqrt{\lambda_2}}^2 = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + 2\sqrt{\lambda_1\lambda_2}
= \trace\Mmat + 2\sqrt{\det\Mmat}, \quad\text{so}\quad \trace\Mmat^{1/2} = \sqrt{\trace\Mmat + 2\sqrt{\det\Mmat}} .
$$

In the 2048 dimensions of Inception features the square root is computed numerically, and small imaginary parts from
round-off are discarded.

**Worked example.** Real features $\Gauss\paren{\zeros, \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}$ and generated
features with mean $(1, 0.5)$ and covariance $\operatorname{diag}(2, 0.5)$. The mean term is $1^2 + 0.5^2 = 1.25$.
$\Mmat = \Sigmamat_1\Sigmamat_2$ has trace $2.5$ and <NoteLink to="determinant">determinant</NoteLink> $0.75$, so $\trace\Mmat^{1/2} = \sqrt{2.5 + 2\sqrt{0.75}}
= 2.057$, and the covariance term is $2 + 2.5 - 2 \times 2.057 = 0.386$. $\text{FID} = 1.636$, which matches
`scipy.linalg.sqrtm`.

## Pitfalls

- **Biased with finite samples.** FID estimated from $N$ samples is biased upwards, and the bias depends on the
  generator, so rankings can change with $N$ <Cite id="chong2020" />. Compare at the same, large $N$ (50,000 is common),
  or extrapolate to $N \to \infty$. <NoteLink to="kernel-inception-distance">KID</NoteLink> has an unbiased estimator.
- **Only two moments.** FID sees means and covariances of features, not the full distributions. Very different
  distributions with the same two moments score 0.
- **Feature extractor.** The Inception network and its preprocessing define the space. Differences in resizing and
  image format change FID by several points; use a reference implementation.
- **One number for two failures.** Low fidelity and low diversity both raise FID. <NoteLink to="precision-and-recall-for-generative-models">Precision
  and recall</NoteLink> separate them.
