<Definition>
For a reference multispectral image and an estimate with $K$ bands, and $\xvec_n, \hat{\xvec}_n \in \reals^K$ the
spectra of pixel $n$:

$$
\text{SAM}_n = \arccos\frac{\xvec_n^\top \hat{\xvec}_n}{\norm{\xvec_n}\,\norm{\hat{\xvec}_n}}, \qquad
\text{ERGAS} = 100\,\frac{h}{l}\sqrt{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\paren{\frac{\text{RMSE}_k}{\mu_k}}^2},
$$

where $\text{RMSE}_k$ and $\mu_k$ are the <Gloss name="rmse">root-mean-squared error</Gloss> and the mean of band $k$, and $h/l$ is the ratio of
the high-resolution to the low-resolution pixel size <Cite id="kruse1993" />.
</Definition>

## Spectral angle mapper

SAM treats each pixel's spectrum as a vector and measures the angle between the reference and the estimate, averaged
over pixels. The angle ignores the vector's length, so it is insensitive to overall illumination or gain and sensitive
only to the shape of the spectrum, which carries the material information. SAM is 0 for spectra that are exact multiples
of each other. It is the angular form of <NoteLink to="dot-product">cosine similarity</NoteLink> (see
<NoteLink to="pairwise-distances-and-cosine-similarity">pairwise distances and cosine similarity</NoteLink>).

## ERGAS and RASE

ERGAS, the relative dimensionless global error in synthesis introduced by Wald, normalises each band's RMSE by the
band's mean, so bands with different radiometric levels count equally, and scales by the resolution ratio so that
results at different resolution ratios are comparable. Lower is better; values below about 3 are often taken as
good. RASE, the relative average spectral error, instead normalises the pooled RMSE by the mean radiance $\mu$ over all
bands:

$$
\text{RASE} = \frac{100}{\mu}\sqrt{\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{RMSE}_k^2} .
$$

**Worked example.** Two pixels with three bands have reference spectra $(100, 80, 60)$ and $(90, 85, 70)$ and estimates
$(98, 84, 58)$ and $(95, 80, 72)$. The spectral angles are $1.98^\circ$ and $2.90^\circ$, a mean SAM of $2.44^\circ$.
The per-band RMSEs are $3.81$, $4.53$ and $2.00$ against band means $95$, $82.5$ and $65$; with $h/l = 1/4$,
$\text{ERGAS} = 1.08$. The overall mean is $80.83$, and $\text{RASE} = 4.46$.

## Scoring without a reference

Pansharpening fuses a low-resolution multispectral image with a high-resolution panchromatic one, and at full resolution
there is no reference image to compare with. QNR (quality with no reference) combines two distortion indices computed
from the inputs <Cite id="alparone2008" />: $D_\lambda$ measures how much the relationships between bands in the fused
image differ from those in the original multispectral image (spectral distortion), and $D_s$ how much each band's
relationship to the panchromatic image changes across scales (spatial distortion). Then

$$
\text{QNR} = (1 - D_\lambda)^{\alpha}(1 - D_s)^{\beta},
$$

usually with $\alpha = \beta = 1$; 1 is ideal.

## Pitfalls

- **Degree or radian.** SAM is reported in either; state the unit.
- **The resolution ratio.** ERGAS values are only comparable at the same, correctly stated $h/l$.
- **Reduced-resolution protocols.** Reference-based scores are often computed by degrading the inputs and treating the
  originals as references (Wald's protocol). Performance at reduced resolution need not carry over to full resolution.
