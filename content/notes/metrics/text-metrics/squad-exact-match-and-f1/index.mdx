<Definition>
For a predicted answer string $\hat a$ and gold answers $a_1, \dots, a_m$, both scores first normalise the strings:
lowercase, remove punctuation, remove the articles "a", "an" and "the", and collapse whitespace. Then

$$
\mathrm{EM} = \max_i\ \indicator\brack{\mathrm{norm}(\hat a) = \mathrm{norm}(a_i)}, \qquad
\mathrm{F1} = \max_i\ \frac{2 P_i R_i}{P_i + R_i},
$$

where $P_i$ and $R_i$ are the precision and recall of the normalised prediction's tokens against the tokens of
$a_i$, counted as multisets. Both are averaged over questions and reported in $[0, 1]$ or as percentages
<Cite id="rajpurkar2016" />.
</Definition>

## Why two scores

Exact match is strict: an answer with one extra word scores 0. Token F1 gives partial credit for overlap. The gap
between them shows how often a system finds the right region of the passage but not its exact boundaries. The
maximum over gold answers accounts for legitimate variation, since SQuAD collected several human answers per
question for its development and test sets <Cite id="rajpurkar2016" />.

The normalisation matters as much as the formula. It makes "The Eiffel Tower" and "eiffel tower" equal, which is
right, and it discards articles because annotators include them inconsistently.

## Worked example

Gold answers "the Eiffel Tower" and "Eiffel Tower in Paris"; prediction "Eiffel Tower, Paris". After normalisation
these are "eiffel tower", "eiffel tower in paris" and "eiffel tower paris".

- **Exact match.** The prediction equals neither gold answer, so EM is 0.
- **F1 against "eiffel tower".** Two tokens are shared: $P = 2/3$, $R = 1$, F1 $= 0.8$.
- **F1 against "eiffel tower in paris".** Three tokens are shared: $P = 1$, $R = 3/4$, F1 $= 0.857$.

The reported F1 is the maximum, $0.857$.

## Pitfalls

- **Bag of tokens.** Token F1 ignores order: "Paris Eiffel Tower" scores the same as "Eiffel Tower Paris".
- **Unanswerable questions.** In datasets that include questions with no answer, such as SQuAD 2.0, a correct
  abstention is scored as an exact match of empty strings, and any predicted text for such a question scores 0.
- **Generative answers.** Free-form answers from a generative model rarely match the extractive gold spans exactly,
  so EM underestimates them; F1 is less harsh but still lexical.
