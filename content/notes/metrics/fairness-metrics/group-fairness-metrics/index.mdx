<Definition>
Let $A$ be a protected attribute defining groups, $Y$ the true label and $\hat Y$ a binary prediction. Common <NoteLink to="fairness-criteria">group
fairness criteria</NoteLink> require equality across groups $a$ of:

| criterion | requires, for all groups $a$ | compares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| demographic parity | $P(\hat Y = 1 \mid A = a)$ equal | positive-prediction rates |
| equal opportunity | $P(\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = 1, A = a)$ equal | true-positive rates |
| equalised odds | $P(\hat Y = 1 \mid Y = y, A = a)$ equal for $y = 0, 1$ | true- and false-positive rates |
| predictive parity | $P(Y = 1 \mid \hat Y = 1, A = a)$ equal | precision |

The corresponding metrics are the differences or ratios of these quantities between groups <Cite id="hardt2016" />.
</Definition>

## Three families

Barocas, Hardt and Narayanan group the criteria by which <NoteLink to="independence">independence</NoteLink> they require <Cite id="barocas2023" />.

- **Independence:** the prediction is independent of the group, $\hat Y \perp A$. Demographic parity. The disparate
  impact ratio, the ratio of the groups' positive-prediction rates, is its multiplicative form.
- **Separation:** the prediction is independent of the group given the truth, $\hat Y \perp A \mid Y$. Equalised
  odds, and its relaxation equal opportunity, which constrains only the true-positive rate
  <Cite id="hardt2016" />. In <Gloss name="roc" /> terms, every group must operate at the same point.
- **Sufficiency:** the truth is independent of the group given the prediction, $Y \perp A \mid \hat Y$. Predictive
  parity for binary predictions; for scores, calibration within each group.

Each criterion is a comparison of the <NoteLink to="confusion-matrix">confusion-matrix</NoteLink> rates of the groups (see
<NoteLink to="sensitivity-specificity-and-predictive-values">sensitivity, specificity and predictive values</NoteLink>):
rows for separation, columns for sufficiency, the prediction marginal for independence.

## Why they conflict

Precision, the true- and false-positive rates and the base rate $\pi$ of a group are linked by <NoteLink to="bayes-theorem">Bayes' theorem</NoteLink>.
Rearranged, with <Gloss name="fn">false-negative</Gloss> rate $\text{FNR} = 1 - \text{TPR}$,

$$
\text{FPR} = \frac{\pi}{1 - \pi}\cdot\frac{1 - \text{PPV}}{\text{PPV}}\cdot\paren{1 - \text{FNR}}.
$$

If two groups have different base rates $\pi$, then equal precision (sufficiency) and equal FNR force different FPRs:
the classifier cannot satisfy predictive parity and equalised odds at once, unless it is perfect or the base rates
are equal <Cite id="chouldechova2017" />. Kleinberg, Mullainathan and Raghavan proved the corresponding result for
scores: calibration within groups and balance of the scores given the class cannot all hold except in degenerate
cases <Cite id="kleinberg2017" />. Choosing a fairness metric is therefore a choice between incompatible notions of
fairness, not a technical detail.

## Worked example

A classifier applied to two groups of 100 people each:

| group | base rate | <Gloss name="tp" /> | FN | <Gloss name="fp" /> | <Gloss name="tn" /> | positive rate | TPR | FPR | precision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 0.40 | 30 | 10 | 10 | 50 | 0.40 | 0.75 | 0.167 | 0.75 |
| B | 0.20 | 12 | 8 | 8 | 72 | 0.20 | 0.60 | 0.100 | 0.60 |

The demographic parity gap is $0.40 - 0.20 = 0.20$, the equal-opportunity (TPR) gap $0.15$, the FPR gap $0.067$ and the
precision gap $0.15$. For group A the identity gives $\text{FPR} = \frac{0.4}{0.6}\cdot\frac{0.25}{0.75}\cdot 0.75 = 0.167$,
matching the table. Moving group B's threshold can close one gap only by opening another.

## Pitfalls

- **The metric encodes a value judgement.** Demographic parity ignores differences in base rates; equalised odds
  accepts them; predictive parity makes predictions mean the same thing in each group. Which is appropriate depends on
  the application and cannot be settled by the data.
- **Group definitions matter.** Parity across broad groups can hide disparities within subgroups and at their
  intersections.
- **Equalising at the chosen threshold only.** A gap measured at one <NoteLink to="operating-points-and-equal-error-rate">operating point</NoteLink> says nothing about others; and
  equalising thresholds per group may be restricted by law in some settings.
- **Small groups** make every rate uncertain; report counts and
  <NoteLink to="metric-confidence-intervals">confidence intervals</NoteLink>.
