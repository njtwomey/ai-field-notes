<Definition>
A non-intrusive speech quality metric maps a single recording to a predicted quality score, with no access to a clean
reference. Learned metrics, such as DNSMOS <Cite id="reddy2021" /> and NISQA <Cite id="mittag2021" />, are neural
networks trained to predict the mean opinion score (MOS) that human listeners give on a 1 to 5 scale. Signal-based
metrics, such as SRMR <Cite id="falk2010" />, compute a hand-designed quantity that correlates with perceived quality.
Higher is better for all three.
</Definition>

## Why reference-free

Intrusive metrics such as <NoteLink to="perceptual-speech-quality-and-intelligibility">PESQ and STOI</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="signal-to-noise-and-signal-to-distortion-ratios">SI-SDR</NoteLink> need the clean signal. Real recordings, the ones a deployed noise
suppressor processes, have no clean version. Listening tests are the ground truth but are slow and expensive: in a
standard absolute category rating test, listeners rate each clip from 1 (bad) to 5 (excellent) and the scores are
averaged into a MOS. Non-intrusive metrics approximate that test automatically.

## Learned metrics

- **DNSMOS** was built for the Deep Noise Suppression challenges. It is a network trained on a large set of noisy and
  enhanced clips with MOS ratings collected by crowdsourcing, and predicts the MOS of a new clip directly
  <Cite id="reddy2021" />. It is fast enough to score large test sets, which made it the standard automatic measure for
  noise suppression.
- **NISQA** combines a convolutional network over the mel spectrogram with self-attention over time. Besides overall
  quality it predicts four perceptual dimensions: noisiness, coloration, discontinuity and loudness, which say what is
  wrong as well as how much <Cite id="mittag2021" />.

Both are only as good as their training data. They generalise best to degradations like those they were trained on,
and they can be fooled by artefacts they have not seen, including those of the systems being evaluated.

## SRMR

SRMR measures reverberation without learning. The signal passes through an auditory (gammatone) filter bank, see
<NoteLink to="gammatone-filter-bank">gammatone filter bank</NoteLink>, and the envelope of each band is analysed by a
bank of modulation filters. Clean speech concentrates its modulation energy at low modulation frequencies, around the
syllable rate. Reverberation smears the envelopes and adds energy at higher modulation frequencies. SRMR is the ratio
of the modulation energy in the lowest modulation bands to that in the highest, so it falls as reverberation grows
<Cite id="falk2010" />.

## Pitfalls

- **A prediction, not a measurement.** A learned MOS predictor has error of its own, and a system can be tuned to
  please the predictor rather than listeners. Confirm important comparisons with a listening test.
- **Scores are model- and version-specific.** Different releases of the same metric give different numbers; report the
  version.
- **Domain shift.** A predictor trained on noisy telephone-band speech may misjudge music, singing, far-field
  recordings or new codecs.
- **SRMR measures reverberation, not all quality.** Noise and distortion that do not change the modulation spectrum
  much are missed.
