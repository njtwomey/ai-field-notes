<Definition>
PESQ and STOI are intrusive speech metrics: both compare a processed signal with the clean reference. PESQ (ITU-T
P.862) predicts perceived speech quality, the mean opinion score listeners would give, on a scale from $-0.5$ to $4.5$
<Cite id="rix2001" /> <Cite id="itu2001" />. STOI predicts speech intelligibility, the fraction of words listeners
would understand, from a correlation score that lies between 0 and 1 in practice <Cite id="taal2011" />. Higher is
better for both.
</Definition>

## Why not just SNR

Waveform measures such as <NoteLink to="snr-and-si-sdr">SNR and SI-SDR</NoteLink> weight every sample and frequency
equally. Listeners do not. Noise in a quiet pause is more annoying than the same noise under loud speech, the ear's
frequency resolution is coarse at high frequencies, and a small delay or a phase change is inaudible but costly in a
waveform metric. PESQ and STOI model enough of hearing to track listening tests closely, at the cost of being more
complex and less transparent.

## PESQ

PESQ was developed for telephone networks and codecs, where the degraded signal may be delayed, filtered, clipped and
level-shifted relative to the reference <Cite id="rix2001" />. It proceeds in stages:

1. **Level alignment.** Both signals are scaled to a standard listening level.
2. **Input filtering.** Both are filtered to model the frequency response of a telephone handset.
3. **Time alignment.** The delay between the signals is estimated for each utterance, so that variable delay, which
   listeners barely notice, is not counted as distortion.
4. **Auditory transform.** Short frames are mapped to loudness on a perceptual frequency scale (see
   <NoteLink to="auditory-scales">auditory scales</NoteLink>), modelling the ear's frequency resolution and loudness
   perception.
5. **Disturbance.** The difference between the loudness representations is the disturbance. It is computed
   symmetrically and asymmetrically: components added by the degradation, such as noise, disturb listeners more than
   components removed, so the asymmetric disturbance weights additions more.
6. **Aggregation.** The disturbances are aggregated over frequency and time with norms that emphasise the worst
   parts, and combined linearly into a score from $-0.5$ to $4.5$.

The raw score is not on the mean-opinion-score scale. ITU-T P.862.1 maps it to MOS-LQO (listening quality objective)
with a logistic function. P.862 is narrowband (telephone bandwidth, 8 kHz sampling), and a wideband extension, P.862.2,
covers 16 kHz speech; the later standard P.863 (POLQA) supersedes both for new work.

## STOI

STOI targets intelligibility rather than quality, and was designed to be sensitive to the time–frequency processing
done by noise suppressors, which older intelligibility measures handled poorly <Cite id="taal2011" />:

1. **Resample and trim.** Both signals are resampled to 10 kHz and frames in which the clean speech is silent are
   removed.
2. **Short-time spectra.** A short-time Fourier transform with 25.6 ms frames and 50% overlap gives each signal's
   spectrum; the bins are grouped into 15 one-third-octave bands starting at 150 Hz.
3. **Envelope segments.** Each band's envelope is cut into segments of 30 frames, about 384 ms, the time scale of
   syllables.
4. **Normalise and clip.** The processed envelope in each segment is scaled to the clean envelope's energy and
   clipped so that no single band can dominate with a very poor local signal-to-distortion ratio.
5. **Correlate and average.** The correlation coefficient between the clean and processed envelope segments is
   averaged over bands and segments to give the STOI score.

A logistic function fitted to listening tests maps the score to a predicted fraction of words understood. For noise
with strong temporal modulation, such as a single competing talker, extended STOI (ESTOI) correlates spectral
patterns within each segment rather than each band separately, and predicts intelligibility better
<Cite id="jensen2016" />.

## Pitfalls

- **Both need a clean, aligned reference.** They cannot score real recordings with no clean version; non-intrusive
  metrics such as those in <NoteLink to="learned-speech-quality-metrics">learned speech quality metrics</NoteLink>
  fill that gap.
- **Use the standard implementation and state the mode.** PESQ narrowband and wideband scores differ, and
  re-implementations of PESQ and STOI disagree with the reference code by amounts larger than many reported
  improvements.
- **Designed for particular conditions.** PESQ was validated on telephone speech and codecs; its correlation with
  listeners for the artefacts of modern neural enhancement is weaker, which is one reason learned metrics and
  listening tests are reported alongside it.
- **Quality and intelligibility differ.** Aggressive noise suppression can raise PESQ while lowering intelligibility,
  or the reverse. Report both when both matter.
