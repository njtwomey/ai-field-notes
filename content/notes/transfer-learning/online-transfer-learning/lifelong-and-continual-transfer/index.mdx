<Definition>
**Lifelong learning** is learning from a sequence of tasks $\Tcal_1, \Tcal_2, \dots$, one after another, with the aim
that experience with earlier tasks makes later ones easier to learn <Cite id="thrun1998" />. Its transfer is measured in
two directions. **Forward transfer** is the effect of learning earlier tasks on performance on a new task before and while
it is learnt. **Backward transfer** is the effect of learning a new task on performance on earlier ones; negative backward
transfer is forgetting <Cite id="lopezpaz2017" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Transfer learning usually has one source and one target. Lifelong learning makes every task a target when it arrives and a
source afterwards. A learner that only fine-tunes on each task in turn gets forward transfer for free, since each task
starts from the previous weights, and pays for it with forgetting. A learner that freezes what it has learnt cannot forget
but may not transfer. How the continual learner avoids forgetting is covered in <NoteLink to="continual-learning" />; this
note concerns how it transfers.

## Measuring transfer

Both directions are read off the **accuracy matrix** $R_{i,j}$, the test accuracy on task $j$ after training on tasks
$1, \dots, i$. Backward transfer averages $R_{T,j} - R_{j,j}$, the change in each task's accuracy between just after it
was learnt and the end. Forward transfer averages $R_{j-1,j} - \bar b_j$, the accuracy on task $j$ before training on it
minus that of an untrained model <Cite id="lopezpaz2017" />. Definitions, a worked example and the related forgetting
measure are in <NoteLink to="evaluating-continual-learners" />. Two points matter for transfer. The forward-transfer
summary measures zero-shot transfer only. Transfer *during* learning shows on the diagonal: comparing $R_{j,j}$ with the
accuracy of a learner trained on task $j$ alone measures whether earlier tasks helped task $j$ to be learnt, and a
shortfall is <NoteLink to="negative-transfer" />.

Díaz-Rodríguez et al. argue that accuracy and forgetting are not enough. They add memory overhead and computational
efficiency to accuracy and to forward and backward transfer, and combine the metrics into one score for ranking, so that
methods that buy transfer with ever-larger models are compared fairly <Cite id="diazrodriguez2018" />.

## Methods built for forward transfer

### A shared basis: ELLA

The efficient lifelong learning algorithm models each task's parameters as a sparse combination of a shared basis,
$\thetavec_t = \Lmat \svec_t$, with $\Lmat \in \reals^{d \times k}$ shared and $\svec_t \in \reals^k$ sparse
<Cite id="ruvolo2013ella" />. The multi-task objective over $T$ tasks is
$$
\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \min_{\svec_t} \brack{\frac{1}{n_t} \sum_{i=1}^{n_t} \ell\paren{f(\xvec_{ti}; \Lmat \svec_t),
y_{ti}} + \mu \norm{\svec_t}_1} + \lambda \norm{\Lmat}_F^2 .
$$
Optimising it exactly would revisit every task's data whenever $\Lmat$ changes. ELLA removes that need with a second-order
<NoteLink to="taylor-series" /> expansion of each task's loss around its single-task optimum $\hat\thetavec_t$. The
gradient vanishes there, so
$$
\frac{1}{n_t} \sum_i \ell\paren{f(\xvec_{ti}; \thetavec), y_{ti}} \approx \text{const} + \norm{\thetavec -
\hat\thetavec_t}_{\Dmat_t}^2, \qquad \norm{\vvec}_{\Dmat}^2 = \vvec\transpose \Dmat \vvec,
$$
where $\Dmat_t$ is half the Hessian of the task's average loss at $\hat\thetavec_t$. Each task is then summarised by the
pair $(\hat\thetavec_t, \Dmat_t)$, and the objective becomes
$$
\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t} \min_{\svec_t} \brack{\norm{\hat\thetavec_t - \Lmat \svec_t}_{\Dmat_t}^2 + \mu \norm{\svec_t}_1} +
\lambda \norm{\Lmat}_F^2 .
$$
When task $t$ arrives, ELLA fits $\hat\thetavec_t$ and $\Dmat_t$, computes $\svec_t$ by a lasso problem with $\Lmat$ fixed,
and updates $\Lmat$ in closed form from the accumulated sufficient statistics. Earlier codes $\svec_{t'}$ are not
recomputed. Forward transfer comes from the basis: a new task is expressed in terms of components learnt from old ones.
Backward transfer comes from updating $\Lmat$, which changes every earlier task's parameters $\Lmat \svec_{t'}$. The
Hessian weighting is the same device as in elastic weight consolidation; see <NoteLink
to="regularisation-based-continual-learning" />.

### Progressive networks

Progressive neural networks add a new column of layers for each task and freeze the columns of earlier tasks
<Cite id="rusu2016" />. Each layer of the new column receives, besides its own previous layer, lateral connections from
the corresponding layers of all earlier columns. Freezing makes forgetting impossible, and the lateral connections let the
new task reuse any earlier feature, which gives forward transfer. The cost is growth: parameters grow linearly with the
number of tasks, and lateral connections grow quadratically. See <NoteLink to="replay-and-architectural-methods" />.

### Other routes

- **Fine-tune in sequence** gives forward transfer and maximal forgetting; it is the baseline for FWT and BWT.
- **Hierarchical priors updated online** carry forward what tasks have in common while leaving each task its own
  parameters; see <NoteLink to="hierarchical-bayesian-transfer" />.
- **Meta-learning** across the sequence learns an initialisation from which later tasks are fast to learn; see <NoteLink
  to="meta-learning" />.
- **Continual test-time adaptation** faces the unlabelled version of the problem: a deployed model adapts to a target
  distribution that keeps changing, and must not drift away from what it knew <Cite id="wang2022cotta" />; see <NoteLink
  to="test-time-adaptation" />.

## Assumptions

- Tasks are related enough that earlier ones help later ones. For unrelated sequences, forward transfer is zero or
  negative, and the best a lifelong learner can do is not to interfere.
- Task boundaries are known, or can be detected. The metrics above need them.

## Pitfalls

- **Reporting only final accuracy.** $\text{ACC}$ alone cannot distinguish a learner that transferred and forgot from one
  that neither transferred nor forgot.
- **Ignoring growth.** Methods that add parameters per task trivially avoid forgetting; compare at equal memory
  <Cite id="diazrodriguez2018" />.
- **Evaluating on the training order only.** Transfer depends on the order of tasks. Averages over several orders are more
  informative.
