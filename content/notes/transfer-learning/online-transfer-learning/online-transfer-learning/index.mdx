<Definition>
In **online transfer learning** a classifier $h$ has been trained on a source domain, and target examples $(\xvec_t,
y_t)$, $y_t \in \set{-1, +1}$, arrive one at a time. At each round the learner predicts $y_t$, then sees it and updates.
The goal is to make few mistakes on the target stream by using $h$ as well as the target data. Zhao and Hoi's **OTL**
algorithm predicts with a weighted combination of $h$ and a target classifier $f_t$ learnt online, and adjusts the two
weights by exponential weighting <Cite id="zhao2010otl" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

Offline transfer assumes the target data are available before training. In many applications they arrive over time: a
new user's activity labels, a new customer's transactions, a new sensor's readings. The source classifier is useful at
the start, when the target learner has seen nothing, and may become useless later if the target differs. The learner does
not know in advance how related the domains are. It needs a rule that trusts the source while it is better and stops
trusting it when it is not, with a guarantee that holds for any sequence.

## Homogeneous OTL

When the source and target share the input space, OTL keeps the source classifier $h$ fixed and learns a new classifier
$f_t$ on the target stream alone, by the passive-aggressive algorithm <Cite id="crammer2006" /> (see <NoteLink
to="online-learning-for-linear-models" />). Scores are mapped to $[0, 1]$ by $\Pi(z) = \max\set{0, \min\set{1, (z + 1)/2}}$,
and the label $y \in \set{-1, 1}$ to $\Pi(y) \in \set{0, 1}$. The prediction is
$$
\hat y_t = \sgn\paren{w_{1,t}\, \Pi(h(\xvec_t)) + w_{2,t}\, \Pi(f_t(\xvec_t)) - \tfrac12},
$$
with $w_{1,1} = w_{2,1} = 1/2$. After the label is revealed, each classifier suffers the squared loss $\ell_t(g) =
\paren{\Pi(g(\xvec_t)) - \Pi(y_t)}^2$ and the weights are updated by
$$
w_{1,t+1} = \frac{w_{1,t}\, e^{-\eta \ell_t(h)}}{w_{1,t}\, e^{-\eta \ell_t(h)} + w_{2,t}\, e^{-\eta \ell_t(f_t)}}, \qquad
w_{2,t+1} = 1 - w_{1,t+1},
$$
with $\eta = 1/2$. Unrolling the recursion, $w_{1,t} \propto \exp\paren{-\eta \sum_{s < t} \ell_s(h)}$: each weight
decays exponentially with its classifier's cumulative loss. This is the exponentially weighted average forecaster over two
experts <Cite id="cesabianchi2006" />; see <NoteLink to="hedge-and-exponential-weights" />. Separately, $f_t$ takes a
passive-aggressive step on the hinge loss $[1 - y_t f_t(\xvec_t)]_+$ whenever it is positive, with step size $\tau_t =
\min\set{C, [1 - y_t f_t(\xvec_t)]_+ / \kappa(\xvec_t, \xvec_t)}$ for kernel $\kappa$.

### The loss bound

Write $\Sigma_h = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \ell_t(h)$ and $\Sigma_f = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \ell_t(f_t)$ for the cumulative squared losses,
and $p_t = w_{1,t} \Pi(h(\xvec_t)) + w_{2,t} \Pi(f_t(\xvec_t))$ for the mixed prediction. Zhao and Hoi show
<Cite id="zhao2010otl" />
$$
\sum_{t=1}^{T} \paren{p_t - \Pi(y_t)}^2 \le \min\set{\Sigma_h, \Sigma_f} + 2 \ln 2 .
$$
The proof has two steps.

**Exp-concavity.** For fixed $y \in \set{0, 1}$, the function $g(z) = \exp\paren{-\eta (z - y)^2}$ on $z \in [0, 1]$ has
second derivative $g''(z) = g(z)\paren{4\eta^2 (z - y)^2 - 2\eta}$. Since $\abs{z - y} \le 1$, this is non-positive whenever
$\eta \le 1/2$, so $g$ is concave: the squared loss is **$\eta$-exp-concave** on $[0, 1]$ for $\eta \le 1/2$ (see <NoteLink
to="online-newton-step-and-exp-concavity" />). By Jensen's inequality, for weights summing to one,
$$
e^{-\eta (p_t - \Pi(y_t))^2} \ge w_{1,t}\, e^{-\eta \ell_t(h)} + w_{2,t}\, e^{-\eta \ell_t(f_t)} .
$$

**Telescoping.** Let $W_t = \frac12 e^{-\eta \sum_{s < t} \ell_s(h)} + \frac12 e^{-\eta \sum_{s < t} \ell_s(f_s)}$, so that
$W_1 = 1$ and the right-hand side above equals $W_{t+1} / W_t$. Taking logarithms, $(p_t - \Pi(y_t))^2 \le -\frac{1}{\eta} \ln
(W_{t+1}/W_t)$. Summing over $t$, the sum telescopes:
$$
\sum_{t=1}^{T} (p_t - \Pi(y_t))^2 \le -\frac{1}{\eta} \ln W_{T+1} \le -\frac{1}{\eta} \ln\paren{\tfrac12 e^{-\eta \Sigma_h}} =
\Sigma_h + \frac{\ln 2}{\eta},
$$
because $W_{T+1}$ is at least either of its two terms. The same holds with $\Sigma_f$. With $\eta = 1/2$ the constant is
$2 \ln 2$.

### The mistake bound

A mistake at round $t$ means that $p_t$ lies on the wrong side of $1/2$ from $\Pi(y_t) \in \set{0, 1}$, so $(p_t -
\Pi(y_t))^2 \ge 1/4$. Counting mistakes $M$,
$$
\frac{M}{4} \le \sum_{t} (p_t - \Pi(y_t))^2 \le \min\set{\Sigma_h, \Sigma_f} + 2 \ln 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M \le 4 \min\set{\Sigma_h, \Sigma_f} + 8 \ln 2 .
$$
By the same argument each mistake of $h$ contributes at least $1/4$ to $\Sigma_h$. If the losses on correct rounds are
small, $4 \Sigma_h$ is close to the number of mistakes $M_h$ of $h$, and similarly for $f$, so OTL makes about as many
mistakes as the better of the two, plus $8 \ln 2 \approx 5.5$ <Cite id="zhao2010otl" />. It does not need to know in
advance which one that is. A useless source costs at most a constant number of mistakes, which bounds <NoteLink
to="negative-transfer" />.

For a large shift the weight moves to $f$ almost at once. When the target concept later returns to the source concept,

## Heterogeneous OTL

Zhao and Hoi also treat a target whose features extend the source's: $\xvec_t = (\xvec_t^{(1)}, \xvec_t^{(2)})$, where
the first view lives in the source input space and the second is new <Cite id="zhao2010otl" />. **Co-regularised OTL**
learns a classifier on each view, predicts with their average, $\hat y_t = \sgn\paren{\frac12 \paren{f_t^{(1)}(\xvec_t^{(1)}) +
f_t^{(2)}(\xvec_t^{(2)})}}$, and initialises $f_1^{(1)} = h$ and $f_1^{(2)} = 0$. Each round solves
$$
\min_{f^{(1)}, f^{(2)}} \frac{\gamma_1}{2} \norm{f^{(1)} - f_t^{(1)}}^2 + \frac{\gamma_2}{2} \norm{f^{(2)} - f_t^{(2)}}^2 +
C \ell_t, \qquad \ell_t = \brack{1 - y_t\, \tfrac12\paren{f^{(1)}(\xvec_t^{(1)}) + f^{(2)}(\xvec_t^{(2)})}}_+ ,
$$
which keeps both views close to their previous values while correcting the latest mistake. With kernels $\kappa_1,
\kappa_2$ and $k_i = \kappa_i(\xvec_t^{(i)}, \xvec_t^{(i)})$, the solution is
$$
f_{t+1}^{(i)} = f_t^{(i)} + \frac{\tau_t}{2\gamma_i}\, y_t\, \kappa_i(\xvec_t^{(i)}, \cdot), \qquad \tau_t = \min\set{C,
\frac{4 \gamma_1 \gamma_2\, \ell_t}{k_1 \gamma_2 + k_2 \gamma_1}} .
$$

<Derivation title="The step size of co-regularised OTL">
Consider the update direction $f^{(i)} = f_t^{(i)} + a_i\, y_t\, \kappa_i(\xvec_t^{(i)}, \cdot)$, which the representer
theorem guarantees. The penalty is $\frac{\gamma_1}{2} a_1^2 k_1 + \frac{\gamma_2}{2} a_2^2 k_2$, and the new averaged margin
is the old one plus $\frac12(a_1 k_1 + a_2 k_2)$. For a given increase in margin, minimising the penalty sets $a_i \propto
1/\gamma_i$; write $a_i = \tau / (2\gamma_i)$. The margin then rises by $\frac{\tau}{4}\paren{k_1/\gamma_1 +
k_2/\gamma_2}$. Without the cap $C$, the smallest step that removes the loss $\ell_t$ satisfies
$$
\frac{\tau}{4}\paren{\frac{k_1}{\gamma_1} + \frac{k_2}{\gamma_2}} = \ell_t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tau = \frac{4 \gamma_1 \gamma_2\, \ell_t}{k_1 \gamma_2 + k_2 \gamma_1} .
$$
The cap $C$ limits the step on a single noisy example, as in the PA-I variant of passive-aggressive learning
<Cite id="crammer2006" />.
</Derivation>

The mistake bound compares the algorithm with any fixed pair of classifiers $(g^{(1)}, g^{(2)})$ <Cite id="zhao2010otl" />.
If $k_1 \le R_1$ and $k_2 \le R_2$ on every round and $\tau = \min\set{C, 4\gamma_1\gamma_2 / (R_1 \gamma_2 + R_2
\gamma_1)}$, then
$$
M \le \frac{1}{\tau}\paren{\gamma_1 \norm{h - g^{(1)}}^2 + \gamma_2 \norm{g^{(2)}}^2 + 2C \sum_{t=1}^{T} \ell_t(g^{(1)},
g^{(2)})} ,
$$
where $\ell_t(g^{(1)}, g^{(2)})$ is the averaged hinge loss of the comparator. Starting the first view at $h$ replaces the
usual $\norm{g^{(1)}}^2$ by $\norm{h - g^{(1)}}^2$. The source helps exactly as much as it is close to a good classifier
on the shared features. A later journal version extends the analysis and experiments <Cite id="zhao2014otl" />.

## Bayesian online transfer

A Bayesian model gives a different route to the same goal: use the source posterior as the target prior and update it
online as target labels arrive. Assumed-density filtering keeps a Gaussian approximation to the posterior and projects it
back after each example <Cite id="opper1998" /> (see <NoteLink to="sequential-bayesian-updating" />). In the community Bayes
point machine of Diethe, Twomey and Flach, a new person's classifier starts from the posterior over weights learnt from
other people and is updated this way after each label. Evaluated as an online learner, which predicts each example before
its label is revealed, this gave higher cumulative accuracy than the same model started from a generic prior
<Cite id="diethe2016active" />, and on real accelerometer data it predicted with about 70% to 75% accuracy before its
first target label <Cite id="diethe2016active" /> <Cite id="twomey2015evidence" />. See <NoteLink
to="active-transfer-learning" />, where the same online learner also chooses which labels to request.

The two routes weigh the source differently. OTL weights the source *classifier* by its observed losses and can discard
it entirely. The Bayesian route weights the source *prior* by the amount of target data, and never discards it, but its
influence shrinks as the target likelihood grows.

## Trade-offs

- **The source is fixed.** OTL never updates $h$. If the source is partly right, a mixture of a fixed $h$ and a new $f$
  cannot express "$h$ with a correction"; heterogeneous OTL's initialisation of a view at $h$ can.
- **Slow switching.** Cumulative-loss weights adapt slowly after a change in which classifier is better. Sharing or
  discounting the weights trades this against noise.
- **Squared loss on clipped scores.** The bound concerns losses of predictions mapped to $[0, 1]$. A classifier with
  small margins has non-zero loss even when it is right, so $4\Sigma$ can exceed its mistake count.
