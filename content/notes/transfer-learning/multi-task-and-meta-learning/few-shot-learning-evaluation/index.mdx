<Definition>
**Few-shot evaluation** measures how well a method learns classes it has not seen. The classes of a dataset are split
into disjoint meta-training, meta-validation and meta-test sets. An **episode** samples $N$ classes from one split, $K$
labelled support examples per class and a number of query examples per class. The method adapts on the support set and
is scored on the query set. The reported figure is the mean query accuracy over many meta-test episodes, usually 600 or
more, with a 95% confidence interval <Cite id="vinyals2016" /> <Cite id="ravi2017" />.
</Definition>

Omniglot was introduced as a test of human-like concept learning <Cite id="lake2015" />; miniImageNet by Vinyals et
al. <Cite id="vinyals2016" />, with the class split of Ravi and Larochelle now standard <Cite id="ravi2017" />.
Meta-Dataset varies the number of classes and examples per episode and draws them from ten sources, so a method cannot
tune to one configuration <Cite id="triantafillou2020" />. The cross-domain benchmark of Guo et al. measures what happens
when the new classes come from a different kind of image <Cite id="guo2020cdfsl" />.

## Reporting uncertainty

Each episode gives an accuracy $a_e$. Over $E$ independent episodes with mean $\bar a$ and standard deviation $s$, the
mean has standard error $s/\sqrt{E}$, and the usual interval is
$$
\bar a \pm 1.96 \frac{s}{\sqrt{E}} .
$$
The $1.96$ is the $97.5$th percentile of the standard normal distribution, justified by the central limit theorem for
the average of independent episode accuracies (see <NoteLink to="confidence-intervals" />).

### Worked example

With $E = 600$ episodes and a per-episode standard deviation of $8$ percentage points, typical of 5-way 5-shot
miniImageNet, the interval half-width is $1.96 \times 8 / \sqrt{600} = 0.64$ points. For 1-shot episodes, whose
accuracies vary more, a standard deviation of $20$ points gives $1.60$ points. Two methods reported at $60.2 \pm 0.6$
and $60.8 \pm 0.6$ are not distinguished by these intervals. Evaluating both methods on the same episodes and computing
the interval of the per-episode difference is more sensitive, because the difficulty of an episode affects both
methods alike.

## Baselines that must be reported

Several studies found that elaborate meta-learning was matched by simple transfer.

- **Pretrain and fit a linear head.** Chen et al. trained a network by ordinary classification on all meta-training
  classes, then fitted a new linear or cosine-similarity classifier on each episode's support set. With deeper
  backbones the differences between methods shrank, and in a cross-domain setting, miniImageNet to birds, the baseline
  compared favourably with meta-learning methods <Cite id="chen2019closer" />.
- **Pretrain and use logistic regression.** Tian et al. found that a representation learnt by supervised or
  self-supervised training on the meta-training set, followed by logistic regression on the support set, outperformed
  the meta-learning methods of the time <Cite id="tian2020rethinking" />.
- **Fine-tune.** Dhillon et al. showed that fine-tuning a network trained with cross-entropy is a strong baseline, and
  that fine-tuning transductively, using the unlabelled query examples as well, beat the published results on four
  benchmarks with one set of hyperparameters <Cite id="dhillon2020" />.
- **Under domain shift.** On the cross-domain benchmark, all meta-learning methods tested underperformed simple
  fine-tuning, by $12.8$ points of accuracy on average <Cite id="guo2020cdfsl" />.

These results do not show that meta-learning is useless. They show that the representation matters more than the
adaptation rule on current benchmarks, and that a method must beat a well-tuned transfer baseline with the same backbone
before its gains can be credited to meta-learning.

## Pitfalls

- **Transductive evaluation without saying so.** Batch normalisation computed on the query batch shares information
  among query examples, which makes the evaluation transductive. Nichol et al. note that MAML was evaluated this way, and
  found that transduction raised accuracy in every case they tested; transductive and inductive results are not
  comparable <Cite id="nichol2018reptile" />.
- **Different backbones.** A deeper backbone can add more accuracy than the method being proposed. Compare methods
  with the same backbone and training budget.
- **Tuning on meta-test classes.** Hyperparameters, including the number of inner steps and the stopping time, must be
  chosen on the meta-validation classes.
- **Overlap between splits.** Meta-test classes that are near-duplicates of meta-training classes, or a pretrained
  backbone trained on data containing the meta-test classes, turn a few-shot test into a many-shot one.
- **One configuration.** Results at 5-way 1-shot and 5-way 5-shot say little about 20-way or imbalanced support sets
  <Cite id="triantafillou2020" />.
