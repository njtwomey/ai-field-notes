<Definition>
The **mean map** (MeanMap) estimates the average feature vector of each class from the average feature vector of each
bag and the bags' known proportions, and plugs the estimate into the likelihood of a conditional exponential family
<Cite id="quadrianto2009" />. **Inverse calibration** (InvCal) replaces each bag with its mean and fits a support vector
regression whose output, passed through a sigmoid, matches the bag's proportion <Cite id="ruping2010" />. Both learn an
instance classifier from label proportions without guessing instance labels.
</Definition>

## Problem

Maximum-likelihood training of a linear-in-parameters classifier needs a statistic of the labels, not the labels
themselves. The mean map exploits this. Write a conditional exponential family with joint feature map
$\phi(\xvec, y)$ and parameter $\thetavec$:

$$
p(y \mid \xvec, \thetavec) = \exp\LP\phi(\xvec, y)^\top\thetavec - g(\thetavec \mid \xvec)\RP, \qquad
g(\thetavec \mid \xvec) = \log\sum_{y}\exp\LP\phi(\xvec, y)^\top\thetavec\RP.
$$

The regularised negative log-likelihood of labelled data $\set{(\xvec_i, y_i)}_{i=1}^{n}$ is

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} g(\thetavec \mid \xvec_i) - n\,\muvec_{XY}^\top\thetavec + \lambda\norm{\thetavec}^2, \qquad
\muvec_{XY} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi(\xvec_i, y_i).
$$

The labels enter only through $\muvec_{XY}$. The log-partition terms need the inputs alone. An estimate of
$\muvec_{XY}$ is therefore enough to fit $\thetavec$ <Cite id="quadrianto2009" />.

## Mechanism: the mean map

**Step 1: bag means.** For bag $k$ and each candidate label $y'$, compute
$\muvec^{\text{set}}[k, y'] = \frac{1}{\abs{B_k}}\sum_{\xvec \in B_k}\phi(\xvec, y')$. No labels are needed.

**Step 2: invert the mixing.** Assume that an instance's distribution given its class does not depend on its bag,
$p(\xvec \mid y, k) = p(\xvec \mid y)$. Then $p(\xvec \mid k) = \sum_y \pi_{k,y}\,p(\xvec \mid y)$, and taking the
expectation of $\phi(\xvec, y')$ on both sides gives

$$
\muvec^{\text{set}}[k, y'] = \sum_{y}\pi_{k,y}\,\muvec^{\text{class}}[y, y'], \qquad
\muvec^{\text{class}}[y, y'] = \expect_{\xvec \sim p(\xvec \mid y)}\LB\phi(\xvec, y')\RB.
$$

In matrix form $\muvec^{\text{set}} = \Pimat\,\muvec^{\text{class}}$, with $\Pimat$ the $K \times c$ matrix of proportions.
If $\Pimat$ has full column rank,

$$
\hat{\muvec}^{\text{class}} = (\Pimat^\top\Pimat)^{-1}\Pimat^\top\muvec^{\text{set}}.
$$

**Step 3: reassemble.** With class prior $p(y)$ (the overall proportion),
$\hat{\muvec}_{XY} = \sum_{y} p(y)\,\hat{\muvec}^{\text{class}}[y, y]$.

**Step 4: fit.** Minimise $\sum_i g(\thetavec \mid \xvec_i) - n\,\hat{\muvec}_{XY}^\top\thetavec + \lambda\norm{\thetavec}^2$,
a convex problem. Kernels enter through $\phi$ in the usual way.

Quadrianto et al. prove uniform convergence of the estimate under the independence assumption
<Cite id="quadrianto2009" />. The assumption is the method's weak point: when bags are formed by a variable correlated
with $\xvec$, the class means differ across bags and Step 2 averages them wrongly.

## Mechanism: the Laplacian mean map

For binary labels $y \in \set{-1, +1}$ and linear models, Patrini, Nock, Rivera and Caetano show that the **mean
operator** $\muvec_S = \frac{1}{m}\sum_i y_i\xvec_i$ is a minimal sufficient statistic for the labels for a family of
symmetric proper scoring losses including the logistic and square losses <Cite id="patrini2014" />. Estimating
$\muvec_S$ needs the $2K$ bag-and-class means $\bvec_k^{+}$ and $\bvec_k^{-}$. Each bag gives one equation,
$\bvec_k = \pi_k\bvec_k^{+} + (1 - \pi_k)\bvec_k^{-}$, so the system has $K$ equations and $2K$ unknowns. The mean map
closes it by assuming $\bvec_k^{\pm}$ are the same for every bag. The **Laplacian mean map** (LMM) instead solves the
underdetermined system with a penalty $\gamma\trace(\Xmat^\top\Lmat\Xmat)$, where $\Lmat$ is a graph Laplacian over
bags: similar bags are asked to have similar class means rather than identical ones. The solution is closed-form. The
**alternating mean map** (AMM) starts from the LMM solution and alternates between fitting the classifier and
re-estimating the mean operator.

## Mechanism: inverse calibration

Platt scaling calibrates a classifier by passing its decision value through a sigmoid. Rüping inverts the process
<Cite id="ruping2010" />. For a bag with proportion $\pi_k$, the target decision value is the logit
$y_k = \sigma^{-1}(\pi_k) = -\log(1/\pi_k - 1)$, with $\pi_k$ clipped to $[\varepsilon, 1 - \varepsilon]$. The linear
model $f(\xvec) = \wvec^\top\xvec + b$ is required to hit the target on average over the bag:

$$
\min_{\wvec, b, \xivec, \xivec^*}\ \frac{1}{2}\norm{\wvec}^2 + C\sum_{k}(\xi_k + \xi_k^*)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
y_k - \varepsilon_k - \xi_k \le \frac{1}{\abs{B_k}}\sum_{j \in B_k}(\wvec^\top\xvec_j + b) \le y_k + \varepsilon_k + \xi_k^*.
$$

This is $\varepsilon$-insensitive support vector regression with one super-instance per bag. The tolerance is set on
the probability scale, $\abs{\sigma(y) - \pi_k} \le \varepsilon$. A first-order Taylor expansion of
$\sigma^{-1}(\pi_k + \varepsilon)$ about $\pi_k$ gives the logit-scale tolerance

$$
\varepsilon_k = \frac{\varepsilon}{\pi_k(1 - \pi_k)},
$$

wider for bags near 0 or 1, where the logit is steep. The dual depends on the data only through
$\frac{1}{\abs{B_k}\abs{B_l}}\sum_{i \in B_k, j \in B_l}K(\xvec_i, \xvec_j)$, so kernels apply.

## Cost

The mean map needs one pass over the data for the bag means and a $c \times c$ solve. The final fit costs the same as
an ordinary conditional exponential-family fit. InvCal is an SVR over $K$ super-instances, cheap when bags are large.
LMM solves one $2K \times 2K$ system.

## Trade-offs

- **Assumptions.** The mean map requires bag-independent class-conditionals. LMM weakens this to smoothness across
  similar bags. InvCal assumes that a bag's mean decision value determines its proportion, which fails when the data
  in a bag have high variance or the bag distribution changes with the bag <Cite id="poyiadzi2018" />.
- **Multiclass.** The mean map handles $c$ classes directly through $\phi(\xvec, y)$. InvCal is binary.
- **Control of bag proportions.** Neither method constrains its predictions on the training bags to reproduce the
  given proportions, unlike ∝SVM or <NoteLink to="llp-label-propagation">LP-LLP</NoteLink>.
- **Empirical record.** In Patrini et al.'s experiments on fourteen domains, LMM and AMM tended to outperform MeanMap,
  InvCal and ∝SVM, and on the largest domains half a dozen bag proportions sufficed <Cite id="patrini2014" />.
