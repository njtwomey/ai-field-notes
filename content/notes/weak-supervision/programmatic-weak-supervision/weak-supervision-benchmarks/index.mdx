<Definition>
**WRENCH** (<NoteLink to="weak-supervision">Weak supervision</NoteLink> bENCHmark) is a benchmark platform for weak supervision by Zhang, Yu, Li, Wang, Yang, Yang
and Ratner <Cite id="zhang2021wrench" />. It fixes the three things that made earlier comparisons unreliable: the
datasets, the weak supervision sources applied to them, and the evaluation protocol. It includes 22 real-world datasets
for classification and sequence tagging, real <Gloss name="labelling-function">labelling functions</Gloss>, synthetic and procedural labelling-function
generators, and modular implementations of label models, end models and joint models.
</Definition>

## Why a benchmark was needed

Zhang et al. identify three problems in the earlier literature <Cite id="zhang2021wrench" />.

- **Private and custom datasets.** Weak supervision is motivated by production needs, so many evaluations used
  data that could not be shared.
- **Hidden source variance.** A weak supervision dataset is defined by its inputs, its labels **and** its labelling
  functions. Two papers could report on datasets with the same name but entirely different labelling functions, and
  hence entirely different problems.
- **Protocol variance.** A two-stage pipeline can be evaluated at the label model or at the end model, with hard or
  soft labels, on covered points only or on all points. Papers differed on all of these.

## What is compared

WRENCH treats a weak supervision method as a composition of modules <Cite id="zhang2021wrench" />:

| Task | Module | Methods |
| ---- | ------ | ------- |
| Classification | Label model | majority vote (MV), weighted MV, Dawid–Skene (DS), <Gloss name="data-programming">data programming (DP)</Gloss>, MeTaL, <NoteLink to="label-model-estimation">FlyingSquid</NoteLink> (FS) |
| Classification | End model | <NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink>, <Gloss name="mlp" />, <Gloss name="bert" />, <Gloss name="roberta" />, COSINE-BERT, COSINE-RoBERTa |
| Classification | Joint model | Denoise |
| Sequence tagging | Label model | <Gloss name="hmm" />, conditional HMM (CHMM), plus the classification label models applied per token |
| Sequence tagging | End model | LSTM-CNNs-CRF, BERT |
| Sequence tagging | Joint model | Consensus Network (ConNet) |

A **two-stage** method pairs a label model with an end model trained on its output. A **joint** (one-stage) method
trains both together. Every pairing, with hard and with soft labels, gives more than 120 method variants: 83 for
classification and 46 for sequence tagging. A **gold** baseline trains each end model on the true labels.

## Synthetic and procedural labelling functions

The synthetic generator draws LF outputs from the conditionally independent model, with control over the mean and the
spread of the LFs' accuracies and propensities (how often they vote). Label models diverge from each other when the
spread of accuracies is large or the propensity is small; with high propensity they converge <Cite id="zhang2021wrench" />.

The procedural generator builds LFs from a labelled dataset and a feature lexicon, for example one LF per $n$-gram and
label, and keeps those meeting thresholds on accuracy, pairwise correlation or data dependence (accuracy that varies
across clusters of the data). On Yelp and YouTube, label models performed alike on the most accurate LFs, but differed
widely on correlated or data-dependent LFs, where DP, MeTaL and FS clearly outperformed simpler models. Even for one
type of LF, the ranking of label models changed between datasets.

The authors draw these conclusions <Cite id="zhang2021wrench" />.

- **No method consistently wins.** The best weak supervision method differs across datasets. Designing a general-purpose
  method remains open.
- **MeTaL and majority vote are the label models most worth trying** for classification, averaged over datasets and end
  models. Deeper end models are better, and fine-tuned pre-trained <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss> beat applying the label model to the
  test data directly on most text tasks.
- **Source quality dominates.** Both label and end models do well only when the labelling functions are reasonably
  good. With very noisy LFs (Basketball) or very low coverage (MIT-Restaurants), a large gap to full supervision
  remains, so LF quality should be checked before trusting any weak supervision model.
- **Soft labels help deeper end models,** an effect the authors relate to <NoteLink to="cross-entropy-and-perplexity">label smoothing</NoteLink>.
- **Uncovered data should be used.** Training the end model only on points that received at least one vote discards
  data; COSINE, which also uses uncovered points, did well.
- **For sequence tagging,** HMM-based label models, which model dependencies between tokens, were best on 7 of 8
  datasets; CHMM, which also uses the input features, had the best average. On MIT-Restaurants, where the LFs cover
  little, majority vote was better than the probabilistic models. The tagging scheme (IO against BIO) changed F1 by up
  to 10%.

## How to read label-model comparisons

- **Report the labelling functions.** A comparison is about a dataset and a set of LFs. Results do not transfer to a
  different set of LFs on the same inputs.
- **Separate the stages.** Say whether a number is the label model on test data, an end model trained on its labels,
  or a joint model, and compare like with like. WRENCH notes papers that compared an end model coupled with majority
  vote against a label model used alone.
- **Include majority vote.** It is cheap, has no parameters and is often within noise of the best label model. The
  modelling-advantage analysis in <NoteLink to="snorkel" /> explains when weighting can beat it.
- **Include the gold baseline.** The gap to training on true labels is the real measure of how much the weak labels
  cost.

The companion survey by Zhang, Hsieh, Yu, Zhang and Ratner reviews label models, end models, joint models and
related paradigms (semi-supervised, active and <NoteLink to="transfer-learning">transfer learning</NoteLink>) that can be combined with programmatic weak
supervision <Cite id="zhang2022survey" />.
