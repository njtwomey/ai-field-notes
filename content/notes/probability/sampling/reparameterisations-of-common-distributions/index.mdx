<Definition>
A distribution $q_{\phivec}$ is reparameterisable when a sample can be written as $z = g_{\phivec}(\epsilon)$, with
$\epsilon$ drawn from a fixed distribution $p(\epsilon)$ that does not depend on $\phivec$ and $g$ differentiable in
$\phivec$. Then $\nabla_{\phivec} \expect_{q_{\phivec}}[f(z)] = \expect_{p(\epsilon)}[f'(g_{\phivec}(\epsilon))\,
\nabla_{\phivec} g_{\phivec}(\epsilon)]$, estimated by averaging over samples of $\epsilon$ (see the
<NoteLink to="reparameterisation-trick">reparameterisation trick</NoteLink>). This note lists $g$ and
$\partial z / \partial \phivec$ for the distributions met in practice.
</Definition>

## Four routes to a pathwise sample

Every entry below uses one of four constructions.

1. **Location–scale.** If $z = \mu + \sigma \epsilon$ with $\epsilon$ from the standard member of the family, then
   $\partial z / \partial \mu = 1$ and $\partial z / \partial \sigma = \epsilon$.
2. **Quantile transform.** If the cdf $F_{\phivec}$ has a closed-form inverse, $z = F_{\phivec}^{-1}(u)$ with
   $u \sim \Unif(0, 1)$ (see <NoteLink to="inverse-transform-sampling">inverse-transform sampling</NoteLink>).
3. **Composition.** A differentiable transformation of a reparameterisable variable is reparameterisable. The <NoteLink to="derivative">chain rule</NoteLink>
   gives the derivative.
4. **Implicit differentiation.** If only the cdf can be differentiated, hold the uniform $u = F_{\phivec}(z)$ fixed and
   differentiate the identity $F_{\phivec}(z) = u$ with respect to $\phivec$:

$$
\frac{\partial F_{\phivec}}{\partial z}\,\frac{\partial z}{\partial \phivec} + \frac{\partial F_{\phivec}}{\partial \phivec} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\partial z}{\partial \phivec} = -\,\frac{\partial F_{\phivec}(z) / \partial \phivec}{q_{\phivec}(z)} .
$$

The sample $z$ can come from any sampler, including a rejection sampler; only the derivative uses the implicit formula
<Cite id="figurnov2018" />. This is route 2 without inverting the cdf.

## Continuous univariate distributions

| Distribution | Sampler $z = g_{\phivec}(\epsilon)$ | Derivatives | Route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gaussian $\Gauss(\mu, \sigma^2)$ | $\mu + \sigma\epsilon$, $\epsilon \sim \Gauss(0, 1)$ | $\partial_\mu z = 1$, $\partial_\sigma z = \epsilon$ | 1 |
| Log-normal | $\exp(\mu + \sigma\epsilon)$ | $\partial_\mu z = z$, $\partial_\sigma z = z\epsilon$ | 3 |
| Uniform $\Unif(a, b)$ | $a + (b - a)u$ | $\partial_a z = 1 - u$, $\partial_b z = u$ | 1 |
| Exponential $\Exp(\lambda)$ | $-\log(u)/\lambda$ | $\partial_\lambda z = -z/\lambda$ | 2 |
| Laplace$(\mu, b)$ | $\mu - b\,\operatorname{sign}(u - \tfrac12)\log(1 - 2\abs{u - \tfrac12})$ | $\partial_\mu z = 1$, $\partial_b z = (z - \mu)/b$ | 1, 2 |
| Logistic$(\mu, s)$ | $\mu + s\log\big(u/(1-u)\big)$ | $\partial_\mu z = 1$, $\partial_s z = (z - \mu)/s$ | 1, 2 |
| Gumbel$(\mu, \beta)$ | $\mu - \beta\log(-\log u)$ | $\partial_\mu z = 1$, $\partial_\beta z = (z - \mu)/\beta$ | 1, 2 |
| Cauchy$(x_0, \gamma)$ | $x_0 + \gamma\tan\big(\pi(u - \tfrac12)\big)$ | $\partial_{x_0} z = 1$, $\partial_\gamma z = (z - x_0)/\gamma$ | 1, 2 |
| Weibull$(\lambda, k)$ | $\lambda(-\log u)^{1/k}$ | $\partial_\lambda z = z/\lambda$, $\partial_k z = -z\log(z/\lambda)/k$ | 2 |
| Kumaraswamy$(a, b)$ | $\big(1 - (1 - u)^{1/b}\big)^{1/a}$ | closed form by the chain rule | 2 |
| Truncated Gaussian on $[l, r]$ | $\mu + \sigma\Phi^{-1}\big(\Phi(\alpha) + u(\Phi(\beta) - \Phi(\alpha))\big)$ | chain rule through $\Phi$, $\Phi^{-1}$ | 2 |
| Gamma$(\alpha, \beta)$ | any sampler for $\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha, 1)$, then $z/\beta$ | $\partial_\alpha z$ implicit, $\partial_\beta z = -z/\beta$ | 4, 1 |
| Student $t_\nu(\mu, \sigma)$ | $\mu + \sigma\,\epsilon/\sqrt{v/\nu}$, $v \sim \chi^2_\nu = \operatorname{Gamma}(\nu/2, \tfrac12)$ | $\partial_\nu$ through the gamma, implicit | 1, 4 |
| von Mises$(\mu, \kappa)$ | $\mu + $ a sample of $\operatorname{vM}(0, \kappa)$ | $\partial_\kappa z$ implicit | 1, 4 |

In the truncated Gaussian, $\alpha = (l - \mu)/\sigma$ and $\beta = (r - \mu)/\sigma$. The location–scale derivative
$\partial_\sigma z = (z - \mu)/\sigma$ recovers $\epsilon$, so the scale <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> is the standardised sample in every
location–scale family.

**Gamma.** The gamma quantile function has no closed form, and the standard sampler of Marsaglia and Tsang is a
rejection sampler <Cite id="marsaglia2000" />. Two routes give pathwise gradients. The implicit route above needs
$\partial F / \partial \alpha$ of the regularised incomplete <NoteLink to="gamma-function">gamma function</NoteLink>, which libraries compute by series or
numerical differentiation <Cite id="figurnov2018" />. <NoteLink to="rejection-sampling">Rejection-sampling</NoteLink> reparameterisation differentiates through the
accepted proposal of the Marsaglia–Tsang sampler and corrects for the acceptance step <Cite id="naesseth2017" />. Once
$\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha, 1)$ is reparameterised, the rate is a scale: $z/\beta$.

## Multivariate distributions

- **Multivariate Gaussian** $\Gauss(\muvec, \Sigmamat)$: $\zvec = \muvec + \Lmat\epsilonvec$ with $\Lmat\Lmat^\top =
  \Sigmamat$ the <NoteLink to="cholesky-decomposition">Cholesky factor</NoteLink> and $\epsilonvec \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$. Parameterise $\Lmat$ directly as a lower
  triangular matrix with a positive diagonal (for example through $\exp$ or softplus of the diagonal), so every $\Lmat$ is
  valid. Then $\partial \zvec / \partial \muvec = \Imat$ and $\partial z_i / \partial L_{ij} = \epsilon_j$ for $j \le i$.
  A diagonal Gaussian is the special case $\Lmat = \diag(\sigmavec)$; a low-rank-plus-diagonal covariance uses
  $\zvec = \muvec + \Bmat\epsilonvec_1 + \diag(\dvec)\epsilonvec_2$.
- **Dirichlet** $\operatorname{Dir}(\alphavec)$: draw $g_k \sim \operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha_k, 1)$ independently and
  set $z_k = g_k / \sum_j g_j$. The derivative composes the implicit gamma derivatives with the normalisation. The
  **beta** distribution is the two-component case, $z = g_1/(g_1 + g_2)$.
- **Kumaraswamy as a beta substitute.** The Kumaraswamy distribution on $(0, 1)$ has a closed-form quantile function
  and a shape close to the beta. It is used in place of the beta when a simple pathwise sampler matters more than
  conjugacy, as in stick-breaking <Gloss name="vae">variational autoencoders</Gloss> <Cite id="nalisnick2017" />.
- **Normalising flows**: any invertible differentiable map applied to a reparameterised base sample is reparameterised
  (route 3); see <NoteLink to="normalising-flow">normalising flows</NoteLink>.

## Discrete distributions and relaxations

A categorical sample changes by jumps, so $\partial z / \partial \phivec$ is zero almost everywhere and the pathwise
estimator is useless. Three workarounds are standard.

- **Gumbel-softmax (Concrete).** By the <NoteLink to="gumbel-max-trick">Gumbel-max trick</NoteLink>,
  $\argmax_k(\log \pi_k + g_k)$ with $g_k$ independent standard Gumbel variables is an exact categorical sample.
  Replacing the argmax by a softmax with temperature $\tau > 0$,

$$
y_k = \frac{\exp\big((\log\pi_k + g_k)/\tau\big)}{\sum_j \exp\big((\log\pi_j + g_j)/\tau\big)},
$$

  gives a point on the simplex that is differentiable in $\pivec$ and tends to the one-hot sample as $\tau \to 0$
  <Cite id="jang2017" /> <Cite id="maddison2017" />. Small $\tau$ reduces the bias of the relaxation and raises the
  variance of the gradient.
- **Straight-through estimator.** Use the hard one-hot sample in the forward pass and the gradient of the relaxed
  sample in the backward pass. The estimator is biased but often works in practice.
- **Score function.** The <NoteLink to="score-function-estimator">score-function estimator</NoteLink> is unbiased for any
  distribution, discrete or not, at the cost of higher variance; control variates reduce it
  <Cite id="mohamed2020" />.

Mixtures are discrete in their component label. A mixture sample can be made pathwise in the component parameters by
summing over components analytically, or in all parameters by implicit differentiation of the mixture cdf, which is
available in one dimension.

## Worked examples

**Gaussian: pathwise against score function.** For $z \sim \Gauss(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with $\mu = 0.5$ and
$\sigma = 1.5$, the target is $\partial_\sigma \expect[z^2] = \partial_\sigma(\mu^2 + \sigma^2) = 2\sigma = 3$. The
pathwise estimator averages $2z\epsilon$; over 200,000 samples it gives 3.006. The score-function estimator averages
$z^2\big((z - \mu)^2/\sigma^3 - 1/\sigma\big)$ and gives 3.036 from the same samples, with a per-sample variance about
10 times larger.

**Gamma: the implicit derivative.** For $\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha = 2, 1)$ and the sample $z = 1.5$, the cdf is
$u = F(1.5) = 0.442$. The implicit formula gives $\partial z / \partial \alpha = -(\partial F/\partial \alpha)/q(z) =
0.937$. Differentiating the quantile function directly, $\partial F^{-1}(u)/\partial \alpha$ at fixed $u$, gives the same
0.937. Averaged over samples, the implicit derivatives estimate $\partial \expect[z]/\partial \alpha = 1$; 200,000
samples give 1.001.

**Gumbel-softmax.** With $\pivec = (0.2, 0.3, 0.5)$ and Gumbel noise from $u = (0.1, 0.6, 0.35)$, the hard sample is
category 2. The relaxed sample is $(0.076, 0.511, 0.414)$ at $\tau = 1$, $(0.013, 0.595, 0.392)$ at $\tau = 0.5$ and
$(0.000, 0.890, 0.110)$ at $\tau = 0.1$, approaching the one-hot vector $(0, 1, 0)$.

## Pitfalls

- **Constrained parameters.** Scales, rates and Cholesky diagonals must stay positive. Optimise an unconstrained value
  and map it through $\exp$ or softplus; the chain rule adds one factor to each derivative.
- **Heavy tails.** For the Cauchy and Student $t$ with small $\nu$, $f(z)$ may have no finite mean, and neither
  estimator is then meaningful.
- **Relaxation bias.** Gumbel-softmax gradients are gradients of a different, relaxed objective. Anneal $\tau$ or check
  the result against an unbiased estimator.
- **Numerical cdf derivatives.** Implicit gradients for the gamma family near $\alpha \to 0$ are ill-conditioned;
  libraries switch to asymptotic expansions there <Cite id="figurnov2018" />.
