<Definition>
Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be independent with $a_i \le X_i \le b_i$ almost surely, and let $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. For
every $s > 0$,

$$
\prob(S_n - \expect S_n \ge s) \le \exp\paren{-\frac{2s^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n (b_i - a_i)^2}}.
$$

The same bound holds for $\prob(S_n - \expect S_n \le -s)$. If every $X_i \in [a, b]$ and $\bar X_n = S_n/n$, then
$\prob(\abs{\bar X_n - \expect\bar X_n} \ge \epsilon) \le 2\exp(-2n\epsilon^2/(b - a)^2)$ <Cite id="hoeffding1963" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A variable confined to an interval of width $b - a$ has variance at most $(b - a)^2/4$. Hoeffding's inequality says
that such a variable also has tails no heavier than a Gaussian with that variance. The mean of $n$ bounded draws then
behaves, in its tails, like a Gaussian with standard deviation $(b - a)/(2\sqrt n)$. The bound is non-asymptotic: it
holds at every $n$, with no approximation.

The only inputs are the ranges. The bound needs no knowledge of the distributions beyond their support, which makes it
the default tool when a quantity is an average of bounded losses, rewards or indicators.

## Hoeffding's lemma

The inequality follows from a bound on the moment-generating function of one bounded variable.

**Lemma.** If $\expect[X] = 0$ and $a \le X \le b$, then $\expect[e^{tX}] \le e^{t^2(b - a)^2/8}$ for every $t \in
\reals$.

**Proof.** The function $x \mapsto e^{tx}$ is convex, so on $[a, b]$ it lies below the chord joining its endpoint
values:

$$
e^{tx} \le \frac{b - x}{b - a}e^{ta} + \frac{x - a}{b - a}e^{tb}, \qquad a \le x \le b.
$$

Taking expectations and using $\expect[X] = 0$ gives $\expect[e^{tX}] \le \frac{b}{b - a}e^{ta} - \frac{a}{b - a}
e^{tb}$. Write $\theta = -a/(b - a)$, which lies in $[0, 1]$ because $a \le 0 \le b$, and $h = t(b - a)$. Then the
right side equals $e^{L(h)}$ with

$$
L(h) = -\theta h + \log\paren{1 - \theta + \theta e^h}.
$$

Direct differentiation gives $L(0) = 0$, $L'(0) = 0$, and

$$
L''(h) = \frac{\theta e^h}{1 - \theta + \theta e^h}\paren{1 - \frac{\theta e^h}{1 - \theta + \theta e^h}} = u(1 - u)
\le \frac14,
$$

where $u$ is a number in $[0, 1]$. Taylor's theorem with the Lagrange remainder gives $L(h) = L(0) + hL'(0) +
\frac{h^2}{2}L''(\xi) \le h^2/8$ for some $\xi$ between 0 and $h$. Hence $\expect[e^{tX}] \le e^{h^2/8} =
e^{t^2(b - a)^2/8}$.

## Proof of the inequality

Apply the <NoteLink to="chernoff-bounds">Chernoff bound</NoteLink> to $S_n - \expect S_n$. For $t > 0$, Markov's
inequality on $e^{t(S_n - \expect S_n)}$ and independence give

$$
\prob(S_n - \expect S_n \ge s) \le e^{-ts}\prod_{i=1}^n\expect\brack{e^{t(X_i - \expect X_i)}}.
$$

Each centred variable $X_i - \expect X_i$ has mean 0 and lies in an interval of width $b_i - a_i$, so the lemma bounds
each factor:

$$
\prob(S_n - \expect S_n \ge s) \le \exp\paren{-ts + \frac{t^2}{8}\sum_{i=1}^n(b_i - a_i)^2}.
$$

The exponent is a quadratic in $t$, minimised at $t = 4s/\sum_i(b_i - a_i)^2$. Substituting this $t$ gives the
exponent $-2s^2/\sum_i(b_i - a_i)^2$. The lower tail follows by applying the result to $-X_i$, and the two-sided bound
adds the two.

## Comparison with Chebyshev

For the mean of $n$ draws in $[0, 1]$, Chebyshev's inequality with the worst-case variance $1/4$ gives
$\prob(\abs{\bar X_n - \mu} \ge \epsilon) \le 1/(4n\epsilon^2)$, and Hoeffding gives $2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. Setting each
bound equal to $\delta$ and solving for $n$:

$$
n_{\text{Chebyshev}} = \frac{1}{4\delta\epsilon^2}, \qquad n_{\text{Hoeffding}} = \frac{\log(2/\delta)}{2\epsilon^2}.
$$

The dependence on the accuracy $\epsilon$ is the same, $1/\epsilon^2$. The dependence on the confidence is $1/\delta$
for Chebyshev and $\log(1/\delta)$ for Hoeffding. High confidence is cheap under Hoeffding and expensive under
Chebyshev. The ratio of the two sample sizes is $n_{\text{Chebyshev}}/n_{\text{Hoeffding}} =
1/(2\delta\log(2/\delta))$. It is $2.7$ at $\delta = 0.05$ and about $34\,000$ at $\delta = 10^{-6}$.

## Worked example: estimating a coin's bias

How many flips guarantee $\abs{\bar X_n - p} < 0.01$ with probability at least $0.95$? Hoeffding with $b - a = 1$
requires $2e^{-2n(0.01)^2} \le 0.05$:

$$
n \ge \frac{\log(2/0.05)}{2 \times 10^{-4}} = \frac{3.689}{2 \times 10^{-4}} = 18\,445.
$$

Chebyshev requires $n \ge 50\,000$ for the same guarantee. The normal approximation suggests $9\,604$ but is not a
guarantee. The gap between $18\,445$ and $9\,604$ is the price of a bound that holds for every $n$ and every $p$.

Read in the other direction, $n$ observations in $[0, 1]$ give the confidence interval

$$
\bar X_n \pm \sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{2n}},
$$

which covers $\expect[\bar X_n]$ with probability at least $1 - \delta$. With $n = 10\,000$ test examples and
$\delta = 0.05$, a measured accuracy is within $\pm 0.0136$ of the true accuracy.

## In machine learning

- **Test-set error.** A model's test error is a mean of $n$ indicator losses, so it is within
  $\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)/(2n)}$ of the true error with probability $1 - \delta$, provided the model was chosen without
  looking at the test set.
- **Finite hypothesis classes.** Hoeffding's inequality for each hypothesis, combined with a union bound over
  $\abs{\Hcal}$ hypotheses, gives the <NoteLink to="probably-approximately-correct-learning">PAC</NoteLink> bound: with probability
  $1 - \delta$, every $h \in \Hcal$ has $\abs{\hat R(h) - R(h)} \le \sqrt{(\log\abs{\Hcal} + \log(2/\delta))/(2n)}$
  <Cite id="shalevshwartz2014" />.
- **Bandits.** <NoteLink to="upper-confidence-bound-algorithm">UCB1</NoteLink> adds $\sqrt{2\log t/n_a}$ to each arm's mean reward, a Hoeffding
  radius at a confidence level that tightens with time.

## Refinements

- **Variance-aware bounds.** Hoeffding's inequality ignores the variance. When $\var(X_i) = \sigma^2$ is much smaller
  than $(b - a)^2/4$, Bernstein's inequality, $\prob(\bar X_n - \mu \ge \epsilon) \le
  \exp\paren{-n\epsilon^2/(2\sigma^2 + 2(b - a)\epsilon/3)}$, is much tighter <Cite id="boucheron2013" />.
- **Martingales.** The Azuma–Hoeffding inequality replaces independence with martingale differences bounded in
  intervals of the same widths, with the same exponent.
- **Functions of many variables.** <NoteLink to="mcdiarmids-inequality">McDiarmid's inequality</NoteLink> extends the
  bound from sums to any function that changes by a bounded amount when one input changes.
- **Sampling without replacement.** Hoeffding showed that the same bound holds for the mean of a sample drawn without
  replacement from a finite population <Cite id="hoeffding1963" />.

## Assumptions

- **Bounded support**, known in advance. Unbounded variables need a moment-generating-function condition instead,
  such as sub-Gaussian tails.
- **Independence**, or a martingale structure. For dependent sequences such as Markov chain samples, the effective
  sample size is smaller.

## Pitfalls

- **Loose when the variance is small.** For rare events ($p$ near 0) the Bernoulli variance $p(1 - p)$ is far below
  $1/4$, and Bernstein's or the Chernoff KL bound can be orders of magnitude tighter.
- **Data-dependent choices break it.** The bound holds for a fixed function of the data. Selecting the best of many
  models on the same data requires a union bound or a uniform bound.
