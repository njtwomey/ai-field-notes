<Definition>
A **normalising flow** defines a density on $\reals^d$ by transforming a base distribution $p_{\zvec}$, usually
$\Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$, through an invertible, differentiable map $f_{\thetavec} : \reals^d \to \reals^d$:
$\xvec = f_{\thetavec}(\zvec)$ <Cite id="papamakarios2021" />. The map is a composition $f = f_K \circ \dots \circ
f_1$ of simple invertible layers. With $\zvec = f^{-1}(\xvec)$ and intermediate values $\hvec_k$, the log-density is

$$
\log p_{\xvec}(\xvec) = \log p_{\zvec}\paren{f^{-1}(\xvec)} + \sum_{k=1}^K \log\abs{\det
\frac{\partial f_k^{-1}(\hvec_k)}{\partial \hvec_k}},
$$

and the flow is trained by maximising the exact log-likelihood of the data.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Start with a Gaussian cloud and deform space until the cloud has the shape of the data. Where the deformation
compresses volume, probability density rises; where it expands volume, density falls. Invertibility means no
probability mass is created or lost, so the density of any data point can be computed by mapping it back to the base
space and correcting for the change in volume along the way. "Normalising" refers to the reverse direction: the flow
turns the data distribution into a normal one.

## Exact likelihood by change of variables

For invertible $f$ with $\xvec = f(\zvec)$, the <NoteLink to="change-of-variables">change-of-variables
formula</NoteLink> gives

$$
p_{\xvec}(\xvec) = p_{\zvec}\paren{f^{-1}(\xvec)}\, \abs{\det \Jmat_{f^{-1}}(\xvec)},
$$

where $\Jmat_{f^{-1}}$ is the $d \times d$ <NoteLink to="jacobian">Jacobian</NoteLink> of the inverse map. The
determinant measures how $f^{-1}$ scales a small volume around $\xvec$. For a composition, the
<NoteLink to="derivative">chain rule</NoteLink> makes the Jacobian a product of the layers' Jacobians, and the
<NoteLink to="determinant">determinant</NoteLink> of a product is the product of determinants. The log-determinants of
the layers therefore add, which gives the sum in the definition.

Two operations must be cheap for the model to be practical:

- **The inverse**, to map data to the base space for training, and the forward map, to sample.
- **The log-determinant.** For a general $d \times d$ Jacobian it costs $O(d^3)$, far too much for images with
  $d \approx 10^5$.

Flow architectures are designed around these two constraints.

## Coupling layers

A **coupling layer** splits the input into two parts, $\xvec = (\xvec_A, \xvec_B)$, passes $\xvec_A$ through
unchanged, and transforms $\xvec_B$ with parameters computed from $\xvec_A$. RealNVP uses an **affine coupling**
<Cite id="dinh2017" />:

$$
\yvec_A = \xvec_A, \qquad \yvec_B = \xvec_B \odot \exp\paren{\svec(\xvec_A)} + \tvec(\xvec_A),
$$

where $\svec$ (scale) and $\tvec$ (translation) are arbitrary neural networks and $\odot$ is the elementwise product.

**The inverse is explicit.** $\yvec_A$ equals $\xvec_A$, so $\svec$ and $\tvec$ can be recomputed from the output:

$$
\xvec_A = \yvec_A, \qquad \xvec_B = \paren{\yvec_B - \tvec(\yvec_A)} \odot \exp\paren{-\svec(\yvec_A)}.
$$

The networks $\svec$ and $\tvec$ never need to be inverted, so they can be as complex as desired.

**The log-determinant is a sum.** Order the coordinates as $(A, B)$. The Jacobian is block lower triangular:

$$
\frac{\partial \yvec}{\partial \xvec} = \begin{pmatrix} \Imat & \zeros \\ \dfrac{\partial \yvec_B}{\partial \xvec_A} &
\diag\paren{\exp \svec(\xvec_A)} \end{pmatrix}.
$$

The determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, so

$$
\log\abs{\det \frac{\partial \yvec}{\partial \xvec}} = \sum_j s_j(\xvec_A).
$$

The off-diagonal block, which contains the derivatives of the networks, never enters. The cost is linear in $d$.

**Mixing the coordinates.** One coupling layer leaves $\xvec_A$ unchanged, so successive layers alternate which part is
transformed. RealNVP uses checkerboard and channel-wise masks for images, with a multi-scale architecture that factors
out half of the dimensions at intervals <Cite id="dinh2017" />. NICE, the predecessor, used additive coupling
($\svec = \zeros$), which preserves volume <Cite id="dinh2015" />. Glow adds learned invertible $1 \times 1$
convolutions between couplings, a learned permutation of channels whose log-determinant costs $O(c^3)$ for $c$
channels per layer <Cite id="kingma2018" />.

## Worked example

A two-dimensional flow maps data to base space by one affine coupling: $z_1 = x_1$ and $z_2 = x_2\, e^{s(x_1)} +
t(x_1)$. Suppose the networks output $s(1) = 0.5$ and $t(1) = -1$. For $\xvec = (1, 2)$:

- Base point: $z_1 = 1$ and $z_2 = 2e^{0.5} - 1 = 2 \times 1.649 - 1 = 2.297$.
- Log-determinant of the map from $\xvec$ to $\zvec$: $s(x_1) = 0.5$.
- Log-density: $\log \Gauss(1; 0, 1) + \log \Gauss(2.297; 0, 1) + 0.5 = -1.419 - 3.557 + 0.5 = -4.477$.
- Inverse check: $x_2 = (2.297 - (-1))\, e^{-0.5} = 3.297 \times 0.607 = 2.000$.

## Other flow families

- **Autoregressive flows** transform coordinate $j$ with a scale and shift that depend on coordinates $1, \dots, j - 1$.
  Their Jacobian is triangular too. They are exact <NoteLink to="autoregressive-generative-models">autoregressive
  models</NoteLink> with Gaussian conditionals, and one direction (density or sampling) is sequential
  <Cite id="papamakarios2021" />.
- **Flows for variational inference** transform the approximate posterior of a <Gloss name="vae" /> to make it more
  flexible than a diagonal Gaussian <Cite id="rezende2015" />.
- **Continuous flows** define $f$ as the solution of an <Gloss name="ode" />. The
  <NoteLink to="probability-flow-ode">probability flow ODE</NoteLink> of a
  <NoteLink to="score-based-generative-models">score-based diffusion model</NoteLink> is one.

## Pitfalls

- **No <NoteLink to="dimensionality-reduction">dimensionality reduction</NoteLink>.** $\zvec$ has the same dimension as
  $\xvec$, so the latent space is as large as the data and is not a compressed code.
- **Topology is preserved.** A continuous bijection cannot turn one connected Gaussian blob into several disconnected
  clusters. Flows approximate separated modes with thin bridges of low density.
- **Discrete data need dequantisation.** Pixel values are integers. A continuous density on them can grow without
  bound, so uniform noise is added to each value before training <Cite id="papamakarios2021" />.
- **Sample quality lags.** Flows give exact likelihoods, but their samples have generally been less sharp than those of
  <Gloss name="gan" plural /> and <Gloss name="diffusion-model">diffusion models</Gloss> of similar size.
