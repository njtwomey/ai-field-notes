<Definition>
An attention sink is a key position that receives a large fraction of the attention weight in many heads and layers, for
most queries, regardless of its content. In autoregressive <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss> the
sinks are the first few tokens of the sequence <Cite id="xiao2024" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A softmax row is a probability distribution: its weights are positive and sum to 1. A head sometimes has nothing
useful to retrieve for a given query, for example a head that tracks quotation marks when there are none. It cannot
output "no attention". It can, however, put most of its weight on a token whose value vector contributes little, so the
weighted average is close to zero. The model learns to reserve a token for that purpose.

In a causal model the first token is the natural choice. Every later query can see it, while a token at position $t$ is
visible only to queries at $t$ and after. A single consistent sink is therefore easiest to learn at the start
<Cite id="xiao2024" />.

## Details

**Sinks act like <NoteLink to="bias-terms-and-dropout-in-modern-transformers">key biases</NoteLink>.** Gu et al. found
that sink tokens behave like a learned bias on the keys: they collect extra attention while their values contribute
little to the output. The phenomenon emerged after sufficient training, was encouraged by
<Gloss name="weight-decay">weight decay</Gloss>, and disappeared in models up to 1 billion parameters when
<NoteLink to="scaled-dot-product-attention">softmax attention</NoteLink> was replaced by sigmoid attention without
normalisation <Cite id="gu2025sink" />. Sun et al. traced sinks to a few **massive activations**, a few hidden-state
entries about 10,000 times the median magnitude in Llama 2 7B, located at the starting token and a few delimiter tokens
and nearly independent of the input. They act as fixed bias terms and concentrate attention on their tokens
<Cite id="sun2024massive" />.

**Position, not meaning.** Replacing the first four tokens of Llama-2-13B's input with line breaks left the model still
attending heavily to them. Keeping those line breaks in a 1024-token window gave perplexity 5.60, close to the 5.40 with
the original tokens. The sink is defined by its position <Cite id="xiao2024" />.

**A zero sink is softmax off by one.** Adding 1 to the softmax denominator,

$$
\operatorname{softmax}_1(\svec)_j = \frac{e^{s_j}}{1 + \sum_l e^{s_l}},
$$

lets the weights sum to less than 1 <Cite id="miller2023" />. This is exactly ordinary softmax over one extra key with
score 0 and value $\zeros$: that key's term $e^0 = 1$ enters the denominator, and its contribution to the output is
$\frac{1}{1 + \sum_l e^{s_l}} \cdot \zeros = \zeros$. The weight not given to real tokens goes to nothing.

**<Gloss name="vit">Vision transformers</Gloss>.** Darcet et al. found high-norm tokens in low-information background
patches of supervised and self-supervised vision transformers, repurposed for internal computation. Adding a few extra
learnable **register** tokens to the input moved those computations onto the registers and removed the artefacts from
the attention maps <Cite id="darcet2024" />.

## Streaming with sinks

**Window attention fails.** Keeping only the most recent $w$ tokens' keys and values in the
<NoteLink to="key-value-cache">KV cache</NoteLink> bounds memory for long streams. When the first tokens are evicted,
the heads that parked weight on them must redistribute it over the remaining tokens, and the model's outputs change
sharply. For Llama-2-13B with a 1024-token window, perplexity on the first book of PG19 rose to 5158
<Cite id="xiao2024" />.

**StreamingLLM keeps the sinks.** Xiao et al. keep the keys and values of the first four tokens permanently, plus a
rolling window of recent tokens. With four sink tokens and 1020 recent ones, the same Llama-2-13B setting had perplexity
5.40. Positions are assigned by place in the cache, not in the original text, so rotary or <Gloss name="alibi" />
offsets stay within the trained range. Models including Llama-2, MPT, Falcon and Pythia then modelled streams of 4
million tokens with stable perplexity, up to 22.2 times faster per token than recomputing a sliding window
<Cite id="xiao2024" />.

**One sink is enough if trained for.** Llama-2 prefixes documents before chunking, so a random token often sits at
position 0 during training, and the models learned to use several initial tokens as sinks. Xiao et al. pretrained
160-million-parameter models with a dedicated learnable sink token at the start of every sample. Keeping only that token
plus 1023 recent ones gave perplexity 18.01, the same as keeping four initial tokens in the baseline (18.05)
<Cite id="xiao2024" />.

## Worked example

A head at query position 5 has scores $4.0, 0.5, 0.2, 1.0, 0.3$ over keys 1 to 5, where key 1 is a sink with a value
near $\zeros$. The softmax gives weights $0.887, 0.027, 0.020, 0.044, 0.022$. The head's output is essentially
$0.113$ times an average of the real tokens' values: it is mostly idle.

Evict key 1, as window attention does, and renormalise over keys 2 to 5: the weights become
$0.238, 0.176, 0.392, 0.195$. The same head now outputs a full-strength average of the other tokens: their total weight
rises from $0.113$ to 1, a factor of almost 9. Every layer above receives an input unlike those it saw in training.

With $\operatorname{softmax}_1$ over keys 2 to 5 instead, the weights are $0.208, 0.154, 0.342, 0.170$, summing to
$0.874$: the head can stay partly idle without a sink token.

## Assumptions

- The model uses softmax attention. The explanation rests on the constraint that weights sum to 1.
- Sink behaviour is learned. It varies between models, layers and heads, and some heads attend to content as usual.

## Pitfalls

- **Streaming does not extend memory.** StreamingLLM keeps generation stable over long streams, but tokens that have
  left the window are gone. It does not let the model recall them.
- **Positions within the cache.** Reusing the original text positions after eviction creates gaps in the rotary
  offsets. StreamingLLM re-applies <NoteLink to="positional-encoding">position encodings</NoteLink> by cache slot.
- **Sinks cannot be removed after training.** They are part of how a trained model computes. Changing the softmax or
  evicting sink tokens alters every layer's inputs. Alternatives such as a dedicated sink token or sigmoid attention
  must be chosen before pretraining <Cite id="gu2025sink" />.
