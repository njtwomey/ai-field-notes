<Definition>
A **transformer** is a neural network that processes a set or sequence of tokens with a stack of identical blocks.
Each block lets every token gather information from the others by self-attention, then transforms each token
separately with a feed-forward layer; residual connections and normalisation wrap both. Order enters only through
positional encodings, and masks decide which tokens may attend to which <Cite id="vaswani2017" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Transformers replaced recurrent networks as the standard sequence model from 2017, because attention connects any two
positions in one step and the whole sequence is processed in parallel during training. They are the architecture of
<Gloss name="llm">large language models</Gloss>, of most <Gloss name="machine-translation">machine translation</Gloss>
and speech recognition systems, and, through the vision transformer, of much of computer vision. Protein structure
prediction, recommendation over interaction sequences, time-series forecasting and multimodal models use them too
<Cite id="jurafsky2025" />.

The 2017 design has been changed in almost every part since. This topic follows both: the original architecture, and
the choices that have replaced its components in current models.

## Main ideas

**Attention routes information by content.** Each token emits a query, a key and a value. Scaled dot-product attention
gives each query a softmax-weighted average of the values, with weights from query-key similarity, and multi-head
attention runs several such averages in parallel. <NoteLink to="transformer">The original transformer</NoteLink>
assembles these into an <Gloss name="seq2seq">encoder-decoder</Gloss> for translation; its subtopic covers
<NoteLink to="scaled-dot-product-attention" />, <NoteLink to="multi-head-attention" />,
<NoteLink to="causal-masking" />, cross-attention and a hand-computed forward pass.

**Position must be supplied.** Attention ignores order, so a transformer adds position explicitly.
<NoteLink to="positional-encoding">Positional encodings</NoteLink> range from absolute vectors added to the input to
relative schemes, the <Gloss name="rope">rotary position embedding (RoPE)</Gloss> and <Gloss name="alibi" />, and
methods that extend a trained model to longer contexts.

**Masking and stacking define the family.** An <NoteLink to="encoder-only-transformers">encoder-only model</NoteLink>
sees the whole input and suits classification and embeddings; a <NoteLink to="decoder-only-transformers">decoder-only
model</NoteLink> generates text; an encoder-decoder separates reading from writing.
<NoteLink to="transformer-architecture-families" /> compares them and the objectives they train with.

**The block has been redesigned for scale.** Current decoders normalise before each sublayer with RMSNorm, rotate
queries and keys by position, use gated feed-forward layers and drop biases and dropout; the
<NoteLink to="modern-decoder-block" /> assembles these choices. <NoteLink to="attention-variants" /> shrink the
key-value cache with grouped-query and latent attention, bound attention logits, and explain attention sinks.

**Large models need their own training and analysis tools.** <NoteLink to="training-and-analysis-of-transformers" />
covers loss spikes and their fixes, hyperparameter transfer across widths, and the residual stream view used to
interpret what heads compute.
