<Definition>
This topic covers two questions about a transformer once its architecture is fixed. The first is how to train it
reliably as it grows, and how to choose its hyperparameters without tuning each size afresh. The second is what a
trained transformer computes internally: what its <NoteLink to="multi-head-attention">attention heads</NoteLink> do and
how information moves between its layers <Cite id="elhage2021" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A training run for a large model is too expensive to repeat. Settings that train a small transformer well can make a
large one diverge or suffer loss spikes, and a <Gloss name="learning-rate">learning rate</Gloss> tuned at one width is
wrong at another <Cite id="wortsman2023" />. The training notes explain why, and give the fixes now standard in
<Gloss name="llm">large language models</Gloss>.

The analysis notes serve interpretability. They give the vocabulary used to describe circuits inside
<Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss>, such as heads that copy earlier tokens, and show why looking at
attention weights alone explains little. These tools support debugging, auditing and the study of
<Gloss name="icl">in-context learning</Gloss>.

## Main ideas

**Instability has identifiable causes.** <NoteLink to="training-stability-at-scale" /> traces divergence and spikes to
three mechanisms: attention logits that grow until the softmax saturates, output logits drifting under the
shift-invariant softmax, and Adam updates that vanish when gradients fall towards $\epsilon$. The fixes are
QK normalisation, an auxiliary z-loss on the output logits, warmup and gradient clipping.

**Scale initialisation and learning rate with width.** In the standard parameterisation the best learning rate shifts
as a model widens. <NoteLink to="maximal-update-parameterisation" /> scales each layer's initialisation and learning
rate with width so that its features move by a width-independent amount per step. Hyperparameters tuned on a narrow
proxy model then transfer to the full-size model.

**Heads specialise, but weights mislead.** <NoteLink to="attention-head-analysis" /> reviews what individual heads
attend to, such as fixed offsets, syntactic relations and coreference, and finds that most heads can be pruned with
little loss. A head's effect on the output depends on the values it moves as well as on its attention weights, so the
weights alone are a weak explanation.

**The residual stream is a shared workspace.** The <NoteLink to="residual-stream-view" /> treats each attention head
and feed-forward layer as reading from and adding to one vector per token. A head factors into a QK matrix, which
decides where it looks, and an OV matrix, which decides what it writes. The output of an attention-only model expands
into a sum over paths through the heads, which makes circuits such as induction heads visible.
