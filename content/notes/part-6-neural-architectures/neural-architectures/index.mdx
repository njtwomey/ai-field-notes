<Definition>
A **neural architecture** is the arrangement of layers that turns an input into an output in a neural network: which
units connect to which, which weights are shared, and how information flows between positions. All the architectures
in this Part are differentiable functions trained by gradient descent with gradients from backpropagation
<Cite id="goodfellow2016" />. They differ in the structure they assume about their input, and that assumption is what
lets them learn from realistic amounts of data <Cite id="bronstein2021" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Neural networks are the dominant models for images, audio, text, molecules and any other data with rich internal
structure, and they are increasingly used on tables and time series. A practitioner chooses an architecture by
matching it to the data: a grid, a graph, a sequence or a set. The same building blocks recur across choices, so
learning them once pays off everywhere. This Part covers the architectures, the methods that make them trainable, and
the two largest applications of them: <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss> and generative models.

## Main ideas

**Fully connected layers and how to train them.** The <Gloss name="mlp">multilayer perceptron</Gloss> alternates affine
maps and nonlinearities and can approximate any continuous function. Deep networks need careful initialisation,
normalisation, <NoteLink to="residual-connections">residual connections</NoteLink>, adaptive optimisers and
regularisation to train well. <NoteLink to="feedforward-networks" /> covers both, and traces the older
<NoteLink to="energy-based-networks">energy-based networks</NoteLink> from <NoteLink to="hopfield-network">Hopfield
nets</NoteLink> to <NoteLink to="boltzmann-machine">Boltzmann machines</NoteLink>.

**Structure as an assumption.** Each later architecture restricts the fully connected layer to respect a symmetry of
its input. <NoteLink to="convolutional-networks" /> share small filters across positions of a grid, so shifting the
input shifts the output. <NoteLink to="graph-neural-networks" /> aggregate messages from neighbours without regard to
order, so relabelling nodes relabels outputs. Attention treats its input as a set and adds position explicitly.

**Sequences: recurrence, attention and linear recurrence.** <NoteLink to="sequence-models" /> introduces the recurrent
family, which carries a hidden state forward one step at a time and uses gates to keep gradients alive.
<NoteLink to="transformers" /> replace recurrence with self-attention, which connects all positions in one step and
trains in parallel, and the topic follows how the 2017 design has been changed since.
<NoteLink to="efficient-and-state-space-sequence-models" /> recover linear cost in sequence length with sparse or
<NoteLink to="linear-attention">linear attention</NoteLink> and structured state-space layers such as
<Gloss name="mamba" />.

**Language models.** <NoteLink to="large-language-models" /> are <NoteLink to="decoder-only-transformers">decoder-only
transformers</NoteLink> trained to predict the next token at scale. The topic covers decoding and evaluation,
<NoteLink to="prompting">prompting</NoteLink> and retrieval, fine-tuning and preference optimisation, and
<NoteLink to="decision-models">decision models</NoteLink> that read answers from a single forward pass.

**Generative models.** <NoteLink to="deep-generative-models" /> fit a distribution over data and sample from it. The
families differ in whether the likelihood is exact, how fast sampling is, and whether there is a useful latent space:
<NoteLink to="autoencoders-and-variational-autoencoders" />, <Gloss name="ar">autoregressive models</Gloss>,
<NoteLink to="normalising-flow">normalising flows</NoteLink>, <Gloss name="gan" plural /> and
<Gloss name="energy-based-model">energy-based models</Gloss> in
<NoteLink to="adversarial-flow-energy-based-and-autoregressive-models" />, and <NoteLink to="diffusion-models" />.

**Networks constrained by physics.** <NoteLink to="physics-informed-neural-networks" /> train a network to satisfy a
differential equation as well as data, by adding the equation's residual to the loss.
