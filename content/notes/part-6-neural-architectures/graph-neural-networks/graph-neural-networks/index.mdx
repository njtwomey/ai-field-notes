<Definition>
A **graph neural network** maps a graph with a feature vector per node, and optionally per edge, to a vector per node,
per edge or per graph. Its layers update each node from its own vector and an aggregate of messages from its
neighbours, using functions shared by all nodes. The aggregate ignores the order of the neighbours, so relabelling the
nodes relabels the outputs in the same way. Most graph networks are instances of this message-passing scheme
<Cite id="gilmer2017" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Molecules are atoms joined by bonds, citation networks are papers joined by references, and a recommender's data is
users joined to the items they bought. A <NoteLink to="multilayer-perceptron" /> needs a fixed-length input in a fixed
order. A graph has neither: a node can have any number of neighbours, and no numbering of the nodes is preferred. A
graph neural network takes the graph as given and learns from node features and connectivity together
<Cite id="hamilton2020grl" />.

Predictions come at three levels: a label per node, such as a paper's field; a score per pair of nodes, for
recommendation or knowledge-base completion; and a value per graph, such as a molecule's solubility. Graph generation
runs the other way and produces new graphs.

## Main ideas

**Permutation equivariance fixes the form of a layer.** Relabelling the nodes by a permutation matrix $\Pmat$ turns the
node features $\Xmat \in \reals^{n \times d}$, for $n$ nodes with $d$ features each, into $\Pmat\Xmat$, and the
adjacency matrix $\Amat$ into $\Pmat\Amat\Pmat^\tr$. A node-level layer must permute its output in the same way, and a
graph-level readout must not change at all. Functions shared across nodes and combined by sums, means or maxima over
neighbours satisfy both. <NoteLink to="graphs-as-neural-network-inputs" /> develops the argument and the input formats.

**Nodes aggregate messages from their neighbours.** A <NoteLink to="message-passing-neural-network" /> computes, in
each round, $\mvec_v = \bigoplus_{w \in \Ncal(v)} M(\hvec_v, \hvec_w, \evec_{vw})$ and
$\hvec_v' = U(\hvec_v, \mvec_v)$. Here $\hvec_v$ is the vector of node $v$, $\Ncal(v)$ its set of neighbours,
$\evec_{vw}$ an optional edge vector, $M$ and $U$ shared networks, and $\bigoplus$ an order-free aggregator. $K$ rounds
carry information $K$ hops. The layers differ in how they weight the messages. The
<NoteLink to="graph-convolutional-network" /> fixes the weights from node degrees, the
<NoteLink to="graph-attention-network" /> learns them by a softmax over each neighbourhood, and
<NoteLink to="gated-and-edge-conditioned-message-passing" /> computes the message map from the edge's features.

**Graph convolution is spectral filtering.** The graph Laplacian $\Lmat = \Dmat - \Amat$, with $\Dmat$ the diagonal
matrix of node degrees, has eigenvectors ordered from smooth to oscillating, the graph analogue of Fourier modes. A
spectral filter multiplies each mode by a function of its eigenvalue. A degree-$K$ polynomial in $\Lmat$ needs no
eigenvectors and reaches exactly $K$ hops, and the graph convolutional network is its first-order case.
<NoteLink to="graph-laplacian-and-graph-fourier-transform" /> and <NoteLink to="spectral-graph-convolution" /> derive
both facts.

**Colour refinement bounds what message passing distinguishes.** A message-passing network never separates two graphs
to which the <NoteLink to="weisfeiler-leman-test" /> gives the same colour histogram. Such a network cannot tell a
6-cycle from two disjoint triangles, and so cannot count triangles. Sum aggregation with an injective update reaches
the bound, as in the <NoteLink to="graph-isomorphism-network" />. Extra node features
(<NoteLink to="positional-and-structural-encodings" />) and
<NoteLink to="higher-order-and-subgraph-graph-neural-networks" /> go past it at extra cost.

**Depth meets two limits.** Repeated averaging drives node features towards the top eigenvector of the propagation
matrix, so the nodes of a deep graph convolutional network become indistinguishable
(<NoteLink to="oversmoothing" />). A node's receptive field grows exponentially with depth while its vector keeps a
fixed width, so signal from distant nodes is squeezed through bottleneck edges
(<NoteLink to="oversquashing-and-graph-rewiring" />).
<NoteLink to="residual-and-jumping-knowledge-graph-networks">Residual and jumping-knowledge connections</NoteLink>,
rewiring and the <NoteLink to="graph-transformer" />, in which every node attends to every other, counter these
limits. Most message-passing models in use have two to six layers.

**Each task needs its own output stage.** A pair of nodes is scored by a decoder on its two endpoint vectors
(<NoteLink to="link-prediction-with-graph-neural-networks">link prediction</NoteLink>). A graph is summarised by a
permutation-invariant readout such as a sum (<NoteLink to="graph-pooling-and-readout" />). Neighbour averaging hurts
when linked nodes tend to have different labels
(<NoteLink to="graph-neural-networks-under-heterophily">heterophily</NoteLink>). Gains reported on small fixed
benchmark splits often vanish under realistic splits and tuned baselines
(<NoteLink to="graph-learning-benchmarks">benchmark pitfalls</NoteLink>).

- <NoteLink to="message-passing-layers" /> defines the framework and its main layers.

  generation, and ends with benchmarks.
- <NoteLink to="scalable-heterogeneous-and-dynamic-graph-networks" /> adapts message passing to graphs that are large,
  typed, timed or joined by hyperedges.

Four notes on graph learning sit in other topics. <NoteLink to="graphsage" /> aggregates a fixed-size sample of each
node's neighbours and embeds nodes unseen in training; it is in the recommender chapter, beside its descendant
<NoteLink to="pinsage" />. <NoteLink to="geometric-deep-learning" /> derives message passing, convolution and
rotation-equivariant layers from the symmetries of their input domains: permutations of a graph, translations of a
grid, rotations of space <Cite id="bronstein2021" />. <NoteLink to="knowledge-graph-embeddings" /> gives each entity
and relation of a knowledge graph a vector and scores triples, a lookup-table alternative to a graph network encoder
for typed link prediction. <NoteLink to="self-supervised-learning-on-graphs" /> pretrains graph encoders without
labels, by contrasting views of a graph or reconstructing masked node features.
