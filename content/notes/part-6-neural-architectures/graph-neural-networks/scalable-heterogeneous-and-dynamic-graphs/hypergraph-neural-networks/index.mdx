<Definition>
A hypergraph has $n$ nodes and $m$ hyperedges, each hyperedge a set of any number of nodes. Its incidence matrix
$\Hmat \in \set{0, 1}^{n \times m}$ has $H_{ve} = 1$ when node $v$ belongs to hyperedge $e$. A hypergraph neural network
updates node vectors in two stages: every hyperedge aggregates the vectors of its member nodes, then every node
aggregates the vectors of the hyperedges it belongs to. HGNN implements the two stages as the normalised product
$\Dmat_v^{-1/2}\Hmat\Wmat\Dmat_e^{-1}\Hmat^{\tr}\Dmat_v^{-1/2}$ <Cite id="feng2019hgnn" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A paper with three authors is one relation among three people. A graph can only record pairs, so it must either
replace the paper by three author–author edges or add the paper as an extra node. Many relations come in groups of
more than two: the items in one shopping basket, the genes in one pathway, the atoms in one ring, the users in one group
chat.

The **clique expansion** joins every two members of a hyperedge by an edge. It loses information. Three authors who
wrote one paper together and three authors who wrote three separate two-author papers give the same triangle. The **star
expansion** keeps the information by turning each hyperedge into a node of a second type, joined to its members. Two
rounds of <NoteLink to="message-passing-neural-network">message passing</NoteLink> on the star expansion, member to
hyperedge and hyperedge to member, are the two stages of a hypergraph network.

## Details

### Incidence matrix and degrees

The degree of node $v$ is $d(v) = \sum_e w_e H_{ve}$, the weighted number of hyperedges containing it, with hyperedge
weights $w_e$ (often all 1). The degree of hyperedge $e$ is $\delta(e) = \sum_v H_{ve}$, its number of members. Collect
them in diagonal matrices $\Dmat_v \in \reals^{n \times n}$ and $\Dmat_e \in \reals^{m \times m}$, and the weights in
$\Wmat = \diag(w_1, \dots, w_m)$ <Cite id="feng2019hgnn" />.

### Two-stage message passing

With node vectors as the rows of $\Xmat \in \reals^{n \times F}$, the simplest two-stage layer takes means:

$$
\Emat = \Dmat_e^{-1}\Hmat^{\tr}\Xmat, \qquad \Xmat' = \Dmat_v^{-1}\Hmat\Emat.
$$

Row $e$ of $\Emat \in \reals^{m \times F}$ is the mean of hyperedge $e$'s members. Row $v$ of $\Xmat'$ is the mean of
the hyperedges containing $v$. A learned weight matrix and a nonlinearity complete the layer, as in a
<NoteLink to="graph-convolutional-network">graph convolution</NoteLink>. Each stage is a sum over a set, so the layer is
permutation-equivariant in the nodes and in the hyperedges, and it accepts hyperedges of any size.

### HGNN

HGNN normalises symmetrically, as a GCN does <Cite id="feng2019hgnn" />:

$$
\Xmat^{(l+1)} = \sigma\paren{\Dmat_v^{-1/2}\Hmat\Wmat\Dmat_e^{-1}\Hmat^{\tr}\Dmat_v^{-1/2}\Xmat^{(l)}\Thetamat^{(l)}},
$$

with learned $\Thetamat^{(l)} \in \reals^{F_l \times F_{l+1}}$. The matrix
$\Dmat_v^{-1/2}\Hmat\Wmat\Dmat_e^{-1}\Hmat^{\tr}\Dmat_v^{-1/2}$ is the identity minus the normalised hypergraph
Laplacian of Zhou, Huang and Schölkopf, $\Delta = \I - \Dmat_v^{-1/2}\Hmat\Wmat\Dmat_e^{-1}\Hmat^{\tr}\Dmat_v^{-1/2}$
<Cite id="zhou2006hypergraph" />. HGNN derives the layer as a first-order approximation of a spectral filter on this
Laplacian, as GCN does on the graph Laplacian; when every hyperedge has two members, $\Delta$ is the
<NoteLink to="spectral-clustering">normalised graph Laplacian</NoteLink> up to a factor of $\tfrac12$
<Cite id="feng2019hgnn" />. The <NoteLink to="graph-laplacian-and-graph-fourier-transform">graph Laplacian</NoteLink>
note covers the spectral view.

The product is never formed as an $n \times n$ matrix. It is computed right to left: scale the rows of $\Xmat\Thetamat$,
multiply by $\Hmat^{\tr}$ (node to hyperedge), scale, multiply by $\Hmat$ (hyperedge to node), scale. Each product
touches every incidence once, so a layer costs $O\paren{\sum_e \delta(e)\, F}$.

### Learned aggregation

The mean is a fixed choice. AllSet writes both stages as learnable functions of multisets, one from members to a
hyperedge and one from hyperedges to a node, each built like a <NoteLink to="deep-sets">deep sets</NoteLink> network
or a set transformer <Cite id="chien2022allset" />. HGNN and other propagation rules are special cases of the framework.

### Where hypergraphs come from

Some data are hypergraphs by nature: co-authorship (papers as hyperedges), co-citation, baskets of purchased items,
email threads. Others are built. HGNN forms one hyperedge per node, joining it to its $K$
<NoteLink to="nearest-neighbours">nearest neighbours</NoteLink> in feature space, which gives $n$ hyperedges of $K + 1$
members each <Cite id="feng2019hgnn" />. Several feature sets give several groups of hyperedges, concatenated as columns
of $\Hmat$.

## Worked example

Six authors have scalar features $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$. Three papers join them: $e_1 = \set{1, 2, 3}$, $e_2 = \set{3, 4}$
and $e_3 = \set{4, 5, 6}$. Authors 3 and 4 are in two papers; the others are in one.

**Two-stage mean.**

1. Node to hyperedge: $e_1$ averages $1, 2, 3$ to $2$; $e_2$ averages $3, 4$ to $3.5$; $e_3$ averages $4, 5, 6$ to $5$.
2. Hyperedge to node: authors 1 and 2 are only in $e_1$ and get $2$. Author 3 averages $e_1$ and $e_2$ to
   $(2 + 3.5)/2 = 2.75$. Author 4 averages $e_2$ and $e_3$ to $(3.5 + 5)/2 = 4.25$. Authors 5 and 6 get $5$.

The new vector is $(2, 2, 2.75, 4.25, 5, 5)$.

**Clique expansion.** The cliques give seven edges: $1{-}2$, $1{-}3$, $2{-}3$, $3{-}4$, $4{-}5$, $4{-}6$, $5{-}6$. One
round of averaging each author with its neighbours gives $(2, 2, 2.5, 4.5, 5, 5)$. Author 3 now weighs author 4 as one
neighbour in four, a quarter, whereas the two-stage pass gave the paper $\set{3, 4}$ half of author 3's update.

**What the expansion loses.** Replace $e_1$ by three two-author papers $\set{1, 2}$, $\set{2, 3}$, $\set{1, 3}$. The
clique expansion is the same seven edges, so its output is unchanged. The two-stage pass changes: the pairs average to
$1.5$, $2.5$ and $2$, author 1 (in $\set{1, 2}$ and $\set{1, 3}$) gets $(1.5 + 2)/2 = 1.75$ instead of $2$, and author 3
(in $\set{2, 3}$, $\set{1, 3}$ and $e_2$) gets $(2.5 + 2 + 3.5)/3 \approx 2.67$ instead of $2.75$. Only the hypergraph
model tells one three-author paper from three two-author papers.

**HGNN normalisation.** With $\Wmat = \I$ and $\Thetamat = 1$, the degrees are $d = (1, 1, 2, 2, 1, 1)$ and
$\delta = (3, 2, 3)$. The hyperedge values are averages of $x_u/\sqrt{d(u)}$:
$e_1 = (1 + 2 + 3/\sqrt2)/3 \approx 1.707$, $e_2 = (3/\sqrt2 + 4/\sqrt2)/2 \approx 2.475$ and
$e_3 = (4/\sqrt2 + 5 + 6)/3 \approx 4.609$. Each author sums its hyperedges and divides by $\sqrt{d(v)}$: author 3 gets
$(1.707 + 2.475)/\sqrt2 \approx 2.957$, author 4 gets $(2.475 + 4.609)/\sqrt2 \approx 5.009$, and the others get the
value of their single hyperedge. Nodes in many hyperedges are scaled down on both stages, as high-degree nodes are in a
GCN.

## Assumptions

- **Group membership is the signal.** A hyperedge treats its members symmetrically. Roles inside a group, such as a
  paper's first and last author, need features on the incidences or a heterogeneous model.
- **Homophily within hyperedges.** Mean aggregation helps when members of a hyperedge tend to share labels, as edges
  do for a graph convolution.
- **Hyperedges are given or well constructed.** A built hypergraph, such as $K$ nearest neighbours, inherits the
  quality of the feature space and the choice of $K$.

## Pitfalls

- **Clique expansion of large hyperedges.** A hyperedge of $k$ members becomes $k(k - 1)/2$ edges. One mailing list of
  1,000 people gives 499,500 edges and dominates the graph, while the star expansion adds 1,000 incidences.
- **Mean over hyperedge sizes.** A two-member hyperedge and a 500-member hyperedge count equally in a node's mean. If
  large hyperedges are weak relations, down-weight them through $w_e$.
- **Indistinguishable structures.** Two-stage mean aggregation inherits the
  <NoteLink to="expressive-power-of-message-passing">limits of message passing</NoteLink>: hypergraphs with the same
  local multisets of sizes and features get the same outputs. AllSet replaces the fixed mean in each stage by a learned
  multiset function <Cite id="chien2022allset" />; as on graphs, a sum-based multiset function separates multisets that
  a mean merges, such as $\set{1}$ and $\set{1, 1}$.
