<Definition>
The standard <NoteLink to="message-passing-neural-network">message passing network</NoteLink> assumes one graph that
fits in memory, with one kind of node, one kind of edge, pairwise edges and no time. This chapter relaxes each
assumption in turn. It covers training on graphs too large for one device, graphs whose nodes and edges have types,
graphs that arrive as a stream of timed events, and hypergraphs whose edges join any number of nodes.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Graphs in practice break the textbook setting. A social or citation graph can have a hundred million nodes and over a
billion edges. A knowledge graph or a relational database has many entity types and hundreds of relation types.
Payments, messages and purchases arrive with timestamps, and their order matters. Co-authorship, shopping baskets and
group chats relate several nodes at once. Each case needs a change to how messages are computed, which nodes are
visited, or what an edge is. The changes keep the core of <Gloss name="gnn">message passing</Gloss>: local aggregation
by functions shared across the graph.

## Main ideas

**Bound the part of the graph a step touches.** An $L$-layer network's output at one node depends on up to $d^L$ nodes
for average degree $d$, the neighbourhood explosion.
<NoteLink to="neighbour-sampling-and-scalable-graph-networks">Neighbour sampling and scalable graph networks</NoteLink>
compares the remedies. GraphSAGE samples a fixed number of neighbours per node and layer. Cluster-GCN trains on dense
clusters of a partitioned graph <Cite id="chiang2019clustergcn" />. GraphSAINT samples subgraphs and reweights them to
stay unbiased. SGC and SIGN precompute the graph products, so training is a <Gloss name="mlp">multilayer
perceptron</Gloss> on node rows. The trade is between exact aggregation, memory, and the variance of the gradient.

**Give each type its own parameters.** In a <NoteLink to="heterogeneous-graph-networks">heterogeneous graph
network</NoteLink>, the edge type selects the message function. The relational graph convolution keeps one weight matrix
per relation and shares them through a few basis matrices when relations are many <Cite id="schlichtkrull2018rgcn" />.
HAN aggregates along hand-chosen meta-paths, typed paths such as author–paper–author, and HGT builds attention from
projections chosen by the types of the two endpoints and the edge.

**Let time order the messages.** A <NoteLink to="temporal-graph-networks">temporal graph network</NoteLink> keeps a
memory per node, updated by a recurrent cell whenever the node takes part in an event, and computes embeddings at query
time by attention over recent neighbours with encoded time gaps <Cite id="rossi2020tgn" />. Information then flows only
along paths whose events happen in increasing time, and the evaluation must split by time to avoid training on the
future.

**Let an edge join many nodes.** <NoteLink to="hypergraph-neural-networks">Hypergraph neural networks</NoteLink> pass
messages from nodes to hyperedges and back through the incidence matrix <Cite id="feng2019hgnn" />. Replacing each
hyperedge by a clique loses which nodes belonged together; the two-stage pass keeps it.

## How the notes fit together

- Start with the scalable-training note: its sampling and precomputation apply to every model in the chapter, and
  heterogeneous and temporal models at scale use the same loaders.
- The heterogeneous and temporal notes both require the Part III note on
  <NoteLink to="heterogeneous-and-temporal-graphs">heterogeneous and temporal graphs</NoteLink>, which describes the
  data: type maps, meta-paths, snapshots, event streams and time-respecting splits.
- The hypergraph note needs only message passing. It pairs with the heterogeneous note, since the star expansion of a
  hypergraph is a graph with two node types.
