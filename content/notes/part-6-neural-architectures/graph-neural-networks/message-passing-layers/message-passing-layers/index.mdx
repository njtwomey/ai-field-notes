<Definition>
A <Gloss name="gnn">message-passing</Gloss> layer maps a vector per node of a graph to a new vector per node. Each node
computes a message from every neighbour, combines the messages with an aggregator that ignores their order, and updates
its own vector from the result. The layers in this chapter differ in how a message is weighted, how the messages are
combined and how the update is computed.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Graph-structured data has no grid and no ordering of nodes: molecules, citation and social networks, road maps, meshes,
knowledge graphs. A network that reads such data must give the same answer however the nodes are numbered, and must
handle nodes with any number of neighbours. A message-passing layer meets both requirements by construction. Stacked two
to six deep, such layers produce node vectors for node classification, pairs of them for
<NoteLink to="link-prediction-with-graph-neural-networks">link prediction</NoteLink>, and a pooled vector for
graph-level prediction such as a molecular property.

## Main ideas

**One framework.** A <NoteLink to="message-passing-neural-network">message passing neural network</NoteLink> computes,
in each round, $\mvec_v = \bigoplus_{w \in \Ncal(v)} M(\hvec_v, \hvec_w, \evec_{vw})$ and
$\hvec_v' = U(\hvec_v, \mvec_v)$, where $\bigoplus$ is a sum, mean or maximum. Locality and
<NoteLink to="convolutional-layer">weight sharing</NoteLink> carry over from convolution, and the order-free aggregator
makes every layer equivariant to relabelling the nodes <Cite id="gilmer2017" />. $K$ rounds let information travel $K$
hops. The choice of aggregator decides what a node can tell about its neighbourhood: a sum counts neighbours, a mean
sees their proportions, a maximum sees only the extreme.

**Fixed weights from the structure.** The <NoteLink to="graph-convolutional-network">graph convolutional
network</NoteLink> weights neighbour $w$ of $v$ by $1/\sqrt{\tilde d_v \tilde d_w}$, the degrees counting self-loops, so
a layer is $\sigma(\hat\Amat \Hmat \Wmat)$. The rule is the first-order case of the polynomial spectral filters of
<NoteLink to="spectral-graph-convolution">spectral graph convolution</NoteLink>, with a renormalisation that keeps the
propagation matrix's eigenvalues in $(-1, 1]$. It is cheap, strong on graphs whose neighbours share labels, and smooths
features when stacked deeply.

**Weights learned from the features.** The <NoteLink to="graph-attention-network">graph attention network</NoteLink>
replaces the degree weights by a softmax over each neighbourhood of scores computed from both endpoints, with several
heads. The original score ranks every node's neighbours in the same order, which is static attention; GATv2 moves the
nonlinearity so the ranking can depend on the receiving node.

**Sampled neighbourhoods.** <NoteLink to="graphsage">GraphSAGE</NoteLink> concatenates a node's own vector with an
aggregate of a fixed-size sample of its neighbours. Sampling bounds the cost of a minibatch on graphs with millions of
nodes, and the learned functions embed nodes unseen in training. The note sits in the recommender-systems chapter, where
its descendant PinSAGE is used.

**Messages shaped by edges, updates with gates.** <NoteLink to="gated-and-edge-conditioned-message-passing">Gated and
edge-conditioned message passing</NoteLink> generates the message matrix from the edge's features, such as bond type and
distance, and updates each node with a <Gloss name="gru" /> cell or an edge gate. Edge conditioning lets the same
neighbour send different messages along different bonds. Gating keeps states bounded over many rounds.

## How the notes fit together

Start with the message passing neural network: it defines the framework, works an example by hand and compares the
aggregators. The graph convolutional network and the graph attention network are the two most used instances, and can
be read in either order; the convolution note also needs the spectral filters of the foundations chapter. The gated and
edge-conditioned note builds on the framework and on the gated recurrent unit. GraphSAGE can be read after the framework
note.

The neighbouring chapters take the layers further. <NoteLink to="foundations-of-graph-neural-networks">Foundations of
graph neural networks</NoteLink> gives the inputs, the Laplacian and spectral filtering.
<NoteLink to="expressiveness-of-graph-neural-networks">Expressiveness of graph neural networks</NoteLink> asks which
graphs these layers can tell apart, and introduces the <NoteLink to="graph-isomorphism-network">graph isomorphism
network</NoteLink>. <NoteLink to="depth-and-long-range-information-in-graph-networks">Depth and long-range information
in graph networks</NoteLink> explains why stacking many layers stops helping.
