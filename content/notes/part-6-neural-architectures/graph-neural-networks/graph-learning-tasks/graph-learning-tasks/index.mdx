<Definition>
Graph learning tasks are the prediction problems a <Gloss name="gnn">graph neural network</Gloss> is trained for,
classified by what is predicted: a label per node, a score per pair of nodes, a value per graph, or a whole new graph.
Each level needs its own output stage on top of message passing, its own way of splitting data into training and test
parts, and has its own way of failing. The notes of this chapter take one task each, with the benchmarks that measure
them.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A <NoteLink to="message-passing-neural-network">message passing network</NoteLink> turns a graph into one vector per
node. That vector is only the middle of a model. Classifying papers in a citation graph uses the node vectors directly.
Recommending a product scores pairs of a user and an item. Predicting a drug's solubility reduces the whole molecule to
one number. Designing a new molecule runs the other way, from noise to a graph. The architecture of the encoder matters
less for these tasks than three questions it does not answer by itself: whether the neighbours' information helps or
hurts, how a vector per node becomes a prediction about a pair or a graph, and whether the evaluation measures what it
claims <Cite id="hamilton2020grl" />.

## Main ideas

**Neighbours can mislead.** Averaging a node with its neighbours helps when linked nodes share labels and erases the
node's own evidence when they share labels only some of the time. The cure is to keep the node's representation apart
from its neighbours', or to let the propagation weights turn negative. The
<NoteLink to="graph-neural-networks-under-heterophily">graph neural networks under heterophily</NoteLink> note derives
where averaging fails and measures homophily.

**Pairs are not two nodes.** A link predictor scores a pair from its two <NoteLink to="node-embeddings">node
embeddings</NoteLink>, trained against sampled non-edges. Nodes that are structurally interchangeable get equal
embeddings, so the decoder cannot tell a nearby candidate from a distant one; models that label the pair's
neighbourhood, or add its common neighbours, fix it. <NoteLink to="link-prediction-with-graph-neural-networks">Link
prediction with graph neural networks</NoteLink> develops the graph autoencoder and its successors.

**A graph needs an invariant summary.** Graph-level prediction reduces node vectors to one vector that does not depend
on node order. The choice of sum, mean, maximum or attention decides what survives: only the sum keeps the number of
nodes. Hierarchical pooling coarsens the graph in stages, at a cost that has rarely paid off on benchmarks.
<NoteLink to="graph-pooling-and-readout">Graph pooling and readout</NoteLink> covers both, and
<NoteLink to="molecular-property-prediction-with-graph-networks">molecular property prediction with graph
networks</NoteLink> works a full example, a sum readout against a fingerprint baseline on a scaffold split.

**Generation has no natural order.** The same graph has up to $n!$ adjacency matrices, one per node ordering, so a
likelihood must sum over orderings or fix one. Autoregressive, one-shot and diffusion generators handle this in
different ways (<NoteLink to="graph-generative-models">graph generative models</NoteLink>).

**Benchmarks overstate progress.** Small fixed splits, duplicated nodes, test edges left in the message passing graph
and untuned baselines have all produced gains that vanish under careful re-evaluation. The newer suites, such as the
Open Graph Benchmark, fix realistic splits and metrics <Cite id="hu2020ogb" />.
<NoteLink to="graph-learning-benchmarks">Graph learning benchmarks and their pitfalls</NoteLink> covers what the suites
test and how to read a result.

## How the notes fit together

Start with heterophily, which continues the node-level story of the
<NoteLink to="graph-convolutional-network">graph convolutional network</NoteLink>. Link prediction is independent of it
and needs only that note. Pooling and readout comes before the molecular example, which uses a sum readout. Graph
generation uses message passing and the <NoteLink to="variational-autoencoder">variational autoencoder</NoteLink>. The
benchmarks note comes last, because its pitfalls refer to every task: homophily for nodes, negative sampling for links,
scaffold splits for molecules.
