<Definition>
A **feedforward network** maps an input to an output through layers of units, each computing a weighted sum of the
previous layer's outputs followed by a nonlinearity, with no feedback from later layers to earlier ones. Its weights
are fitted by gradient descent on a loss, with gradients computed by backpropagation <Cite id="goodfellow2016" />. This
topic also covers energy-based networks, an older family in which symmetric connections define an energy over the
units' joint state <Cite id="hertz1991" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The <Gloss name="mlp">multilayer perceptron</Gloss> is the base case of deep learning. It is a flexible function
approximator for tabular data, and it is the building block that other architectures specialise:
<NoteLink to="convolutional-layer">convolutional layers</NoteLink> restrict its weights to local, shared filters,
attention layers make its mixing weights depend on the input, and the feed-forward sublayer of every
transformer block is a two-layer perceptron. The training methods in this topic,
adaptive optimisers, normalisation, <NoteLink to="residual-connections">residual connections</NoteLink> and
regularisation, are what make any deep architecture trainable in practice.

Energy-based networks matter for a different reason. They connect neural networks to statistical physics, and their
modern form, the modern Hopfield network, has an update rule that is the attention operation of transformers.

## Main ideas

**Layers of simple units.** A perceptron is a linear classifier. Stacking units with nonlinear activations gives the
multilayer perceptron, which can approximate any continuous function with one hidden layer and represents some
functions exponentially more efficiently with depth. <NoteLink to="foundations-of-feedforward-networks" /> develops
these results.

**Depth makes training hard.** Gradients through many layers vanish or explode, the loss is non-convex and badly
conditioned, and large networks can memorise their training data. <NoteLink to="training-deep-networks" /> covers the
remedies: initialisation scaled to the fan-in, batch and <NoteLink to="layer-normalisation">layer
normalisation</NoteLink>, residual connections, <NoteLink to="adam">Adam</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="learning-rate-schedules">learning-rate schedules</NoteLink>, and regularisers from
<Gloss name="weight-decay">weight decay</Gloss> to <NoteLink to="dropout">dropout</NoteLink> and data augmentation.

**Networks can be defined by an energy.** In a <NoteLink to="hopfield-network">Hopfield network</NoteLink> the units are
binary spins with symmetric weights, and updates descend an energy
function, so stored patterns become stable states that the network recalls from partial cues. A
<NoteLink to="boltzmann-machine">Boltzmann machine</NoteLink> samples states with probability proportional to $e^{-E/T}$
and learns its weights from data; the <Gloss name="restricted-boltzmann-machine">restricted Boltzmann machine</Gloss>
makes this tractable, and stacks of them formed <Gloss name="deep-belief-network">deep belief networks</Gloss>.
<NoteLink to="energy-based-networks" /> traces this line from the <NoteLink to="ising-model" /> to the
<NoteLink to="modern-hopfield-network" />.
