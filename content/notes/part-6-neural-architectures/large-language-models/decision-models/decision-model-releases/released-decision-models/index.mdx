<Definition>
The **released decision models** are the three model lines that first implemented the decision-model interface: a
text and typed questions in, one answer per question with a probability for every allowed answer out. They share the
interface and differ in openness, base model, training loss, training data and serving
<Cite id="typesafe2026launch" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The core mechanism, reading answer probabilities from the logits of one-token answer codes, can be implemented in
several ways. The three releases show the main choices side by side: a closed hosted model with an unpublished
post-training method, an open model trained with a loss over the candidate codes only, and an open model trained as an
ordinary next-token predictor of the answer letter. Comparing them shows which choices matter for accuracy,
calibration, cost and ease of deployment.

## Main ideas

**A closed model with calibrated rewards.** <NoteLink to="jev" /> is TypeSafe AI's hosted model. It reads the state once
for all questions and is post-trained with <Gloss name="rlcd" />, a <Gloss name="rl">reinforcement learning</Gloss>
method that has not been published.

**An open model trained over the candidates.** <NoteLink to="nimble" /> is Bespoke Labs' <Gloss name="lora" /> adapter
on Qwen3.5-9B, trained by cross-entropy over the answer codes on 2,676 synthetic contrastive examples. Its scorer shares
one read of the state across questions <Cite id="bespoke2026nimble" />.

**An open model trained as a chat model.** <NoteLink to="tev1" /> is Together AI's family of LoRA fine-tunes of
Qwen3.5-4B and 0.8B, trained to reply with one answer letter by next-token cross-entropy on 37,840 examples. Any chat
server can run it, and its option probabilities are the letter tokens' probabilities <Cite id="together2026tev1" />.

**Side by side.** <NoteLink to="decision-models-compared" /> tabulates the three on weights, base model, readout,
loss, data, limits and calibration, and sets out the published and independent results. Jev and Nimble are within
half a point on the one benchmark all three were run on. Independent tests find their accuracy overlapping and Jev the
cheapest per decision. Nimble and Tev1 differ in loss and data at once, so the published results cannot separate the
effect of the loss from that of the data.
