<Definition>
A **large language model** is a transformer, almost always decoder-only, with billions of parameters, trained to
predict the next token on a large corpus of text. It defines a probability for every continuation of every prompt. It
is used by prompting it, by adapting its weights to a task or to human preferences, and by building systems that
retrieve documents, call tools or read decisions from its output probabilities <Cite id="jurafsky2025" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

<NoteLink to="autoregressive-transformer-language-model">Next-token prediction</NoteLink> on enough text produces a
model that can perform tasks described in its prompt: translation, summarisation, <Gloss name="qa">question
answering</Gloss>, classification and code generation, often from a few examples and without training
<Cite id="brown2020" />. <Gloss name="sft">Instruction tuning</Gloss> and preference optimisation turn such a model into
an assistant that follows requests <Cite id="ouyang2022" />. Large language models are now the default starting point
for most text tasks, and the pretrained model is reused across many applications.

## Main ideas

**One objective, scaled.** The model minimises next-token cross-entropy. Its loss falls as a power law in parameters and
training tokens, and a compute budget is best split between the two in roughly equal proportion. Text is generated one
token at a time by a decoding rule, and the model is evaluated by perplexity and by benchmarks.
<NoteLink to="language-models-and-decoding" /> covers the model, decoding strategies,
<NoteLink to="speculative-decoding">speculative decoding</NoteLink> and evaluation.

**The prompt is the interface.** <NoteLink to="working-with-large-language-models" /> covers what can be done without
changing the weights: prompting, <Gloss name="icl">in-context learning</Gloss> from demonstrations,
<Gloss name="cot">chain-of-thought</Gloss> reasoning, <Gloss name="rag">retrieval-augmented generation</Gloss>,
<NoteLink to="tool-use-and-agents">tool use</NoteLink> and agent loops, and detecting hallucinations.

**Adaptation changes the weights.** <NoteLink to="adapting-pretrained-language-models" /> covers supervised fine-tuning,
parameter-efficient methods such as <Gloss name="lora" /> and <Gloss name="qlora" />, instruction tuning,
<NoteLink to="reward-models">reward models</NoteLink>, <Gloss name="dpo">direct preference optimisation</Gloss>, and
<Gloss name="rlvr">reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards</Gloss> and <Gloss name="grpo">group-relative policy
optimisation</Gloss>. Each changes something different at its own cost in data, memory and risk.

**Decisions without generation.** <NoteLink to="decision-models" /> answer typed questions in one
forward pass by reading the next-token probabilities of one-token answer
codes. They return a probability for every allowed answer, and their latency depends on input length alone.
