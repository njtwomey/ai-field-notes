<Definition>
A **<Gloss name="llm">large language model</Gloss>** is a decoder-only transformer trained to predict the next token of
text, which defines a probability $p(x_t \mid x_{<t})$ for every token given those before it. **Decoding** turns these
next-token distributions into generated text, one token at a time. Evaluation measures how well the model predicts
held-out text and how well its outputs solve tasks <Cite id="jurafsky2025" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Every use of a large language model, from chat to code generation to the <NoteLink to="decision-models">decision
models</NoteLink> of this Part, rests on the same object: a next-token distribution computed by a causally masked
transformer. This topic describes that object, how its quality grows with scale, how text is drawn from it, and how its
quality is measured. The choice of decoding method changes output quality and diversity as much as many changes to the
model, and the cost of decoding dominates the cost of serving.

## Main ideas

**Next-token prediction at scale.** An <NoteLink to="autoregressive-transformer-language-model" /> minimises
$-\sum_t \log p(x_t \mid x_{<t})$ over large corpora. Its loss falls as a power law in the number of parameters $N$ and
of training tokens $D$. For a compute budget $C \approx 6ND$, the compute-optimal recipe grows $N$ and $D$ in equal
proportion. Large enough models perform tasks described in their prompt without further training
<Cite id="brown2020" />.

**Decoding trades quality against diversity.** <NoteLink to="decoding-strategies" /> covers greedy and
<Gloss name="beam-search">beam search</Gloss>, which pick high-probability continuations, and sampling at a temperature
$T$ from $\operatorname{softmax}(\zvec/T)$. Top-$k$ and nucleus (top-$p$) sampling truncate the unreliable tail of the
distribution before sampling.

**Generation can be faster without changing its distribution.** Each generated token needs a full pass of the model,
and that pass is limited by memory bandwidth rather than arithmetic. <NoteLink to="speculative-decoding" /> drafts
several tokens with a small model and checks them in one parallel pass of the large one; an accept-reject rule makes
the output exactly distributed as the large model's.

**Evaluation is intrinsic or extrinsic.** <NoteLink to="language-model-evaluation" /> measures held-out log-likelihood
as perplexity or <NoteLink to="perplexity-as-a-metric">bits per byte</NoteLink>, and task ability by benchmarks.
Multiple-choice items compare the log-likelihoods of the options, and code benchmarks use the unbiased pass@$k$
estimator.
