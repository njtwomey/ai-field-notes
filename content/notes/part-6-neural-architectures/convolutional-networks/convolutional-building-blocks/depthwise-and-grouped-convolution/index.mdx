<Definition>
A **grouped convolution** with $g$ groups splits the $C$ input channels and the $C'$ output channels of a
<NoteLink to="convolutional-layer">convolutional layer</NoteLink> into $g$ consecutive blocks of $C/g$ and $C'/g$
channels. Output block $b$ is a standard convolution of input block $b$ alone, so the layer holds $k^2 C C'/g$ weights.
A **depthwise convolution** is the case $g = C$: each channel is filtered by its own $k \times k$ kernel, and no
channels are mixed. A **depthwise separable convolution** is a depthwise convolution followed by a
<NoteLink to="one-by-one-convolution">$1 \times 1$ convolution</NoteLink> that mixes the channels.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A standard $k \times k$ layer does two jobs at once. It combines neighbouring pixels, and it combines channels: every
output channel has a $k \times k$ kernel on every input channel. On wide feature maps the second job dominates the cost,
because it multiplies the kernel area by the product $CC'$ of the channel counts.

Grouped convolution cuts the channel job into independent pieces. With $g = 4$ groups of a 256-channel layer, each block
of 64 output channels reads only its own 64 input channels. The layer is four smaller layers side by side, and it costs
a quarter as much. Depthwise convolution goes to the end: every channel is filtered alone, and the layer does only the
spatial job. A separable convolution then gives the channel job to a $1 \times 1$ layer. The two jobs are paid for
separately, $k^2 C$ for the spatial filtering and $CC'$ for the channel mixing, instead of their product.

## Details

### Definition with indices

Write $C_g = C/g$ and $C'_g = C'/g$. Output channel $c'$ lies in block $b(c') = \lfloor c'/C'_g \rfloor$ and reads the
input channels $bC_g, \dots, (b + 1)C_g - 1$ of that block. With stride 1 and zero padding $p$, as in the convolutional
layer,

$$
y_{c', i, j} = b_{c'} + \sum_{c''=0}^{C_g - 1} \sum_{u, v} w_{c', c'', u, v}\; x_{b(c')C_g + c'',\, i - p + u,\, j - p + v}.
$$

The weight tensor has shape $[C', C/g, k, k]$, which is the shape libraries store.

**Block-diagonal view.** Lay out a full kernel tensor of shape $[C', C, k, k]$ and set to zero every kernel $w_{c', c}$
whose input channel is outside the block of $c'$. Seen as a $C' \times C$ matrix of $k \times k$ kernels, the result is
block diagonal with $g$ blocks. A full convolution with this kernel computes exactly the grouped convolution. The figure
checks this on a random input. Grouping is therefore a constraint on a standard layer, not a new operation: it removes
$1 - 1/g$ of the channel connections.

### Cost

Per output position the layer performs $k^2 C C'/g$ multiply-accumulates, and in total $k^2 C C' H'W'/g$. Both weights
and compute fall by the factor $g$.

A depthwise layer with $C' = C$ has $k^2 C$ weights. Some layers give each channel $m$ kernels, a channel multiplier,
for $C' = mC$ outputs and $mk^2C$ weights. A depthwise separable layer from $C$ to $C'$ channels costs

$$
k^2 C + C C' \qquad \text{against} \qquad k^2 C C'
$$

weights, and the same expressions times $H'W'$ in multiply-accumulates. The ratio is

$$
\frac{k^2 C + C C'}{k^2 C C'} = \frac{1}{C'} + \frac{1}{k^2}.
$$

For $k = 3$ and a wide layer, $1/C'$ is small and the saving approaches $k^2 = 9$. Howard et al. report 8 to 9 times
less computation for MobileNet's $3 \times 3$ layers <Cite id="howard2017mobilenets" />.

### Separable as a factorised kernel

The separable layer computes $z_{c'} = \sum_c p_{c', c}\, (d_c \star x_c)$, with $d_c$ the depthwise kernel of channel
$c$ and $p_{c', c}$ the pointwise weights. Convolution is linear, so this equals a standard layer with kernels

$$
w_{c', c, u, v} = p_{c', c}\; d_{c, u, v}.
$$

Every kernel acting on input channel $c$ is a scaled copy of one spatial pattern $d_c$. A standard layer can use a
different pattern for every pair $(c', c)$. The separable layer assumes that spatial structure and cross-channel
structure can be learned apart. Chollet built the Xception network on that hypothesis, replacing every
<NoteLink to="inception-networks">Inception module</NoteLink> by depthwise separable convolutions
<Cite id="chollet2017xception" />.

### History and practice

- **<NoteLink to="classic-convolutional-network-architectures">AlexNet</NoteLink>** split most of its layers into two
  groups so that each half ran on its own <Gloss name="gpu" />, a hardware constraint rather than a design choice
  <Cite id="krizhevsky2012" />.
- **ResNeXt** replaced the $3 \times 3$ layer of each <Gloss name="resnet" /> bottleneck by a grouped layer with 32
  groups and widened the block to keep the cost. At equal cost, more groups gave higher accuracy than more depth or
  width <Cite id="xie2017resnext" />.
- **MobileNet** is built from depthwise separable layers <Cite id="howard2017mobilenets" />. MobileNetV2 wraps the
  depthwise layer between a $1 \times 1$ expansion and a $1 \times 1$ projection <Cite id="sandler2018mobilenetv2" />;
  see <NoteLink to="efficient-convolutional-networks">efficient convolutional networks</NoteLink>.
- **ConvNeXt** uses a $7 \times 7$ depthwise layer as the spatial mixer of every block, with $1 \times 1$ layers doing
  all channel mixing, the same division of labour as attention and the feed-forward block of a transformer
  <Cite id="liu2022" />; see <NoteLink to="modern-convolutional-networks">modern convolutional networks</NoteLink>.

Grouped layers stacked directly on grouped layers never exchange information between groups. Networks restore the flow
with a $1 \times 1$ layer after the grouped one, or by permuting channels between groups, the channel shuffle of
ShuffleNet <Cite id="zhang2018shufflenet" />.

## Worked example

**Cost.** Take a MobileNet-style layer with $C = C' = 256$, $k = 3$ and a $14 \times 14$ map, without biases.

1. **Standard.** $9 \cdot 256 \cdot 256 = 589{,}824$ weights, and $589{,}824 \cdot 196 = 1.16 \times 10^8$
   multiply-accumulates.
2. **Grouped, $g = 32$.** $589{,}824 / 32 = 18{,}432$ weights, each group a $3 \times 3$ layer from 8 to 8 channels.
3. **Depthwise.** $9 \cdot 256 = 2{,}304$ weights, one $3 \times 3$ kernel per channel.
4. **Pointwise.** $256 \cdot 256 = 65{,}536$ weights.
5. **Separable total.** $2{,}304 + 65{,}536 = 67{,}840$ weights, and $67{,}840 \cdot 196 = 1.33 \times 10^7$
   multiply-accumulates.

The ratio is $589{,}824 / 67{,}840 = 8.69$, which matches $1/(1/256 + 1/9) = 8.69$. The pointwise layer holds 97% of the
separable layer's weights, so the $1 \times 1$ layer is now the expensive part.

**Mechanism.** Take two input channels of length 4, $x_1 = (1, 2, 3, 4)$ and $x_2 = (4, 0, 4, 0)$, and two-tap
depthwise kernels $d_1 = (1, -1)$ and $d_2 = (0.5, 0.5)$, cross-correlated without padding.

- Channel 1 takes differences of neighbours: $(1 - 2,\ 2 - 3,\ 3 - 4) = (-1, -1, -1)$.
- Channel 2 takes averages of neighbours: $(2, 2, 2)$.

Neither output knows about the other channel. A pointwise layer with
$\Pmat = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$ mixes them at each position: the first output is $-1 + 2 = 1$
and the second is $-1 - 2 = -3$ at every position. The equivalent standard layer has kernels
$w_{c', c} = p_{c', c}\, d_c$: output 1 uses $(1, -1)$ on channel 1 and $(0.5, 0.5)$ on channel 2, and output 2 uses
$(1, -1)$ and $(-0.5, -0.5)$. Applied directly, the first output is $(1 - 2) + 0.5(4 + 0) = 1$ at position 0, the same
number.

## Assumptions

- Spatial patterns and channel combinations can be learned separately, or within groups, without losing much. The
  factorised kernel $p_{c', c}\, d_c$ cannot express a layer that needs a different spatial pattern for every channel
  pair.
- Information that must cross groups has another route, a $1 \times 1$ layer or a shuffle.

## Pitfalls

- **<Gloss name="flop" plural /> are not speed.** A depthwise layer performs $k^2$ multiply-accumulates per value it
  reads, so it is limited by memory bandwidth, not arithmetic. On GPUs a separable layer runs much less than 9 times
  faster than the standard layer it replaces, and wide grouped layers can be slower per FLOP than dense ones.
- **Divisibility.** Both channel counts must be divisible by $g$. Libraries raise an error otherwise.
- **Isolated groups.** Two grouped layers in a row with the same grouping are $g$ separate networks. Without a mixing
  layer between them the groups never share information.
- **Normalisation per group.** Depthwise layers have few weights per channel and their outputs vary widely in scale
  between channels. A normalisation layer usually follows each one.
