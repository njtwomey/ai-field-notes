<Definition>
Convolution beyond images applies the convolutional layer to data on a regular grid with one axis, such as audio,
sensor recordings and text, or with three axes, such as video. The layer keeps its two assumptions, locality and weight
sharing, and changes the number of axes its kernel spans. Sequence models add causality, so that an output never reads
the future, and dilation, so that the receptive field grows exponentially with depth.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A <NoteLink to="convolutional-layer">convolutional layer</NoteLink> needs only a grid on which nearby points are
related and the same pattern can occur anywhere. A waveform, an <Gloss name="ecg" />, an accelerometer trace and the
word vectors of a sentence are one-dimensional grids. A video clip is a three-dimensional grid of frames, rows and
columns. Convolutional networks on these grids classify heart rhythms and human activities, encode raw speech for
recognition, generate audio sample by sample, forecast series and recognise actions in video
<Cite id="kiranyaz2019" />.

## Main ideas

**A one-dimensional layer is a learned filter bank.** Each kernel slides along time, spans every input channel and
responds where the signal looks like it, so a layer of $C'$ kernels is $C'$ learned finite impulse response filters. Its
parameter count does not depend on the length of the sequence. Trained kernels resemble the matched filters of the
events they detect, adjusted to reject look-alikes.
<NoteLink to="one-dimensional-convolutional-networks">One-dimensional convolutional networks</NoteLink> develops the
layer, its receptive field and its uses in time-series classification, speech and text.

**Causal convolution makes a convolutional network autoregressive.** Padding only on the left lets every output read
the current and earlier inputs and nothing later, so a model can be trained on every next-value prediction of a
sequence in one parallel pass. Doubling the dilation at every layer gives a receptive field of $2^L$ steps from $L$
layers with kernel size 2. WaveNet built a generative model of raw audio from such stacks, with gated activations and
a softmax over 256 μ-law levels <Cite id="oord2016c" />. <NoteLink to="wavenet-and-causal-convolution">WaveNet and
causal convolution</NoteLink> derives the receptive field and the cost of generation.

**A three-dimensional kernel sees motion.** A moving edge is a slanted surface in the space-time volume of a video, and
a kernel spanning several frames can be tuned to its slant. Full 3-D kernels multiply the cost of a layer by the
temporal kernel size. Inflating a pretrained image network gives a 3-D network that starts from what the image network
knows <Cite id="carreira2017" />, and factorising the kernel into spatial and temporal parts, or across channels, cuts
the cost. <NoteLink to="video-and-three-dimensional-convolution">Video and three-dimensional convolution</NoteLink>
covers C3D, I3D, $(2{+}1)$-D, two-stream and channel-separated designs.
