<Definition>
A convolutional network architecture is a fixed arrangement of convolutional layers, downsampling, normalisation and
shortcuts that maps an image to features and class scores. Most image architectures share one template: a stem that
reduces the resolution, stages of repeated blocks in which the channels grow as the resolution falls, and
<NoteLink to="pooling-and-strides">global pooling</NoteLink> with a linear classifier. The architectures in this
chapter differ in the block, in how blocks are connected and in how the network is scaled to a budget.
</Definition>

## What it is for

An architecture decides how much a network can represent for a given cost. The same layers, arranged differently, give
networks whose parameters, multiply-adds and accuracy differ by an order of magnitude. VGG-16 and ResNet-50 are both
built from $3 \times 3$ convolutions, yet ResNet-50 is three times deeper with a fifth of the parameters
<Cite id="he2016" />. Image classifiers on <Gloss name="imagenet" /> are the usual test bed. The trained networks then
serve as backbones for detection, segmentation and other vision tasks, so a better classifier architecture improves
the whole of <NoteLink to="computer-vision">computer vision</NoteLink>.

## Main ideas

**Deeper stacks of small kernels.** LeNet-5, AlexNet and VGG alternate convolution and pooling and end in dense layers.
VGG replaced large kernels by stacks of $3 \times 3$ ones, which have the same
<NoteLink to="receptive-field-and-dilated-convolution">receptive field</NoteLink> with fewer weights and more
nonlinearities. <NoteLink to="classic-convolutional-network-architectures">LeNet, AlexNet, VGG and ResNet</NoteLink>
traces this line from 1998 to 2015 and counts the parameters and multiply-adds of each network.

**Shortcuts make depth trainable.** Plain networks deeper than about 20 layers train worse than shallower ones. A
<NoteLink to="residual-network">residual network</NoteLink> adds the block's input to its output, so each block learns
a correction to the identity and the gradient reaches every block through the shortcuts. Residual networks of 152
layers train from random initialisation <Cite id="he2016" />. A <NoteLink to="densely-connected-network">densely
connected network</NoteLink> concatenates the outputs of all earlier layers in a block instead of adding them, so later
layers reuse earlier features directly.

**Cheaper blocks by factorising the convolution.** Most of the cost of a full convolution is channel mixing repeated at
every kernel position. <NoteLink to="inception-networks">Inception networks</NoteLink> run several kernel sizes in
parallel behind $1 \times 1$ reductions. <NoteLink to="efficient-convolutional-networks">Efficient convolutional
networks</NoteLink> split the convolution into a depthwise filter per channel and a $1 \times 1$ mixing layer, as in
MobileNet, and EfficientNet scales depth, width and resolution together under a cost budget.
<NoteLink to="squeeze-and-excitation">Squeeze-and-excitation</NoteLink> adds a cheap gate that rescales each channel
from a global summary of the image.

**The transformer-era recipe.** <NoteLink to="modern-convolutional-networks">Modern convolutional networks</NoteLink>
rebuild a ResNet step by step with the choices of vision transformers: a patchify stem, $7 \times 7$ depthwise kernels,
inverted bottlenecks, layer normalisation and a long training recipe. The result, ConvNeXt, matches Swin transformers
of the same cost <Cite id="liu2022" />. Much of the 2020 gap between the two families was training, not architecture.

**Convolution against attention.** A convolution mixes a fixed neighbourhood with weights that depend only on the
offset. Self-attention mixes all positions with weights computed from the content.
<NoteLink to="convolution-versus-attention-in-vision">Convolution versus attention in vision</NoteLink> compares the two
as <NoteLink to="inductive-bias">inductive biases</NoteLink>, with their cost on a grid of patches, and places
<NoteLink to="sliding-window-and-sparse-attention">windowed attention</NoteLink>, hybrids and
<NoteLink to="mlp-mixer">MLP-Mixer</NoteLink> between them.

**Parts and wholes.** A <NoteLink to="capsule-network">capsule network</NoteLink> replaces scalar detectors by vectors
whose length is the probability that a part is present and whose direction is its pose. Routing by agreement assigns
parts to the wholes whose predicted poses they agree with. Capsules remain a research line rather than a practical
backbone.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume the <NoteLink to="convolutional-layer">convolutional layer</NoteLink> and the other layers of
<NoteLink to="building-blocks-of-convolutional-networks">building blocks of convolutional networks</NoteLink>,
especially pooling, the receptive field and normalisation. <NoteLink to="residual-connections">Residual
connections</NoteLink> gives the gradient and signal-propagation argument for shortcuts in any deep network. A
textbook treatment of the same line, with code, is in <Cite id="zhang2023d2l" />.

These architectures are the backbones of <NoteLink to="dense-prediction-with-convolutional-networks">dense
prediction</NoteLink>, which adds decoders and feature pyramids for segmentation and detection, and of the tasks in
<NoteLink to="image-classification">image classification</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="object-detection">object
detection</NoteLink>. The <NoteLink to="vision-transformer">vision transformer</NoteLink> replaces the convolutional
stages with attention over patches, and <NoteLink to="geometric-deep-learning">geometric deep learning</NoteLink>
generalises convolution's translation equivariance to other symmetries.
