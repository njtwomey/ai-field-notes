_The setup follows Ben Moseley's harmonic oscillator example <Cite id="moseley2021" />._

<Definition>
A mass on a spring with damping moves by

$$
x''(t) + 2\zeta\omega_0 \, x'(t) + \omega_0^2 \, x(t) = 0, \qquad t \in [0, 1],
$$

with natural frequency $\omega_0 = 15$ and damping ratio $\zeta = 0.2$. It is released at rest from $x(0) = 1$. Ten
noise-free observations $x(t_l)$ are given at evenly spaced times from $t = 0$ to $t = 0.24$, about the first half
swing. The task is to recover $x(t)$ on all of $[0, 1]$ with a neural network, once from the observations alone and
once from the observations and the equation.
</Definition>

## Setup

- $x(t)$ is the displacement of the mass from rest and $t$ is time, in dimensionless units.
- $\omega_0 > 0$ is the natural frequency: the angular frequency at which the mass would swing without damping. With
  $\omega_0 = 15$ an undamped swing lasts $2\pi/15 \approx 0.42$, so $[0, 1]$ holds about two and a half swings.
- $\zeta \ge 0$ is the damping ratio. For $\zeta < 1$ the motion oscillates while it decays (underdamped).
- The network $x_{\thetavec}(t)$ is a <NoteLink to="multilayer-perceptron">multilayer perceptron</NoteLink> with one
  input, two hidden layers of 32 hyperbolic tangent units and one output, with parameters $\thetavec$. Its input is
  $2t - 1$, so that $[0, 1]$ maps to $[-1, 1]$.

The **data-only** network minimises the mean squared misfit to the observations,

$$
L_{\text{data}}(\thetavec) = \frac{1}{10} \sum_{l=1}^{10} \paren{x_{\thetavec}(t_l) - x(t_l)}^2 .
$$

The **physics-informed** network minimises the same misfit plus $\lambda$ times the mean squared residual at $N_c = 40$
collocation points $\tau_i$ spread evenly over $[0, 1]$:

$$
L_{\text{PINN}}(\thetavec) = L_{\text{data}}(\thetavec)
  + \frac{\lambda}{N_c} \sum_{i=1}^{N_c} r_{\thetavec}(\tau_i)^2, \qquad
r_{\thetavec}(t) = \frac{x_{\thetavec}''(t)}{\omega_0^2} + \frac{2\zeta}{\omega_0} x_{\thetavec}'(t) + x_{\thetavec}(t),
$$

with $\lambda = 1$. The residual $r_{\thetavec}$ is the equation divided by $\omega_0^2$, so that its three terms are of
the size of $x$; undivided, the $\omega_0^2 x$ term would be 225 times larger than the data term. The derivatives
$x_{\thetavec}'$ and $x_{\thetavec}''$ are the network's own, taken by automatic differentiation in $t$. No
observation is needed at a collocation point.

Both networks start from the same weights. Each takes 200 steps of <NoteLink to="adam">Adam</NoteLink> with
<Gloss name="learning-rate">learning rate</Gloss> 0.03, then up to 1000 steps of full-batch <Gloss name="l-bfgs" />.

## What the equation says about the model family

The equation alone does not fix the motion. It fixes a family of motions, and the observations choose one member.

The equation is linear with constant coefficients, so its solutions are combinations of exponentials $e^{\mu t}$.
Substituting $x = e^{\mu t}$ gives the
<NoteLink to="characteristic-polynomial">characteristic equation</NoteLink>

$$
\mu^2 + 2\zeta\omega_0 \mu + \omega_0^2 = 0, \qquad
\mu = -\zeta\omega_0 \pm i\omega_d, \qquad \omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2},
$$

for $\zeta < 1$. The real part $-\zeta\omega_0$ sets the decay and the imaginary part $\omega_d$, the damped frequency,
sets the swing. Every real solution is

$$
x(t) = e^{-\zeta\omega_0 t} \paren{A \cos \omega_d t + B \sin \omega_d t}
$$

for some constants $A$ and $B$. The family is two-dimensional: the equation fixes the decay rate and the frequency, and
leaves the amplitude and phase free. Every member has zero residual, including $A = B = 0$.

The physics-informed loss therefore splits the work. The residual term pushes the network towards the two-dimensional
family; among functions in or near the family, the data term prefers the member through the observations. Ten
observations over half a swing are more than enough to fix two numbers, which is why they suffice once the equation
supplies the rest. The data-only network has no family to be pushed towards: it fits the observations and, beyond
them, follows whatever its weights happen to favour.

<SolutionFamily />

If the closed form were known, fitting $A$ and $B$ by least squares would be a two-parameter regression and needs no
network. The physics-informed network never uses the closed form; it uses only the equation. The oscillator is a check
of the method, chosen because the closed form exists to measure the error against. Equations without a closed form are
where the method earns its cost.

## Solution

The observations come from the member with $x(0) = 1$ and $x'(0) = 0$. At $t = 0$ the general solution gives $x(0) = A$,
so $A = 1$. Its derivative is
$x'(t) = e^{-\zeta\omega_0 t} \paren{(B\omega_d - \zeta\omega_0 A) \cos \omega_d t - (A\omega_d + \zeta\omega_0 B) \sin \omega_d t}$,
so $x'(0) = B\omega_d - \zeta\omega_0 A = 0$ and $B = \zeta\omega_0 / \omega_d$. The motion is

$$
x(t) = e^{-\zeta\omega_0 t} \paren{\cos \omega_d t + \frac{\zeta\omega_0}{\omega_d} \sin \omega_d t}.
$$

With $\omega_0 = 15$ and $\zeta = 0.2$: $\zeta\omega_0 = 3$, $\omega_d = 15\sqrt{0.96} \approx 14.70$ and
$B \approx 0.204$. The envelope $e^{-3t}$ falls to $e^{-3} \approx 0.05$ by $t = 1$.

## Simulation

The figure trains both networks in the browser and keeps a checkpoint every ten steps. The error is the relative
$L_2$ error on 151 evenly spaced times in $[0, 1]$,
$\norm{x_{\thetavec} - x}_2 / \norm{x}_2$.

<OscillatorPinn />

With the settings above, the two networks end as follows.

| Network            | After 200 Adam steps | After L-BFGS | Mean squared residual on the grid |
| ------------------ | -------------------- | ------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Data only          | 422%                 | 650%         | 4.6                               |
| Physics-informed   | 35%                  | 2.9%         | $7 \times 10^{-5}$                |

The data-only network passes through all ten observations and then leaves the motion entirely: an error of several
times the motion's own size. More training makes it worse, because L-BFGS fits the observations more exactly and
nothing constrains the rest of the interval. The physics-informed network carries the oscillation through the two
swings it never observed. Its residual on the fine grid, between the collocation points, is as small as at the points
themselves.

Changing one setting at a time from the defaults gives these physics-informed errors after training.

| Change                              | Physics-informed error | What it shows                                                  |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sine activation                     | under 0.1%             | an activation that oscillates suits an oscillating solution    |
| Softplus activation                 | 8%                     | smooth, but a poorer fit to the swing than tanh                |
| <Gloss name="relu" /> activation                     | 40%                    | $x'' = 0$ almost everywhere; the residual misses the curvature |
| 10 collocation points               | 28%                    | about four points per swing are too few                        |
| 5 collocation points                | 27%                    | the equation is enforced at too few places                     |
| $\lambda = 0.01$                    | 7%                     | the equation is too weak against the data term                 |
| $\lambda = 100$                     | 0.1%                   | with exact data and an exact equation, more weight helps       |
| Data up to $t = 0.1$                | 2%                     | even a quarter swing of data fixes the two constants           |
| Adam only, 3000 steps               | 9%                     | L-BFGS finishes the job far faster                             |

Every row keeps the data-only network above 100% error.

## What it illustrates

- The residual term of a <NoteLink to="physics-informed-neural-networks">physics-informed network</NoteLink> constrains
  the function wherever there are collocation points, which need no labels. Data constrain it only where there are
  data.
- The equation defines a family of solutions, here found through the
  <NoteLink to="characteristic-polynomial">characteristic polynomial</NoteLink>; the data or conditions choose the
  member.
- The residual needs a smooth network: a ReLU network's second derivative is zero almost everywhere, as the
  <NoteLink to="activation-functions">activation</NoteLink> comparison shows.
- Fitting data says nothing about where there are none. The data-only network has near-zero training error and an
  error of several times the signal beyond the data. A network can represent the true motion
  (<NoteLink to="universal-approximation-theorem">universal approximation</NoteLink>), but nothing in the data-only loss
  selects it.
- Adam then <NoteLink to="quasi-newton-methods">L-BFGS</NoteLink> on a fixed, full-batch loss converges in a fraction of
  the steps Adam alone needs <Cite id="raissi2019" />.

## Variations

- **Unknown damping.** Make $\zeta$ a trainable parameter next to $\thetavec$. The same loss then fits the motion and
  estimates $\zeta$ from the observations: an inverse problem.
- **Hard initial conditions.** The trial function $x_{\thetavec}(t) = 1 + t^2 N_{\thetavec}(t)$ meets $x(0) = 1$ and
  $x'(0) = 0$ for every $\thetavec$ <Cite id="lagaris1998" />. Without observations, the residual alone then determines
  the motion.
- **A longer interval or a higher frequency.** More swings in the domain make the target higher in frequency relative
  to the network's input range. Networks fit low frequencies first <Cite id="rahaman2019" />, so training slows; sine
  activations <Cite id="sitzmann2020" /> and Fourier-feature inputs counter it.
- **Noisy observations.** With noise in the data, a large $\lambda$ no longer helps without limit: the equation then
  acts as a regulariser that smooths the noise, and $\lambda$ trades the fit to the data against the fit to the
  equation.
- **A forcing term.** With $x'' + 2\zeta\omega_0 x' + \omega_0^2 x = f(t)$ the residual gains $-f(t)/\omega_0^2$, and
  the family becomes a particular solution plus the same two-dimensional family.
