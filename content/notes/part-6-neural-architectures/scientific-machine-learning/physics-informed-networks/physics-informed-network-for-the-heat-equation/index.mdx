<Definition>
Solve the heat equation

$$
u_t = \kappa\, u_{xx}, \qquad x \in [-1, 1], \quad t \in [0, 1],
$$

with diffusivity $\kappa = 0.1$, initial profile $u(x, 0) = \sin \pi x$ and end values $u(-1, t) = u(1, t) = 0$, by a
<NoteLink to="physics-informed-neural-networks">physics-informed network</NoteLink> trained without observations inside
the domain. Compare it with the exact solution from the Fourier series, and measure how its error changes with the
number of collocation points $N_r$.
</Definition>

## Setup

- $u(x, t)$ is a temperature and $\kappa > 0$ the diffusivity, the coefficient called $D$ in the
  <NoteLink to="heat-equation">heat equation</NoteLink> note.
- The network $u_{\thetavec}(x, t)$ is a <Gloss name="mlp">multilayer perceptron</Gloss> with inputs scaled to
  $[-1, 1]^2$, two hidden layers of 12 hyperbolic tangent units and one output:
  $2 \cdot 12 + 12 + 12 \cdot 12 + 12 + 12 + 1 = 205$ parameters.
- The loss is the mean squared residual $r = u_t - \kappa u_{xx}$ at $N_r$ collocation points, a scrambled Sobol set,
  plus the mean squared misfits at 50 evenly spaced points on the initial line and 25 on each end, with weight 1.
- Training is 300 steps of <NoteLink to="adam">Adam</NoteLink> at <Gloss name="learning-rate">learning rate</Gloss>
  0.005, then 100 steps of full-batch <Gloss name="l-bfgs" />, under a second per run.
- The error is the relative $L_2$ error $\norm{u_{\thetavec} - u}_2 / \norm{u}_2$ on a grid of $41 \times 21$ points.

## Solution

Separate variables, $u = X(x)\,T(t)$. The equation becomes $T'/(\kappa T) = X''/X$, and both sides equal a constant
$-\mu$. The space part $X'' = -\mu X$ with $X(\pm 1) = 0$ has the solutions

$$
X_n(x) = \sin\paren{\frac{n\pi (x + 1)}{2}}, \qquad \mu_n = \paren{\frac{n\pi}{2}}^2, \qquad n = 1, 2, \dots,
$$

and the time part is $T_n(t) = e^{-\kappa \mu_n t}$. The solution is the
<NoteLink to="fourier-series">Fourier sine series</NoteLink> $u = \sum_n b_n e^{-\kappa \mu_n t} X_n(x)$ with $b_n$ the
coefficients of the initial profile <Cite id="evans2010" />. The initial profile is a single term:
$X_2(x) = \sin(\pi x + \pi) = -\sin \pi x$, so $b_2 = -1$ and every other $b_n = 0$. With $\mu_2 = \pi^2$,

$$
u(x, t) = e^{-\kappa \pi^2 t} \sin \pi x .
$$

At $\kappa = 0.1$ the amplitude falls to $e^{-0.987} \approx 0.37$ by $t = 1$. Substituting back checks it:
$u_t = -\kappa\pi^2 u$ and $u_{xx} = -\pi^2 u$, so $u_t - \kappa u_{xx} = 0$.

Mode $n$ decays at the rate $\kappa (n\pi/2)^2$, so higher modes die faster. An error in the network's fit of the
initial profile at high frequency is damped by the equation itself as $t$ grows. The problem is smooth, low in
frequency and stable forward in time: the gentle case for a physics-informed network, whose
<NoteLink to="failure-modes-of-physics-informed-networks">failure modes</NoteLink> are all about high frequencies and
steep features.

With 4 points the network drives the residual at those points to $4 \times 10^{-7}$ while the residual on the grid is
1.4, three million times larger, and the error is 50%: the network has 205 parameters and only 4 residual
constraints. At 64 points the two residuals are within a factor of two, and from 32 points on the error stops falling
with $N_r$ and stays near 1%, the level set by the network's size and the short training. A longer budget (16 units,
500 Adam and 300 L-BFGS steps, an $81 \times 41$ grid) shows the same pattern one level lower: 17% error at 4 points,
3.2% at 16, 0.27% at 64 and 0.22% at 256. For this smooth solution, a few dozen points spread over the domain suffice.

## What it illustrates

- **The residual constrains the function only where it is evaluated.** With few collocation points the network meets
  the equation at those points and departs from it between them. The gap between the residual at the points and on the
  grid measures it, and it closes as the points become dense relative to the solution's length scale.
- **The conditions carry the rest.** The initial and boundary points fix the member of the solution family, as in the
  <NoteLink to="physics-informed-network-for-a-damped-oscillator">damped oscillator</NoteLink>. Building them into the
  network, $u = \sin \pi x + t(1 - x^2)\, N_{\thetavec}(x, t)$, removes their terms
  (<NoteLink to="hard-constraints-for-physics-informed-networks">hard constraints</NoteLink>).
- **A smooth problem is easy.** A network of 337 parameters trained for 800 steps on 128 points reaches 0.25% here,
  while one of 921 parameters trained for 1500 steps leaves a 24% error on
  <NoteLink to="physics-informed-network-for-burgers-equation">Burgers' equation</NoteLink>, whose front is 0.0065 wide.

## Variations

- **Larger $\kappa$.** The solution decays faster, $e^{-\kappa\pi^2}$ at $t = 1$, and the relative error is dominated by
  late times where the solution is small.
- **A rougher initial profile.** A profile with several modes, such as a step, puts energy into high $n$. The network
  must fit those modes at $t = 0$, where <NoteLink to="implicit-neural-representation">spectral bias</NoteLink> slows
  it, before diffusion removes them.
- **Unknown diffusivity.** With observations of $u$ and $\kappa$ trainable, the same setup becomes an
  <NoteLink to="inverse-problems-with-physics-informed-networks">inverse problem</NoteLink>, whose figure uses this
  equation.
- **Backward in time.** Recovering $u(x, 0)$ from $u(x, 1)$ is ill-posed: mode $n$ must be amplified by
  $e^{\kappa (n\pi/2)^2}$, so noise at high $n$ explodes. The loss is unchanged in form, and a physics-informed network
  trained on final-time data returns a smooth initial profile only because of its own spectral bias.
