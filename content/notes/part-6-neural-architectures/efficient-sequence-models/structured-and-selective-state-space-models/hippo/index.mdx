<Definition>
**HiPPO** (high-order polynomial projection operators) is a rule for choosing the matrices of a linear system
$\dot\hvec(t) = \Amat(t)\hvec(t) + \Bmat(t)u(t)$, with state $\hvec(t) \in \reals^N$ and scalar input $u(t)$, so that
at every time $t$ the state holds the $N$ coefficients of the best approximation of the input history
$\{u(s) : s \le t\}$ by a polynomial of degree $N - 1$. "Best" is least squares under a measure $\mu^{(t)}$ that says
how much each past instant counts. The uniform measure on a sliding window $[t - \theta, t]$ gives **HiPPO-LegT**
(translated Legendre); the uniform measure on the whole history $[0, t]$ gives **HiPPO-LegS** (scaled Legendre)
<Cite id="gu2020hippo" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A recurrent network must compress everything it has read into a fixed-size state. A
<NoteLink to="recurrent-neural-network">recurrent network</NoteLink> with random weights does this badly: the influence
of an input decays or grows geometrically with its age, and its <NoteLink
to="vanishing-and-exploding-gradients">gradients vanish or explode</NoteLink> over long spans. HiPPO asks what a
memory of $N$ numbers should hold and answers with approximation theory.

A function on an interval is well summarised by its first $N$ coefficients in an orthogonal polynomial basis, as a
periodic function is by its first <NoteLink to="fourier-series">Fourier coefficients</NoteLink>. The coefficients are
inner products, an <NoteLink to="orthogonal-projection">orthogonal projection</NoteLink> onto the span of the first
$N$ polynomials. As time advances, the history grows by one instant and the interval moves or stretches. The
coefficients change, and HiPPO shows that for Legendre polynomials on these intervals they change by a linear
differential equation driven by the newest input. That equation is a state-space system, so a linear recurrent layer
can maintain the projection online, one input at a time, with no record of the past beyond the $N$ coefficients.

## The projection

Let $P_n$ be the Legendre polynomial of degree $n$ on $[-1, 1]$: $P_0(z) = 1$, $P_1(z) = z$ and
$(n+1)P_{n+1}(z) = (2n+1)zP_n(z) - nP_{n-1}(z)$. The $P_n$ are orthogonal on $[-1, 1]$ with
$\int_{-1}^{1} P_n P_k\,dz = 2\delta_{nk}/(2n+1)$, and $P_n(1) = 1$, $P_n(-1) = (-1)^n$.

At time $t$, let the history window be $[a_t, t]$ with length $\ell_t = t - a_t$: $a_t = t - \theta$ for LegT and
$a_t = 0$ for LegS. Map the window onto $[-1, 1]$ by $z = 2(s - a_t)/\ell_t - 1$, and define the basis

$$
g_n^{(t)}(s) = \sqrt{2n+1}\,P_n\!\paren{\frac{2(s - a_t)}{\ell_t} - 1}, \qquad n = 0, \dots, N - 1.
$$

Under the uniform measure $d\mu^{(t)}(s) = ds/\ell_t$ on the window these functions are orthonormal: the factor
$2n+1$ cancels the norm $2/(2n+1)$, and the change of variable contributes $\ell_t/2$, which the measure divides by
$\ell_t$. The best approximation is therefore given by inner products,

$$
h_n(t) = \frac{1}{\ell_t}\int_{a_t}^{t} u(s)\,g_n^{(t)}(s)\,ds, \qquad
u(s) \approx \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} h_n(t)\,g_n^{(t)}(s) \ \text{ for } s \in [a_t, t].
$$

The vector $\hvec(t) = (h_0(t), \dots, h_{N-1}(t))$ is the memory. $h_0(t)$ is the mean of $u$ over the window, $h_1$
measures its trend, and higher coefficients carry finer detail.

## The sliding window: LegT

Differentiate $h_n(t)$ with $\ell_t = \theta$ fixed. Both ends of the integral move, and the basis moves with them:

$$
\dot h_n(t) = \frac{1}{\theta}\Big[u(t)\,g_n^{(t)}(t) - u(t - \theta)\,g_n^{(t)}(t - \theta)\Big]
+ \frac{1}{\theta}\int_{t-\theta}^{t} u(s)\,\frac{\partial g_n^{(t)}(s)}{\partial t}\,ds.
$$

Each piece is a combination of the coefficients or of the input.

1. **The new input.** At $s = t$ the argument is $z = 1$, so $g_n^{(t)}(t) = \sqrt{2n+1}$. The first term is
   $\sqrt{2n+1}\,u(t)/\theta$.
2. **The input leaving the window.** At $s = t - \theta$, $z = -1$ and $g_n^{(t)}(t - \theta) = \sqrt{2n+1}(-1)^n$.
   The value $u(t - \theta)$ is no longer stored, so HiPPO replaces it by its reconstruction from the state,
   $u(t - \theta) \approx \sum_k h_k(t)\sqrt{2k+1}(-1)^k$ <Cite id="gu2020hippo" />.
3. **The moving basis.** $z$ falls at the rate $2/\theta$ as $t$ grows, so
   $\partial g_n^{(t)}/\partial t = -(2/\theta)\sqrt{2n+1}\,P_n'(z)$. The derivative of a Legendre polynomial is a sum
   of lower ones, $P_n' = \sum_{k < n,\ n - k \text{ odd}} (2k+1)P_k$, so the integral is
   $-(2/\theta)\sqrt{2n+1}\sum_{k<n,\ n-k \text{ odd}} \sqrt{2k+1}\,h_k(t)$.

Collecting the coefficient of $h_k$ from pieces 2 and 3 gives $\dot\hvec = \Amat\hvec + \Bmat u$ with

$$
A_{nk} = -\frac{\sqrt{2n+1}\sqrt{2k+1}}{\theta}\begin{cases} 1 & k < n, \\ (-1)^{n-k} & k \ge n, \end{cases}
\qquad B_n = \frac{\sqrt{2n+1}}{\theta}.
$$

For $k < n$ the two pieces combine: $(-1)^{n+k} + 2 = 1$ when $n - k$ is odd, and $(-1)^{n+k} = 1$ when it is even.
The system is time-invariant. It is the Legendre memory unit of Voelker and colleagues written in an orthonormal basis,
and HiPPO's derivation explains where that unit's matrices come from <Cite id="gu2020hippo" />.

## The whole history: LegS

For LegS the window is $[0, t]$ and stretches as $t$ grows. The same three-step calculation, with the stretch in place
of the slide, gives a system whose matrices are divided by $t$ <Cite id="gu2020hippo" />:

$$
\dot\hvec(t) = \frac{1}{t}\Amat\hvec(t) + \frac{1}{t}\Bmat u(t), \qquad
A_{nk} = -\begin{cases} \sqrt{2n+1}\sqrt{2k+1} & n > k, \\ n + 1 & n = k, \\ 0 & n < k, \end{cases}
\qquad B_n = \sqrt{2n+1}.
$$

The matrix $\Amat$ is lower triangular, so its eigenvalues are its diagonal entries $-1, -2, \dots, -N$. No input
leaves the window, so LegS needs no reconstruction step and its online state equals the projection up to
discretisation error.

<Derivation title="Deriving the LegS system">
With $\ell_t = t$ and $a_t = 0$, $h_n(t) = \frac{1}{t}\int_0^t u(s)\,g_n^{(t)}(s)\,ds$ and $z = 2s/t - 1$. The product
rule gives three terms:

$$
\dot h_n = -\frac{1}{t}h_n + \frac{1}{t}u(t)\,g_n^{(t)}(t) + \frac{1}{t}\int_0^t u(s)\,\frac{\partial g_n^{(t)}(s)}{\partial t}\,ds.
$$

The second term is $\sqrt{2n+1}\,u(t)/t$, since $z = 1$ at $s = t$. For the third, $\partial z/\partial t = -2s/t^2
= -(z+1)/t$, so $\partial g_n^{(t)}/\partial t = -\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{2n+1}\,(z+1)P_n'(z)$. The identity
$(z+1)P_n'(z) = nP_n(z) + \sum_{k<n}(2k+1)P_k(z)$ follows from $P_n' = \sum_{k<n,\,n-k\text{ odd}}(2k+1)P_k$ and
$zP_n' = nP_n + P_{n-1}'$. Substituting $P_k = g_k/\sqrt{2k+1}$ turns the integral into
$-\frac{1}{t}\paren{n\,h_n + \sum_{k<n}\sqrt{2n+1}\sqrt{2k+1}\,h_k}$. Adding the three terms,

$$
\dot h_n = \frac{1}{t}\Big[-(n+1)h_n - \sum_{k<n}\sqrt{2n+1}\sqrt{2k+1}\,h_k + \sqrt{2n+1}\,u(t)\Big],
$$

which is row $n$ of the LegS system.
</Derivation>

## Properties of LegS

- **No timescale to tune.** If the input is played faster, $v(s) = u(\alpha s)$, then the state follows the same path
  faster, $\hvec_v(t) = \hvec_u(\alpha t)$. LegS has no window length and no step size that must match the data
  <Cite id="gu2020hippo" />.
- **Gradients decay polynomially.** The sensitivity of the state at time $t_1$ to the input at $t_0 < t_1$ is
  $\Theta(1/t_1)$, not geometric in $t_1 - t_0$ as for a contracting recurrence <Cite id="gu2020hippo" />.
- **Error falls with $N$.** For an input with Lipschitz constant $L$, the approximation error on $[0, t]$ is
  $O(tL/\sqrt{N})$; with $k$ bounded derivatives it is $O(t^k N^{-k+1/2})$ <Cite id="gu2020hippo" />.

## Worked example

Take LegS with $N = 4$ and a step input: $u(s) = 0$ for $s < 1$ and $u(s) = 1$ for $s \ge 1$. Read the state at
$t = 2$.

**The matrices.** With $\sqrt3 = 1.732$, $\sqrt5 = 2.236$, $\sqrt7 = 2.646$,

$$
\Amat = -\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1.732 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 2.236 & 3.873 & 3 & 0 \\ 2.646 & 4.583 & 5.916 & 4
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \Bmat = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1.732 \\ 2.236 \\ 2.646 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

The entry $3.873$ is $\sqrt{3}\sqrt{5}$, $4.583$ is $\sqrt{3}\sqrt{7}$ and $5.916$ is $\sqrt{5}\sqrt{7}$.

**The coefficients.** At $t = 2$ the map is $z = s - 1$, so the step sits at $z = 0$ and $u = 1$ on $z \in [0, 1]$.
With $ds = dz$ and $\ell_t = 2$, $h_n = \tfrac12\sqrt{2n+1}\int_0^1 P_n(z)\,dz$. The four integrals are
$\int_0^1 1\,dz = 1$, $\int_0^1 z\,dz = \tfrac12$, $\int_0^1 \tfrac12(3z^2 - 1)\,dz = 0$ and
$\int_0^1 \tfrac12(5z^3 - 3z)\,dz = -\tfrac18$. So

$$
\hvec(2) = \paren{0.5,\ \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4},\ 0,\ -\tfrac{\sqrt7}{16}} = (0.5,\ 0.433,\ 0,\ -0.165).
$$

Running the LegS recurrence over 2000 samples (step $0.001$, bilinear rule) gives the same four numbers to within
$2 \times 10^{-4}$.

**A check on the dynamics.** Row 0 of the system reads $\dot h_0 = \frac1t(-h_0 + u(t))$. At $t = 2$ this is
$\frac12(-0.5 + 1) = 0.25$. Directly, $h_0(t) = (t-1)/t$ is the fraction of the history after the step, and its
derivative $1/t^2$ is $0.25$ at $t = 2$.

**The reconstruction.** $u(s) \approx \sum_n h_n\sqrt{2n+1}P_n(z) = 0.5 + 0.75\,z - 0.4375\,P_3(z)$, with
$P_3(z) = \tfrac12(5z^3 - 3z)$.

| $s$ | $z$ | $P_3(z)$ | reconstruction | input |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | $-1$ | $-1$ | $0.188$ | 0 |
| 0.5 | $-0.5$ | $0.4375$ | $-0.066$ | 0 |
| 1 | 0 | 0 | $0.5$ | jump |
| 1.5 | $0.5$ | $-0.4375$ | $1.066$ | 1 |
| 2 | 1 | 1 | $0.813$ | 1 |

Four numbers locate the jump at its midpoint value $0.5$. The cubic overshoots on both sides of the discontinuity,
the polynomial analogue of the Gibbs phenomenon of a <NoteLink to="fourier-series">Fourier series</NoteLink>.

## From HiPPO to S4

HiPPO was first used inside a gated recurrent cell, where the LegS memory reached 98.3% test accuracy on permuted
sequential <Gloss name="mnist" /> <Cite id="gu2020hippo" />. A <NoteLink to="structured-state-space-models">structured
state-space layer</NoteLink> (S4) instead uses the LegS matrices in a time-invariant system $\dot\hvec = \Amat\hvec +
\Bmat u$, without the factor $1/t$, discretised with a learned step $\Delta$ <Cite id="gu2022s4" />. Gu and colleagues
later showed that this time-invariant system projects the history onto Legendre polynomials in an exponentially warped
time, so recent inputs are resolved finely and old ones coarsely, and that $\Delta$ sets the length of history the
warp covers <Cite id="gu2023hippo" />.

Two consequences shaped later models. The LegS matrix is not diagonalisable in a numerically useful basis, which led
S4 to its normal-plus-low-rank kernel algorithm <Cite id="gu2022s4" />. And the normal part of LegS, the matrix
**HiPPO-N** obtained by adding back a rank-one term, equals $-\tfrac12\eye$ plus a skew-symmetric matrix. Its
eigenvalues all have real part $-\tfrac12$, and it diagonalises stably. Diagonal layers such as S4D, S5 and the
<NoteLink to="linear-recurrent-unit-and-s5">linear recurrent unit</NoteLink> start from its eigenvalues or from rings
of eigenvalues that imitate them <Cite id="smith2023s5" />.

## Assumptions

- **A continuous input sampled finely.** The derivation treats $u$ as a function of continuous time. A sequence is read
  as samples $u_k = u(k\Delta t)$, and the discretised system approximates the continuous projection.
- **A uniform measure.** LegT and LegS weight the window uniformly. Other measures give other systems: an exponentially
  decaying measure gives HiPPO-LagT with Laguerre polynomials <Cite id="gu2020hippo" />.
- **Smooth histories compress well.** The error bounds depend on the smoothness of $u$. A history full of
  discontinuities needs many coefficients.

## Pitfalls

- **Stiffness.** LegS has eigenvalues down to $-N$. The eigenvalues of LegT grow in proportion to $N/\theta$, with
  moduli up to $58.5/\theta$ for $N = 32$. Forward Euler with too large a step diverges. The bilinear rule and the
  <NoteLink to="state-space-representation">zero-order hold</NoteLink> are stable for any step
  <Cite id="gu2020hippo" />.
- **LegT forgets approximately.** The input leaving the window is reconstructed from the state, not known, so LegT's
  state is only close to the windowed projection, and its error grows near discontinuities.
- **LegS blurs the past.** A fixed $N$ spread over a growing interval loses resolution on old inputs at rate $1/t$.
  Tasks that need sharp recall of one distant token need a larger $N$ or a different mechanism.
- **The time-invariant variant is a different system.** S4's use of the LegS matrices without $1/t$ does not hold the
  uniform projection of the history. Its state is the warped projection described above.
