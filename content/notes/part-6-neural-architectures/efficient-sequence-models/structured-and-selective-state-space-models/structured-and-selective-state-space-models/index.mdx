<Definition>
**Structured and selective state-space models** are sequence layers that run a discretised linear system
$\hvec_t = \bar\Amat_t\hvec_{t-1} + \bar\Bmat_t x_t$, $y_t = \Cmat_t\hvec_t$ over their input, with nonlinear layers
between them. "Structured" refers to the special form of the state matrix, diagonal or diagonal plus low rank, that
makes the layer cheap to compute. "Selective" refers to coefficients computed from the current token, which let the
layer choose what to store <Cite id="gu2022s4" /> <Cite id="gu2023mamba" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A transformer layer costs $O(T^2)$ for $T$ tokens and generates each token by reading a cache that grows with $T$. A
recurrent network keeps a fixed-size state but is slow to train and forgets over long spans. State-space layers aim at
both properties at once: training in parallel over the sequence, as a convolution or a scan, and generation with a
fixed-size state at constant cost per token. They have been used for long-range classification benchmarks with sequences
of 16,384 steps, for <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss>, and for audio and genomic sequences.

## Main ideas

**A linear system with a meaningful state.** <NoteLink to="hippo">HiPPO</NoteLink> derives state matrices for which
the state holds the coefficients of the best polynomial approximation of the input history, over a sliding window
(LegT) or the whole past (LegS). It answers what a memory of $N$ numbers should contain, and its matrices initialised
the first <NoteLink to="deep-state-space-models">deep state-space models</NoteLink>.

**Linear and time-invariant means convolution.** A <NoteLink to="structured-state-space-models">structured state-space
layer</NoteLink> (S4) discretises the continuous system with a learned step. Because its coefficients are the same at
every position, the layer is a <NoteLink to="wavenet-and-causal-convolution">causal convolution</NoteLink> with kernel
$\bar K_k = \Cmat\bar\Amat^k\bar\Bmat$, trained by FFT and run as a recurrence at generation. Diagonal state matrices
(S4D) make the kernel a sum of damped oscillations and replace S4's normal-plus-low-rank algorithm.

**A scan instead of a kernel.** <NoteLink to="linear-recurrent-unit-and-s5">S5 and the linear recurrent unit</NoteLink>
evaluate the diagonal recurrence directly with a <NoteLink to="parallel-scan" />. S5 shares one multi-input state
across channels; the LRU reaches the same recurrence from a plain recurrent network, with eigenvalues learned inside
the unit disc, initialised on a ring near 1, and normalised inputs.

**Long filters and gates.** <NoteLink to="long-convolution-models">Long convolution models</NoteLink> keep the
convolutional view and change how the filter is made. H3 multiplies projections of the input around two state-space
convolutions, and Hyena generates filters as long as the sequence with a small network of position, interleaved with
elementwise gates.

**Selection.** <NoteLink to="mamba" /> computes the step $\Delta_t$ and the maps $\Bmat_t$ and $\Cmat_t$ from each
token. A large step overwrites the state with the token and a small one keeps the state, so the layer can store and
ignore tokens by content. The layer is no longer a convolution and trains with a hardware-aware scan.
<NoteLink to="mamba-2-and-state-space-duality">Mamba-2</NoteLink> restricts the transition to a scalar per head, which
turns the layer into masked linear attention with a semiseparable decay mask and lets it be computed with
<NoteLink to="matrix-multiplication">matrix multiplications</NoteLink> <Cite id="dao2024" />.
