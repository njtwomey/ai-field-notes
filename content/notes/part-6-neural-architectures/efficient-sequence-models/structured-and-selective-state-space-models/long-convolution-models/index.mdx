<Definition>
A **long convolution layer** computes the <NoteLink to="wavenet-and-causal-convolution">causal convolution</NoteLink>
$y_t = \sum_{j=0}^{t} h_j v_{t-j}$ of an input sequence $v_0, \dots, v_{L-1}$ with a filter $h$ as long as the sequence,
$L$ taps, and evaluates it with the fast Fourier transform in $O(L\log L)$ time. **H3** and **Hyena** build
attention-free sequence layers from long convolutions and elementwise multiplicative gates applied to linear projections
of the input. In Hyena the filter is **implicit**: its taps are the outputs of a small network applied to an encoding of
the lag $t$, so the number of parameters does not grow with $L$ <Cite id="fu2023h3" /> <Cite id="poli2023hyena" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A <NoteLink to="structured-state-space-models">structured state-space layer</NoteLink> (S4) is a long convolution in
disguise. Its kernel $\bar K_k = \Cmat\bar\Amat^k\bar\Bmat$ has $L$ taps but only $O(N)$ parameters, and training
convolves with it by FFT. The state space is one way to generate a long filter from few parameters with a good
<NoteLink to="inductive-bias">inductive bias</NoteLink>: smooth, decaying, made of damped oscillations. Long convolution
models ask which parts of S4 matter and replace the rest.

Two observations drive the family. The first is that the filter can be generated in other ways, as long as the
parameter count is independent of $L$ and the filter decays. The second is that a convolution is time-invariant: it
weights an input by its distance, never by its content, and for that reason S4 fails at recalling a token that
followed an earlier one. Attention solves recall with products of projections of the input, $\Qmat\Kmat^\tr\Vmat$.
H3 and Hyena add the same kind of product, an elementwise gate by a projection of the input, between convolutions.
The convolutions carry information across long distances; the gates make the overall map depend on the data.

## Fast convolution

A causal convolution of length $L$ computed directly costs $\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}(t + 1) = L(L + 1)/2$ multiplies, the same
quadratic cost as attention. The <NoteLink to="convolution-theorem">convolution theorem</NoteLink> removes it. The
<NoteLink to="discrete-fourier-transform">discrete Fourier transform</NoteLink> of length $M$ turns a **circular**
convolution of two length-$M$ sequences into an elementwise product:
$\operatorname{DFT}(h \circledast v) = \operatorname{DFT}(h) \odot \operatorname{DFT}(v)$.

A circular convolution wraps around: index $t - j$ is taken modulo $M$, so late inputs leak into early outputs. A
linear causal convolution follows by zero-padding both sequences to $M \ge 2L - 1$. The padded tail absorbs the wrap,
and the first $L$ outputs of the circular result are the causal outputs. The algorithm is three
<NoteLink to="fast-fourier-transform">FFTs</NoteLink> of length $M$ and $M$ pointwise products, $O(L\log L)$ in total.

## H3

H3 (Hungry Hungry Hippos) was designed from two failures of S4 on synthetic language tasks: recalling tokens that
follow a specific earlier token, and comparing tokens across the sequence <Cite id="fu2023h3" />. It projects each
input $\xvec_t$ to $\qvec_t, \kvec_t, \vvec_t$, like attention, and computes

$$
\yvec = \qvec \odot \operatorname{SSM}_{\text{diag}}\paren{\operatorname{SSM}_{\text{shift}}(\kvec) \odot \vvec}.
$$

- $\operatorname{SSM}_{\text{shift}}$ has a shift matrix as state matrix, so its state holds the last few inputs and
  the layer is a short convolution over $\kvec$. It lets the product with $\vvec_t$ see the key of the previous
  tokens.
- $\operatorname{SSM}_{\text{diag}}$ is a diagonal state space (S4D), a long convolution that accumulates the products
  over the whole sequence.
- The final product with $\qvec_t$ reads the accumulated memory, as a query reads keys and values in
  <NoteLink to="linear-attention">linear attention</NoteLink>.

On two-layer models, H3 solved the induction-head task (100% against 35.6% for S4D) and
<NoteLink to="long-range-and-recall-benchmarks">associative recall</NoteLink> (99.8% against 86.0%), matching attention
<Cite id="fu2023h3" />. As a <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss> it came within 0.4 perplexity of a
transformer on OpenWebText, and a hybrid that kept two attention layers beat the transformer by 1.0 perplexity
<Cite id="fu2023h3" />.

## Hyena

### The operator

The Hyena operator of order $N$ projects the input to $N + 1$ sequences $v, x^1, \dots, x^N$, each of length $L$, and
alternates long convolutions with gates <Cite id="poli2023hyena" />:

$$
z^1 = v, \qquad z^{n+1}_t = x^n_t\,(h^n * z^n)_t \quad (n = 1, \dots, N), \qquad y = z^{N+1}.
$$

In matrix form, $y = \Dmat_x^N\Smat_h^N \cdots \Dmat_x^1\Smat_h^1 v$, where $\Smat_h^n$ is the lower-triangular
Toeplitz matrix of filter $h^n$ and $\Dmat_x^n = \diag(x^n)$. The matrix multiplying $v$ depends on the input through
the gates: Hyena is a **data-controlled** linear map, as attention's $\operatorname{softmax}(\Qmat\Kmat^\tr)$ is.
Order 2 with filters generated by state spaces is H3 <Cite id="poli2023hyena" />. The projections are followed by a
short explicit convolution of width 3, which gives each gate local context.

### Implicit filters

Each filter is generated by a feed-forward network of the lag, times a window:

$$
h_t = \operatorname{window}(t)\cdot\operatorname{FFN}\paren{\operatorname{PE}(t)}, \qquad
\operatorname{window}(t) = e^{-\alpha t} + b.
$$

- $\operatorname{PE}(t) = \paren{t,\ \operatorname{Re}\rho_0(t), \dots, \operatorname{Re}\rho_{K-1}(t),\
  \operatorname{Im}\rho_0(t), \dots, \operatorname{Im}\rho_{K-1}(t)}$ with $\rho_k(t) = e^{i2\pi kt/L}$, a vector of
  $2K + 1$ features. At initialisation, $K$ acts as a low-pass cut-off on the filters <Cite id="poli2023hyena" />.
- The FFN uses sine activations $\sin(\omega\,\cdot)$, which let a small network produce high-frequency filters. The
  paper's language models use depth 4, width 64 and $\omega = 14$ <Cite id="poli2023hyena" />.
- The window biases filters towards recent inputs. The decay $\alpha$ differs across channels, and the bias $b$ keeps
  long lags from being forced to zero.

The parameter count is that of the FFN, independent of $L$. A filter for a longer sequence is the same network
evaluated at more positions.

### Results

At 125 million parameters, Hyena of order 3 matched a transformer's perplexity on WikiText-103 (18.6 for both). At
sequence length 2048 it reached transformer quality on the Pile with 20% less training compute, and the operator ran
twice as fast as optimised attention at length 8K and 100 times as fast at length 64K <Cite id="poli2023hyena" />.
The same operator at single-nucleotide resolution with contexts of up to a million tokens is HyenaDNA
<Cite id="nguyen2023hyenadna" />.

## Worked example

**FFT convolution and the wrap.** Take $v = (1, 2, 0, -1)$ and $h = (1, 0.5, 0.25, 0)$. The causal convolution, term by
term:

- $y_0 = 1 \times 1 = 1$.
- $y_1 = 1 \times 2 + 0.5 \times 1 = 2.5$.
- $y_2 = 1 \times 0 + 0.5 \times 2 + 0.25 \times 1 = 1.25$.
- $y_3 = 1 \times (-1) + 0.5 \times 0 + 0.25 \times 2 + 0 \times 1 = -0.5$.

Now use DFTs of length $M = 4$ with no padding. $\operatorname{DFT}(v) = (2,\ 1 - 3i,\ 0,\ 1 + 3i)$ and
$\operatorname{DFT}(h) = (1.75,\ 0.75 - 0.5i,\ 0.75,\ 0.75 + 0.5i)$. Their product is
$(3.5,\ -0.75 - 2.75i,\ 0,\ -0.75 + 2.75i)$, and the inverse DFT gives $(0.5,\ 2.25,\ 1.25,\ -0.5)$. The first two
entries are wrong. $y_0 = 0.5$ includes $h_1 v_3 = -0.5$, and $y_1 = 2.25$ includes $h_2 v_3 = -0.25$: the last
input has wrapped round to the start. Padding both sequences with zeros to $M = 8$ gives the causal values
$(1, 2.5, 1.25, -0.5)$ in the first four entries.

**A Hyena operator of order 2.** Keep $v$ and $h^1 = h$, and take gates $x^1 = (1, 0, 1, 1)$, $x^2 = (0.5, 1, 1, 2)$
and a second filter $h^2 = (1, -1, 0, 0)$, a difference of neighbours.

1. $h^1 * v = (1, 2.5, 1.25, -0.5)$, as above. Gating by $x^1$ gives $z^2 = (1, 0, 1.25, -0.5)$: the gate deletes
   position 1.
2. $h^2 * z^2 = (1,\ 0 - 1,\ 1.25 - 0,\ -0.5 - 1.25) = (1, -1, 1.25, -1.75)$.
3. Gating by $x^2$ gives $y = z^3 = (0.5, -1, 1.25, -3.5)$.

Changing $x^1$, which is a projection of the input, changes which positions survive into the second convolution. The
same filters applied to a different input give a different linear map.

## Assumptions

- **Time-invariant filters.** Each filter is the same at every position; data dependence comes only from the gates.
- **Whole sequences at once.** The FFT works on a whole sequence. An implicit filter has no state-space form, so
  generation one token at a time has no constant-size state. H3's filters are state spaces and can run as
  recurrences.
- **Padding to twice the length.** The FFT size is at least $2L - 1$, rounded to a fast size, usually a power of 2.

## Pitfalls

- **Forgetting the padding.** A length-$L$ FFT computes a circular convolution, which leaks future inputs into early
  outputs and breaks causality.
- **Slow generation.** With an implicit filter each new token needs a sum over the whole history, $O(L)$ per token.
  Selective state spaces such as <NoteLink to="mamba" /> keep a fixed-size
  state instead.
- **Recall needs gates and state.** Long convolutions alone do not solve associative recall. H3's results came from the
  multiplicative structure, and hybrids that keep a few attention layers were still better at language modelling
  <Cite id="fu2023h3" />.
- **FFT efficiency on hardware.** FFTs use the matrix-multiply units of <Gloss name="gpu" plural /> poorly. H3
  introduced FlashConv, a fused FFT convolution that decomposes the transform into block matrix products, for this
  reason <Cite id="fu2023h3" />.
