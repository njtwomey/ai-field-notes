<Definition>
An **S5 layer** (simplified state space layer) maps an input sequence $\uvec_1, \dots, \uvec_L \in \reals^H$ to outputs
$\yvec_k \in \reals^H$ through one shared state $\hvec_k \in \complex^P$ with a diagonal transition:

$$
\hvec_k = \bar\Lambdamat\,\hvec_{k-1} + \bar\Bmat\,\uvec_k, \qquad \yvec_k = \operatorname{Re}\paren{\Cmat\hvec_k} + \Dmat\uvec_k,
$$

where $\bar\Lambdamat = \diag(\bar\lambda_1, \dots, \bar\lambda_P)$, $\bar\Bmat \in \complex^{P \times H}$,
$\Cmat \in \complex^{H \times P}$ and $\Dmat$ is diagonal. S5 obtains $\bar\Lambdamat$ and $\bar\Bmat$ by discretising a
continuous system with one learned step per state, and computes all $L$ states with a parallel scan
<Cite id="smith2023s5" />. The **linear recurrent unit** (LRU) is the same recurrence reached from the recurrent-network
side: the eigenvalues are learned directly as $\lambda_j = \exp(-e^{\nu_j} + i e^{\theta_j})$, initialised on a ring
of radii near 1, and the input is scaled by $\gamma_j = \sqrt{1 - \abs{\lambda_j}^2}$ <Cite id="orvieto2023" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A <NoteLink to="structured-state-space-models">structured state-space layer</NoteLink> (S4) gives each of the $H$
channels its own single-input, single-output system with $N$ states and trains it as a long convolution. Two of its
ingredients turn out not to be needed. The convolution is one way to evaluate a linear recurrence in parallel; a
<NoteLink to="parallel-scan">parallel scan</NoteLink> is another, and the scan does not care whether the system mixes
channels or changes over time. The intricate HiPPO matrix and its normal-plus-low-rank kernel algorithm can be replaced
by a diagonal matrix with well-chosen eigenvalues.

S5 keeps the continuous-time view of S4 and changes the computation: one multi-input, multi-output system, diagonal,
run by a scan. The LRU drops the continuous-time view. It starts from a plain
<NoteLink to="recurrent-neural-network">recurrent network</NoteLink> $\hvec_k = \sigma(\Amat\hvec_{k-1} + \Bmat\uvec_k)$
and makes four changes, each of which helps on long-range tasks: remove the nonlinearity $\sigma$, diagonalise $\Amat$
over the complex numbers, parameterise its eigenvalues so they stay inside the unit circle, and normalise the input
so the state does not grow when the eigenvalues approach the circle. The result is a recurrence of the S5 form,
reached without HiPPO, without discretisation and without a convolution <Cite id="orvieto2023" />.

In both layers the eigenvalues carry the memory. A state with $\abs{\lambda} = r$ forgets a fraction $1 - r$ of itself
per step, so its contribution halves every $\ln\tfrac12 / \ln r$ steps: 6.6 steps at $r = 0.9$ and 69 steps at
$r = 0.99$. A spread of radii close to 1 gives a bank of memories with different lengths.

## S5: one shared state, run by a scan

### From many small systems to one

S4 runs $H$ separate systems with $N$ states each, $HN$ states in all, and mixes channels only in the layer that
follows. S5 runs one system with $P$ states whose input matrix $\Bmat \in \complex^{P \times H}$ reads all channels
and whose output matrix $\Cmat \in \complex^{H \times P}$ writes all of them <Cite id="smith2023s5" />. With $P$
comparable to S4's $N$, S5 stores $P$ state numbers where S4 stores $HN$.

A shared state removes the convolutional mode's advantage. The kernel of a multi-input, multi-output system is a
sequence of $H \times H$ matrices $\Cmat\bar\Lambdamat^k\bar\Bmat$, and convolving with it costs $O(H^2 L \log L)$.
The recurrence costs $O(PHL)$ for the products $\bar\Bmat\uvec_k$ and $\Cmat\hvec_k$, plus $O(PL)$ for the diagonal
scan, so S5 evaluates the recurrence directly. When $P = O(H)$ its cost matches S4's up to the factor $\log L$
<Cite id="smith2023s5" />.

### The scan

Because $\bar\Lambdamat$ is diagonal, each state is a scalar recurrence $h_{k,j} = \bar\lambda_j h_{k-1,j} + b_{k,j}$
with $b_{k,j} = (\bar\Bmat\uvec_k)_j$. Step $k$ is the pair $(\bar\lambda_j, b_{k,j})$, and two consecutive steps
combine as $(a, b) \bullet (a', b') = (a'a,\ a'b + b')$. The operator is associative, so all $L$ states follow from a
scan in $O(\log L)$ parallel rounds with $O(PL)$ total work <Cite id="martin2018" />. Nothing in the combination
requires the pairs to be equal across steps. That is what later lets <NoteLink to="mamba">Mamba</NoteLink> make them
depend on the input.

### Discretisation and initialisation

S5 parameterises the continuous diagonal matrix $\Lambdamat$, a step $\Delta_j$ per state, and discretises by the
zero-order hold, $\bar\lambda_j = e^{\lambda_j\Delta_j}$ and $\bar\Bmat = \Lambdamat^{-1}(\bar\Lambdamat - \eye)\Bmat$
<Cite id="smith2023s5" />. It initialises $\Lambdamat$ with the eigenvalues of **HiPPO-N**, the normal part of the
<NoteLink to="hippo">HiPPO-LegS</NoteLink> matrix, which equals $-\tfrac12\eye$ plus a skew-symmetric matrix and so has
eigenvalues $-\tfrac12 + i\omega$ and a well-conditioned eigenbasis. The steps are drawn log-uniformly from
$[0.001, 0.1]$. Conjugate eigenvalues come in pairs, so only half are stored and the output takes twice the real part
<Cite id="smith2023s5" />.

A step per state has a second use. If observations arrive at irregular times, step $k$ can use the actual gap
$\Delta t_k$, giving a different $\bar\Lambdamat_k$ at every position. The convolutional mode cannot represent this; the
scan handles it unchanged <Cite id="smith2023s5" />.

## The linear recurrent unit

### A stable parameterisation

A real matrix generally has complex eigenvalues. The rotation $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, for
example, has eigenvalues $\pm i$. To keep the expressiveness of a dense recurrence in diagonal form, the LRU uses a
complex diagonal $\Lambdamat$ <Cite id="orvieto2023" />. Each eigenvalue is written as

$$
\lambda_j = \exp\paren{-e^{\nu_j} + i\,e^{\theta_j}}, \qquad \nu_j, \theta_j \in \reals.
$$

Its modulus is $\abs{\lambda_j} = \exp(-e^{\nu_j})$. Because $e^{\nu_j} > 0$, the modulus lies in $(0, 1)$ for every
value of $\nu_j$, so gradient steps can never make the recurrence unstable. Its phase is $e^{\theta_j}$. The exponential
on the phase makes small phases easy to represent.

### Initialisation on a ring

The LRU draws eigenvalues uniformly, by area, on the ring $r_{\min} \le \abs\lambda \le r_{\max}$ with phases in
$[0, \theta_{\max}]$. With $u_1, u_2$ uniform on $[0, 1]$,

$$
\nu = -\tfrac12\log\paren{u_1(r_{\max}^2 - r_{\min}^2) + r_{\min}^2}, \qquad \theta = \theta_{\max}u_2,
\qquad \lambda = e^{-\nu + i\theta}.
$$

The modulus squared $e^{-2\nu}$ is then uniform on $[r_{\min}^2, r_{\max}^2]$, which is what uniform by area means for a
ring <Cite id="orvieto2023" />. The defaults depend on the task. <NoteLink to="image-classification">Image
classification</NoteLink> from pixel sequences used $[0.9, 0.999]$ with the full phase range $2\pi$.
<NoteLink to="long-range-and-recall-benchmarks">Path-X</NoteLink>, with sequences of 16,384 steps, used
$[0.999, 0.9999]$ and phases only up to $\pi/10$ <Cite id="orvieto2023" />.

### Normalisation

Eigenvalues near 1 make the state large. Feed the scalar recurrence $h_k = \lambda h_{k-1} + b_k$ with independent
zero-mean inputs of variance $\sigma^2$. Unrolled, $h_k = \sum_{m \ge 0} \lambda^m b_{k-m}$, and the terms are
uncorrelated, so

$$
\expect\abs{h_k}^2 = \sigma^2\sum_{m \ge 0}\abs\lambda^{2m} = \frac{\sigma^2}{1 - \abs\lambda^2}.
$$

At $\abs\lambda = 0.999$ the variance is 500 times the input's. The LRU multiplies the input of state $j$ by
$\gamma_j = \sqrt{1 - \abs{\lambda_j}^2}$, which cancels the factor exactly at initialisation:

$$
\hvec_k = \Lambdamat\hvec_{k-1} + \gammavec \odot (\Bmat\uvec_k), \qquad \yvec_k = \operatorname{Re}(\Cmat\hvec_k) + \Dmat\uvec_k.
$$

The vector $\gammavec$ is stored as $\log\gamma_j$ and trained. Fixing $\gamma_j$ to $\sqrt{1 - \abs{\lambda_j}^2}$ at
every step works about as well <Cite id="orvieto2023" />.

### What the LRU shows

Removing the recurrent nonlinearity improved accuracy rather than hurting it, and diagonal linear recurrences trained
about 8 times faster than a dense <Gloss name="relu" /> recurrence on sequential <Gloss name="cifar-10" />
<Cite id="orvieto2023" />. With all four changes the LRU reached 94.2% on Path-X, against 96.4% reported for S4 and
98.6% for S5 in the same comparison. The conclusion is that a deep stack of linear recurrences with nonlinear layers
between them is enough, and what S4 needed from its continuous-time theory was eigenvalues near the unit circle and a
controlled forward pass. The LRU is the base of the gated recurrence of Griffin, described in
<NoteLink to="gated-linear-recurrence" />.

## Worked example

Take an LRU with two states and one input channel, $\Bmat = (1, 1)^\tr$, with
$\lambda_1 = 0.9\,e^{i\pi/4}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.99\,e^{i\pi/20}$.

**Normalisation.** $\gamma_1 = \sqrt{1 - 0.81} = 0.4359$ and $\gamma_2 = \sqrt{1 - 0.9801} = 0.1411$. Without them the
white-noise gains $1/(1 - \abs\lambda^2)$ would be $5.26$ and $50.25$; with them both are 1.

**Impulse response of state 1.** Feed $u = (1, 0, 0, \dots)$. Then $h_{0,1} = \gamma_1 = 0.4359$ and each step
multiplies by $\lambda_1 = 0.6364 + 0.6364i$:

| $k$ | $h_{k,1}$ | $\operatorname{Re} h_{k,1}$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | $0.4359$ | $0.4359$ |
| 1 | $0.2774 + 0.2774i$ | $0.2774$ |
| 2 | $0.3531i$ | $0$ |
| 3 | $-0.2247 + 0.2247i$ | $-0.2247$ |
| 4 | $-0.2860$ | $-0.2860$ |
| 5 | $-0.1820 - 0.1820i$ | $-0.1820$ |

The modulus falls by $0.9$ per step and the phase turns by $45°$, so the real part oscillates with period 8 and decays
with half-life 6.6 steps.

**State 2.** $h_{0,2} = 0.1411$, $h_{1,2} = 0.1411 \times 0.99\,e^{i\pi/20} = 0.1380 + 0.0219i$, and after ten steps
$h_{10,2} = 0.1411 \times 0.99^{10}\,e^{i\pi/2} = 0.1276i$. The state turns by $9°$ per step and halves every 69 steps.

**Scan.** Two steps of state 1 combine as $(\lambda_1, b_1) \bullet (\lambda_1, b_2) = (\lambda_1^2,\ \lambda_1 b_1 +
b_2)$. With $b_1 = 0.4359$ and $b_2 = 0$, the second component is $\lambda_1 \times 0.4359 = 0.2774 + 0.2774i$, the
same $h_{1,1}$ as the sequential step.

With $r_{\max} = 0.99$ the output is still ringing at the end of the 256-step window. Dragging both radii down to 0.9 or

rounding error for every setting.

## Assumptions

- **Time invariance, in the LRU and default S5.** The transition does not depend on the input. Selection by content
  needs input-dependent coefficients, as in <NoteLink to="mamba" /> and
  <NoteLink to="gated-linear-recurrence">gated linear recurrences</NoteLink>.
- **Nonlinearity between layers.** The recurrence is linear; position-wise <Gloss name="mlp">multilayer
  perceptrons</Gloss> or gated linear units between recurrent layers supply the nonlinearity <Cite id="orvieto2023" />.
- **Complex arithmetic.** The state is complex and the output is a real part. Implementations store real and imaginary
  parts, or pairs of conjugate eigenvalues.

## Pitfalls

- **Radii too far from 1.** With $r_{\max} = 0.9$ nothing older than about 30 steps survives. Long-range tasks need
  radii within $10^{-3}$ of 1 or closer.
- **Unconstrained eigenvalues.** Learning $\lambda$ directly, or the real part of $\log\lambda$ without the inner
  exponential, lets an update push $\abs\lambda$ above 1 and the recurrence diverges on long sequences.
- **Forgetting the normalisation.** Without $\gamma$, states with radii near 1 dominate the output at initialisation and
  their gradients are large; training becomes unstable.
- **Factor 2 in conjugate pairs.** Storing one eigenvalue of each conjugate pair requires the output to take twice the
  real part. Mixing conventions between the scan and a convolution check gives outputs that differ by a factor of 2.
