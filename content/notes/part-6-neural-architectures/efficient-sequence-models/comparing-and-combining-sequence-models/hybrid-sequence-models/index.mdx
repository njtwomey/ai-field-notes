<Definition>
A **hybrid sequence model** is a deep network whose token-mixing layers are of two kinds: self-attention layers, which
compare every token with every earlier token, and layers with a fixed-size state, such as
<NoteLink to="mamba">Mamba</NoteLink>, a <NoteLink to="gated-linear-recurrence">gated linear recurrence</NoteLink> or
<NoteLink to="linear-attention">linear attention</NoteLink>. The two kinds are combined in a fixed pattern, most often
by interleaving whole layers in a fixed ratio. Each token mixer is followed by a feed-forward or
<NoteLink to="mixture-of-experts">mixture-of-experts</NoteLink> sublayer, as in a
<NoteLink to="transformer">transformer</NoteLink> <Cite id="lieber2024" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Attention and recurrent layers fail in opposite ways. Attention keeps every earlier token in its
<NoteLink to="key-value-cache">key-value (KV) cache</NoteLink>, so it can retrieve any of them exactly, but the cache
and the cost grow with the context. A state-space layer compresses the whole past into a fixed-size state, so each token
costs the same whatever the context, but details that were not kept in the state are gone. The
<NoteLink to="long-range-and-recall-benchmarks">recall benchmarks</NoteLink> measure the second failure: a fixed-size
state answers associative-recall queries well only while the number of stored pairs is small against the state.

Most token mixing in a <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss> is local or summarising, which a recurrent
layer does well. A minority of operations need exact lookup: copying a name from far back, or matching the pattern of a
few-shot prompt. A few attention layers are enough to supply these, and the rest of the network can be cheaper. The
design question is then how few attention layers suffice, where they go, and whether they need to see the whole
context.

## Details

### Three ways to combine the layers

- **Interleaved layers.** Whole layers alternate in a repeating pattern: Jamba, Griffin, Samba, Nemotron-H, MiniMax-01,
  Qwen3-Next and Kimi Linear. This is the common design. Each layer runs one kind of kernel, and the attention layers
  are spread through the depth.
- **Parallel heads.** Each layer runs <NoteLink to="multi-head-attention">attention heads</NoteLink> and state-space
  heads side by side on the same input and averages their normalised outputs: Hymba <Cite id="dong2024hymba" />. Every
  layer then has both exact recall and a summary.
- **A shared attention block.** One attention block, with one set of weights, is called at several depths of a Mamba
  stack: Zamba <Cite id="glorioso2024zamba" />. The calls share parameters but each keeps its own KV cache.

### Jamba

Jamba groups layers into blocks of 8, with one attention layer and seven Mamba layers per block, a ratio of 1:7
<Cite id="lieber2024" />. Every second layer replaces its <Gloss name="mlp" /> with a
<NoteLink to="mixture-of-experts">mixture of experts</NoteLink> of 16 experts, of which the top 2 are used per token.
The released model has 4 blocks (32 layers), 52B parameters, 12B of them active per token, supports a context of 256K
tokens and fits on one 80 GB <Gloss name="gpu" />.

**KV cache.** Only attention layers keep a KV cache. With one attention layer in eight, Jamba's cache is one eighth of
that of a transformer with the same number of layers and heads. At 256K tokens in 16-bit precision, Jamba's cache takes
4 GB, against 32 GB for Mixtral 8x7B and 128 GB for Llama-2 70B <Cite id="lieber2024" />.

**Throughput.** At a context of 128K tokens Jamba processes 3 times as many tokens per second as Mixtral 8x7B
<Cite id="lieber2024" />. The Mamba layers do the bulk of the mixing at linear cost.

**Positions.** Jamba's attention layers use no explicit <NoteLink to="positional-encoding">positional
encoding</NoteLink>. The Mamba layers see tokens in order, so they supply position information to the layers above them.
Results with and without rotary embeddings were similar <Cite id="lieber2024" />.

### What the attention layers provide

Lieber et al. compared pure attention, pure Mamba and hybrid models of the same size. At 7B parameters the hybrid
reached a lower training loss than either pure model. The pure Mamba model did worse on tasks that depend on
<Gloss name="icl">in-context learning</Gloss>, such as following the output format shown in a few-shot prompt: on IMDB
sentiment classification it often answered "Very Good" instead of the required "Positive" or "Negative", and scored 48.8
against 84.1 for attention and 90.9 for the hybrid. The authors attribute the gap to
<NoteLink to="induction-heads-and-circuits">induction heads</NoteLink>, attention patterns that find an earlier
occurrence of the current token and copy what followed it, and show one in the hybrid's attention layers
<Cite id="lieber2024" />.

A controlled study at 8B parameters reached the same conclusion <Cite id="waleffe2024" />. Pure Mamba and
<NoteLink to="mamba-2-and-state-space-duality">Mamba-2</NoteLink> models matched a transformer trained on the same data,
up to 3.5T tokens, on many tasks but fell behind on five-shot <Gloss name="mmlu" />, on phone-book lookup and on
long-context reasoning. A hybrid of about 43% Mamba-2 layers, 7% attention layers and 50% MLP layers beat the
transformer on all 12 standard tasks evaluated, by 2.65 points on average, and was predicted to generate up to 8 times
faster.

### Published layer patterns

- **Griffin and Hawk.** Hawk stacks <NoteLink to="residual-network">residual blocks</NoteLink> whose token mixer is the
  RG-LRU of the <NoteLink to="gated-linear-recurrence">gated linear recurrence</NoteLink> note. Griffin repeats two
  RG-LRU blocks and one block of local <Gloss name="mqa">multi-query attention</Gloss> with a window of 1,024 tokens
  <Cite id="de2024griffin" />. The attention is local, so the whole model's memory is bounded. On a phone-book lookup
  task Griffin retrieved perfectly up to the length of its window and Hawk failed as the book grew. Griffin matched
  Llama-2 with over 6 times fewer training tokens.
- **Zamba.** A 7B model with a Mamba backbone and one shared block of attention and MLP, called every 6 Mamba blocks (13
  calls in 76 layers). The input embeddings are concatenated to the <NoteLink to="residual-connections">residual
  stream</NoteLink> at every call, and each call has its own linear map back into the stream
  <Cite id="glorioso2024zamba" />. Sharing the weights cuts parameters; the KV cache still grows with every call.
- **Samba.** The unit is Mamba, MLP, <NoteLink to="sliding-window-and-sparse-attention">sliding-window
  attention</NoteLink>, MLP, so half the token mixers are attention, each with a window of 2,048 tokens. The 3.8B model
  has 64 layers. Trained on sequences of 4K tokens, Samba retrieved passkeys perfectly up to 256K tokens after brief
  fine-tuning <Cite id="ren2025samba" />. No layer attends beyond 2,048 tokens: the Mamba layers carry information
  across windows.
- **Hymba.** Every one of its 32 layers runs attention heads and SSM heads in parallel. Only layers 1, 16 and 32 use
  global attention; the rest use sliding windows. Adjacent layers share one KV cache, and 128 learned meta tokens are
  prepended to every input. The 1.5B model reports a cache 11.67 times smaller and 3.49 times the throughput of
  Llama-3.2-3B with higher average accuracy <Cite id="dong2024hymba" />.
- **Nemotron-H.** About 8% of the layers are attention, spread evenly; the rest split equally between Mamba-2 and FFN
  layers. The 8B model has 52 layers: 24 Mamba-2, 4 attention and 24 FFN; the 56B model has 118 layers with 10
  attention layers. The first layer is Mamba-2, and every attention layer is followed by an FFN layer. The models
  infer up to 3 times faster than transformers of similar size and accuracy <Cite id="nvidia2025nemotronh" />.

### Linear and full attention mixes

The recurrent layers of a hybrid can be linear-attention layers instead of state-space layers, which makes the model a
mix of two kinds of attention.

- **MiniMax-01** places one softmax-attention layer after every 7 lightning-attention layers, an I/O-aware form of
  linear attention, for 80 layers in total. The model has 456B parameters, 45.9B active, and trains on contexts of up to
  1M tokens <Cite id="minimax2025" />.
- **Qwen3-Next** repeats 3 <NoteLink to="delta-rule-and-test-time-training">Gated DeltaNet</NoteLink> layers and 1
  gated attention layer 12 times, for 48 layers, each followed by a mixture of experts <Cite id="qwen2025next" />.
- **Kimi Linear** repeats 3 layers of Kimi Delta Attention, a gated delta rule, and 1 layer of
  <NoteLink to="multi-head-latent-attention">multi-head latent attention</NoteLink>. The latent-attention layers use
  <Gloss name="nope">no positional encoding</Gloss>, which leaves position to the recurrent layers as in Jamba. Against
  full latent attention trained the same way, it reports up to 75% less KV cache and up to 6 times the decoding
  throughput at a 1M-token context <Cite id="kimi2025linear" />.

The same idea appears inside transformers as a mix of sliding-window and global attention. Gemma 2 alternates the two
1:1 with a window of 4,096 tokens; Gemma 3 uses 5 local layers per global layer with a window of 1,024 tokens, to keep
the KV cache small at long contexts <Cite id="gemmateam2025" />.

### Design choices

- **Ratio.** Fewer attention layers give a smaller cache and higher throughput; too few lose recall. Jamba found 1:3
  and 1:7 similar in quality and chose 1:7 for efficiency <Cite id="lieber2024" />. Nemotron-H and the 8B study settle
  near 7 to 8% attention layers; the delta-rule hybrids use 1 in 4.
- **Placement.** Attention layers are spread through the depth, not grouped, so that every stage of processing can
  reach any earlier token. Nemotron-H keeps the first layer recurrent; Hymba puts its global layers at the first, middle
  and last positions.
- **Global or windowed attention.** Windowed attention bounds the cache, at the cost of exact recall beyond the window:
  Griffin's phone-book accuracy fell at its window length. A few global layers among windowed ones keep long-range
  lookup at a fraction of the cost.
- **Kind of recurrent layer.** Mamba, Mamba-2, RG-LRU, lightning attention and gated delta rules have all been used.
  The 2025 models with a 1:3 ratio use gated delta rules, which edit one association of the state at a time instead of
  only decaying it. Mamba-2's duality with masked attention makes the two kinds of layer share much of their
  implementation <Cite id="dao2024" />.
- **Positions.** With recurrent layers below them, attention layers can drop positional encodings (Jamba, Kimi Linear).

## Worked example

Take a decoder of 32 token-mixing layers with 8 key-value heads of width 128, at a context of $L = 131{,}072$ tokens
(128K) in 16-bit precision. Each attention layer stores a key and a value per head per token:

$$
2 \times 8 \times 128 \times 2\ \text{bytes} = 4{,}096\ \text{bytes per token per layer}.
$$

1. **All attention.** $32 \times 131{,}072 \times 4{,}096$ bytes $= 2^{34}$ bytes $= 16$ GiB.
2. **Jamba's ratio, 1:7.** 4 of the 32 layers are attention: $4/32$ of the cache, 2 GiB.
3. **Qwen3-Next's ratio, 1:3.** 8 attention layers: 4 GiB.
4. **Samba's pattern.** 16 layers of sliding-window attention with a window of 2,048 tokens each keep at most 2,048
   tokens: $16 \times 2{,}048 \times 4{,}096$ bytes $= 128$ MiB, whatever the context beyond 2,048.
5. **Hymba's pattern.** 3 global layers and 29 windowed layers with a window of 1,024 tokens:
   $(3 \times 131{,}072 + 29 \times 1{,}024) \times 4{,}096$ bytes $\approx 1.61$ GiB, about 10% of step 1 before Hymba
   shares caches between adjacent layers.

The recurrent layers add a state whose size does not depend on $L$. A Mamba layer of inner width $4{,}096 \times 2$ with
state size 16 holds $8{,}192 \times 16 = 131{,}072$ numbers, 256 KiB in 16-bit precision: per layer, the cache of 64
tokens of one attention layer. Steps 2 and 3 scale with $L$ like a transformer, at a smaller slope; step 4 stops growing
at the window, which is why window hybrids are the cheapest at very long contexts and also the ones that cannot look a
token up exactly beyond the window.

## Assumptions

- **Most mixing is compressible.** The design assumes that a few exact-recall layers suffice and the rest of the
  network can work from a compressed state.
- **Memory is the bottleneck.** The savings matter when the KV cache or long-context attention limits
  <NoteLink to="minibatching-and-batch-size">batch size</NoteLink> or context length. At short contexts a hybrid runs at
  about the speed of a transformer.
- **Ratios transfer.** Published ratios were tuned at one scale and data mix; the 8B study and Jamba's ablations are the
  main evidence that one attention layer per 6 or 7 recurrent layers keeps quality.

## Pitfalls

- **Cache still grows.** Global attention layers keep a cache that is smaller but still linear in context length. At
  very long contexts it dominates again.
- **Windows hide recall failures.** A model whose attention is all windowed passes short-context benchmarks and
  passkey tests, which a recurrent state can carry, and fails exact lookup of many facts beyond the window.
- **Two kinds of kernels.** Training and serving need efficient implementations of both layer types, and the two have
  different parallelisation and caching schemes. Prefix caching and <NoteLink to="speculative-decoding">speculative
  decoding</NoteLink> must save and restore recurrent states as well as KV caches.
- **Comparisons at unequal budgets.** Hybrids are often compared with transformers trained on fewer tokens or with a
  different data mix; the controlled 8B study is the cleaner comparison <Cite id="waleffe2024" />.
