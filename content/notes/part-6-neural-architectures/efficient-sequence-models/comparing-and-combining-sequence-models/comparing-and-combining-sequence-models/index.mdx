<Definition>
This topic compares the token-mixing layers of <NoteLink to="sequence-models">sequence models</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="scaled-dot-product-attention">softmax attention</NoteLink> against layers with a fixed-size state
(state-space layers, gated linear recurrences, linear attention and delta rules), and covers the models that combine
them. It asks three questions: what each kind of layer can retrieve and compute, how the kinds are mixed in one network,
and how state-space layers carry over from text to other data.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The efficient sequence layers of the neighbouring topics, <NoteLink to="structured-and-selective-state-space-models" />
and <NoteLink to="linear-recurrent-models" />, generate each token at a constant cost and train in parallel. They
replace the growing <NoteLink to="key-value-cache">key-value cache</NoteLink> of attention by a state of fixed size. The
notes here say what that replacement loses, in tasks a model designer can test, and how current
<Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss> recover it with a few attention layers. They also cover the use
of state-space layers on long non-text inputs, where their linear cost matters most.

## Main ideas

**Two kinds of long-context ability.** Carrying a signal across thousands of steps and holding many facts at once are
different demands. <NoteLink to="long-range-and-recall-benchmarks" /> separates them: <Gloss name="s4" /> solved the
16,384-step Path-X task of Long Range Arena, while fixed-state layers fall behind attention on multi-query associative
recall as the number of stored pairs grows against the state. On the Pile, recall of earlier n-grams accounted for 82%
of the perplexity gap between attention and gated-convolution models <Cite id="arora2024zoology" />. A counting argument
sets the limit: a state of $s$ numbers cannot store more pairs than its bits allow, and a $d \times d$ linear memory
holds at most $d$ exact associations.

**What a fixed-size state cannot compute.** <NoteLink to="expressiveness-of-linear-recurrences" /> treats state
tracking: parity, counting mod $m$ and composing permutations. Diagonal transitions with entries in $[0, 1]$, as in
Mamba and gated linear attention, cannot compute parity at every length with finite precision, and stacks of them, like
transformers, lie in $\mathrm{TC}^0$ and cannot compose permutations of five objects at fixed depth unless
$\mathrm{TC}^0 = \mathrm{NC}^1$ <Cite id="merrill2024illusion" />. Negative eigenvalues give parity; input-dependent
non-diagonal transitions, as in DeltaNet with a widened step, DeltaProduct and
<NoteLink to="rwkv-and-retentive-networks">RWKV-7</NoteLink>, give permutations and regular languages while keeping a
parallel scan.

**Hybrids.** <NoteLink to="hybrid-sequence-models" /> interleave a few attention layers among recurrent ones. Jamba and
MiniMax-01 use one attention layer per 7 others, Nemotron-H about 8% attention layers, Qwen3-Next and Kimi Linear one
per 3 delta-rule layers, Samba alternates Mamba with <NoteLink to="sliding-window-and-sparse-attention">sliding-window
attention</NoteLink>, Hymba runs attention and SSM heads in parallel in every layer, and Zamba calls one shared
attention block every 6 Mamba blocks. A controlled 8B study found a hybrid with 7% attention layers better than a
transformer on all 12 tasks tested <Cite id="waleffe2024" />.

**Beyond text.** <NoteLink to="state-space-models-beyond-text" /> covers images, audio, DNA and
<NoteLink to="time-series">time series</NoteLink>. Images need a scan order and bidirectional scans (Vision Mamba,
VMamba's four-route scan); DNA needs both directions and reverse-complement symmetry (Caduceus); raw audio needs stable
recurrences for generation (SaShiMi).
