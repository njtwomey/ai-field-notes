<Definition>
A **linear-recurrent transformer** keeps the transformer's block structure but replaces softmax self-attention with a
token mixer that is a linear recurrence with <NoteLink to="first-order-linear-odes">exponential decay</NoteLink>. The
**retentive network** (RetNet) uses $\Smat_n = \gamma\Smat_{n-1} + \kvec_n^\tr\vvec_n$ and outputs $\qvec_n\Smat_n$,
with a decay $0 < \gamma < 1$ per head <Cite id="sun2023retnet" />. **RWKV** uses a normalised, per-channel decaying
average of values weighted by $e^{k}$ <Cite id="peng2023rwkv" />. Both train in parallel like a transformer and generate
like a recurrent network.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Causal <NoteLink to="linear-attention">linear attention</NoteLink> is already a recurrent network: its state adds one
<NoteLink to="matrix-multiplication">outer product</NoteLink> $\kvec_n^\tr\vvec_n$ per token and never forgets. Every
past token keeps full weight, so the state fills with old associations. Multiplying the state by a factor $\gamma < 1$
before each addition makes old tokens fade: a token $n - m$ steps back has weight $\gamma^{n-m}$. The model then has a
soft recency bias, much like the decaying kernel of a <NoteLink to="structured-state-space-models">state-space
layer</NoteLink>, while keeping linear attention's query–key matching.

## Retention

### Recurrent and parallel forms

Let $\qvec_n, \kvec_n \in \reals^{1 \times d}$ and $\vvec_n \in \reals^{1 \times d_v}$ be row vectors. The recurrent
form of retention is

$$
\Smat_n = \gamma\Smat_{n-1} + \kvec_n^\tr\vvec_n, \qquad \ovec_n = \qvec_n\Smat_n, \qquad \Smat_0 = \zeros.
$$

Unrolling, $\Smat_n = \sum_{m \le n} \gamma^{n-m}\kvec_m^\tr\vvec_m$, so

$$
\ovec_n = \sum_{m \le n} \gamma^{n-m}\,(\qvec_n\kvec_m^\tr)\,\vvec_m.
$$

Stacking the rows gives the parallel form

$$
\Omat = \paren{\Qmat\Kmat^\tr \circ \Dmat}\Vmat, \qquad
D_{nm} = \begin{cases} \gamma^{n-m} & n \ge m, \\ 0 & n < m, \end{cases}
$$

where $\circ$ is the elementwise product <Cite id="sun2023retnet" />. $\Dmat$ combines the
<NoteLink to="causal-masking">causal mask</NoteLink> with the decay. The parallel form is used for training: it is two
matrix products, like attention without the softmax. The recurrent form is used for generation: the state has
$d \times d_v$ entries per head, and each token costs $O(dd_v)$.

RetNet also multiplies $\qvec_n$ and $\kvec_m$ by rotations $e^{in\theta}$ and $e^{-im\theta}$, so the score depends on
the offset $n - m$, the idea behind <NoteLink to="rotary-position-embedding">rotary position embedding</NoteLink>. The
rotations fold into $\qvec$ and $\kvec$ and leave the recurrent and parallel forms, and the chunkwise form below,
unchanged.

### Chunkwise form

For long sequences the parallel form's $n \times n$ matrix is too large, and the recurrent form is slow on a
<Gloss name="gpu" />. The chunkwise form splits the sequence into chunks of $B$ tokens. Let position $n = (i-1)B + j$ be
the $j$-th token of chunk $i$, and let $\Rmat_{i-1}$ be the state after the last token of chunk $i - 1$. Split the sum
for $\ovec_n$ into tokens inside chunk $i$ and tokens before it. Every token before the chunk reaches position $n$
through $\Rmat_{i-1}$, decayed by $j$ further steps, so

$$
\ovec_n = \underbrace{\sum_{m\ \text{in chunk}\ i,\ m \le n} \gamma^{n-m}(\qvec_n\kvec_m^\tr)\vvec_m}_{\text{parallel form within the chunk}}
+ \underbrace{\gamma^{j}\,\qvec_n\Rmat_{i-1}}_{\text{recurrent across chunks}}.
$$

The state carried forward is
$\Rmat_i = \gamma^B\Rmat_{i-1} + \sum_{j=1}^{B} \gamma^{B-j}\kvec_{(i-1)B+j}^\tr\vvec_{(i-1)B+j}$, the recurrence
applied $B$ times at once <Cite id="sun2023retnet" />. The within-chunk part costs $O(B^2 d)$ per chunk and the
cross-chunk part $O(Bdd_v)$. For $n/B$ chunks the total is $O(nBd + ndd_v)$: linear in $n$, and made of matrix products.

### Multi-scale retention

Each head has its own decay, $\gamma = 1 - 2^{-5-h}$ for heads $h = 0, 1, \dots$ <Cite id="sun2023retnet" />. A decay
$\gamma$ gives a memory of about $1/(1 - \gamma)$ tokens, so eight heads span 32 to 4096 tokens. Because retention has
no softmax, head outputs differ in scale; RetNet normalises each head with
<NoteLink to="normalisation-in-convolutional-networks">GroupNorm</NoteLink> and multiplies the result by a swish gate
$\operatorname{swish}(\Xmat\Wmat_G)$ before the output projection.

A RetNet of 6.7B parameters decodes 8.4 times faster than a transformer with a <Gloss name="kv-cache" /> at sequence
length 8K and uses 70% less memory. Its throughput and latency do not depend on sequence length
<Cite id="sun2023retnet" />.

### Worked example

Take one head with scalar $q_n = k_n = 1$, $\gamma = 0.5$ and values $v = (1, 2, 3, 4)$. The parallel form gives
$o_n = \sum_{m \le n} 0.5^{n-m}v_m = (1,\ 2.5,\ 4.25,\ 6.125)$.

In chunks of $B = 2$: chunk 1 gives $o_1 = 1$ and $o_2 = 0.5 + 2 = 2.5$, and carries $R_1 = 0.5 \times 1 + 2 = 2.5$.
In chunk 2, the within-chunk parts are $3$ and $0.5 \times 3 + 4 = 5.5$, and the cross-chunk parts are
$0.5^1 \times 2.5 = 1.25$ and $0.5^2 \times 2.5 = 0.625$. The outputs $o_3 = 4.25$ and $o_4 = 6.125$ match.

## RWKV

RWKV names its four vectors receptance $\rvec$, weight $\wvec$, key $\kvec$ and value $\vvec$
<Cite id="peng2023rwkv" />. Each block has a time-mixing sublayer, which plays the role of attention, and a
channel-mixing sublayer, which plays the role of the feed-forward layer.

**Token shift.** Every projection reads a per-channel interpolation of the current and previous inputs,
$\kvec_t = \Wmat_k(\muvec_k \circ \xvec_t + (1 - \muvec_k) \circ \xvec_{t-1})$, and likewise for $\rvec_t$ and
$\vvec_t$. This gives each layer a direct view of the previous token.

**The WKV operator.** For each channel separately, with a learned decay $w \ge 0$ and a bonus $u$ for the current
token,

$$
\operatorname{wkv}_t = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{t-1} e^{-(t-1-i)w + k_i}v_i + e^{u + k_t}v_t}{\sum_{i=1}^{t-1} e^{-(t-1-i)w + k_i} + e^{u + k_t}}.
$$

It is a weighted average of past values. The weight of token $i$ is $e^{k_i}$, a content-dependent score, times a
decay $e^{-(t-1-i)w}$ that falls with distance. The output of time mixing is $\Wmat_o(\sigma(\rvec_t) \circ
\operatorname{wkv}_t)$: the receptance gates how much of the average each channel lets through.

**Recurrent form.** Let $a_{t}$ and $b_{t}$ be the numerator and denominator sums over tokens up to $t$, decayed to
position $t + 1$. Each gains one term and decays by $e^{-w}$ per step:

$$
a_t = e^{-w}a_{t-1} + e^{k_t}v_t, \qquad b_t = e^{-w}b_{t-1} + e^{k_t}, \qquad
\operatorname{wkv}_t = \frac{a_{t-1} + e^{u + k_t}v_t}{b_{t-1} + e^{u + k_t}}.
$$

The recurrence for $a_t$ follows from
$\sum_{i \le t} e^{-(t-i)w + k_i}v_i = e^{-w}\sum_{i \le t-1} e^{-(t-1-i)w + k_i}v_i + e^{k_t}v_t$. Generation therefore
needs two numbers per channel per layer and $O(d)$ time per token <Cite id="peng2023rwkv" />. Implementations subtract a
running maximum of the exponents, as in the online softmax, to avoid overflow.

With one channel, $w = \ln 2$ (decay $0.5$ per step), $u = 0$, all $k_t = 0$ and $v = (1, 2, 3, 4)$, the recurrence
gives $\operatorname{wkv} = (1,\ 1.5,\ 2.2,\ 3.0)$. For example, at $t = 3$ the numerator is
$0.5 \times 1 + 1 \times 2 + 3 = 5.5$ and the denominator $0.5 + 1 + 1 = 2.5$. RetNet's unnormalised sum on the same
values was $(1, 2.5, 4.25, 6.125)$; RWKV's normalisation keeps the output on the scale of the values.

**Channel mixing.** A gated feed-forward layer with token shift, a squared <Gloss name="relu" /> and a sigmoid
receptance gate.

RWKV models from 169M to 14B parameters were trained on the Pile, with language-modelling performance close to
transformers of the same size <Cite id="peng2023rwkv" />.

## RWKV-5, RWKV-6 and RWKV-7

Later RWKV versions keep the time-mixing and channel-mixing sublayers and change the state. Each step moves the layer
closer to <NoteLink to="gated-linear-recurrence">gated linear attention</NoteLink> and then to the
<NoteLink to="delta-rule-and-test-time-training">delta rule</NoteLink>.

**Eagle (RWKV-5): a matrix state.** The two numbers per channel become a $d \times d$ matrix per head, written by outer
products as in retention, with a decay per key channel $\wvec = \exp(-\exp(\omegavec)) \in (0, 1)^d$ and a bonus
$\uvec$ for the current token <Cite id="peng2024eagle" />:

$$
\Smat_t = \operatorname{diag}(\wvec)\,\Smat_{t-1} + \kvec_t^\tr\vvec_t, \qquad
\ovec_t = \rvec_t\paren{\Smat_{t-1} + \operatorname{diag}(\uvec)\,\kvec_t^\tr\vvec_t}.
$$

The receptance $\rvec_t$ plays the role of the query, and the normalisation by the decayed sum of weights is dropped:
each head's output is normalised by a <NoteLink to="layer-normalisation">LayerNorm</NoteLink> per head (a GroupNorm over
heads) and gated by $\operatorname{SiLU}(\gvec_t)$. The state now stores $d$ associations per head instead of a weighted
average.

**Finch (RWKV-6): a decay from the token.** The decay becomes $\wvec_t = \exp(-\exp(\dvec_t))$, where $\dvec_t$ is
computed from an interpolation of $\xvec_t$ and $\xvec_{t-1}$ through a low-rank map <Cite id="peng2024eagle" />. The
token shift itself becomes data-dependent in the same way. The state is then

$$
\Smat_t = \operatorname{diag}(\wvec_t)\,\Smat_{t-1} + \kvec_t^\tr\vvec_t,
$$

and the weight of key $m$ at query $n$ is the product $\wvec_{m+1} \odot \dots \odot \wvec_{n-1}$ instead of a power of
one decay. A token can now erase the memory, as in <NoteLink to="mamba">Mamba</NoteLink>. Eagle was released at 0.46B
to 7.5B parameters and Finch at 1.6B and 3.1B.

**The decay mask.** For a scalar decay per head the mask of the parallel form is a matrix $\Dmat$ with
$D_{nm} = a_{m+1}a_{m+2}\cdots a_n$ for $m \le n$ and 0 above the diagonal. A fixed decay gives $D_{nm} = \gamma^{n-m}$,
constant along each diagonal. A decay per token breaks that structure, but $\Dmat$ is still a product of per-step
factors: with $L_n = \sum_{j \le n}\log a_j$, $D_{nm} = e^{L_n - L_m}$. The parallel form
$(\Qmat\Kmat^\tr \circ \Dmat)\Vmat$ and the recurrence $\Smat_t = a_t\Smat_{t-1} + \kvec_t^\tr\vvec_t$ then give the
same outputs, as for retention. RWKV-6 decays each key channel separately, which gives one such mask per channel; the
chunked kernels keep the cumulative log decays for the same reason.

<DecayMaskForms />

**Goose (RWKV-7): a generalised delta rule.** RWKV-7 stores the transpose, values by keys, and updates it by

$$
\Smat_t = \Smat_{t-1}\paren{\operatorname{diag}(\wvec_t) - \hat\kappavec_t^\tr\,(\avec_t \odot \hat\kappavec_t)}
+ \vvec_t^\tr\tilde\kvec_t,
$$

with a decay per channel $\wvec_t$, an <Gloss name="icl">in-context learning</Gloss> rate $\avec_t \in (0, 1)^d$, a
unit-length removal key $\hat\kappavec_t$ and a replacement key $\tilde\kvec_t$, all computed from the token
<Cite id="peng2025rwkv7" />. With $\wvec_t = \ones$, a scalar rate $\avec_t = \beta_t\ones$ and
$\tilde\kvec_t = \beta_t\hat\kappavec_t$ the update is the delta rule: before writing $\vvec_t$ under a key, remove what
the state already returns for it. The transition is a diagonal matrix minus a rank-one matrix, so it is no longer
diagonal, and its eigenvalues stay in $[-1, 1]$. Peng et al. prove that this lets the layer track state that diagonal
recurrences and transformers cannot, including the composition of permutations of five elements and the recognition of
every regular language with a fixed number of layers. RWKV-7 models from 0.1B to 2.9B parameters were trained on up to
3.1 trillion tokens.

## Comparison

| Property                 | Softmax attention            | RetNet                               | RWKV (original)                     |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| State per layer and head | KV cache, grows with $n$     | matrix, $d \times d_v$               | two vectors, $2d$                   |
| Token interaction        | $\exp(\qvec^\tr\kvec)$       | $\qvec\kvec^\tr$ times $\gamma^{n-m}$ | $e^{k_i}$ times per-channel decay   |
| Normalisation            | softmax over keys            | GroupNorm on the output              | division by the decayed sum of weights |
| Generation cost per token | $O(nd)$                     | $O(dd_v)$                            | $O(d)$                              |
| Training                 | parallel, $O(n^2 d)$         | parallel or chunkwise                | parallel over time with a custom kernel |

## Assumptions

- **Fixed decay.** In RetNet, the original RWKV and RWKV-5 the decay does not depend on the input. A token is forgotten
  at the same rate whatever it contains. RWKV-6, RWKV-7 and <NoteLink to="mamba">Mamba</NoteLink> make the decay
  input-dependent.
- **Linearity in the state.** All these recurrences are linear in the state, which is what allows the parallel and
  chunkwise forms. Up to RWKV-6 the transition is diagonal; RWKV-7's diagonal-plus-rank-one transition needs the
  chunked algorithms of the delta rule.

## Pitfalls

- **Bounded memory.** The state has a fixed size, so exact recall of arbitrary earlier tokens degrades with context
  length, as with other <NoteLink to="recurrent-networks">recurrent models</NoteLink>.
- **Decay sets the horizon.** A head with $\gamma = 1 - 2^{-5}$ has an effective memory of about 32 tokens. Long-range
  dependencies depend on the slowest heads.
- **Numerical range.** The exponentials in the WKV sums overflow without the running-maximum trick. The powers
  $\gamma^{n-m}$ underflow for long chunks in low precision.
