<Definition>
**Triggering** restricts the analysis of an <Gloss name="ab-test" /> to the **triggered** users, those for whom the treatment could
have made a difference, such as users who reached the checkout page that was changed. The trigger condition is
evaluated in the same way in both arms: in control it records that the user would have seen the change, which is
called counterfactual logging. **Dilution** converts the effect measured on triggered users into the effect on all
users. If a share $q$ of users trigger and the treatment has no effect on the rest, the absolute effect per user in the
whole population is $q$ times the effect per triggered user <Cite id="kohavi2020" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Users who never reach the changed page contribute outcomes that the treatment cannot move. They add variance to
both arms and nothing to the difference. Removing them from both arms leaves the difference unchanged and shrinks
the noise. The removal is valid only when the rule that removes a user does not depend on the arm, so that the
triggered users of each arm remain comparable random samples of the same population.

## Details

Let a share $q$ of users trigger. Triggered users have metric mean $\mu_t$ and variance $\sigma_t^2$ in control, and
the treatment changes their mean by $\Delta_t$. Untriggered users have mean $\mu_u$ and variance $\sigma_u^2$, and the
treatment does not change them.

**The diluted effect.** Over all users, the control mean is $\mu = q\mu_t + (1 - q)\mu_u$ and the treatment mean is
$q(\mu_t + \Delta_t) + (1 - q)\mu_u$. Their difference is

$$
\Delta = q\,\Delta_t.
$$

As a relative lift,

$$
\frac{\Delta}{\mu} = \frac{\Delta_t}{\mu_t} \cdot \frac{q\mu_t}{\mu}.
$$

The relative lift on triggered users is multiplied by $q\mu_t/\mu$, the share of the metric's total that comes from
triggered users, not by the share of users.

**Why triggering needs fewer users.** Let $K = 2(z_{1-\alpha/2} + z_{1-\beta})^2$, where $z_q$ is the standard normal
$q$-quantile. By the formula in <NoteLink to="a-b-test-power">power for A/B tests</NoteLink>, the analysis of all users
needs $K\sigma^2/\Delta^2 = K\sigma^2/(q\Delta_t)^2$ users per arm, where $\sigma^2$ is the per-user variance over all
users. The triggered analysis needs $K\sigma_t^2/\Delta_t^2$ triggered users per arm. Only a share $q$ of enrolled users
trigger, so it needs $K\sigma_t^2/(q\Delta_t^2)$ enrolled users per arm. The ratio of the two requirements is

$$
R = \frac{K\sigma^2/(q\Delta_t)^2}{K\sigma_t^2/(q\Delta_t^2)} = \frac{\sigma^2}{q\,\sigma_t^2}.
$$

The variance of a mixture of two groups is the average within-group variance plus the variance of the group means,
by the <NoteLink to="law-of-total-expectation">law of total variance</NoteLink>:

$$
\sigma^2 = q\sigma_t^2 + (1 - q)\sigma_u^2 + q(1 - q)(\mu_t - \mu_u)^2.
$$

Dividing by $q\sigma_t^2$,

$$
R = 1 + \frac{(1 - q)\sigma_u^2 + q(1 - q)(\mu_t - \mu_u)^2}{q\,\sigma_t^2} \ge 1,
$$

because every term in the fraction is non-negative. The triggered analysis therefore never needs more users than the
analysis of all users, and it needs strictly fewer whenever $q < 1$ and the untriggered users vary or differ in mean
from the triggered ones.

<Derivation title="The variance decomposition">
Let $T$ indicate that a user triggers, with $P(T = 1) = q$. The second moment splits by group:
$\expect[Y^2] = q(\sigma_t^2 + \mu_t^2) + (1 - q)(\sigma_u^2 + \mu_u^2)$. The mean is $\mu = q\mu_t + (1 - q)\mu_u$.
Hence

$$
\sigma^2 = \expect[Y^2] - \mu^2 = q\sigma_t^2 + (1 - q)\sigma_u^2 + q\mu_t^2 + (1 - q)\mu_u^2 - \paren{q\mu_t + (1 - q)\mu_u}^2.
$$

Expanding the square, the last three terms are $q(1 - q)\mu_t^2 + q(1 - q)\mu_u^2 - 2q(1 - q)\mu_t\mu_u =
q(1 - q)(\mu_t - \mu_u)^2$.
</Derivation>

## Worked example

A change to the checkout page can affect only users who reach checkout: $q = 10\%$ of users. The metric is purchase
conversion. Users who reach checkout convert at $\mu_t = 20\%$ and the change is expected to raise that to 22%, so
$\Delta_t = 0.02$. Users who never reach checkout still buy through other routes at $\mu_u = 5\%$. Take $\alpha = 0.05$
and power 0.8, so $K = 15.698$.

1. **Triggered analysis.** $\sigma_t^2 = 0.2 \times 0.8 = 0.16$, so $15.698 \times 0.16 / 0.02^2 = 6{,}279$ triggered
   users per arm are needed. At $q = 0.1$ that means 62,791 enrolled users per arm.
2. **All users.** The overall conversion is $\mu = 0.1 \times 0.20 + 0.9 \times 0.05 = 0.065$, so
   $\sigma^2 = 0.065 \times 0.935 = 0.060775$. The diluted effect is $\Delta = 0.1 \times 0.02 = 0.002$. The requirement
   is $15.698 \times 0.060775 / 0.002^2 = 238{,}508$ users per arm.
3. **The ratio.** $R = 238{,}508 / 62{,}791 = 3.80$. The decomposition gives the same number: $\sigma^2 = 0.016 +
   0.04275 + 0.002025$, and $0.060775 / (0.1 \times 0.16) = 3.80$. Most of the excess comes from the untriggered users'
   own variance, $(1 - q)\sigma_u^2 = 0.04275$.
4. **Dilution.** The relative lift on triggered users is $0.02/0.20 = 10\%$. Triggered users produce
   $0.1 \times 0.20 / 0.065 = 30.8\%$ of all purchases, so the relative lift on all users is
   $10\% \times 0.308 = 3.08\%$, which equals $0.002/0.065$.

## Assumptions

- **The trigger does not depend on the arm.** The condition is evaluated identically in treatment and control, from
  information that the treatment cannot change. If treatment users trigger by seeing the new page and control users
  by some other rule, the two triggered groups differ before any effect.
- **Untriggered users are unaffected.** Dilution by $q$ assumes the treatment does nothing outside the triggered
  group. A change that alters which users reach checkout violates it.
- **Metrics start at the trigger.** Outcomes recorded before a user first triggers cannot be affected by the treatment
  and are usually excluded.

## Pitfalls

- **Treatment-dependent triggers.** A trigger such as "clicked the new button" exists only in treatment. Comparing
  users who clicked with all control users compares self-selected groups. The trigger must be "would have been shown
  the button", logged in both arms.
- **Sample ratio mismatch among triggered users.** A trigger that behaves differently between arms shows up as an
  unequal split of triggered users even when the overall split is correct. The
  <NoteLink to="sample-ratio-mismatch">sample ratio</NoteLink> check must be run on the triggered population too.
- **Missing affected users.** A trigger that is too narrow drops users the treatment affected and biases the diluted
  estimate. A trigger that is too broad only costs power. Comparing the arms on the untriggered users, where the
  difference should be zero, checks for leakage.
- **Reporting the triggered lift as the overall lift.** A 10% lift on triggered users is a 3.08% lift on all users in
  the example above. Launch decisions and impact estimates need the diluted figure <Cite id="deng2015" />.
