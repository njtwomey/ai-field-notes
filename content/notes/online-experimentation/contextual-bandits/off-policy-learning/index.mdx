<Definition>
**Off-policy learning** takes a log $\Dcal = \set{(x_i, a_i, r_i, p_i)}_{i=1}^{n}$ collected by a <NoteLink to="logging-policies-and-propensities">logging policy</NoteLink> with
propensities $p_i$, and returns a policy

$$
\hat\pi = \argmax_{\pi \in \Pi}\hat V(\pi)
$$

from a class $\Pi$, where $\hat V$ is an <NoteLink to="off-policy-evaluation">off-policy estimate</NoteLink> of the
value $V(\pi) = \expect_x[\sum_a\pi(a \mid x)\mu(x, a)]$. It is also called batch learning from bandit feedback
<Cite id="swaminathan2015" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Supervised learning is told the right label for each example. A log of bandit feedback says only how good the chosen
action was, for actions chosen by someone else's policy. Off-policy evaluation turns such a log into an estimate of any
policy's value. Off-policy learning optimises that estimate. The idea is simple, and the difficulties come from the
optimisation: searching over many policies finds the ones whose estimates are most flattered by noise.

## The IPS objective

With importance weights $w_i(\pi) = \pi(a_i \mid x_i)/p_i$, the IPS estimate of a policy's value is
$\hat V_{\text{IPS}}(\pi) = \frac1n\sum_i w_i(\pi)\,r_i$. For deterministic policies,
$\pi(a_i \mid x_i) = \indicator\set{\pi(x_i) = a_i}$, and

$$
\hat V_{\text{IPS}}(\pi) = \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{r_i}{p_i}\,\indicator\set{\pi(x_i) = a_i}.
$$

Maximising this is weighted classification: each logged row is an example with input $x_i$, label $a_i$ and
importance $r_i/p_i$, and the policy is a classifier rewarded for predicting the logged action on rows with high
importance. Any classifier that accepts example weights can therefore learn a policy. The
<NoteLink to="cost-sensitive-learning">cost-sensitive</NoteLink> version uses the doubly robust estimate of each
action's reward, $\hat\mu(x_i, a) + \indicator\set{a = a_i}(r_i - \hat\mu(x_i, a_i))/p_i$, as a full vector of rewards
for every row <Cite id="dudik2011" />.

For a differentiable stochastic policy $\pi_{\thetavec}(a \mid x)$, such as a softmax over scores, the <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> of the
IPS objective is

$$
\nabla_{\thetavec}\hat V_{\text{IPS}} = \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{r_i}{p_i}\nabla_{\thetavec}\pi_{\thetavec}(a_i \mid x_i)
= \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_i r_i\,\nabla_{\thetavec}\log\pi_{\thetavec}(a_i \mid x_i),
$$

using $\nabla\pi = \pi\nabla\log\pi$. This is the <NoteLink to="score-function-estimator">score-function
estimator</NoteLink> of policy gradient, with the importance weight correcting for sampling from $\pi_0$ instead of
$\pi_{\thetavec}$.

## Two failure modes

**The maximum is optimistic.** Even if $\hat V(\pi)$ is unbiased for every fixed $\pi$, the estimate at the chosen
policy is not. For every $\pi'$, $\max_\pi\hat V(\pi) \ge \hat V(\pi')$. Taking <NoteLink to="expectation">expectations</NoteLink>,
$\expect[\max_\pi\hat V(\pi)] \ge \expect[\hat V(\pi')] = V(\pi')$ for every $\pi'$, and therefore

$$
\expect\brack{\max_{\pi \in \Pi}\hat V(\pi)} \ge \max_{\pi \in \Pi}V(\pi).
$$

The estimated value of the learned policy overstates what it will earn, and more so for a rich class $\Pi$ and a noisy
estimator. This is the <NoteLink to="winners-curse">winner's curse</NoteLink> of policy search. With IPS the noise is
largest for policies that choose actions with small logging propensities, so the search is drawn towards exactly the
policies the log knows least about.

**IPS depends on the reward baseline.** Adding a constant $c$ to every reward does not change which policy is best:
every policy's true value rises by $c$. IPS changes differently for different policies:

$$
\frac1n\sum_i w_i(\pi)(r_i + c) = \hat V_{\text{IPS}}(\pi) + c\,\bar w(\pi), \qquad \bar w(\pi) = \frac1n\sum_i w_i(\pi).
$$

The average weight $\bar w(\pi)$ has expectation 1 but varies between policies in a finite sample. With $c > 0$ the
objective favours policies with large $\bar w$, those that agree with the log on rows with small propensities. With
$c < 0$, as when all rewards are losses, it favours policies with $\bar w$ near 0, which avoid the logged actions
altogether; such a policy scores 0, better than any negative estimate. Swaminathan and Joachims call this **propensity
overfitting** <Cite id="swaminathan2015b" />. The self-normalised estimator has no such dependence:

$$
\frac{\sum_i w_i(\pi)(r_i + c)}{\sum_i w_i(\pi)} = \hat V_{\text{SNIPS}}(\pi) + c.
$$

## Counterfactual risk minimisation

Swaminathan and Joachims bound the true value of a policy below by its estimate minus a term proportional to the
estimate's <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink>, using an empirical Bernstein inequality over the policy class. **Counterfactual risk
minimisation** (CRM) maximises that lower bound <Cite id="swaminathan2015" />:

$$
\hat\pi = \argmax_{\pi \in \Pi}\paren{\hat V_M(\pi) - \lambda\sqrt{\frac{\widehat{\var}_M(\pi)}{n}}},
$$

where $\hat V_M$ is IPS with weights clipped at $M$ and $\widehat{\var}_M$ is the sample variance of its terms. The
penalty is large for policies whose estimate rests on a few heavily weighted rows, so the search prefers policies the
log supports well. Their algorithm POEM applies this to softmax policies trained by gradient ascent. Norm-POEM replaces
the IPS estimate by SNIPS to remove propensity overfitting <Cite id="swaminathan2015b" />.

## Practice

- **Validate on held-out logs.** Because the training estimate is optimistic, estimate the learned policy's value on
  logs not used for learning, and compare it with the logging policy's value on the same logs.
- **Stay close to the logging policy.** Policies that differ from $\pi_0$ everywhere are those the log evaluates worst.
  Penalties on divergence from $\pi_0$, clipping and variance penalties all push the same way.
- **Deploy with exploration.** A learned policy that is deterministic produces logs from which its successor cannot be
  evaluated. Deploying it with some randomisation, and logging the propensities, keeps the loop going
  <Cite id="bottou2013" />.

## Assumptions

- The log satisfies the conditions for off-policy evaluation: overlap with every policy in $\Pi$, correct propensities
  and no unrecorded confounders.
- Contexts and rewards come from the same distribution at deployment as in the log.

## Pitfalls

- **Rewards coded as losses.** With IPS and negative rewards, the learner can maximise the objective by avoiding the
  logged actions. Use SNIPS or DR, or shift rewards so that the baseline is sensible and check that the result does not
  depend on the shift.
- **Rich policy classes on small logs.** A neural policy trained on 10,000 rows with small propensities finds spikes of
  luck in the IPS surface. Restrict the class, penalise variance, or collect more exploration.
- **Reporting the training objective.** The value of $\hat V$ at the learned policy is not an estimate of its
  performance. It is biased upwards by the selection.
