<Definition>
A BiLSTM-CRF tagger is a linear-chain <NoteLink to="conditional-random-field">conditional random field</NoteLink> whose
node potentials come from a bidirectional LSTM. For a sentence $\xvec$ of $N$ tokens and tags $\yvec \in \Ycal^N$, the
network outputs an emission score $o_{n,y}$ for every position $n$ and tag $y$, a learned matrix $\Tmat$ holds a
transition score $T_{u,v}$ for every pair of tags, and

$$
P(\yvec \mid \xvec) = \frac{\exp s(\xvec, \yvec)}{Z(\xvec)}, \qquad s(\xvec, \yvec) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} o_{n, y_n} +
\sum_{n=2}^{N} T_{y_{n-1}, y_n},
$$

where $Z(\xvec)$ sums $\exp s$ over all $\abs{\Ycal}^N$ tag sequences <Cite id="huang2015" /> <Cite id="lample2016" />.
</Definition>

## Architecture

1. **Token representations.** Each word $x_n$ becomes a vector $\evec_n$. Lample et al. concatenate a pretrained word
   embedding with a character-level representation: a bidirectional LSTM reads the word's characters, and its two final
   states capture prefixes, suffixes and capitalisation <Cite id="lample2016" />.
2. **Context.** A forward LSTM reads $\evec_1, \dots, \evec_N$ and a backward LSTM reads them in reverse. Their states
   at position $n$ are concatenated, $\hvec_n = [\overrightarrow{\hvec}_n; \overleftarrow{\hvec}_n]$, so $\hvec_n$
   depends on the whole sentence.
3. **Emissions.** A linear layer maps $\hvec_n$ to one score per tag, $\ovec_n = \Wmat \hvec_n + \bvec \in
   \reals^{\abs{\Ycal}}$.
4. **CRF layer.** The transition matrix $\Tmat \in \reals^{\abs{\Ycal} \times \abs{\Ycal}}$ scores each pair of
   neighbouring tags. Implementations usually add a start tag and an end tag, whose rows and columns score the first and
   last tags of the sentence.

Without the CRF layer the model is a per-token softmax over $\ovec_n$, and each tag is chosen independently. The CRF layer
lets the choice at one position depend on the choice at its neighbours. It learns, for example, that `I-PER` never follows
`O`, which a per-token classifier can violate.

## Link to the linear-chain CRF

In the notation of the conditional random field note, the node and edge potentials are

$$
\psivec_n(y) = \exp(o_{n,y}), \qquad \Psimat(u, v) = \exp(T_{u,v}),
$$

with the same edge potential at every position. Then $\exp s(\xvec, \yvec) = \prod_n \psivec_n(y_n) \prod_n \Psimat(y_n,
y_{n+1})$, and $Z(\xvec)$ is the partition function $Z_{\text{CRF}}$ <Cite id="twomey2016" />. A classical CRF computes
$\log \psivec_n(y) = \lambdavec^\top \fvec(\emptyset, y, \xvec, n)$ from hand-written features; the BiLSTM-CRF computes
it with a network trained jointly with $\Tmat$. The <NoteLink to="conditional-random-field-variants">variants of the CRF</NoteLink> note places
this among the other generalisations.

## The forward algorithm in log space

Training needs $\log Z(\xvec)$, a sum over exponentially many sequences. The CRF note computes it with forward messages
$\alphavec_n = \Psimat^\top \gammavec_{n-1}$ and $\gammavec_n = \alphavec_n \odot \psivec_n$, where $\gammavec_n(v)$ is
the total weight of all tag prefixes that end in $y_n = v$, including the node potential at $n$. Combining the two
updates,

$$
\gammavec_n(v) = \psivec_n(v) \sum_{u} \gammavec_{n-1}(u)\, \Psimat(u, v).
$$

The products overflow for long sentences, so work with $a_n(v) = \log \gammavec_n(v)$. Taking logarithms and
substituting $\psivec_n(v) = e^{o_{n,v}}$ and $\Psimat(u, v) = e^{T_{u,v}}$,

$$
a_1(v) = o_{1,v}, \qquad a_n(v) = o_{n,v} + \log \sum_{u} \exp\big(a_{n-1}(u) + T_{u,v}\big), \qquad \log Z(\xvec) =
\log \sum_{v} \exp a_N(v).
$$

Each step is a <NoteLink to="softmax-and-log-sum-exp">log-sum-exp</NoteLink> over the previous tag, computed stably by
subtracting the maximum. The recursion costs $O(N \abs{\Ycal}^2)$ per sentence and vectorises over a batch. With start
and end tags, $a_1(v)$ gains $T_{\text{start}, v}$ and the final sum gains $T_{v, \text{end}}$. Sentences of different
lengths in one batch need a mask that stops the recursion at each sentence's last token.

**Decoding.** Replacing each log-sum-exp by a maximum, and recording the maximising $u$, gives the Viterbi algorithm,
which returns the most probable tag sequence.

## Training

The loss for one sentence is the negative log-likelihood of its gold tags,

$$
\ell(\xvec, \yvec) = \log Z(\xvec) - s(\xvec, \yvec).
$$

The gold score $s(\xvec, \yvec)$ is a sum of $N$ emission and $N - 1$ transition entries. Everything is differentiable, so
the network, the embeddings and $\Tmat$ are trained together by backpropagation.

**What the gradient is.** $\log Z$ is a log-sum-exp over sequences of $\exp s$, and $s$ is linear in each score, so
$\partial \log Z / \partial o_{n,v}$ is the probability of the sequences with $y_n = v$: the marginal $P(Y_n = v \mid
\xvec)$. Likewise $\partial \log Z / \partial T_{u,v} = \sum_n P(Y_{n-1} = u, Y_n = v \mid \xvec)$. Hence

$$
\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial o_{n,v}} = P(Y_n = v \mid \xvec) - \indicator[y_n = v], \qquad \frac{\partial
\ell}{\partial T_{u,v}} = \sum_{n=2}^{N} \Big( P(Y_{n-1} = u, Y_n = v \mid \xvec) - \indicator[y_{n-1} = u, y_n = v]
\Big).
$$

This is the CRF gradient, expected minus observed counts. Automatic differentiation of the forward recursion computes
these marginals, which is the backward pass of forward–backward, without writing it by hand.

## Worked example

Reuse the chain from the conditional random field note: $N = 3$, two tags, node potentials $\psivec_1 = (2, 1)$,
$\psivec_2 = (1, 1)$ and $\psivec_3 = (1, 3)$, and edge potential $\Psimat = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3
\end{bmatrix}$. In scores, $\ovec_1 = (\log 2, 0)$, $\ovec_2 = (0, 0)$, $\ovec_3 = (0, \log 3)$, $T_{u,u} = \log 3$ and
$T_{u,v} = 0$ for $u \ne v$, with no start or end scores.

- $a_1 = (\log 2, 0) = (0.693, 0)$.
- $a_2(1) = 0 + \log(e^{\log 2 + \log 3} + e^{0 + 0}) = \log 7$ and $a_2(2) = 0 + \log(e^{\log 2} + e^{\log 3}) = \log 5$,
  so $a_2 = (1.946, 1.609)$.
- $a_3(1) = \log(7 \cdot 3 + 5) = \log 26$ and $a_3(2) = \log 3 + \log(7 + 5 \cdot 3) = \log 66$, so $a_3 = (3.258,
  4.190)$.
- $\log Z = \log(26 + 66) = \log 92 = 4.522$, the partition function found in the CRF note.

For the gold tags $\yvec = (1, 1, 2)$, $s = \log 2 + \log 3 + 0 + 0 + \log 3 = \log 18 = 2.890$, so the loss is $4.522 -
2.890 = 1.631$. The marginal at the first position is $P(Y_1) = (56, 36)/92 = (0.609, 0.391)$, so the gradient on
$\ovec_1$ is $(0.609 - 1, 0.391) = (-0.391, 0.391)$: training raises the score of the gold tag and lowers the other.

## Results

Huang, Xu and Yu first applied the bidirectional LSTM with a CRF layer to standard tagging benchmarks, POS tagging,
chunking and NER, and reported accuracy at or near the state of the art <Cite id="huang2015" />. Lample et al. added
character-level word representations and reached 90.94 F1 on CoNLL-2003 English NER without gazetteers
<Cite id="lample2016" />.

## Pitfalls

- **A strong encoder shrinks the CRF's gain.** A bidirectional encoder already sees the whole sentence, and a
  pretrained transformer even more so. The CRF layer then adds little accuracy, though it still guarantees valid tag
  sequences when invalid transitions are fixed to $-\infty$.
- **Masks in batches.** Padding positions must not enter the recursion or the gold score, or $\log Z$ and $s$ refer to
  different sequences.
- **Training cost.** Every sentence runs the forward recursion, $O(N\abs{\Ycal}^2)$, which dominates when the tag set is
  large, as in fine-grained entity typing.
