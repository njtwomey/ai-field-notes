<Definition>
Let $\Hcal_1 \subset \Hcal_2 \subset \cdots$ be a nested sequence of hypothesis classes, fixed before the data, and let
$\pi_1, \pi_2, \dots \ge 0$ be weights with $\sum_k \pi_k \le 1$. Let $\epsilon_k(m, \delta)$ be a
<NoteLink to="uniform-convergence">uniform convergence</NoteLink> bound for $\Hcal_k$: with probability at least
$1 - \delta$ over a sample $S$ of size $m$, every $h \in \Hcal_k$ has $R(h) \le \hat R_S(h) + \epsilon_k(m, \delta)$.
**Structural risk minimisation** (SRM) returns

$$
\hat h = \argmin_{k \ge 1,\ h \in \Hcal_k} \brack{\hat R_S(h) + \epsilon_k(m, \delta\pi_k)}.
$$

With probability at least $1 - \delta$, the bound $R(h) \le \hat R_S(h) + \epsilon_k(m, \delta\pi_k)$ holds for every
class $k$ and every $h \in \Hcal_k$ at once, so it holds for $\hat h$ <Cite id="vapnik1998" />
<Cite id="shalevshwartz2014" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

<NoteLink to="empirical-risk-minimisation">Empirical risk minimisation</NoteLink> needs a class chosen in advance. A
small class has a small estimation error and may contain no good predictor. A large class contains good predictors and
has a large estimation error. This is the
<NoteLink to="approximation-estimation-optimisation-trade-off">approximation–estimation trade-off</NoteLink>, and the
right class depends on the unknown distribution.

SRM lets the data choose the class. It orders the candidate classes by capacity, for example polynomials of increasing
degree, and gives each class a generalisation bound. A hypothesis from a larger class can have a lower training error
but carries a larger capacity term. The selected hypothesis minimises the bound itself, so the bound that justified the
choice also certifies the result.

SRM takes the <NoteLink to="occam-bound">Occam bound</NoteLink> one level up. The Occam bound gives each hypothesis a
share $\delta\pi(h)$ of the failure probability. SRM gives each class a share $\delta\pi_k$ and pays for the class with
its capacity and for the choice of class with $\ln(1/\pi_k)$. In the <NoteLink to="statistical-learning-theory">recipe
for a generalisation bound</NoteLink>, this is the third move, making a bound uniform, applied a second time: first
over the hypotheses of each class, then over the classes.

## Nested classes and weights

A **structure** is the sequence of classes together with a capacity for each:

- polynomial threshold classifiers $x \mapsto \sgn(p(x))$ on the real line with $\deg p \le k$, whose VC dimension is
  $d_k = k + 1$;
- linear predictors $\set{\xvec \mapsto \inner{\wvec}{\xvec} : \norm{\wvec} \le B_k}$ with growing radii $B_k$, whose
  <NoteLink to="rademacher-complexity">Rademacher complexity</NoteLink> grows with $B_k$;
- <NoteLink to="decision-tree">decision trees</NoteLink> with at most $k$ leaves;
- neural networks with $k$ hidden units.

The weights $\pi_k$ encode which classes the analyst expects to need. Two common choices are $\pi_k = 2^{-k}$, which
favours small classes strongly, and $\pi_k = 6/(\pi^2 k^2)$, which charges only $\ln(1/\pi_k) \approx 2\ln k$ for class
$k$. The weights must be fixed before the sample is seen, like the weights of the Occam bound.

The classes need not be nested for the bound to hold. Nesting makes the structure an ordering by capacity, and it lets
each hypothesis be charged for the smallest class that contains it, $k(h) = \min\set{k : h \in \Hcal_k}$.

## The SRM bound

The bound follows from two union bounds.

1. **One class, its own confidence.** Fix $k$. The uniform convergence bound of $\Hcal_k$ at confidence $\delta\pi_k$
   states that

   $$
   \prob\paren{\exists h \in \Hcal_k : R(h) > \hat R_S(h) + \epsilon_k(m, \delta\pi_k)} \le \delta\pi_k.
   $$

2. **Union over the classes.** By the <NoteLink to="union-bound">union bound</NoteLink>, the probability that the bound
   fails for some class is at most $\sum_k \delta\pi_k \le \delta$.
3. **Charge the smallest class.** On the complementary event, every $h$ satisfies the bound of every class that
   contains it. The tightest of these is usually that of $k(h)$, so with probability at least $1 - \delta$, for every
   $h \in \bigcup_k \Hcal_k$,

   $$
   R(h) \le \hat R_S(h) + \epsilon_{k(h)}(m, \delta\pi_{k(h)}).
   $$

The bound holds for all hypotheses simultaneously, so it holds for any hypothesis chosen after seeing the data. SRM
chooses the one with the smallest right-hand side. In practice SRM runs empirical risk minimisation (ERM) in each class,
giving $\hat h_k$, and then picks the class $\hat k$ that minimises $\hat R_S(\hat h_k) + \epsilon_k(m, \delta\pi_k)$.

With the <NoteLink to="vapnik-chervonenkis-dimension">VC bound</NoteLink>, a class of VC dimension
$d_k \le m$ has $\epsilon_k(m, \delta) = \sqrt{2d_k\ln(em/d_k)/m} + \sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)}$
(<NoteLink to="massarts-finite-class-lemma">Massart's lemma</NoteLink> and Sauer–Shelah). The SRM penalty of class $k$
is then

$$
\operatorname{pen}_k = \sqrt{\frac{2d_k\ln(em/d_k)}{m}} + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\pi_k) + \ln(1/\delta)}{2m}}.
$$

The first term is the capacity of the class. The second is the confidence term with the extra cost $\ln(1/\pi_k)$ of
not knowing in advance which class to use.

### SRM competes with the best class

The bound certifies the output. A second inequality compares the output with the best hypothesis of any class.

Fix any class $k$ and any $h \in \Hcal_k$ chosen without the data. On the event of the SRM bound,

$$
R(\hat h) \le \hat R_S(\hat h) + \operatorname{pen}_{k(\hat h)} \le \hat R_S(h) + \operatorname{pen}_k.
$$

The first inequality is the SRM bound and the second is the definition of $\hat h$ as the minimiser. Because $h$ is
fixed, <NoteLink to="hoeffdings-inequality">Hoeffding's inequality</NoteLink> gives
$\hat R_S(h) \le R(h) + \sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)}$ with probability at least $1 - \delta$. Together, with probability
at least $1 - 2\delta$,

$$
R(\hat h) \le \inf_{k}\ \brack{\inf_{h \in \Hcal_k} R(h) + \operatorname{pen}_k} + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{2m}}.
$$

The right side is what an oracle that knew the distribution would get by choosing the class with the best trade-off of
approximation error and penalty. A statement of this form is an <NoteLink to="oracle-inequalities">oracle
inequality</NoteLink> <Cite id="bartlett2002model" /> <Cite id="lugosi1996" />.

### Nonuniform learnability

The union $\Hcal = \bigcup_k \Hcal_k$ may have infinite VC dimension. Polynomial threshold classifiers of every degree
shatter any finite set of points on the line, so by the
<NoteLink to="fundamental-theorem-of-statistical-learning">fundamental theorem of statistical learning</NoteLink>
$\Hcal$ is not <Gloss name="pac" /> learnable. SRM still gives a guarantee whose sample size depends on the hypothesis
it competes with: to come within $\epsilon$ of the best $h \in \Hcal_k$, it needs the $m$ at which
$\operatorname{pen}_k + \sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)}$ falls below $\epsilon$. This weaker guarantee is **nonuniform
learnability**. A class of binary classifiers is nonuniformly learnable if and only if it is a countable union of
classes of finite VC dimension <Cite id="shalevshwartz2014" />.

## Worked example

Take polynomial threshold classifiers of degree $k \ge 1$ on the line, with $d_k = k + 1$ and weights
$\pi_k = 2^{-k}$, which sum to 1. Take $m = 2{,}000$ and $\delta = 0.05$, so $\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)} = 0.0274$. The
training errors in the table are illustrative values of ERM in each class.

| Degree $k$ | $d_k$ | Capacity term | Confidence term | Training error | SRM bound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | 0.126 | 0.030 | 0.300 | 0.456 |
| 2 | 3 | 0.150 | 0.033 | 0.210 | 0.393 |
| 3 | 4 | 0.170 | 0.036 | 0.120 | **0.326** |
| 4 | 5 | 0.187 | 0.038 | 0.115 | 0.340 |
| 10 | 11 | 0.261 | 0.050 | 0.100 | 0.411 |
| 50 | 51 | 0.488 | 0.097 | 0.060 | 0.645 |

1. **The penalty.** For $k = 3$, the capacity term is $\sqrt{2 \times 4 \times \ln(2{,}000e/4)/2{,}000} = 0.170$. The
   confidence term is $\sqrt{(3\ln 2 + \ln 20)/4{,}000} = 0.036$.
2. **The choice.** The bound is smallest at $k = 3$, 0.326. ERM over the union would pick $k = 50$ for its training
   error of 6%, whose bound is 0.645. Past degree 3 the training error falls by less than the capacity term rises.
3. **The price of not knowing the class.** Had degree 3 been fixed in advance, the confidence term would be 0.027 in
   place of 0.036. Choosing among the classes costs 0.008 here.
4. **Vacuity.** The bound of 0.326 is not vacuous, but it is loose. A held-out set of 2,000 examples would pin the
   chosen classifier's risk to within $\sqrt{\ln(1/\delta)/(2m)} = 0.027$ of its test error. The capacity term of
   0.170 is a worst case over all distributions.
5. **Sample size.** For the degree-3 penalty to fall to 0.1, the sample must grow to $m = 9{,}989$; for 0.05, to
   $m = 45{,}708$.

## Regularisation as SRM

A penalty on the norm of the weights is SRM over nested balls
<Cite id="shalevshwartz2014" /> <Cite id="mohri2018" />. Take linear predictors on inputs with $\norm{\xvec} \le \rho$,
the balls $\Hcal_k = \set{\xvec \mapsto \inner{\wvec}{\xvec} : \norm{\wvec} \le 2^k}$ for $k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$, and a
loss with values in $[0, 1]$ that is $L$-Lipschitz in the prediction, such as the ramp loss. The Rademacher complexity
of $\Hcal_k$ is at most $2^k\rho/\sqrt m$, and the <NoteLink to="contraction-lemma">contraction lemma</NoteLink> carries
it through the loss with a factor $L$. The <NoteLink to="rademacher-complexity">Rademacher bound</NoteLink> gives

$$
\epsilon_k(m, \delta) = \frac{2L\,2^k\rho}{\sqrt m} + \sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{2m}}.
$$

With $\pi_k = 1/((k + 1)(k + 2))$, a weight vector with $1 < \norm{\wvec} \le 2^k$ belongs to class
$k(\wvec) = \lceil\log_2\norm{\wvec}\rceil$, and $2^{k(\wvec)} < 2\norm{\wvec}$. The SRM bound becomes

$$
R(\wvec) \le \hat R_S(\wvec) + \frac{4L\rho\norm{\wvec}}{\sqrt m} + \sqrt{\frac{2\ln(k(\wvec) + 2) + \ln(1/\delta)}{2m}}.
$$

The penalty grows linearly in $\norm{\wvec}$ and only logarithmically in the class index. For a convex loss, minimising
$\hat R_S(\wvec) + \lambda\norm{\wvec}$, or $\hat R_S(\wvec) + \lambda\norm{\wvec}^2$ as in
<NoteLink to="ridge-regression">ridge regression</NoteLink>, traces the same family of solutions as the constraint
$\norm{\wvec} \le B$, one $B$ for each $\lambda$. Choosing the strength of a
<NoteLink to="regularisation">regulariser</NoteLink> is choosing a class from the structure. The bound fixes the
strength by theory; <NoteLink to="cross-validation">cross-validation</NoteLink> fixes it from held-out data.

<Gloss name="svm">Support vector machines</Gloss> choose the class after seeing the data: the margin of the trained
machine fixes its norm. SRM as defined above forbids this, because the structure must be fixed in advance. Shawe-Taylor,
Bartlett, Williamson and Anthony extended SRM to such data-dependent hierarchies, which justifies the
<NoteLink to="margin-bounds">margin bounds</NoteLink> of support vector machines <Cite id="shawetaylor1998" />.

## Pitfalls

- **The structure and weights come first.** Choosing the classes or the weights after looking at the data voids the
  union bound, as for the Occam bound.
- **A loose bound picks too simple a model.** The penalty is a worst-case bound, often several times larger than the
  real gap. Minimising it favours small classes. Hold-out estimates and data-dependent penalties, such as the empirical
  Rademacher complexity, select better in practice <Cite id="bartlett2002model" />.
- **SRM is not AIC or BIC.** <NoteLink to="information-criteria">Information criteria</NoteLink> estimate an expected
  out-of-sample log-likelihood or a marginal likelihood for large samples. The SRM penalty is a high-probability bound
  that holds for every sample size and every distribution.
- **Many classes are cheap, but not free.** The term $\ln(1/\pi_k)$ grows slowly, so SRM over a long sequence of
  classes costs little. A structure with very many classes of similar capacity spreads the weight thinly, so each class
  carries a larger term $\ln(1/\pi_k)$ without a smaller capacity term.
