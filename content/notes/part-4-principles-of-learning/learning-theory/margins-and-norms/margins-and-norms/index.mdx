<Definition>
Margins and norms is the part of <NoteLink to="statistical-learning-theory">learning theory</NoteLink> that bounds the
risk of a real-valued classifier $\sgn(h)$ by the fraction of training points it classifies with margin $y\,h(x)$ below
a scale $\gamma$, plus a capacity term of the form (norm of $h$) $\times$ (input radius) $/(\gamma\sqrt m)$. The
capacity is scale-sensitive: it depends on how far the training points sit from the decision boundary and how large the
weights are, and not on the dimension or the number of parameters <Cite id="bartlett1998" /> <Cite id="bartlett2002" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Counting labellings gives no useful bound for a kernel machine with an infinite-dimensional feature space, for a vote of
a thousand trees or for a network with more weights than examples. Such classifiers still generalise. The
scale-sensitive bounds explain part of this, and they connect the theory to how the methods are trained: the
<NoteLink to="support-vector-machine">support vector machine</NoteLink> minimises a <Gloss name="hinge-loss">hinge
loss</Gloss> plus a norm, boosting raises margins, and network training implicitly limits some norms. The bounds give a
way to compare classifiers on the same data, and a vocabulary (normalised margin, margin distribution, spectral
complexity) for doing so.

## Main ideas

**Replace the <NoteLink to="surrogate-losses">0–1 loss</NoteLink> with a Lipschitz one.** The ramp loss lies between the
0–1 loss and the indicator of a margin below $\gamma$, and it is $1/\gamma$-Lipschitz. The
<NoteLink to="contraction-lemma" /> then bounds the <NoteLink to="rademacher-complexity">Rademacher
complexity</NoteLink> of the losses by $1/\gamma$ times that of the scores.

**Norms bound the scores.** Linear functions with $\norm{\wvec} \le B$ on inputs with $\norm{\xvec} \le X$ have
Rademacher complexity at most $BX/\sqrt m$, in any dimension, and the same holds in a kernel's feature space with
$\trace\Kmat$ in place of $mX^2$. The <NoteLink to="margin-bounds">margin bound</NoteLink> combines the two steps; a
<NoteLink to="union-bound">union bound</NoteLink> over a grid of scales lets $\gamma$ be read off the trained
classifier.

**Convex combinations do not increase Rademacher complexity.** A weighted vote of base classifiers lies in their convex
hull, and the convex hull has the same Rademacher complexity as the base class. The
<NoteLink to="margin-theory-of-boosting">margin theory of boosting</NoteLink> uses this to give a bound that does not
grow with the number of rounds, and explains why boosting's test error can keep falling after its training error is
zero.

**Networks are measured by products of norms.** For a deep network, the Lipschitz constant is at most the product of
the layers' spectral norms. <NoteLink to="norm-based-bounds-for-neural-networks">Norm-based bounds</NoteLink> divide the
margin by such products, with Frobenius, path or spectral norms. They are free of parameter counts but are large in
practice, and they can grow with the sample size.
