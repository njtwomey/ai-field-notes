<Definition>
Uniform convergence and capacity is the part of <NoteLink to="statistical-learning-theory">learning theory</NoteLink>
that bounds the largest gap between true and empirical risk over an infinite class of hypotheses,
$\sup_{h \in \Hcal}\abs{R(h) - \hat R_S(h)}$. The bounds take the form of a capacity of $\Hcal$ divided by about
$\sqrt m$, where the capacity is a count of labellings, the VC dimension, a Rademacher average or a covering number
<Cite id="vapnik1971" /> <Cite id="bartlett2002" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A learner that chooses its hypothesis from the data needs a bound that holds for every hypothesis it could have
chosen. For a finite class, a <NoteLink to="union-bound" /> over the hypotheses gives one, at a cost of $\ln\abs\Hcal$.
Most classes in use are infinite: thresholds, half-spaces, kernel machines, neural networks. These notes give the
tools that replace $\ln\abs\Hcal$ by a finite capacity for such classes. They prove that empirical risk minimisation
succeeds on classes of finite VC dimension, give the sample sizes it needs, and supply the Rademacher bounds that the
margin, norm and stability arguments of later sub-chapters build on.

## Main ideas

**Uniform convergence is what empirical risk minimisation needs.** The excess risk of
<NoteLink to="empirical-risk-minimisation">ERM</NoteLink> over the best in the class is at most twice the uniform
deviation. A class whose uniform deviation shrinks for every distribution has the
<NoteLink to="uniform-convergence">uniform convergence</NoteLink> property. The first uniform law, for thresholds on the
line, is the <NoteLink to="glivenko-cantelli-theorem-and-dkw-inequality">Glivenko–Cantelli theorem</NoteLink>, with
the rate $2e^{-2m\epsilon^2}$ of the Dvoretzky–Kiefer–Wolfowitz inequality.

**Symmetrisation makes an infinite class finite.** <NoteLink to="symmetrisation">Symmetrisation</NoteLink> compares
the sample with an independent ghost sample, then with random signs. After it, only the values of the hypotheses on
the $2m$ points matter, and an infinite class acts through finitely many labellings.

**Counting labellings.** The <NoteLink to="growth-function-and-sauer-shelah-lemma">growth function</NoteLink>
$\Pi_\Hcal(m)$ counts the labellings of $m$ points. The Sauer–Shelah lemma bounds it by $(em/d)^d$, polynomial in $m$,
when the <NoteLink to="vapnik-chervonenkis-dimension">VC dimension</NoteLink> $d$ is finite. The result is a gap of
order $\sqrt{d\ln(m/d)/m}$.

**Averaging instead of counting.** <NoteLink to="rademacher-complexity">Rademacher complexity</NoteLink> measures how
well the class correlates with random signs on the sample. It bounds the expected uniform deviation directly, it applies
to real-valued losses, and it depends on the data. Two lemmas compute it:
<NoteLink to="massarts-finite-class-lemma">Massart's lemma</NoteLink> for a finite projection, which recovers the VC
bound, and the <NoteLink to="contraction-lemma">contraction lemma</NoteLink>, which passes a bound from scores to
Lipschitz losses.

**Covering at every scale.** <NoteLink to="covering-numbers-and-metric-entropy">Covering numbers</NoteLink> measure a
class by how many balls of radius $\epsilon$ cover it. <NoteLink to="chaining-and-dudley-entropy-integral">Chaining
</NoteLink> combines covers at all scales into Dudley's entropy integral and removes the $\ln m$ factor of the one-step
argument. The <NoteLink to="pseudo-dimension-and-fat-shattering-dimension">pseudo-dimension and fat-shattering
dimension</NoteLink> bound covering numbers for real-valued classes, and the
<NoteLink to="natarajan-dimension">Natarajan dimension</NoteLink> extends the VC dimension to multiclass problems.

## How the notes fit together

- Start with <NoteLink to="uniform-convergence" />, then the
  <NoteLink to="glivenko-cantelli-theorem-and-dkw-inequality">Glivenko–Cantelli theorem and DKW inequality</NoteLink>
  as the first worked case.
- <NoteLink to="symmetrisation" /> is the step every later bound uses; read it before the capacity measures.
- The combinatorial route runs through the <NoteLink to="growth-function-and-sauer-shelah-lemma">growth
  function</NoteLink> to the <NoteLink to="vapnik-chervonenkis-dimension">VC dimension</NoteLink>.
- The analytic route runs through <NoteLink to="rademacher-complexity" />, with Massart's and the contraction lemma as
  its tools.
- Covering numbers, chaining and the scale-sensitive dimensions refine both routes for real-valued and multiclass
  problems.
- <NoteLink to="measuring-capacity-empirically">Measuring capacity empirically</NoteLink> estimates these quantities on
  data: Rademacher complexity by fitting random signs, memorisation by fitting random labels, and an effective VC
  dimension.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume <NoteLink to="probably-approximately-correct-learning">PAC learning</NoteLink>, the
<NoteLink to="union-bound" />, <NoteLink to="hoeffdings-inequality">Hoeffding's inequality</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="mcdiarmids-inequality">McDiarmid's inequality</NoteLink>. The
<NoteLink to="fundamental-theorem-of-statistical-learning">fundamental theorem of statistical learning</NoteLink> uses
them to show that finite VC dimension, uniform convergence and learnability coincide for binary classification
<Cite id="shalevshwartz2014" />.

The next sub-chapter, <NoteLink to="margins-and-norms">margins and norms</NoteLink>,
applies Rademacher complexity and contraction to classifiers whose capacity is set by a margin and a norm. The
<NoteLink to="limits-of-uniform-convergence">limits of uniform convergence</NoteLink> describe learners that generalise
where every bound of this kind is vacuous.
