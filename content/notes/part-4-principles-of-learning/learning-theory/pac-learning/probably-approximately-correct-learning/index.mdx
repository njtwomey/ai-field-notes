<Definition>
A hypothesis class $\Hcal$ of classifiers is **(agnostic) PAC learnable** if there are a function
$m_{\Hcal}: (0, 1)^2 \to \naturals$ and a learning algorithm with the following property. For every
$\epsilon, \delta \in (0, 1)$ and every distribution $P$ over labelled examples, when the algorithm receives
$m \ge m_{\Hcal}(\epsilon, \delta)$ independent examples from $P$, it returns $\hat h$ with

$$
R(\hat h) \le \min_{h \in \Hcal} R(h) + \epsilon
$$

with probability at least $1 - \delta$ over the sample. The smallest such $m_{\Hcal}$ is the **sample complexity** of
$\Hcal$ <Cite id="valiant1984" /> <Cite id="shalevshwartz2014" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

"Probably approximately correct" names the two ways a learner can fall short. **Approximately**: even with a good
sample, the learned rule may be slightly worse than the best rule in the class, by at most $\epsilon$. **Probably**:
the sample may be unrepresentative, and then no learner can be trusted; this is allowed to happen with probability
at most $\delta$. The sample complexity says how many examples make both failures as rare as required, for every
data distribution at once.

Valiant's original definition assumed the **realisable** case: some $h \in \Hcal$ has zero risk
<Cite id="valiant1984" />. The **agnostic** version drops that assumption and compares with the best $h$ in the
class, which is what practice needs.

## Finite classes

Every finite class is PAC learnable by <NoteLink to="empirical-risk-minimisation">empirical risk
minimisation</NoteLink> (ERM), with a sample complexity that grows only with $\ln\abs{\Hcal}$.
<NoteLink to="hoeffdings-inequality">Hoeffding's inequality</NoteLink> bounds the deviation of one hypothesis's training
error from its risk, and a <NoteLink to="union-bound">union bound</NoteLink> over the $\abs{\Hcal}$ hypotheses makes the
bound hold for all of them at once <Cite id="hoeffding1963" />. In the agnostic case ERM then needs
$m \ge 2(\ln\abs{\Hcal} + \ln(2/\delta))/\epsilon^2$ examples; in the realisable case a consistent learner needs only
$m \ge (\ln\abs{\Hcal} + \ln(1/\delta))/\epsilon$. <NoteLink to="realisable-and-agnostic-learning">Realisable and
agnostic learning</NoteLink> derives both bounds and explains the two rates. The <NoteLink to="occam-bound">Occam
bound</NoteLink> extends them to countable classes, and <NoteLink to="test-set-bounds">test-set bounds</NoteLink> give
the one-hypothesis case that needs no union bound.

## Worked example: Boolean conjunctions

Let the inputs be 10 Boolean variables and let $\Hcal$ be the conjunctions of literals, such as
$x_1 \wedge \neg x_4 \wedge x_7$. Each variable appears positively, negatively or not at all, and one more hypothesis
is always false, so $\abs{\Hcal} = 3^{10} + 1 = 59{,}050$ and $\ln\abs{\Hcal} = 10.99$. Take $\epsilon = 0.05$ and
$\delta = 0.01$.

- **Realisable.** $m \ge (10.99 + \ln 100)/0.05 = (10.99 + 4.61)/0.05 = 311.8$, so 312 examples suffice.
- **Agnostic, from the <NoteLink to="uniform-convergence">uniform deviation</NoteLink>.** Requiring every empirical risk
  to be within $\epsilon$ of its risk,
  $m \ge (\ln\abs{\Hcal} + \ln(2/\delta))/(2\epsilon^2) = (10.99 + 5.30)/0.005 = 3{,}257$.

- **Agnostic, for ERM.** Guaranteeing $R(\hat h) \le \min_{h \in \Hcal} R(h) + \epsilon$ needs deviations within
  $\epsilon/2$, which multiplies the previous figure by 4: $m \ge 13{,}028$.

The agnostic requirement is 10 to 40 times the realisable one. All three grow with $\ln\abs{\Hcal} = 10.99$, not with
$\abs{\Hcal} = 59{,}050$.

## Beyond finite classes

Most useful classes, such as all linear classifiers, are infinite, and $\ln\abs{\Hcal}$ is then infinite. The
<NoteLink to="growth-function-and-sauer-shelah-lemma">growth function</NoteLink>, the number of distinct labellings the
class can produce on $m$ points, replaces $\abs{\Hcal}$. The <NoteLink to="vapnik-chervonenkis-dimension">VC
dimension</NoteLink> is the size of the largest set of points on which the class produces every labelling, and it bounds
the growth function. The <NoteLink to="fundamental-theorem-of-statistical-learning">fundamental theorem of statistical
learning</NoteLink> states that a binary class is PAC learnable if and only if its VC dimension $d$ is finite, with
sample complexity of order $(d + \ln(1/\delta))/\epsilon^2$ in the agnostic case <Cite id="blumer1989" />
<Cite id="shalevshwartz2014" />. <NoteLink to="rademacher-complexity">Rademacher complexity</NoteLink> gives
data-dependent versions of the same bounds.

## Assumptions

- **Independent, identically distributed examples** from the same distribution used for testing.
- **Worst case over distributions.** The guarantee holds for every $P$. That makes it robust, and also loose for the
  benign distributions met in practice.
- **Computation is ignored** in the statistical definition. Valiant also required a polynomial-time learner. Some
  classes are learnable with few samples but only with an intractable search
  (<NoteLink to="efficient-pac-learning">efficient PAC learning</NoteLink>).

## Pitfalls

- **The bound is on the class, not the model.** A PAC bound certifies ERM over $\Hcal$ chosen before seeing the data.
  Choosing $\Hcal$ after looking at the data, for example by trying many model families, enlarges the effective class.
- **Bounds are often vacuous numerically.** For modern networks, $\ln\abs{\Hcal}$ or its analogues exceed the number of
  examples, and the bound exceeds 1. The theory explains rates and trends better than it predicts error bars.
