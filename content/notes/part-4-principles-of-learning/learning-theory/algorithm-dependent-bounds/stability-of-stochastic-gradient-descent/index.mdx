<Definition>
**<Gloss name="sgd">Stochastic gradient descent</Gloss>** on a sample $S = (z_1, \dots, z_m)$ starts from a fixed
$\wvec_0$ and takes $T$ steps $\wvec_{t} = \wvec_{t-1} - \eta_t \nabla\ell(\wvec_{t-1}, z_{i_t})$, where each index
$i_t$ is drawn uniformly from $\set{1, \dots, m}$ and $\eta_t > 0$ is the step size. Suppose that for every example $z$
the loss $\ell(\cdot, z)$ is convex, $L$-Lipschitz ($\norm{\nabla\ell(\wvec, z)} \le L$) and $H$-smooth
($\norm{\nabla\ell(\wvec, z) - \nabla\ell(\vvec, z)} \le H\norm{\wvec - \vvec}$), and that $\eta_t \le 2/H$. Then the
output $\wvec_T$ is uniformly stable in expectation over the indices, with

$$
\beta \le \frac{2L^2}{m}\sum_{t=1}^T \eta_t,
$$

so its <NoteLink to="generalisation-gap">expected generalisation gap</NoteLink> is at most $\beta$
<Cite id="hardt2016sgd" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

<NoteLink to="stability-of-regularised-empirical-risk-minimisation">Regularised empirical risk minimisation</NoteLink>
is stable because its exact minimiser cannot move far. Stochastic gradient descent in practice never reaches a
minimiser. It is stopped after a budget of steps. Its output depends on the data only through the gradients it has
taken, and a single example can only have influenced the gradients on the steps that visited it.

Picture two runs, one on $S$ and one on $S^{(i)}$, which visit the examples in the same order. They take identical
steps until the first visit to the replaced example. At that step the two gradients differ and the runs separate by at
most $2\eta_t L$. On later steps that visit shared examples, a convex smooth loss with a small enough step size cannot
push the runs further apart. So the separation grows only on visits to the replaced example, about once every $m$
steps, and by at most $2\eta_t L$ each time. After $T$ steps the expected separation is at most
$\frac{2L}{m}\sum_t\eta_t$.

The bound depends on the total distance the algorithm could travel, $\sum_t\eta_t$, and on nothing about the model's
size. Training for fewer steps, or with smaller steps, gives a smaller bound on the gap. Hardt, Recht and Singer
summarised this as "train faster, generalize better" <Cite id="hardt2016sgd" />. It is one theoretical reading of
<NoteLink to="early-stopping">early stopping</NoteLink>: the number of steps acts as a regulariser.

In the <NoteLink to="statistical-learning-theory">recipe for a generalisation bound</NoteLink>, this stability bound
exploits the structure of an optimiser rather than of a <NoteLink to="empirical-risk-minimisation">hypothesis
class</NoteLink>. The resulting $\beta$ feeds the bound of <NoteLink to="algorithmic-stability">algorithmic
stability</NoteLink>.

## Coupling two runs

Let $S$ and $S^{(i)}$ differ in the $i$-th example. Run stochastic gradient descent on both with the same index
sequence $i_1, \dots, i_T$ and the same start $\wvec_0$. Write $\wvec_t$ and $\wvec_t'$ for the two runs and
$\Delta_t = \norm{\wvec_t - \wvec_t'}$ for their separation, with $\Delta_0 = 0$. A step is the map
$G_t(\wvec) = \wvec - \eta_t\nabla\ell(\wvec, z)$ for the example $z$ visited. Three facts about such maps control
$\Delta_t$ <Cite id="hardt2016sgd" />.

- **Bounded.** $\norm{G_t(\wvec) - \wvec} = \eta_t\norm{\nabla\ell(\wvec, z)} \le \eta_t L$ for an $L$-Lipschitz loss.
- **Non-expansive.** If the loss is convex and $H$-smooth and $\eta_t \le 2/H$, then
  $\norm{G_t(\wvec) - G_t(\vvec)} \le \norm{\wvec - \vvec}$.
- **Expansive at most by $1 + \eta_t H$.** Without convexity, smoothness alone gives
  $\norm{G_t(\wvec) - G_t(\vvec)} \le (1 + \eta_t H)\norm{\wvec - \vvec}$.

Coupling the two runs through the same indices is legitimate because stability is defined for the expected loss over
the algorithm's randomness, and any joint construction with the correct marginals gives the same expectation.

## The convex bound

**Step 1: one step, two cases.** At step $t$ the visited index $i_t$ is uniform on $\set{1, \dots, m}$.

- With probability $1 - 1/m$ the step visits a shared example. Both runs apply the same map $G_t$, which is
  non-expansive, so $\Delta_t \le \Delta_{t-1}$.
- With probability $1/m$ the step visits position $i$, where the runs hold different examples and apply different maps.
  Each map moves its point by at most $\eta_t L$, so by the triangle inequality $\Delta_t \le \Delta_{t-1} + 2\eta_t L$.

**Step 2: take expectations.** Averaging over the two cases,

$$
\expect\Delta_t \le \paren{1 - \frac1m}\expect\Delta_{t-1} + \frac1m\paren{\expect\Delta_{t-1} + 2\eta_t L}
= \expect\Delta_{t-1} + \frac{2\eta_t L}{m}.
$$

**Step 3: unroll.** Starting from $\Delta_0 = 0$, $\expect\Delta_T \le \frac{2L}{m}\sum_{t=1}^T\eta_t$.

**Step 4: from parameters to losses.** For any test example $z$, the Lipschitz condition gives
$\expect\abs{\ell(\wvec_T, z) - \ell(\wvec_T', z)} \le L\,\expect\Delta_T \le \frac{2L^2}{m}\sum_t\eta_t$. This holds
for every pair of samples differing in one example and every $z$, which is the stated stability.

<Derivation title="Why a gradient step is non-expansive">

For a convex $H$-smooth function $f$, the gradient is co-coercive:
$\inner{\nabla f(\uvec) - \nabla f(\vvec)}{\uvec - \vvec} \ge \frac1H\norm{\nabla f(\uvec) - \nabla f(\vvec)}^2$. Write
$\gvec = \nabla f(\uvec) - \nabla f(\vvec)$ and $G(\uvec) = \uvec - \eta\nabla f(\uvec)$. Expanding the square,

$$
\norm{G(\uvec) - G(\vvec)}^2 = \norm{\uvec - \vvec}^2 - 2\eta\inner{\gvec}{\uvec - \vvec} + \eta^2\norm{\gvec}^2
\le \norm{\uvec - \vvec}^2 - \paren{\frac{2\eta}{H} - \eta^2}\norm{\gvec}^2.
$$

For $\eta \le 2/H$ the bracket is non-negative, so $\norm{G(\uvec) - G(\vvec)} \le \norm{\uvec - \vvec}$. For a
non-convex $H$-smooth function, the triangle inequality and $\norm{\gvec} \le H\norm{\uvec - \vvec}$ give the
expansion factor $1 + \eta H$ instead. If $f$ is also $\lambda$-strongly convex, a similar expansion shows the step
contracts distances by a factor strictly below 1 for small enough $\eta$.

</Derivation>

### Reading the bound

- **Constant step size, $E$ epochs.** With $\eta_t = \eta$ and $T = Em$ steps,
  $\beta \le 2L^2\eta T/m = 2L^2\eta E$. The bound grows linearly with the number of epochs and does not shrink with
  $m$ at a fixed number of epochs.
- **Decaying step size.** With $\eta_t = c/\sqrt t$, $\sum_{t \le T}\eta_t \le 2c\sqrt T$, so
  $\beta \le 4cL^2\sqrt T/m = 4cL^2\sqrt{E/m}$. More data now helps.
- **The risk, not only the gap.** Stopping early lowers $\beta$ but leaves the empirical risk higher. Hardt, Recht and
  Singer balanced the <NoteLink to="approximation-estimation-optimisation-trade-off">optimisation error</NoteLink> of
  averaged stochastic gradient descent against its stability. With a step size tuned to the number of steps $T$, the
  averaged iterate $\bar\wvec_T$ satisfies
  $\expect R(\bar\wvec_T) \le \expect\hat R_S(\wvec_S^*) + \frac{DL}{\sqrt m}\sqrt{\frac{m + 2T}{T}}$, where $\wvec_S^*$
  minimises the empirical risk and $D$ bounds the distance from the start to it. Several passes over the data cost
  nothing in rate when the step size shrinks with $T$ <Cite id="hardt2016sgd" />.

## Strongly convex and non-convex losses

**Strongly convex losses.** If each $\ell(\cdot, z)$ is $\lambda$-strongly convex and $H$-smooth, projected stochastic
gradient descent with a constant step $\eta \le 1/H$ is uniformly stable with $\beta \le 2L^2/(\lambda m)$, for any
number of steps $T$ <Cite id="hardt2016sgd" />. Each shared step contracts the separation, so the runs stop drifting
apart. The constant matches the one for the exact
<NoteLink to="stability-of-regularised-empirical-risk-minimisation">regularised minimiser</NoteLink>.

**Non-convex losses.** Without convexity a shared step can expand the separation by $1 + \eta_t H$, so the runs can
drift apart even when they visit the same examples. Two ideas still give a bound for a loss in $[0, 1]$.

1. **A burn-in period.** The replaced example is first visited after a random number of steps. For any $t_0$, the
   probability that it is visited in the first $t_0$ steps is at most $t_0/m$, and on the complementary event the runs
   coincide up to step $t_0$. So the expected loss difference is at most $t_0/m$ plus $L$ times the expected separation
   given that the runs coincide at $t_0$.
2. **Decaying steps.** With non-increasing steps $\eta_t \le c/t$, the expansion factors $1 + cH/t$ multiply to a
   polynomial in $T$ rather than an exponential.

Optimising over $t_0$ gives

$$
\beta \le \frac{1 + 1/(Hc)}{m - 1}\paren{2cL^2}^{1/(Hc + 1)} T^{Hc/(Hc + 1)},
$$

which behaves like $T^{1 - 1/(Hc + 1)}/m$ <Cite id="hardt2016sgd" />. The bound still favours few steps, but it grows
almost linearly in $T$ when $Hc$ is large. Deep networks are usually trained with step sizes that do not decay like
$c/t$, so the bound does not apply to them as stated.

## Worked example

Take <NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink> with inputs of norm at most 1. The
<NoteLink to="surrogate-losses">logistic loss</NoteLink> $\ell(\wvec, (x, y)) = \ln(1 + e^{-y\inner{\wvec}{x}})$ with
$y \in \set{-1, +1}$ has gradient norm at most $\norm{x} \le 1$, so $L = 1$, and its Hessian is at most
$\frac14 xx\transpose$, so $H = \frac14$. Any step size up to $2/H = 8$ is allowed. Take $m = 50{,}000$ examples.

1. **Constant step size.** With $\eta = 0.1$ and $E$ epochs, $\beta \le 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 0.1 \cdot E = 0.2E$ nats. One
   epoch gives 0.2; ten epochs give 2. The logistic loss at $\wvec = \zeros$ is $\ln 2 = 0.69$ nats, so the bound after
   ten epochs says nothing about the gap.
2. **Decaying step size.** With $\eta_t = 0.5/\sqrt t$ and ten epochs, $T = 500{,}000$ and
   $\sum_t\eta_t \le 2 \cdot 0.5\sqrt{500{,}000} = 707$. The bound is $\beta \le 2 \cdot 707/50{,}000 = 0.028$ nats.
   One epoch gives $0.009$.
3. **Sample size.** For $\beta \le 0.01$ with the decaying step and ten epochs,
   $4cL^2\sqrt{E/m} = 2\sqrt{10/m} \le 0.01$ needs $m \ge 400{,}000$. One epoch needs $m \ge 40{,}000$.
4. **Vacuous or not.** The decaying-step bound is non-vacuous and does not depend on the input dimension. It bounds
   the expected gap in the logistic loss, and with it the expected gap in the 0-1 loss only through the surrogate:
   the 0-1 risk is at most $1/\ln 2$ times the logistic risk.

## Pitfalls

- **The bound is on the expected gap.** Uniform stability in expectation over the indices bounds
  $\expect[R(\wvec_T) - \hat R_S(\wvec_T)]$. High-probability bounds for several passes of stochastic gradient descent
  need the sharper analysis of Feldman and Vondrák <Cite id="feldman2019stability" />.
- **Worst-case constants.** $L$ and $H$ are suprema over all examples and parameters. Kuzborskij and Lampert replaced
  them by data-dependent quantities: in the convex case the bound depends on the risk at the initialisation, and in the
  non-convex case on the curvature near it <Cite id="kuzborskij2018" />.
- **The step-size condition.** Non-expansiveness needs $\eta_t \le 2/H$. Larger steps can expand the
  separation even for convex losses.
- **Stability bounds only the gap.** A run that stops after one step is very stable and fits nothing. The bound
  explains why the gap is small, and the optimisation error must be bounded separately.
- **Non-smooth losses.** The derivation uses smoothness for non-expansiveness. The <Gloss name="hinge-loss">hinge
  loss</Gloss> is not smooth, and its stability needs a different argument.
