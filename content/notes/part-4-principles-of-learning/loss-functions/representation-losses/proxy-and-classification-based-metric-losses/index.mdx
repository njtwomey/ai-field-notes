<Definition>
A **proxy** is a learned vector $\wvec_c \in \reals^d$ that stands for class $c$ in the embedding space. A **proxy
loss** trains an embedding $f(\xvec) \in \reals^d$ by comparing it with the $C$ proxies instead of with other training
examples. With $\wvec_c$ and $f(\xvec)$ both normalised to unit length and $\theta_c$ the angle between them, the
**normalised softmax** loss is the cross-entropy of a softmax over logits $s\cos\theta_c$, with a fixed scale $s > 0$
<Cite id="wang2017" />. **Margin** variants lower the logit of the true class $y$ during training: SphereFace uses
$s\cos(m\theta_y)$ <Cite id="liu2017" />, CosFace uses $s(\cos\theta_y - m)$ <Cite id="wang2018" />, and ArcFace uses
$s\cos(\theta_y + m)$ <Cite id="deng2019" />. After training, the proxies are discarded and the embedding is compared by
<NoteLink to="dot-product">cosine similarity</NoteLink>.
</Definition>

## Why compare with proxies

The <NoteLink to="triplet-and-margin-losses">triplet loss</NoteLink> compares an example with other examples. A dataset
of $n$ examples has $O(n^3)$ triplets, most of which contribute zero loss, so training depends on mining informative
ones. A proxy loss compares each example with $C$ proxies, $O(nC)$ comparisons in total, and every comparison
contributes a <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink>. The proxies are trained together with the network.

**Proxy-NCA** assigns one proxy per class and applies the neighbourhood-components softmax to distances between the
embedding and the proxies <Cite id="movshovitz2017" />:

$$
L_{\text{Proxy-NCA}}(\xvec, y) = -\ln \frac{\exp\paren{-d(f(\xvec), \wvec_y)}}{\sum_{c \ne y} \exp\paren{-d(f(\xvec), \wvec_c)}} ,
$$

where $d$ is the squared <NoteLink to="pairwise-distances-and-cosine-similarity">Euclidean distance</NoteLink> between
normalised vectors. Movshovitz-Attias et al. showed that a triplet loss over proxies bounds the triplet loss over
examples, up to how well each proxy approximates its class. On Cars196, Proxy-NCA converged about three times as fast as
triplet-based losses <Cite id="movshovitz2017" />.

## Classification with normalised weights is a proxy loss

A softmax classifier without bias computes logits $\wvec_c^\top f(\xvec)$. Its weight vectors are proxies. Writing the
logit as $\norm{\wvec_c}\norm{f(\xvec)}\cos\theta_c$ shows that it mixes three things: the angle, the length of the
class weight and the length of the embedding. Normalising both vectors leaves only the angle, so the trained embedding
is organised by the same cosine similarity used at test time <Cite id="wang2017" />.

**Normalised logits need a scale.** With unit vectors, every logit lies in $[-1, 1]$, and the softmax probability of the
true class cannot approach 1. The best case has each embedding equal to its own proxy ($\cos\theta_y = 1$) and the
proxies as far apart as possible. For $C$ unit vectors,
$0 \le \norm{\sum_c \wvec_c}^2 = C + \sum_{c \ne c'} \wvec_c^\top\wvec_{c'}$, so the average cosine between two distinct
proxies is at least $-1/(C - 1)$, and a regular simplex attains it. In that best case, with scale $s$,

$$
p_y = \frac{e^{s}}{e^{s} + (C - 1)\, e^{-s/(C - 1)}} .
$$

Requiring $p_y \ge P$ and solving for $s$ gives

$$
s \ge \frac{C - 1}{C} \ln\frac{(C - 1) P}{1 - P} .
$$

With $C = 10{,}000$ classes, $P = 0.9$ needs $s \ge 11.4$; with $C = 10$ it needs only $s \ge 4.0$. Without a scale the
loss stays high and its gradients do not vanish even for well-separated classes. NormFace derived this bound and learned
$s$; CosFace and ArcFace fix $s = 64$ <Cite id="wang2017" /> <Cite id="wang2018" /> <Cite id="deng2019" />.

## Margins

The normalised softmax separates classes, but it stops pushing once an embedding is closer in angle to its own proxy
than to the others. A face-verification system compares embeddings of new identities by a single cosine threshold, so it
needs each class to be compact and well away from the others. A **margin** lowers the true-class logit during training
only, so the loss keeps pushing until the embedding clears the rival classes by the margin.

Consider two classes whose proxies are $\phi$ apart, and an embedding in their plane at angle $\theta$ from class 1, so
at angle $\phi - \theta$ from class 2. Class 1 wins the training softmax where its logit exceeds class 2's.

- **Normalised softmax**: $\cos\theta > \cos(\phi - \theta)$, so the boundary is $\theta = \phi/2$.
- **ArcFace**: $\cos(\theta + m) > \cos(\phi - \theta)$. Cosine decreases on $[0, \pi]$, so while $\theta + m \le \pi$
  this holds exactly when $\theta + m < \phi - \theta$, that is $\theta < (\phi - m)/2$. The boundary moves toward class
  1 by $m/2$ radians. The same holds for class 2 from its side, which leaves an angular gap of exactly $m$ between the
  two training regions, whatever $\phi$ is <Cite id="deng2019" />.
- **CosFace**: $\cos\theta - m > \cos(\phi - \theta)$. The margin is constant in cosine, so the angular gap it leaves
  depends on $\phi$ <Cite id="wang2018" />.
- **SphereFace**: $\cos(m\theta)$ with an integer $m \ge 2$ multiplies the angle. Its margin grows with $\theta$ and
  vanishes as $\theta \to 0$ <Cite id="liu2017" />.

At test time the margin is dropped. Embeddings that satisfied it in training sit well inside their class's region, so a
single cosine threshold separates same-identity from different-identity pairs with room to spare.

$\cos(\theta + m)$ stops decreasing once $\theta + m > \pi$, so implementations substitute a monotone penalty
in that range.

## Worked example

An embedding is at $40°$ from its class proxy, $60°$ from a second proxy and $90°$ from two more. The scale is $s = 16$.
The rival logits are $16\cos 60° = 8$ and $16\cos 90° = 0$ (twice).

| Loss               | True-class logit                | $p_y$ | Loss $-\ln p_y$ |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- | ----- | --------------- |
| Normalised softmax | $16\cos 40° = 12.26$            | 0.986 | 0.014           |
| CosFace, $m = 0.35$ | $16(\cos 40° - 0.35) = 6.66$   | 0.207 | 1.576           |
| ArcFace, $m = 0.5$  | $16\cos(40° + 28.6°) = 5.83$   | 0.102 | 2.283           |

The embedding is already classified correctly: $40°$ is closer than $60°$. The normalised softmax loss is nearly zero,
so training would stop moving it. Under either margin the true-class logit falls below the rival's, and the loss keeps
pulling the embedding toward its proxy. With $\phi = 90°$, the training boundary is at $\theta = 30.7°$ for CosFace with
$m = 0.35$ and at $30.7°$ for ArcFace with $m = 0.5$; the two margins are nearly equivalent at this angle.

## Assumptions

- **Class labels exist** for every training example, and there are enough examples per class to place its proxy. Proxy
  losses do not apply to purely self-supervised pairs.
- **One proxy per class suffices.** A class with several distinct modes, such as one person with and without glasses,
  is pulled toward a single point. Variants with several proxies per class relax this.
- **The proxy matrix fits in memory.** A face dataset with hundreds of thousands of identities needs a $d \times C$
  weight matrix and a softmax over all $C$ classes at every step.

## Pitfalls

- **Scale too small.** With $s$ below the bound above the loss cannot approach zero, and optimisation keeps pushing
  well-separated classes.
- **Margin too large.** A margin that the data cannot satisfy keeps every example active and can prevent convergence.
  ArcFace uses $m = 0.5$ and CosFace $m = 0.35$ with $s = 64$ <Cite id="deng2019" /> <Cite id="wang2018" />.
- **Label noise.** A mislabelled example is pulled toward the wrong proxy with the full force of the margin.
- **Comparisons across papers.** Under one protocol on CUB-200, normalised softmax, CosFace and ArcFace scored MAP@R
  25.25, 26.70 and 26.45, against 26.53 for the 2006 pair loss <Cite id="musgrave2020" />. Gains attributed to the loss
  often come from the rest of the training setup.
