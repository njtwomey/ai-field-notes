<Definition>
A classifier that outputs a score $s(\xvec)$ predicts the positive class when $s(\xvec)$ exceeds a threshold $\tau$.
Each threshold gives one <NoteLink to="confusion-matrix">confusion matrix</NoteLink>. **ROC and precision-recall
curves** trace two of its rates as $\tau$ sweeps from $+\infty$ to $-\infty$, so one curve summarises every confusion
matrix the classifier can produce.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The <NoteLink to="classification-metrics" /> topic scores a classifier at one fixed threshold. A model is often trained
before the threshold is known, or deployed at several thresholds for different users. Curves compare classifiers
without committing to a threshold, and they show the trade-off between the two kinds of error at a glance. Medical
diagnostics, biometrics, fraud detection and retrieval all report one of these curves or its area
<Cite id="fawcett2006" />.

## Main ideas

**The ROC curve and its area.** The
<NoteLink to="receiver-operating-characteristic-curve-and-area" /> plots the true-positive rate against the
false-positive rate. Both rates are computed within a true class, so the curve does not depend on the class prevalence.
The area under it, AUROC, equals the probability that a randomly chosen positive scores above a randomly chosen
negative. That equivalence links AUROC to the Mann–Whitney statistic and gives it a confidence interval.

**The precision-recall curve.** The <NoteLink to="precision-recall-curve-and-average-precision" /> plots precision
against recall. Precision is a rate within the predicted class, so the curve depends on prevalence. For rare positives
that dependence is useful: a large change in <Gloss name="fp">false positives</Gloss> barely moves the false-positive
rate but sharply lowers precision <Cite id="davis2006" />. Average precision summarises the curve as a step sum, not a
trapezoidal area, and its chance level equals the prevalence.

**Choosing an operating point.** A deployed classifier needs one threshold.
<NoteLink to="operating-points-and-equal-error-rate" /> lists the standard rules: fix one rate and read off the other,
balance the false-positive and <Gloss name="fn">false-negative</Gloss> rates at the equal error rate, maximise Youden's
index, or minimise expected cost given the class priors and the cost of each error.

## How the notes fit together

Start with the ROC curve, since the precision-recall note defines its curve by contrast with it. The operating-points
note comes last and turns either curve back into a single decision rule. All three require the rates from
