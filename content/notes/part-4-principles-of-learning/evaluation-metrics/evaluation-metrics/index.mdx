<Definition>
An **evaluation metric** maps a model's predictions on a test set, together with the true targets, to a number that
measures how good the predictions are. This topic collects the metrics in common use, grouped by the kind of
prediction they score: class labels, scores, probabilities, real values, rankings, clusterings, regions of an image,
generated samples, text and audio.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A metric decides which model is reported as best, which hyperparameters are kept and when a deployed model is retrained.
Different metrics reward different behaviour, so the choice changes conclusions. On a test set with 1% positives a
classifier that always predicts the negative class reaches 99% accuracy and zero recall. A metric must therefore match
the use of the model: whether its outputs drive decisions, rankings or probability estimates, what each kind of error
costs, and how common each class is when deployed. The note <NoteLink to="choosing-a-metric" /> sets out these
questions.

## Main ideas

**A metric answers a question about use.** Classification metrics come in three kinds
<Cite id="sokolova2009" />. Threshold metrics score decisions, ranking metrics score the ordering of scores, and
probabilistic metrics score the probabilities themselves. The kinds are not interchangeable: a classifier can rank
perfectly and still give badly calibrated probabilities.

**Most classification metrics are functions of four counts.** The <NoteLink to="classification-metrics" /> topic builds
accuracy, precision, recall, the $F$-score, the <NoteLink to="matthews-correlation-coefficient">Matthews
correlation</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="cohens-kappa">Cohen's kappa</NoteLink> from the
<NoteLink to="confusion-matrix" />. <NoteLink to="receiver-operating-characteristic-and-precision-recall-curves" />
sweeps the threshold to evaluate every confusion matrix at once.

**Probabilities need proper scores.** <NoteLink to="log-loss-and-brier-score" /> are
<NoteLink to="proper-scoring-rule">strictly proper</NoteLink>: their expected value is optimised only by the true
probabilities. <NoteLink to="calibration-error" /> measures one component of that quality, whether predicted
probabilities match observed frequencies.

**Continuous targets are scored by error size.** <NoteLink to="regression-metrics" /> covers squared and absolute errors
and $R^2$. <NoteLink to="forecasting-metrics" /> adds scale-free and probabilistic scores for time series.
<NoteLink to="correlation-and-agreement" /> measures association and agreement, and
<NoteLink to="distances-and-similarities" /> compares vectors and point sets.

**Structured outputs need their own metrics.** Ranked lists are scored by
<NoteLink to="ranking-and-retrieval-metrics" />, partitions by <NoteLink to="clustering-metrics" />, boxes and masks by
<NoteLink to="detection-and-segmentation-metrics" />, and images by <NoteLink to="image-quality-metrics" />. Generated
samples, which have no reference, are scored as distributions in <NoteLink to="generative-model-metrics" />. Text and
speech have the task-specific scores of <NoteLink to="text-metrics" /> and <NoteLink to="audio-and-speech-metrics" />.

**A metric is an estimate.** A score on a finite test set has sampling error, so two models whose scores differ in the
third decimal place may not differ at all. <NoteLink to="metric-confidence-intervals" /> gives intervals by the Wilson
formula and the bootstrap, and paired comparisons of two models on one test set.

## How the notes fit together

Start with the foundations notes, <NoteLink to="choosing-a-metric" /> and
<NoteLink to="metric-confidence-intervals" />. Then read <NoteLink to="classification-metrics" />, since its confusion
matrix and its precision and recall reappear in the curve, ranking, clustering, detection and text metrics. The
probabilistic metrics follow, then the group-fairness metrics of <NoteLink to="group-fairness-metrics" />, which compare
classification metrics across groups. The regression, forecasting, correlation and distance topics form a second
branch for real-valued outputs. The topics on rankings, clusterings, images, generated samples, text and audio can be
read in any order after these, each on its own.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The metrics assume <NoteLink to="expectation" /> and basic probability. Choosing a model or hyperparameters by a metric
is the subject of <NoteLink to="model-selection" />. Turning scores into decisions under costs, and the theory of proper
scoring rules and calibration, are in <NoteLink to="decision-theory-and-calibration" />. Many metrics are also training
objectives, and <NoteLink to="loss-functions" /> treats them in that role. In Part VII,
<NoteLink to="recommendation-metrics" /> adapts the ranking metrics to recommenders, and the
<NoteLink to="a-b-test" /> measures a metric on live traffic.
