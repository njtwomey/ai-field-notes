<Definition>
**Clustering metrics** score a partition of $n$ items into clusters. **External** metrics compare it with a reference
partition, such as true class labels. **Internal** metrics use only the data and the partition, comparing how tight
the clusters are with how far apart they lie.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Clustering has no single correct answer, so its evaluation answers two separate questions. When reference labels exist,
as in a benchmark, external metrics ask how well the clustering recovers them. Cluster labels are arbitrary, so these
metrics must not depend on which cluster is called 1 and which 2. When no labels exist, internal metrics compare
candidate clusterings of the same data, most often to choose the <NoteLink to="choosing-the-number-of-clusters">number
of clusters</NoteLink> $k$.

## Main ideas

**Scoring without labels.** <NoteLink to="internal-clustering-indices" /> covers the silhouette, Calinski–Harabasz,
Davies–Bouldin and Dunn indices. Each is a ratio of separation between clusters to spread within them. Each also
assumes a notion of cluster shape, usually compact and convex, so an index can prefer a wrong clustering of elongated
or nested clusters.

**Comparing partitions by pairs of items.** Two clusterings agree on a pair of items when both put the pair together
or both keep it apart. <NoteLink to="rand-index-and-adjusted-rand-index" /> counts these agreements. The raw Rand index
stays high for random labels, so the adjusted Rand index subtracts its expected value under random labelling with
fixed cluster sizes <Cite id="hubert1985" />. The <NoteLink to="fowlkes-mallows-index" /> uses only pairs put together,
as the geometric mean of pairwise precision and recall.

**Comparing partitions by information.** <NoteLink to="information-theoretic-clustering-metrics" /> treats the two
cluster labels of an item as two <NoteLink to="random-variable">random variables</NoteLink> and measures their mutual
information. Normalised mutual information (NMI) divides it by a mean of the two entropies, and V-measure is the
harmonic mean of homogeneity and completeness. Adjusted mutual information (AMI) subtracts the value expected by chance,
which otherwise grows with the number of clusters <Cite id="vinh2010" />.

**Chance correction.** Unadjusted external scores increase with the number of clusters even for random partitions.
Comparisons across different numbers of clusters should use the adjusted Rand index or AMI.

## How the notes fit together

The internal indices stand alone. Among the external metrics, start with the Rand index, since the Fowlkes–Mallows
index is defined from the same pair counts. The information-theoretic note is independent of the pair-counting notes
and requires <NoteLink to="mutual-information" />.

## Prerequisites and next steps

Pair counting reuses the true and <Gloss name="fp">false positives</Gloss> of the <NoteLink to="confusion-matrix" />,
applied to pairs of items rather than items. The clustering algorithms these metrics evaluate, such as
<NoteLink to="k-means" />, are in Part V. Scores for a single partition against class labels with known correspondence
are ordinary <NoteLink to="classification-metrics" />.
