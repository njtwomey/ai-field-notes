<Definition>
**Text metrics** score a candidate text produced by a model against one or more reference texts written by people, or,
for perplexity, against the probability the model assigns to held-out text. Most compare the two texts by the units they
share: words, word sequences ($n$-grams), characters or contextual embeddings of tokens.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Speech recognition, <Gloss name="machine-translation">machine translation</Gloss>, summarisation and
<Gloss name="qa">question answering</Gloss> produce text that has many acceptable forms. Human judgement of every output
is slow, so each task adopted an automatic score that correlates with human ratings well enough to compare systems
during development. The scores are tied to their tasks: BLEU to translation <Cite id="papineni2002" />, ROUGE to
summarisation <Cite id="lin2004" />, word error rate to speech recognition. A score that works for one task can mislead
on another.

## Main ideas

**Edit distances.** <NoteLink to="word-and-character-error-rates" /> counts the substitutions, deletions and insertions
that turn a transcript into the reference, divided by the reference length. <NoteLink to="translation-edit-rate" />
adds block shifts, so moving a phrase costs one edit, which suits translation, where word order varies.

**Overlap of $n$-grams.** <NoteLink to="bleu" /> takes the geometric mean of clipped $n$-gram precisions for $n = 1$
to 4 and multiplies it by a brevity penalty. <NoteLink to="rouge" /> asks the opposite question, how much of the
reference the candidate recovers, so it is reported mainly as recall. <NoteLink to="chrf" /> computes an $F_\beta$
score on character $n$-grams, which gives partial credit for inflected and compound words.

**Answer matching.** <NoteLink to="squad-exact-match-and-f1" /> scores short extracted answers by exact match after
normalisation, and by the $F_1$ score of their bags of tokens, taking the best over several gold answers.

**Similarity in embedding space.** Surface overlap gives no credit for paraphrase. <NoteLink to="bertscore" /> embeds
both texts with a pretrained transformer and matches each token to its most similar token in the other text, which
gives a precision, a recall and their harmonic mean.

**Likelihood instead of references.** <NoteLink to="perplexity-as-a-metric" /> is the exponential of a
<Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss>'s average negative log-likelihood per token. It needs no reference
outputs, but it depends on the tokeniser, so models are comparable only under the same tokenisation or a per-byte
normalisation.

## How the notes fit together

Start with word and character error rates, which define the edit-distance view. BLEU, chrF, translation edit rate and
ROUGE form the reference-overlap family and can be read in any order after them. Exact match and token $F_1$ apply
the precision and recall of <NoteLink to="precision-recall-and-f-score" /> to answers. BERTScore and perplexity are
the two model-based scores and come last.
