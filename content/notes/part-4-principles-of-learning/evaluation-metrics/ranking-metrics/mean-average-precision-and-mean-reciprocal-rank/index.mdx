<Definition>
For one query with $R$ relevant items in the collection, a ranked list of $n$ retrieved items and relevance indicators
$\text{rel}_k$ at rank $k$, the <Gloss name="ap">average precision</Gloss> is <Cite id="manning2008" />

$$
\text{AP} = \frac{1}{R}\sum_{k=1}^{n} P@k \cdot \text{rel}_k .
$$

Mean average precision (MAP) averages AP over queries. The reciprocal rank of a query is $1/r$, where $r$ is the rank
of the first relevant item (0 if none is retrieved), and mean reciprocal rank (MRR) averages it over queries
<Cite id="voorhees1999" />.
</Definition>

## Average precision

The sum visits each relevant item once, at the rank where it appears, and adds the precision of the prefix that ends
there. A relevant item near the top has a high precision prefix; one near the bottom has a low one. Relevant items
that are never retrieved contribute 0 to the sum but still count in the divisor $R$. AP is therefore 1 exactly when
every relevant item is retrieved above every non-relevant one.

AP is the <Gloss name="auprc">area under the precision–recall curve</Gloss> of the ranking, computed as a step function.
Each relevant item raises recall by $1/R$, and the sum multiplies that step width by the precision at the step:
$\text{AP} = \sum_k P@k \, \Delta R@k$ with $\Delta R@k = \text{rel}_k / R$. It avoids choosing a cut-off, because every
relevant item's rank enters the score.

**Worked example.** The relevant items are at ranks 1, 3, 4 and 7, and one relevant document was not retrieved
($R = 5$):

$$
\text{AP} = \frac{1}{5}\paren{\frac{1}{1} + \frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{4} + \frac{4}{7}} = \frac{2.988}{5} = 0.598 .
$$

If the missed document were ignored and $R$ taken as the 4 retrieved relevant items, the same ranking would score
$2.988/4 = 0.747$. That second number is what `sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score` returns when given only the
retrieved list, because it cannot know about documents it never sees. The difference is the penalty for incomplete
recall.

## Mean reciprocal rank

MRR looks only at the first relevant item. It suits tasks with one right answer, or where the user stops at the first
useful result: <Gloss name="qa">question answering</Gloss>, known-item search, and next-item recommendation. It is a
harmonic-style average of ranks, so it rewards moving the first hit from rank 2 to rank 1 (a gain of 0.5) far more than
from rank 10 to rank 9 (a gain of about 0.011).

**Worked example.** Three queries have their first relevant item at ranks 1, 3 and 2:

$$
\text{MRR} = \frac{1}{3}\paren{1 + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2}} = 0.611 .
$$

## Choosing between them

| | AP / MAP | MRR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uses | Every relevant item's rank | Only the first relevant item |
| Suits | Many relevant items, recall matters | One answer, or the user stops at the first hit |
| Needs $R$ | Yes | No |
| Graded relevance | No | No |

Neither uses graded relevance; <NoteLink to="normalised-discounted-cumulative-gain">normalised discounted cumulative
gain (NDCG)</NoteLink> does.

The two can disagree about the same pair of rankings. List A has relevant items at ranks 1 and 36; list B has them at
ranks 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12 and 15; the collection has $R = 8$ relevant items. A's reciprocal rank is 1 and B's is
$1/3$, so MRR prefers A. Average precision prefers B:

$$
\text{AP}_A = \frac{1}{8}\paren{\frac{1}{1} + \frac{2}{36}} = 0.132, \qquad
\text{AP}_B = \frac{1}{8}\paren{\frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{4} + \frac{3}{5} + \frac{4}{6} + \frac{5}{8} + \frac{6}{9} + \frac{7}{12} + \frac{8}{15}} = 0.564 .
$$

A user who wants one answer is better served by A; a user who needs all eight relevant items is better served by B.

## Pitfalls

- **"mAP" means different things.** In information retrieval it is the mean of AP over queries. In
  <NoteLink to="object-detection">object detection</NoteLink> it is the mean of an interpolated AP over classes, and
  often over <Gloss name="iou" /> thresholds too. See <NoteLink to="mean-average-precision-for-detection">mean average
  precision for detection</NoteLink>.
- **Unjudged documents.** Pooled evaluations treat unjudged documents as non-relevant, which lowers AP for systems that
  find relevant documents outside the pool.
- **Ties in scores.** When scores tie, the order is arbitrary and AP depends on how ties are broken. Report the tie
  rule or average over orders.
