<Definition>
**Detection and segmentation metrics** score predicted regions of an image against annotated ones. A detector predicts
bounding boxes with class labels and confidences; a segmenter assigns a class, and sometimes an instance, to every
pixel. The metrics first measure the overlap or distance between a predicted and a true region, then aggregate over
regions, classes and images.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Detection and segmentation outputs are sets of regions, not single labels, so
<NoteLink to="classification-metrics">classification metrics</NoteLink> do not apply directly. A metric must decide when
a predicted box counts as a hit, handle several predictions for one object, and reward accurate boundaries. Benchmarks
such as PASCAL VOC and <Gloss name="coco" /> fixed these choices, and published detectors are compared on their
definitions <Cite id="everingham2010" /> <Cite id="lin2014" />. Medical segmentation adds a concern for the worst
boundary error, since one missed protrusion of a tumour matters clinically.

## Main ideas

**Overlap between two regions.** <NoteLink to="intersection-over-union" /> divides the area of overlap by the area of
the union. IoU is zero for all non-overlapping boxes, so as a training loss it gives no gradient there. GIoU, DIoU and
CIoU add penalties for the enclosing box, the distance between centres and the aspect ratio, which restore a gradient.
<NoteLink to="dice-and-segmentation-overlap" /> covers the Dice coefficient $2\abs{A \cap B}/(\abs{A} + \abs{B})$. Dice
is the $F_1$ score of pixel labels and a monotone function of IoU.

**Distance between boundaries.** Overlap scores are dominated by the interior of large regions. The
<NoteLink to="hausdorff-distance" /> measures the largest distance from a point of either boundary to the other, so it
reports the worst boundary error. Its 95th-percentile version, HD95, is less affected by single outlying pixels.

**From matches to a ranking score.** <NoteLink to="mean-average-precision-for-detection" /> matches predictions to
ground truth by IoU in order of confidence. Each match is a <Gloss name="tp">true positive</Gloss>, so the ranked
predictions give a <Gloss name="pr">precision-recall curve</Gloss> per class. Mean average precision (mAP) averages the
area under its envelope over classes; COCO also averages over IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95.

**One score for every pixel and instance.** <NoteLink to="panoptic-quality" /> scores segmentations that label both
countable objects and amorphous regions. Segments match when their IoU exceeds 0.5. Panoptic quality is the product of
segmentation quality, the mean IoU of matches, and recognition quality, an $F_1$ score over matched segments.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="intersection-over-union" />, which every other note uses to match or compare regions. Dice and
the Hausdorff distance are the two standard segmentation scores, one for overlap and one for boundaries. Mean average
precision applies IoU matching to detection. Panoptic quality combines matching and overlap and comes last.
