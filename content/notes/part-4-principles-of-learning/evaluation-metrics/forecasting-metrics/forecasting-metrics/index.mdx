<Definition>
**Forecasting metrics** score predictions $\hat y_t$ of future values $y_t$ of a time series. They start from the
regression errors $y_t - \hat y_t$ and add what forecasting needs: comparison across many series of different scales,
comparison with a naive benchmark, and scoring of forecast distributions and intervals rather than single values.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A retailer forecasts demand for thousands of products, some selling one unit a week and some ten thousand. Mean
absolute error on such a collection is dominated by the largest series. Forecasting competitions and demand-planning
systems therefore need scale-free scores. Forecasts also feed decisions about stock, staffing and capacity, where an
interval or a full distribution matters more than a point. The metrics here address both needs
<Cite id="hyndman2006" />.

## Main ideas

**Percentages remove the scale and add new problems.** <NoteLink to="percentage-errors" /> divides each absolute error
by $\abs{y_t}$. The mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) is undefined when $y_t = 0$, unbounded for over-forecasts and
minimised by forecasts below the median. The symmetric version sMAPE and the weighted version WMAPE each remove some of
these defects and introduce others.

**A benchmark in the denominator.** <NoteLink to="scaled-errors-and-mean-absolute-scaled-error" /> divides a forecast's
mean absolute error by the in-sample mean absolute error of the seasonal naive forecast $\hat y_t = y_{t-m}$. The
mean absolute scaled error (MASE) is scale-free, defined for series containing zeros, and below 1 when the forecast
beats the naive method's typical one-step error.

**Scoring distributions and intervals.** A probabilistic forecast gives a predictive distribution $F$.
<NoteLink to="continuous-ranked-probability-score-and-interval-scores" /> scores it with the continuous ranked
probability score (CRPS), which is in the units of the data and reduces to absolute error for a point forecast. The
interval score rewards narrow <NoteLink to="prediction-intervals">prediction intervals</NoteLink> and penalises
observations that fall outside them. Both are proper scoring rules <Cite id="gneiting2007" />.
