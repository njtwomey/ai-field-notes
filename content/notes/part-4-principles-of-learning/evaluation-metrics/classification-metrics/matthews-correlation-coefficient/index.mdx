<Definition>
For a binary <NoteLink to="confusion-matrix">confusion matrix</NoteLink>,

$$
\text{MCC} = \frac{\text{TP}\cdot\text{TN} - \text{FP}\cdot\text{FN}}
{\sqrt{(\text{TP} + \text{FP})(\text{TP} + \text{FN})(\text{TN} + \text{FP})(\text{TN} + \text{FN})}}.
$$

It is the <NoteLink to="pearson-correlation">Pearson correlation</NoteLink> between the true label and the predicted
label, each coded 0 or 1. It equals 1 for perfect prediction, 0 for a classifier no better than chance, and $-1$ for
perfect disagreement <Cite id="matthews1975" />.
</Definition>

## A correlation of two binary variables

Code the truth as $y \in \set{0, 1}$ and the prediction as $\hat y \in \set{0, 1}$. Over $n$ cases, the Pearson
correlation is $\cov(y, \hat y)/\sqrt{\var(y)\var(\hat y)}$. For 0/1 variables, $\expect[y\hat y] = \text{TP}/n$,
$\expect[y] = (\text{TP} + \text{FN})/n$ and $\expect[\hat y] = (\text{TP} + \text{FP})/n$, so

$$
n^2 \cov(y, \hat y) = n\,\text{TP} - (\text{TP} + \text{FN})(\text{TP} + \text{FP}) = \text{TP}\cdot\text{TN} - \text{FP}\cdot\text{FN},
$$

using $n = \text{TP} + \text{FN} + \text{FP} + \text{TN}$. The variances are $n^2\var(y) = P \cdot N =
(\text{TP} + \text{FN})(\text{FP} + \text{TN})$ and similarly for $\hat y$, which gives the definition. The same
quantity is the phi coefficient of the $2 \times 2$ table in statistics, and $n\,\text{MCC}^2$ is its $\chi^2$
statistic.

Because it is a correlation, MCC is symmetric in its two arguments and unchanged when both the positive and negative
labels are swapped.

## The geometric mean of informedness and markedness

Informedness $B = \text{TPR} + \text{TNR} - 1$ measures how the prediction depends on the truth; markedness
$M = \text{PPV} + \text{NPV} - 1$ measures how the truth depends on the prediction. Powers showed
$\text{MCC}^2 = B \cdot M$ <Cite id="powers2011" />. To see it, write
$a = \text{TP}\cdot\text{TN} - \text{FP}\cdot\text{FN}$ and put each over a common denominator:

$$
B = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}} - \frac{\text{FP}}{\text{FP} + \text{TN}}
= \frac{a}{(\text{TP} + \text{FN})(\text{FP} + \text{TN})}, \qquad
M = \frac{a}{(\text{TP} + \text{FP})(\text{FN} + \text{TN})}.
$$

Their product is $a^2$ over the product of all four marginals, which is $\text{MCC}^2$. So MCC is high only if both
directions of prediction are informative, and it has the sign of $a$.

## Worked example

On the screening data (<Gloss name="tp" /> = 40, <Gloss name="fn" /> = 10, <Gloss name="fp" /> = 90, <Gloss name="tn" /> =
860):

$$
\text{MCC} = \frac{40 \cdot 860 - 90 \cdot 10}{\sqrt{130 \cdot 50 \cdot 950 \cdot 870}}
= \frac{33{,}500}{73{,}296} = 0.457.
$$

Informedness is $0.705$ and markedness $0.296$, and $\sqrt{0.705 \times 0.296} = 0.457$. Accuracy (0.900) and
<NoteLink to="accuracy-and-balanced-accuracy">balanced accuracy</NoteLink> (0.853) look better because they do not
register how few of the flagged cases are positive; $F_1$ (0.444) registers that but not the true negatives. The
classifier that predicts negative for everyone has an undefined MCC, because $\text{TP} + \text{FP} = 0$; by convention
it is set to 0, the chance value.

## Multiclass MCC

For $K$ classes, the definition generalises through the correlation of one-hot label vectors. With confusion matrix
$\Cmat$, row sums $t_k$ (true counts), column sums $p_k$ (predicted counts), $c = \sum_k C_{kk}$ correct predictions
and $n$ cases,

$$
\text{MCC} = \frac{c\,n - \sum_k p_k t_k}{\sqrt{\paren{n^2 - \sum_k p_k^2}\paren{n^2 - \sum_k t_k^2}}}.
$$

For $K = 2$ this reduces to the binary formula. Its minimum is between $-1$ and $0$, depending on the class
distribution. The three-class example in <NoteLink to="averaging-multiclass-metrics">averaging multiclass
metrics</NoteLink> has multiclass MCC 0.507.

## Pitfalls

- **Undefined margins.** Any empty row or column of the binary table makes MCC undefined. Libraries return 0.
- **Not a probability or a rate.** MCC has no direct operational meaning such as "fraction correct". It is best used to
  rank classifiers on the same data, and reported alongside interpretable rates.
- **Chicco and Jurman argue for MCC as the default binary metric** because it is high only when all four cells are good
  <Cite id="chicco2020" />. It still weighs false positives and false negatives by the table's structure, not by their
  costs; with known costs, use expected cost instead.
