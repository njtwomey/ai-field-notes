<Definition>
**Audio and speech metrics** score an audio signal produced by a model, usually against a clean reference recording of
the same sound. They range from energy ratios computed sample by sample to models of human hearing, and to networks
that predict listener ratings with no reference at all.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Speech enhancement removes noise and reverberation, source separation splits a mixture into its sources, and codecs and
text-to-speech systems produce audio from compact inputs. Each needs a score for model selection that tracks what a
listener hears. Listening tests give mean opinion scores, but they are slow and costly, so the metrics here stand in
for them during development.

## Main ideas

**Energy ratios.** <NoteLink to="signal-to-noise-and-signal-to-distortion-ratios" /> compares an estimate with its
reference in decibels. The plain signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) counts a wrong overall gain as error. The scale-invariant
signal-to-distortion ratio (SI-SDR) first rescales the reference to best match the estimate, which makes it the standard
source-separation metric <Cite id="leroux2019" />.

**Matching outputs to sources.** A separation model returns its sources in no fixed order.
<NoteLink to="permutation-invariant-training" /> matches outputs to references by the assignment that minimises the
loss, found by enumeration or the Hungarian algorithm, and applies the same matching in training and evaluation.

**Models of hearing.** <NoteLink to="perceptual-speech-quality-and-intelligibility" /> covers two standard intrusive
metrics. PESQ aligns the degraded and clean signals and passes them through an auditory model to predict perceived
quality. STOI predicts intelligibility from the correlation of short-time band envelopes <Cite id="taal2011" />. Quality
and intelligibility can disagree: a heavily processed signal can sound unnatural and still be understood.

**Scores without a reference.** Recordings made in the field have no clean original.
<NoteLink to="learned-speech-quality-metrics" /> covers DNSMOS and NISQA, networks trained to predict crowdsourced mean
opinion scores, and SRMR, a signal-based measure of reverberation.

## How the notes fit together

Start with SNR and SI-SDR, which the permutation-invariant note requires. PESQ and STOI replace energy ratios with
perceptual models and still need a reference. The non-intrusive metrics drop the reference and come last.
