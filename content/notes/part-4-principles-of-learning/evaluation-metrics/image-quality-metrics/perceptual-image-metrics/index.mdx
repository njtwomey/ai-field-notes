<Definition>
A full-reference perceptual image metric scores a distorted image $y$ against a reference $x$ so that the score orders
pairs of images the way people do. The main families are statistical indices such as the universal quality index (UQI)
<Cite id="wang2002" />, information-theoretic measures such as visual information fidelity (VIF)
<Cite id="sheikh2006" />, and deep-feature distances such as learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS)
<Cite id="zhang2018" /> and DISTS <Cite id="ding2020" />.
</Definition>

## Universal quality index

UQI is the predecessor of <NoteLink to="structural-similarity-index">SSIM</NoteLink>: the same product of luminance,
contrast and structure comparisons, but without the stabilising constants,

$$
Q = \frac{4\sigma_{xy}\mu_x\mu_y}{(\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2)(\mu_x^2 + \mu_y^2)} .
$$

It is undefined for flat patches, where the denominators vanish, which is what SSIM's $C_1$ and $C_2$ fix. For the
patches $x = (50, 60, 70, 80)$ and $y = (52, 58, 75, 79)$, $Q = 0.970$, slightly below their SSIM of $0.975$.

## Visual information fidelity

VIF treats the reference as the output of a natural-image source passed through a model of the visual system, and the
distorted image as the same source passed through a distortion channel and then the visual system. It is the ratio of
the information the viewer can extract from the distorted image to the information they can extract from the
reference, computed with a Gaussian scale-mixture model in a wavelet domain. VIF is 1 for identical images, falls toward
0 with distortion, and can exceed 1 for contrast enhancement that adds visible information.

## LPIPS

LPIPS passes both images through a network trained for <NoteLink to="image-classification">image
classification</NoteLink> (AlexNet, <Gloss name="vgg" /> or SqueezeNet), normalises each layer's activations to unit
length across channels, and compares them:

$$
d(x, y) = \sum_{l} \frac{1}{H_l W_l}\sum_{h, w} \norm{\wvec_l \odot \paren{\hat{\yvec}^{\,l}_{hw}(x) - \hat{\yvec}^{\,l}_{hw}(y)}}^2 .
$$

The per-channel weights $\wvec_l$ are fitted to a dataset of human two-alternative forced-choice judgments. Lower is
better. Deep features turn out to predict human similarity judgments far better than <Gloss name="psnr" /> or SSIM,
including for distortions such as warps that pixel metrics handle badly.

## DISTS

DISTS also compares deep features, but splits the comparison into structure (correlation of feature maps) and texture
(their means and variances), in the spirit of SSIM. Because texture is compared through statistics rather than
position, DISTS tolerates resampled textures such as grass or hair, which look the same to a viewer but differ pixel by
pixel.

## Pitfalls

- **Backbone dependence.** LPIPS values from different networks are not comparable; report the backbone and version.
- **Adversarial exploitation.** A model trained to minimise a learned metric can find images that the metric scores
  as similar to the reference but that people judge as dissimilar. Evaluate with a different metric from the one used
  for training.
- **Domain shift.** Learned metrics are fitted to natural photographs; on medical, satellite or synthetic images their
  agreement with human judgment is unknown.
