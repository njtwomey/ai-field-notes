<Definition>
**Image quality metrics** score how close an image produced by a system is to a reference image of the same scene.
Compression, denoising, super-resolution, inpainting and image restoration all produce such pairs. A good metric agrees
with how people judge the difference between the two images.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Image processing models are trained and compared on paired data: a degraded input, the model's output and the clean
original. Human ratings of every output are too slow and costly for training and model selection, so a metric stands
in for them. The metrics in this topic are all full-reference: they need the clean original. Scores for generated
images with no reference belong to <NoteLink to="generative-model-metrics" />.

## Main ideas

**Pixel error on a log scale.** <NoteLink to="peak-signal-to-noise-ratio" /> expresses the mean squared error between
two images in decibels relative to the largest pixel value. PSNR is simple and universally reported, but it treats
every pixel independently. A blur and a small shift of the whole image can have the same PSNR and look very different.

**Local structure.** <NoteLink to="structural-similarity-index" /> compares two images window by window through their
local means, contrasts and correlation <Cite id="wang2004" />. It separates distortions that PSNR scores identically,
such as a change of brightness and a loss of edges.

**Perception, by design or by training.** <NoteLink to="perceptual-image-metrics" /> covers two generations of
metrics. The universal quality index (UQI) and visual information fidelity (VIF) are designed from image statistics and
information theory. LPIPS and DISTS compare features from a deep network and are calibrated or trained on human
similarity judgements <Cite id="zhang2018" />. Learned metrics agree best with people on the distortions they were
trained on.

**Images with many bands.** Satellite images have several spectral bands per pixel.
<NoteLink to="remote-sensing-image-metrics" /> scores them per pixel spectrum, with the spectral angle mapper (SAM),
and per band, with ERGAS and RASE. QNR scores pansharpening without a reference.

## How the notes fit together

Start with PSNR, since SSIM and the perceptual metrics are each motivated by a failure of the one before. The
remote-sensing note applies the same ideas to multispectral data and can be read after PSNR.
