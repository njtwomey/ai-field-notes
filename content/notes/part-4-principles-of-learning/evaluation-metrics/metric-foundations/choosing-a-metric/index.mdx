<Definition>
A metric is a function of a model's outputs and the true labels that summarises performance in one number. Choosing
one means choosing which aspect of performance counts: correct decisions at one threshold, the ordering of cases by
score, or the quality of predicted probabilities. Metrics that measure different aspects rank models differently, so
the choice follows from how the model will be used <Cite id="ferri2009" />.
</Definition>

## Three kinds of classification metric

Ferri, Hernández-Orallo and Modroiu found that classification metrics fall into three families that disagree with
each other but agree within a family <Cite id="ferri2009" />:

| family | question it answers | examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| threshold metrics | how good are the decisions at this threshold? | accuracy, $F_1$, <Gloss name="mcc" />, $\kappa$, <NoteLink to="accuracy-and-balanced-accuracy">balanced accuracy</NoteLink> |
| ranking metrics | how well do the scores order positives above negatives? | <Gloss name="auroc" />, <Gloss name="ap">average precision</Gloss> |
| probabilistic metrics | how good are the predicted probabilities? | <NoteLink to="cross-entropy-and-perplexity">log loss</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="log-loss-and-brier-score">Brier score</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="calibration-error">calibration error</NoteLink> |

A model used to make yes/no decisions at a known threshold should be judged by a threshold metric at that threshold.
A model used to rank or to triage, where the threshold is chosen later or varies, should be judged by a ranking metric.
A model whose probabilities feed into further decisions, such as expected-cost calculations, should be judged by a
probabilistic metric, and its calibration checked.

## Questions that decide the choice

1. **Do the errors cost the same?** If <Gloss name="fp">false positives</Gloss> and <Gloss name="fn">false
   negatives</Gloss> have known, different costs, the right metric is the expected cost itself, and the right threshold
   follows from the costs (see <NoteLink to="cost-sensitive-learning">cost-sensitive learning</NoteLink>). Single-number
   metrics such as $F_1$ or MCC encode implicit, fixed trade-offs that rarely match the real costs.
2. **What is the deployment prevalence?** Precision, $F_1$ and average precision depend on it; recall, specificity,
   balanced accuracy and AUROC do not. Evaluate prevalence-dependent metrics at the prevalence the model will face.
3. **Do the true negatives matter?** In retrieval they do not, and precision, recall and $F_1$ fit. In most
   classification problems they do, and MCC or balanced accuracy are safer.
4. **Does every class matter equally?** If so, macro-average; if every case matters equally, micro-average (see
   <NoteLink to="averaging-multiclass-metrics">averaging multiclass metrics</NoteLink>).
5. **Is the metric a sum over cases?** Accuracy, log loss and expected cost are, so they decompose and can be
   optimised directly. AUROC and $F_1$ are not, which makes them awkward to optimise and to average across folds.

## Report baselines and uncertainty

Every metric needs a reference point. Report what a trivial classifier scores: the majority class for accuracy, the
prevalence for precision and average precision, 0.5 for AUROC and balanced accuracy, the
<NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink> of the base rate for log loss. Report a
<NoteLink to="metric-confidence-intervals">confidence interval</NoteLink>, which is wide for any metric that conditions
on a rare class.

## Pitfalls

- **Goodhart's law.** A metric that becomes the target is optimised at the expense of what it was meant to measure.
  Keep the evaluation metric tied to the use, and check several.
- **Ranking metrics hide the threshold.** A model with a high AUROC can still make bad decisions at the threshold
  actually used. Hand argued that AUROC also implies a different cost distribution for different classifiers, and
  proposed the H-measure as a coherent alternative <Cite id="hand2009" />.
- **Metric and loss need not match.** A model trained with cross-entropy is often evaluated with accuracy or $F_1$.
  Tune the threshold for the evaluation metric on validation data.
