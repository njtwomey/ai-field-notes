<Definition>
Draw $n = 50$ training rows with $d = 20$ inputs $\xvec \sim \Gauss(\boldsymbol 0, \eye_{20})$ and target
$y = \xvec^\top\betavec + \varepsilon$, where $\beta_j = 2 \cdot 0.75^{j - 1}$ for $j = 1, \dots, 20$ and $\varepsilon
\sim \Gauss(0, 2^2)$. Fit <NoteLink to="ridge-regression">ridge regression</NoteLink> with an unpenalised intercept at
25 penalties $\lambda$ spaced evenly in $\log\lambda$ from 0.1 to 1000. Estimate the prediction error at each $\lambda$
by 10-fold <NoteLink to="cross-validation">cross-validation</NoteLink>, by leave-one-out cross-validation and by
generalised cross-validation, choose $\lambda$ by each, and apply the one-standard-error rule to the 10-fold curve.
Compare every choice with the true error of the fitted model, measured on 20 000 fresh rows.
</Definition>

## Setup

The ridge fit at penalty $\lambda$ minimises $\norm{\yvec - b\ones - \Xmat\wvec}^2 + \lambda\norm{\wvec}^2$ over the
intercept $b$ and the coefficients $\wvec \in \reals^{20}$. Its fitted values are linear in $\yvec$,
$\hat\yvec = \Smat_\lambda\yvec$, with the smoother matrix

$$
\Smat_\lambda = \frac1n\ones\ones^\top + \Xmat_c\paren{\Xmat_c^\top\Xmat_c + \lambda\eye}^{-1}\Xmat_c^\top,
$$

where $\Xmat_c$ is the design with each column centred. The first term fits the intercept; the second is the ridge fit
to the centred data. Its trace is $1 + \operatorname{df}(\lambda)$, with
$\operatorname{df}(\lambda) = \sum_j d_j^2/(d_j^2 + \lambda)$ from the singular values $d_j$ of $\Xmat_c$ (see
<NoteLink to="effective-degrees-of-freedom">effective degrees of freedom</NoteLink>).

The quantities asked for, all with squared-error loss:

- $\operatorname{CV}_{10}(\lambda)$, the mean of the 10 fold errors $e_k(\lambda)$, and its
  <NoteLink to="standard-error">standard error</NoteLink> $\operatorname{SE}(\lambda) = s(\lambda)/\sqrt{10}$, with
  $s(\lambda)$ the standard deviation of the fold errors;
- $\operatorname{LOO}(\lambda) = \frac1n\sum_i \paren{(y_i - \hat y_i)/(1 - S_{ii})}^2$, the leave-one-out error, which
  for a linear smoother needs one fit (see <NoteLink to="leave-one-out-cross-validation">leave-one-out
  cross-validation</NoteLink>);
- $\operatorname{GCV}(\lambda) = n\norm{\yvec - \hat\yvec}^2/(n - \trace\Smat_\lambda)^2$, which replaces each
  leverage $S_{ii}$ by the average $\trace\Smat_\lambda/n$ (see
  <NoteLink to="generalised-cross-validation">generalised cross-validation</NoteLink>) <Cite id="craven1979" />;
- the true conditional error $\operatorname{Err}_\Dcal(\lambda)$ of the ridge fit to all 50 rows.

The minimisers are $\hat\lambda_\text{CV}$, $\hat\lambda_\text{LOO}$ and $\hat\lambda_\text{GCV}$. The
<NoteLink to="one-standard-error-rule">one-standard-error rule</NoteLink> picks $\hat\lambda_\text{1SE}$, the largest
$\lambda$ with $\operatorname{CV}_{10}(\lambda) \le \operatorname{CV}_{10}(\hat\lambda_\text{CV}) +
\operatorname{SE}(\hat\lambda_\text{CV})$ <Cite id="breiman1984" /> <Cite id="hastie2009" />. A larger $\lambda$ is a
simpler model: fewer effective degrees of freedom.

## Solution

All numbers below are for the data set with seed 1 in the figure.

**Step 1: the 10-fold curve.** Each fold fits on 45 rows and scores the other 5. Part of the curve:

| $\lambda$ | $\operatorname{df}$ | $\operatorname{CV}_{10}$ | $\operatorname{SE}$ | $\operatorname{LOO}$ | $\operatorname{GCV}$ | $\operatorname{Err}_\Dcal$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.1 | 19.93 | 6.79 | 1.07 | 5.89 | 5.64 | 5.92 |
| 1 | 19.31 | 6.47 | 1.02 | 5.68 | 5.43 | 5.76 |
| 4.64 | 17.31 | 5.98 | 1.07 | 5.38 | 5.04 | 5.45 |
| 6.81 | 16.37 | 5.93 | 1.14 | 5.39 | 5.00 | 5.40 |
| 10 | 15.22 | 5.99 | 1.24 | 5.50 | 5.06 | 5.42 |
| 21.5 | 12.36 | 6.49 | 1.49 | 6.14 | 5.62 | 5.89 |
| 31.6 | 10.72 | 6.94 | 1.61 | 6.67 | 6.16 | 6.41 |
| 46.4 | 9.04 | 7.49 | 1.72 | 7.30 | 6.83 | 7.11 |
| 100 | 5.90 | 8.74 | 1.91 | 8.68 | 8.35 | 8.84 |
| 1000 | 0.91 | 11.53 | 2.29 | 11.59 | 11.55 | 12.51 |

The minimum is at $\hat\lambda_\text{CV} = 6.81$, with $\operatorname{CV}_{10} = 5.93$ and $\operatorname{SE} = 1.14$.

**Step 2: leave-one-out.** Each leave-one-out fit uses 49 rows, so its models are closer to the 50-row model than the
45-row models of 10-fold. The curve lies below the 10-fold curve by 0.9 at $\lambda = 0.1$ and by 0.5 near the minimum:
the gap is largest where the fit has the most effective parameters and gains most from 4 extra rows. Refitting 50 times
at each $\lambda$ and the closed form with $S_{ii}$ give the same numbers. The minimum is at
$\hat\lambda_\text{LOO} = 4.64$, with $\operatorname{LOO} = 5.38$; at 6.81 it is 5.39, so the two penalties are nearly
tied.

**Step 3: generalised cross-validation.** At $\lambda = 6.81$ the residual sum of squares is 106.5 and
$\trace\Smat_\lambda = 1 + 16.37 = 17.37$. Then

$$
\operatorname{GCV}(6.81) = \frac{50 \times 106.5}{(50 - 17.37)^2} = \frac{5325}{1064.7} = 5.00.
$$

GCV is minimised at $\hat\lambda_\text{GCV} = 6.81$. It sits below leave-one-out at every $\lambda$ here. The factor
$1/(1 - S_{ii})^2$ is convex in the leverage, so applying it to the average leverage gives less than averaging it over
the points, unless the large residuals sit at small leverages.

**Step 4: the one-standard-error rule.** The threshold is $5.93 + 1.14 = 7.07$. The largest $\lambda$ with
$\operatorname{CV}_{10} \le 7.07$ is $31.6$ ($\operatorname{CV}_{10} = 6.94$); the next, $46.4$, has 7.49. So
$\hat\lambda_\text{1SE} = 31.6$, with $\operatorname{df} = 10.7$ against 16.4 at the minimum.

**Step 5: against the truth.** The true error is smallest at $\lambda = 6.81$, where it is 5.40. The 10-fold and GCV
choices hit it exactly; leave-one-out's choice, 4.64, costs 0.05. The one-standard-error choice has true error 6.41,
about 19% worse. The rule bought a model with six fewer effective parameters at the price of a clearly larger error.

**Step 6: the estimates themselves.** At its minimum, $\operatorname{CV}_{10} = 5.93$ overstates the true 5.40 by
0.53, about half a standard error, and $\operatorname{GCV} = 5.00$ understates it by 0.40. The estimates scatter
around the truth by amounts comparable to the standard error, and the minimum of a curve is biased low as an estimate
of the chosen model's error (see <NoteLink to="overfitting-in-model-selection">overfitting in model
selection</NoteLink>).

## Simulation

<RidgePenaltyCv />

Across 100 data sets drawn the same way (seeds 1 to 100), the average true error of each choice was:

| Choice | Mean true error |
| --- | --- |
| best $\lambda$ on the grid (oracle) | 6.02 |
| 10-fold minimum | 6.35 |
| leave-one-out minimum | 6.33 |
| GCV minimum | 6.32 |
| one-standard-error rule | 7.18 |

The three minimisers are almost interchangeable, each about 5% above the oracle. The one-standard-error rule was better
than the 10-fold minimum on 22 of the 100 data sets and worse on average by 0.83.

## What it illustrates

- **Three estimators, one answer.** For a linear smoother, <NoteLink to="k-fold-cross-validation">$K$-fold
  cross-validation</NoteLink>, leave-one-out and GCV are different estimates of the same curve, and their minimisers
  agree to within a grid step or two. Leave-one-out and GCV cost one fit per $\lambda$; 10-fold costs ten.
- **The bias of $K$-fold.** Fold models trained on $n(K - 1)/K$ rows predict worse than the final model, so the
  10-fold curve lies above leave-one-out, most where the model is most flexible.
- **Standard errors are wide.** One standard error, about 1.1, is larger than the whole variation of the curve between
  $\lambda = 1$ and $\lambda = 21.5$. The data cannot locate the best penalty precisely, which is the motivation for
  the one-standard-error rule. Even this standard error is too small; see
  <NoteLink to="variance-of-cross-validation-estimates">variance of cross-validation estimates</NoteLink>.
- **The one-standard-error rule is a preference, not a correction.** It buys a simpler model. When the truth has many
  small coefficients, as here, the extra shrinkage costs accuracy. When simplicity has value of its own, or the minimum
  is unstable across resamples, the trade can be worth it.
- **The curve tracks expected, not conditional, error.** How close each estimate comes to this data set's true curve
  changes from seed to seed; see <NoteLink to="what-cross-validation-estimates">what cross-validation
  estimates</NoteLink>.

## Variations

- **More folds.** With $K = 20$ the same data give $\operatorname{CV}_{20}(0.1) = 5.95$ against leave-one-out's 5.89:
  each fold model trains on 47.5 rows on average, and the curve moves most of the way to leave-one-out.
- **More data.** With $n = 200$ (seed 1), the three curves agree to within 0.04 at small $\lambda$, the standard error
  at the minimum falls to 0.40, and every minimiser lands within two grid steps of the true best penalty.
- **Other <NoteLink to="regularisation">regularisation</NoteLink> selectors.** The evidence, the L-curve and the
  discrepancy principle choose $\lambda$ without holding data out; see
  <NoteLink to="choosing-the-regularisation-strength">choosing the regularisation strength</NoteLink>.
