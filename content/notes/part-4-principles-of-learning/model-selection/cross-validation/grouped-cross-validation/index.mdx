<Definition>
Let each record $i$ carry a group label $g(i) \in \set{1, \dots, G}$: the patient, user, device or site it comes from.
**Grouped cross-validation** assigns whole groups to folds, so that every group lies in exactly one fold and no group
contributes records to both a training set and its test fold. **Group $K$-fold** forms $K$ folds of groups;
**leave-one-group-out** uses $K = G$, one group per fold. The estimate is computed as in
<NoteLink to="k-fold-cross-validation">$K$-fold cross-validation</NoteLink>.
</Definition>

## Problem

Records of one group resemble each other more than records of different groups: several scans of one patient share
the patient's anatomy, many sessions of one user share the user's habits. Random folds put some of a group's records in
training and others in the test fold. A flexible model can then predict a test record from its siblings, by recognising
the group, and score well without learning anything that carries over to a new group.

Whether that is wrong depends on what will be new in use. If the model will meet new records of the groups it was
trained on, record-wise folds imitate deployment. If it will meet new groups, as a diagnostic model meets new patients,
record-wise folds are optimistic and grouped folds imitate deployment (see
<NoteLink to="matching-the-split-to-deployment">matching the split to deployment</NoteLink>). In a comparison on
wearable-sensor data and a simulation, record-wise cross-validation often massively overestimated the accuracy that
subject-wise cross-validation reported, and almost half of the clinical prediction studies reviewed alongside had used
the record-wise method <Cite id="saeb2017" />.

## Mechanism

1. **Choose the grouping variable**: the unit that will be new in use. For a model deployed to new patients at the same
   hospitals, the patient; for a model deployed to new hospitals, the hospital.
2. **Assign groups to folds.** Group $K$-fold balances the folds by number of records: scikit-learn's `GroupKFold`
   takes groups largest first and puts each into the fold with the fewest records so far. Leave-one-group-out gives
   each group its own fold. `GroupShuffleSplit` draws random sets of groups, the grouped form of
   <NoteLink to="repeated-and-monte-carlo-cross-validation">Monte Carlo cross-validation</NoteLink>.
3. **Cross-validate** as usual, with every data-dependent step fitted inside the training folds.
4. **Group the inner loop too.** Hyperparameters tuned by record-wise cross-validation inside a grouped outer loop are
   tuned for the wrong deployment.

## Subject-wise and record-wise splits

A random-intercept model shows how far the two estimates can part. Let record $i$ of group $j$ be

$$
y_{ij} = f(\xvec_{ij}) + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij},
$$

with a group effect $u_j$ of mean zero and variance $\tau^2$, and record noise $\varepsilon_{ij}$ of mean zero and
variance $\sigma^2$, all independent. The **intraclass correlation** $\rho = \tau^2/(\tau^2 + \sigma^2)$ is the
correlation between two records of the same group. Consider a model that knows $f$ and estimates a group's effect by
the mean residual of its $m$ training records, $\hat u_j = u_j + \bar\varepsilon_j$, where $\bar\varepsilon_j$ is the
mean of their $m$ noise terms.

- **A new record of a seen group** is predicted by $f(\xvec) + \hat u_j$. The error is
  $\varepsilon - \bar\varepsilon_j$, a difference of independent terms, with expected square $\sigma^2(1 + 1/m)$.
- **A record of a new group** has no $\hat u_j$, so the prediction is $f(\xvec)$. The error is $u + \varepsilon$, with
  expected square $\tau^2 + \sigma^2$.

Record-wise $K$-fold cross-validation leaves about $m(K-1)/K$ siblings of each test record in training, so it estimates
the first quantity. Grouped cross-validation estimates the second. Their ratio,

$$
\frac{\tau^2 + \sigma^2}{\sigma^2\paren{1 + K/(m(K-1))}} = \frac{1}{(1 - \rho)\paren{1 + K/(m(K-1))}},
$$

grows without bound as $\rho \to 1$: when records of a group are near copies, the record-wise estimate describes
memorisation. When $\rho = 0$ the groups carry no information and the two estimates agree up to the factor for the
missing siblings.

The same correlation sets the precision of a grouped estimate. The variance of a mean over $n = Gm$ records in $G$
groups of $m$ is the independent-records variance multiplied by the **design effect** $1 + (m - 1)\rho$
<Cite id="kish1965" />. The effective number of independent records is $n/(1 + (m-1)\rho)$, which for $\rho$ near one
is close to the number of groups $G$. A grouped estimate on 20 patients with 50 scans each is about as precise as one
on 20 independent scans, not 1,000.

## Worked example

Twenty patients each contribute $m = 6$ records to a regression with group variance $\tau^2 = 1$ and record noise
$\sigma^2 = 0.25$, so $\rho = 1/1.25 = 0.8$. The model learns patient effects, as a flexible model on patient-specific
features can.

1. **Record-wise 5-fold.** Each test record has on average $6 \times 4/5 = 4.8$ siblings in training. The estimated
   <Gloss name="mse">mean squared error</Gloss> is about $0.25 \times (1 + 1/4.8) = 0.30$.
2. **Group 5-fold.** Each test patient is absent from training. The estimate is about $\tau^2 + \sigma^2 = 1.25$.
3. **Deployment to new patients** has error $1.25$. The record-wise estimate is too small by a factor of four.
4. **Precision.** The design effect is $1 + 5 \times 0.8 = 5$, so the 120 records carry the information of about 24
   independent ones, a little more than the 20 patients.

The figure shows the same pattern for classification. With 30 entities of 6 rows, a small spread of rows around each
entity's centre and a 1-nearest-neighbour classifier, record-wise 5-fold cross-validation estimates an accuracy of
0.89 on average over 12 datasets, matching the accuracy of 0.88 on new rows of the entities seen. Group 5-fold and
leave-one-group-out give 0.73 and 0.72, close to the accuracy of 0.71 on new entities.

## Trade-offs

- **Few groups, high variance.** The estimate's precision follows the number of groups. With ten groups,
  leave-one-group-out averages ten numbers, each from one group.
- **Pessimism from fewer groups in training.** Each fold model sees $(K - 1)/K$ of the groups. When groups are few,
  this is the main bias of the grouped estimate.
- **Unequal folds.** Groups of different sizes make folds of different sizes. Averaging over records weights large
  groups more; averaging the per-group scores of leave-one-group-out weights groups equally. Report the one that
  matches how the model will be judged, and look at the spread of per-group scores, which shows the groups the model
  fails on.
- **Stratification and grouping conflict.** Balancing classes across folds while keeping groups whole is possible only
  approximately; scikit-learn's `StratifiedGroupKFold` does it greedily.
- **Other dependence.** Groups are one kind of dependence. Dependence through space or time calls for blocks or
  forward-only splits (see <NoteLink to="spatial-and-blocked-cross-validation">spatial and blocked
  cross-validation</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="time-series-cross-validation">time-series
  cross-validation</NoteLink>). Blocking on random-effect structure is one of the strategies reviewed for ecological
  data, where random folds badly underestimated predictive error <Cite id="roberts2017cv" />.

## Pitfalls

- **Grouping on the wrong level.** Grouping by visit when a patient has several visits still leaks the patient.
  Hierarchies (hospital, patient, visit) are grouped at the level that is new in use.
- **Unknown groups.** Duplicate or near-duplicate records without a shared identifier defeat grouping (see
  <NoteLink to="detecting-leakage">detecting leakage</NoteLink>).
- **Group statistics computed on all data.** A feature such as a patient's mean over all records, or a target encoding
  by user, computed before splitting carries test records into training (see
  <NoteLink to="data-leakage">data leakage</NoteLink>).
- **Record-wise tuning inside a grouped evaluation.** The inner loop of
  <NoteLink to="nested-cross-validation">nested cross-validation</NoteLink> must be grouped as well, or the selected
  hyperparameters favour memorisation.
- **Reporting the record-wise score for a new-group deployment.** It estimates the error on new records of known groups,
  a different quantity.
