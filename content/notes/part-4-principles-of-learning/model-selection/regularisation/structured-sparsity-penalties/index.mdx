<Definition>
A **structured sparsity penalty** is a convex regulariser whose minimisers have zeros, or equalities, in patterns fixed
in advance rather than coefficient by coefficient. The main examples are:

- the **group lasso** $\sum_g \sqrt{p_g}\norm{\wvec_g}_2$, which sets whole groups $\wvec_g$ of $p_g$ coefficients to
  zero together <Cite id="yuan2006" />;
- the **fused lasso** and **total variation** $\sum_i \abs{w_{i+1} - w_i}$, which make neighbouring coefficients equal
  <Cite id="tibshirani2005" /> <Cite id="rudin1992" />;
- the **nuclear norm** $\norm{\Wmat}_* = \sum_k \sigma_k(\Wmat)$, the sum of a matrix's singular values, which sets
  singular values to zero and so makes the matrix low-rank.
</Definition>

## Intuition

The <NoteLink to="lasso">lasso</NoteLink> decides about each coefficient alone. Often the coefficients come with
structure that says which decisions belong together.

- **Groups.** A categorical feature with five levels is encoded by four dummy columns. Keeping two of them and dropping
  two makes no sense: the feature is in or out. The same holds for the basis functions of one input in an additive
  model, or for one gene's measurements across several assays.
- **Order.** Coefficients indexed by time, position along a chromosome or pixel location tend to change in a few
  places and stay constant elsewhere. The useful zeros are in the differences between neighbours, not in the values.
- **Matrices.** In multi-task regression, matrix completion or a recommender's rating matrix, the parameter is a matrix
  that is believed to be driven by a few factors. The useful zeros are in its singular values.

Each structure gets its own norm, built so that its ball has corners exactly at the structured solutions. The geometry
of <NoteLink to="penalised-and-constrained-regularisation">penalised and constrained regularisation</NoteLink> then
does the rest. A loss gradient lands on a corner for an open set of data, so the structure appears exactly, not
approximately.

## Group lasso

Partition the coefficients into groups $g = 1, \dots, G$, with $\wvec_g$ the $p_g$ coefficients of group $g$ and
$\Xmat_g$ their columns. The group lasso solves

$$
\hat\wvec = \argmin_{\wvec}\ \tfrac12\norm{\yvec - \Xmat\wvec}^2 + \lambda\sum_{g=1}^G \sqrt{p_g}\,\norm{\wvec_g}_2.
$$

The Euclidean norm $\norm{\wvec_g}_2$ is not squared. Squared, it would be ridge on the group and never reach zero.
Unsquared, it has a corner at $\wvec_g = \zeros$: its <NoteLink to="subgradients">subdifferential</NoteLink> there is
the whole unit ball $\set{\uvec : \norm\uvec_2 \le 1}$, and away from zero it is the single unit vector
$\wvec_g/\norm{\wvec_g}_2$.

**Block soft thresholding.** Suppose the columns of each group are orthonormal, $\Xmat_g^\top\Xmat_g = \eye$, and let
$\zvec_g = \Xmat_g^\top\rvec_g$, where $\rvec_g$ is the partial residual with group $g$ left out. Holding the other
groups fixed, the problem for $\wvec_g$ is $\frac12\norm{\zvec_g - \wvec_g}^2 + \lambda\sqrt{p_g}\norm{\wvec_g}_2$ plus
a constant. Zero must lie in the subdifferential $\wvec_g - \zvec_g + \lambda\sqrt{p_g}\,\partial\norm{\wvec_g}_2$.

- If $\wvec_g = \zeros$, the condition is $\zvec_g \in \lambda\sqrt{p_g}\set{\uvec : \norm\uvec_2 \le 1}$, that is
  $\norm{\zvec_g}_2 \le \lambda\sqrt{p_g}$.
- If $\wvec_g \ne \zeros$, then $\wvec_g(1 + \lambda\sqrt{p_g}/\norm{\wvec_g}_2) = \zvec_g$. So $\wvec_g$ points along
  $\zvec_g$, and taking norms gives $\norm{\wvec_g}_2 = \norm{\zvec_g}_2 - \lambda\sqrt{p_g}$.

Together,

$$
\hat\wvec_g = \paren{1 - \frac{\lambda\sqrt{p_g}}{\norm{\zvec_g}_2}}_+\zvec_g.
$$

The group is kept or dropped on the strength of its whole vector $\zvec_g$, and a kept group is shrunk towards zero
along its own direction. This is the group's <NoteLink to="proximal-gradient-methods">proximal operator</NoteLink>, and
<NoteLink to="coordinate-descent">block coordinate descent</NoteLink> cycles it over the groups. With groups of size one
it is soft thresholding, and the group lasso is the lasso.

**Why $\sqrt{p_g}$.** If a group carries no signal, $\zvec_g$ is noise with $p_g$ coordinates, and $\norm{\zvec_g}_2^2$
grows like $p_g\sigma^2$. An unweighted threshold would let large groups in more often by chance. The weight
$\sqrt{p_g}$ scales the threshold with the typical size of a null group <Cite id="yuan2006" />.

**Variants.** The **sparse group lasso** adds $\alpha\lambda\norm{\wvec}_1$ to a share $(1 - \alpha)$ of the group
penalty, so that kept groups can also have zeros inside them <Cite id="simon2013" />. Its proximal operator soft
thresholds each coordinate and then block thresholds each group. **Overlapping** and **hierarchical** groups encode
rules such as "an interaction enters only with its main effects" <Cite id="bach2012" />.

## Fused lasso and total variation

For coefficients with an order, penalise the differences between neighbours. **One-dimensional total variation
denoising** fits a signal $\yvec \in \reals^n$ by

$$
\hat\thetavec = \argmin_{\thetavec}\ \tfrac12\norm{\yvec - \thetavec}^2 + \lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\abs{\theta_{i+1} - \theta_i}.
$$

The penalty is the $L^1$ norm of $\Dmat\thetavec$, where $\Dmat$ is the first-difference operator. Its zeros are in
the differences, so the solution is **piecewise constant**: a few jumps, flat in between. Rudin, Osher and Fatemi
introduced it for images, where the jumps are edges <Cite id="rudin1992" />.

**The optimality conditions.** Introduce a dual variable $u_i$ for each difference. The subgradient condition reads
$\theta_j - y_j + u_{j-1} - u_j = 0$ for each $j$, with $u_0 = u_n = 0$, and $u_i = \lambda\sgn(\theta_{i+1} -
\theta_i)$ at a jump, $\abs{u_i} \le \lambda$ where neighbours are equal. Rearranged,

$$
u_j = \sum_{i \le j}(\theta_i - y_i).
$$

So $u_j$ is the running sum of the residuals. It must stay within $[-\lambda, \lambda]$ and touch $\pm\lambda$ at each
jump. This is the **taut string** picture: the cumulative sum of $\hat\thetavec$ is a string pulled tight inside a tube
of half-width $\lambda$ around the cumulative sum of $\yvec$. Condat's direct algorithm follows this string in one pass,
exactly, in time linear in $n$ on real signals <Cite id="condat2013" />.

**The fused lasso** adds an $L^1$ penalty on the values,
$\lambda_1\norm{\thetavec}_1 + \lambda_2\norm{\Dmat\thetavec}_1$, to get a signal that is piecewise constant and zero on
most pieces <Cite id="tibshirani2005" />. For the signal approximator ($\Xmat = \eye$) the solution is the soft
thresholding at $\lambda_1$ of the total variation solution at $\lambda_2$ <Cite id="friedman2007" />.

**The generalised lasso** penalises $\norm{\Dmat\thetavec}_1$ for any matrix $\Dmat$ <Cite id="tibshirani2011" />.
Second differences give **trend filtering**, whose solutions are piecewise linear with a few kinks. Differences along
the edges of a graph give fusion over a network.

Total variation is the $L^1$ counterpart of Tikhonov's roughness penalty $\norm{\Lmat_1\thetavec}^2$ (see
<NoteLink to="tikhonov-regularisation">Tikhonov regularisation</NoteLink>). The squared penalty charges a jump of size
$h$ an amount $h^2$ if taken at once and only $h^2/k$ if spread over $k$ steps, so it spreads every jump into a ramp.
The absolute penalty charges $h$ either way, so it leaves the jump where the data put it.

## Nuclear norm

For a matrix parameter $\Wmat \in \reals^{p \times q}$ with singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge 0$,
the rank counts the non-zero $\sigma_k$, an $L^0$ count. The <NoteLink to="matrix-norms">nuclear norm</NoteLink>
$\norm{\Wmat}_* = \sum_k\sigma_k$ is the corresponding $L^1$ norm, and on the spectral-norm unit ball it is the convex
envelope of the rank <Cite id="recht2010" />.

**Singular value thresholding.** The proximal operator of $\lambda\norm{\cdot}_*$ at a matrix $\Zmat$ with
<Gloss name="svd" /> $\Zmat = \Umat\diag(\sigma_k)\Vmat^\top$ soft thresholds the singular values and keeps the singular
vectors <Cite id="cai2010" />:

$$
\argmin_{\Wmat}\ \tfrac12\norm{\Zmat - \Wmat}_F^2 + \lambda\norm{\Wmat}_* = \Umat\diag\paren{(\sigma_k - \lambda)_+}\Vmat^\top.
$$

Directions of the matrix with weak evidence are removed, and the rest are shrunk by $\lambda$. **Matrix completion**
fills in a partly observed matrix by minimising the squared error on the observed entries plus $\lambda\norm{\Wmat}_*$;
Soft-Impute alternates filling the missing entries with the current fit and applying singular value thresholding
<Cite id="mazumder2010" />. See also <NoteLink to="matrix-factorisation-for-recommendation">matrix factorisation for
recommendation</NoteLink>.

## One idea: atomic norms

The three penalties and the lasso are instances of one construction. Choose a set of simple **atoms**: signed
coordinate vectors for the lasso, unit vectors supported on one group for the group lasso, unit-norm rank-one matrices
$\uvec\vvec^\top$ for the nuclear norm. The atomic norm of $\wvec$ is the smallest total weight with which $\wvec$ can
be written as a non-negative combination of atoms, and its unit ball is the convex hull of the atoms
<Cite id="chandrasekaran2012" />. The atoms are the corners, and penalising the atomic norm favours solutions built from
few atoms.

## Worked example

**Group lasso.** Take three groups with orthonormal columns and $\lambda = 1$: group 1 with $\zvec_1 = (3, 4)$, group 2
with $\zvec_2 = (0.6, 0.8)$, and a single coefficient $z_3 = 2$.

1. Group 1 has $\norm{\zvec_1}_2 = 5$ and threshold $\lambda\sqrt2 = 1.41$. Its factor is $1 - 1.41/5 = 0.717$, so
   $\hat\wvec_1 = (2.15, 2.87)$.
2. Group 2 has $\norm{\zvec_2}_2 = 1 < 1.41$, so $\hat\wvec_2 = (0, 0)$. Both coefficients leave together, on the
   evidence of the group as a whole.
3. Group 3 is a single coefficient with threshold 1: soft thresholding gives $\hat w_3 = 1$.

**Total variation.** Take $\yvec = (1, 1.2, 0.9, 3.1, 2.8, 3.0)$ and $\lambda = 0.5$. Guess two flat pieces with one
upward jump between positions 3 and 4.

1. On a flat piece the residuals must sum to $\pm\lambda$ at its end. So the left level is its mean plus $\lambda/3$:
   $1.033 + 0.167 = 1.2$. The right level is its mean minus $\lambda/3$: $2.967 - 0.167 = 2.8$.
2. Check the running sums. On the left, $\theta_i - y_i = (0.2, 0, 0.3)$, so $u = (0.2, 0.2, 0.5)$: inside
   $[-0.5, 0.5]$, and equal to $+\lambda$ at the upward jump. On the right, $\theta_i - y_i = (-0.3, 0, -0.2)$, so
   $u$ continues $(0.2, 0.2, 0)$ and ends at 0. All conditions hold.
3. The solution is $\hat\thetavec = (1.2, 1.2, 1.2, 2.8, 2.8, 2.8)$. The jump in the data means, 1.93, is shrunk to
   1.6. Each level moves towards the other by $\lambda$ divided by its length, so the jump shrinks by
   $\lambda(\frac13 + \frac13) = 0.33$. Total variation biases jumps as the lasso biases coefficients.
4. The quadratic penalty $2\norm{\Dmat\thetavec}^2$ with the same data gives $(1.28, 1.43, 1.68, 2.32, 2.58, 2.72)$: no
   flat pieces and the jump spread over the whole signal.

**Fused lasso.** Adding $\lambda_1 = 1.5$ soft thresholds the total variation solution: $(0, 0, 0, 1.3, 1.3, 1.3)$.
The low piece is set to zero and the high piece shrunk by 1.5.

**Nuclear norm.** A $3 \times 3$ matrix with singular values $(5, 2, 0.5)$, thresholded at $\lambda = 1$, keeps its
singular vectors and gets singular values $(4, 1, 0)$: rank 3 becomes rank 2.

## Assumptions

- **The structure is known before the data are seen.** Groups, orderings and the choice of a low-rank parameter are
  part of the model. Choosing the groups after looking at which coefficients came out large reuses the data and makes
  every later estimate of error optimistic.
- **Comparable scales within a group.** The group norm adds squared coefficients, so columns within a group should be
  standardised. Yuan and Lin's closed form assumes orthonormal columns within each group; general solvers handle
  correlated columns by solving each block exactly.

## Pitfalls

- **Shrinkage of what is kept.** Kept groups, jumps and singular values are all shrunk by $\lambda$, like lasso
  coefficients. Refitting without the penalty on the selected structure removes the bias, at the cost of the selection
  bias described in <NoteLink to="sparsity-inducing-penalties">sparsity-inducing penalties</NoteLink>.
- **Staircasing.** Total variation fits a ramp with a staircase of small steps. Smooth trends call for trend filtering
  or a quadratic penalty.
- **Degrees of freedom for choosing λ.** The <NoteLink to="effective-degrees-of-freedom">effective degrees of
  freedom</NoteLink> of the generalised lasso fit $\yvec \approx \thetavec$ are estimated without bias by the dimension
  of its null space at the solution: for one-dimensional total variation, the number of flat pieces
  <Cite id="tibshirani2011" />. Counting parameters by the length of $\thetavec$ overstates the complexity, and plugging
  the count of pieces into $C_p$ or <NoteLink to="steins-unbiased-risk-estimate">SURE</NoteLink> chooses $\lambda$
  without <NoteLink to="cross-validation">cross-validation</NoteLink>.
- **Overlapping groups change the meaning.** Summing norms over overlapping groups sets unions of groups to zero, so
  the selected support is the complement of such a union. When the support should itself be a union of groups, a
  different penalty, the latent group lasso, is needed <Cite id="bach2012" />.
