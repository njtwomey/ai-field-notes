<Definition>
**Probabilistic inference** computes the quantities a probabilistic model implies once data are observed: the posterior
distribution of unobserved variables, their marginals and most probable values, predictions for new data, and the
evidence $p(\Dcal)$ that compares models. Exact inference is possible only for models with special structure. The
other methods trade accuracy for speed by sampling, optimisation or local approximation.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A probabilistic model states assumptions as a joint distribution over observed and latent variables. Learning,
prediction and uncertainty estimates all follow from conditioning that distribution on data, by
<NoteLink to="bayes-theorem" />. The conditioning requires sums or integrals over the latent variables, and their cost
grows exponentially with the number of variables in general <Cite id="koller2009" />. Inference algorithms make these
computations feasible. They drive <NoteLink to="topic-models">topic models</NoteLink>, Gaussian process classifiers,
hidden Markov models, matchmaking in online games, click prediction for advertising and the latent-variable models
behind variational autoencoders.

## Main ideas

**Structure makes inference cheaper.** <NoteLink to="graphical-models" /> encode how a joint distribution factorises and
which conditional independences it has. <NoteLink to="bayesian-network">Bayesian networks</NoteLink>,
<Gloss name="mrf">Markov random fields</Gloss> and <NoteLink to="factor-graph">factor graphs</NoteLink> are the three
standard forms, and inference algorithms run on them directly <Cite id="bishop2006" />.

**Exact inference pushes sums inside products.** <NoteLink to="exact-inference" /> covers
<NoteLink to="variable-elimination">variable elimination</NoteLink>, <Gloss name="bp">belief propagation</Gloss> and the
<NoteLink to="junction-tree-algorithm">junction tree algorithm</NoteLink>. Their cost is exponential in the treewidth of
the graph, which is small for chains and trees and large for grids and dense models.

**Sample from the posterior.** <NoteLink to="markov-chain-monte-carlo-methods" /> simulate a
<NoteLink to="markov-chain">Markov chain</NoteLink> whose <NoteLink to="markov-chain-monte-carlo">stationary
distribution</NoteLink> is the posterior and average over its states. They are asymptotically exact and need the
posterior only up to a constant, at the price of many model evaluations and careful convergence checks.

**Optimise an approximation.** <NoteLink to="variational-inference" /> chooses the member of a tractable family closest
to the posterior in <Gloss name="kl">Kullback–Leibler divergence</Gloss>, by maximising the <Gloss name="elbo">evidence
lower bound</Gloss>. Mean-field, stochastic, black-box and amortised variants scale it to large datasets and to models
with neural networks <Cite id="murphy2022" />.

**Pass approximate messages.** <NoteLink to="message-passing-approximations" /> keeps the local form of belief
propagation and drops exactness: <NoteLink to="loopy-belief-propagation">loopy belief propagation</NoteLink> runs it on
graphs with cycles, and <Gloss name="ep">expectation propagation</Gloss> projects each message onto an exponential
family by moment matching.

**Write the model, generate the inference.** <NoteLink to="probabilistic-programming" /> systems such as Stan and Pyro
take a model written as a program and derive the inference automatically. <NoteLink to="model-based-machine-learning" />
applies the same idea to bespoke models for one problem, each solved by message passing. <NoteLink to="skill-rating" />
is the best-known family of such models, from Elo to <Gloss name="trueskill" />.

## How the notes fit together

Start with <NoteLink to="graphical-models" />, which provides the language. <NoteLink to="exact-inference" /> follows
and defines the answers the approximate methods aim for. The three approximate families, sampling, variational and
message passing, can be read in any order; each overview compares its method with the others. Probabilistic
programming, model-based machine learning and skill rating apply these methods to whole models and come last.
