<Definition>
Child $n$ has, for each allergen $a$ and age $t \in \{1, 3, 5, 8\}$, a binary sensitisation state $s_{nat}$. The states
form a <NoteLink to="markov-chain">Markov chain</NoteLink> over ages with probabilities of initial sensitisation, gain
and retention. Skin-prick and IgE tests observe each state with test-specific error rates. A latent class $z_n$ per
child selects which chain probabilities apply. Inference returns the posterior over each child's class and states, and
the class-specific probabilities <Cite id="winn2023asthma" />.
</Definition>

## The problem

Many children are sensitised to allergens such as house-dust mite, cat, dog, pollen, milk or egg, and some go on to
develop asthma. Being sensitised, or atopic, is a crude predictor: most atopic children never develop asthma. The
Manchester Asthma and Allergy Study followed about a thousand children from birth, testing them for sensitisation at
ages 1, 3, 5 and 8. The question was whether there are distinct *patterns* of sensitisation, and whether some patterns
predict asthma better than atopy does <Cite id="simpson2010asthma" />. This is explanatory modelling: the aim is to find
structure a clinician can interpret, not only to predict.

## The model, one assumption at a time

The chapter's assumptions <Cite id="winn2023asthma" />:

1. If a child is sensitised to an allergen, a test for it is positive with high probability.
2. If a child is not sensitised, a test is positive with low probability.
3. For each type of test, the <Gloss name="fp">false-positive</Gloss> and <Gloss name="fn">false-negative</Gloss> rates
   are the same for every test in the study.
4. Whether a child is sensitised at one age depends only on whether they were sensitised at the previous age.
5. The probabilities of initially having, gaining and retaining sensitisation to an allergen are the same for all
   children.

Assumptions 1 to 3 make the tests noisy observations of a hidden state, with two shared parameters per test type, four
in all: $\pr(\text{positive} \mid s = 1)$ and $\pr(\text{positive} \mid s = 0)$ for skin and for IgE, each with a Beta
prior. Assumption 4 makes the states over age a two-state <NoteLink to="hidden-markov-model">hidden Markov
model</NoteLink>. Assumption 5 shares the chain probabilities across children, one set per allergen.

Assumption 5 is the one the chapter then relaxes. Children differ in how they become sensitised, so it is replaced: each
child belongs to one of $K$ latent classes, and each class has its own initial, gain and retain probabilities for every
allergen. The class selects the parameters through a <NoteLink to="gates-in-factor-graphs">gate</NoteLink>
<Cite id="minka2008gates" />. The model becomes a mixture of hidden Markov models.

## Inference

For one child and one allergen, given the class and the parameters, the posterior over the four states is computed by
the <Gloss name="forward-backward">forward–backward algorithm</Gloss> on a chain of length four. The class multiplies in
the evidence of each class's chain for all of the child's allergens, and the gate's message to $z_n$ is that evidence.
The parameters are learned by <Gloss name="ep">expectation propagation</Gloss> over all children at once, with Beta
messages to every probability. Two practical points from the chapter:

- **Symmetry.** The classes are exchangeable a priori, so message passing started from a symmetric state stays there.
  Random initial messages on the class variables break the symmetry.
- **Number of classes.** The model is given more classes than it needs, and unused classes stay empty. With five classes
  available, four were used <Cite id="winn2023asthma" />.

## What the classes showed

The classes had distinct, interpretable patterns: a large class of children with almost no sensitisation, and smaller
classes differing in which allergens they became sensitised to and when. One class, children sensitised early and to
many allergens, carried a much higher risk of asthma than the others, much more than atopy defined by any single test
<Cite id="simpson2010asthma" />. The same analysis on a second birth cohort found similar classes and associations
<Cite id="lazic2013" />.

## Assumptions

- **Binary sensitisation.** Test results are thresholded; the size of a skin wheal or an IgE level is not used.
- **First-order dynamics.** The state at age 8 depends on age 5 only, not on the whole history. Four time points give
  little room to test richer dynamics.
- **Independent allergens given the class.** Within a class, a child's sensitisations to different allergens are
  independent. Correlation between allergens is carried entirely by the class.

## Pitfalls

- **Classes are not diseases.** A latent class is a pattern the model finds useful for explaining the data. Reading it
  as a biological mechanism needs outside evidence, such as the finding of similar classes and associations in a second
  birth cohort.
- **Missing tests.** Not every child was tested at every age. The model handles a missing test by leaving its variable
  unobserved; a test missing for reasons related to the child's health would bias the result.
