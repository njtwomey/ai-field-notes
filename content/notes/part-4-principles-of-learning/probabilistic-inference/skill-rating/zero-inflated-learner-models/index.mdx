<Definition>
A standard <NoteLink to="item-response-theory">learner model</NoteLink> assumes that any failure to answer an item correctly ($Y = 0$) reflects a deficit in the student's latent ability $\theta$. A **zero-inflated learner model (ZILM)** introduces a mixture mechanism: an observed zero may either be an **ability zero** (the student attempted the item but failed) or a **structural zero** arising because the delivery, response type, or presentation of the item is inaccessible to the student <Cite id="twomey2022" />.
</Definition>

## Motivation: Accessibility and Equity in Assessment

In computerized assessment and educational testing, questions are presented through specific delivery and response mechanisms: timed multiple-choice questions, audio listening clips, complex text-heavy problem descriptions, or freehand drawing tasks.

For neurodivergent students—such as learners with dyslexia, dyscalculia, ADHD, or sensory processing differences (SPD)—an unsuitable format can prevent engagement or comprehension independent of the underlying subject competence. When scored with standard <NoteLink to="item-response-theory">Item Response Theory (IRT)</NoteLink>, these accessibility barriers produce uncalibrated incorrect responses that drastically deflate estimated abilities $\hat\theta$, systematically penalising students with specific accessibility needs.

## Mathematical Formulation

Let $Y_{pi} \in \{0, 1\}$ denote student $p$'s binary score on item $i$. Let $p(x_{pi})$ denote the base probability of a correct response according to an underlying cognitive model, such as the two-parameter logistic (2PL) IRT model:

$$
p(x_{pi}) = \sigma(a_i(\theta_p - b_i)) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-a_i(\theta_p - b_i))},
$$

where $\theta_p$ is latent ability, $b_i$ is item difficulty, and $a_i$ is discrimination.

In IRT-ZILM, each response opportunity is subject to a **learning quality factor (LQF)** $1 - \pi_{pi}$, where $\pi_{pi} \in [0, 1]$ represents the probability of a structural zero:

$$
P(Y_{pi} = 1 \mid \xvec_{pi}) = (1 - \pi_{pi})\, p(x_{pi}),
$$

$$
P(Y_{pi} = 0 \mid \xvec_{pi}) = \pi_{pi} + (1 - \pi_{pi})(1 - p(x_{pi})).
$$

### Parameterising Structural Zeros

The zero-inflation probability $\pi_{pi}$ is parameterised via a logistic link over interactions between the student's neurodivergent conditions $\zvec_p$ and the item's format attributes $\xvec_i$:

$$
\operatorname{logit}(\pi_{pi}) = w_0 + \sum_{k} z_{pk} \sum_{f} x_{if} W_{fk},
$$

where $w_0$ is a baseline failure rate across all students, $z_{pk} \in \{0, 1\}$ indicates whether student $p$ has condition $k$, and $x_{if}$ encodes whether item $i$ requires format feature $f$ (e.g., dense prose reading, rapid audio playback, or manual spatial dexterity).

When an item format is fully accessible ($x_{if} = 0$ for all unsuitable features), $\pi_{pi}$ collapses to the base rate $\sigma(w_0)$, and the model behaves as standard IRT with an asymptotic ceiling.

## Disentangling Ability from Accessibility

Given an observed failure $Y_{pi} = 0$, Bayes' rule yields the posterior probability that the zero was structural rather than caused by low ability:

$$
P(\text{structural zero} \mid Y_{pi} = 0) = \frac{\pi_{pi}}{\pi_{pi} + (1 - \pi_{pi})(1 - p(x_{pi}))}.
$$

When a high-ability student ($\theta_p \gg b_i$, so $p(x_{pi}) \approx 1$) scores zero on an item whose format clashes with their condition, the denominator term $(1 - p(x_{pi}))$ is near zero, driving the posterior to near $1.0$. The model correctly attributes the failure to the context and does not degrade the student's estimated ability $\hat\theta_p$.

## Comparison with Baseline Models

1. **Standard 2PL IRT:** Forces $\pi_{pi} = 0$. Unsuitable formats induce negative ability bias ($\expect[\hat\theta - \theta] < 0$) specifically for neurodivergent learners, widening the **equity gap**.
2. **Linear Knowledge-Tracing Machine (KTM):** Incorporates condition-format features directly into the ability logit:
   $$
   \operatorname{logit} P(Y_{pi} = 1) = a_i(\theta_p - b_i) + \sum_{k, f} z_{pk} x_{if} W_{fk}.
   $$
   While KTM allows format penalties, it treats format barriers as an additive shift in proficiency rather than a multiplicative hurdle, failing to capture the asymmetry of structural non-engagement.
3. **IRT-ZILM:** Models the dual generative process directly. As shown in empirical evaluations, IRT-ZILM eliminates group ability bias, dramatically closes the equity gap, and accurately detects which zero scores were caused by accessibility hurdles <Cite id="twomey2022" />.
