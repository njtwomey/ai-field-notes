<Definition>
**Expectation propagation examples** are a sequence of fully worked applications of expectation propagation (EP) to
models of increasing size. Each example writes out the posterior as a product of factors, gives each factor an
approximate site, and runs the remove-include-project cycle by hand. The numbers are checked against an exact or
numerically integrated posterior.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation" /> note states the algorithm in general. A reader who knows the general
algorithm still has to work out, for a given model, what a site looks like, which moments the projection matches and
what the tilted distribution is. The examples answer these three questions for the models where EP is most used:
censored and probit likelihoods, robust mixtures, Gaussian process classifiers and skill-rating
<NoteLink to="factor-graph">factor graphs</NoteLink>. They also compare EP with the
<NoteLink to="laplace-approximation" /> and with mean-field <NoteLink to="mean-field-variational-inference">variational
Bayes</NoteLink> on the same data, so the differences are visible as numbers rather than claims.

## Main ideas

**One closed-form update drives most of the examples.** A Gaussian cavity multiplied by a step function has tilted
moments in closed form, written with the functions $v(t)$ and $w(t)$ of a standardised threshold $t$. The note
<NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-truncated-gaussian" /> derives them. Probit regression, Gaussian process
classification and the comparison factors of TrueSkill all reduce to this update.

**Revisiting sites removes the order dependence of a single pass.** <NoteLink to="assumed-density-filtering" />
absorbs each factor once and projects, so its answer depends on the order of the factors. EP keeps one site per factor
and refines each against the others until they agree, which gives the same answer for every order.

**The projection family sets which moments are matched.** For a Gaussian family the projection matches mean and
variance. For a Beta family it matches $\expect[\log w]$ and $\expect[\log(1-w)]$, the expected
<NoteLink to="sufficient-statistic">sufficient statistics</NoteLink>, as
<NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-mixture-weight" /> shows for a mixture weight $w \in [0, 1]$.

**Sites on one-dimensional projections keep updates cheap.** When a factor depends on the parameters only through a
scalar $\xvec_i^\top\wvec$, its site is a rank-one Gaussian and its update is one-dimensional.
<NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-probit-regression" /> uses this for a weight vector, and
<NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-gaussian-process-classification" /> uses it for the latent function values of a
<Gloss name="gp">Gaussian process</Gloss>.

**EP is message passing.** On a factor graph with one Gaussian per variable, each EP site update is a message from a
factor to a variable. <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-on-a-factor-graph" /> works this out for three players and
two games, the model behind <NoteLink to="trueskill" />.

**EP can fail.** Sites may take negative precision, cavities may become improper, and updates may oscillate on
multimodal posteriors. <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-failure-modes" /> collects these failures and the fixes:
damping, power EP and double-loop algorithms.

## How the notes fit together

Start with the truncated Gaussian, since later examples call its update. Assumed density filtering comes next and
motivates the revisiting of sites. <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-clutter-problem" />, Minka's original example
<Cite id="minka2001" />, is the first full EP run with several sweeps and a negative-precision site. The mixture weight
changes the approximating family. Probit regression and Gaussian process classification scale the probit update to a
vector and to a function. The factor-graph example recasts EP as message passing. The failure-modes note closes the
sequence and applies to all the others. Most examples follow Minka's thesis <Cite id="minka2001thesis" />.
