<Callout variant="credit" title="Provenance: Minka, the clutter problem">
The clutter problem is Thomas Minka's. He introduced expectation propagation with it, in his UAI 2001 paper and in
chapter 3 of his PhD thesis <Cite id="minka2001" /> <Cite id="minka2001thesis" />. The model, the prior and clutter
variances, and the EP, Laplace and variational updates follow his treatment. The one-dimensional data, the worked
tables and the figure are new.
</Callout>

<Definition>
An unknown mean $\theta$ has prior $\Gauss(0, 100)$. Each observation $x_i$ is drawn from $\Gauss(\theta, 1)$ with
probability $1 - w$ and from a clutter distribution $\Gauss(0, 10)$ with probability $w$:

$$
p(\theta \mid x_{1:n}) \propto \Gauss(\theta \mid 0, 100)\prod_{i=1}^n \brack{(1 - w)\,\Gauss(x_i \mid \theta, 1) + w\,\Gauss(x_i \mid 0, 10)}.
$$

With $w = 0.2$ and data $x = (-2.5, 0.5, 1.5, 2, 3)$, approximate this posterior by a Gaussian using expectation
propagation (EP), step by step, and compare it with the exact posterior, the Laplace approximation and
<NoteLink to="mean-field-variational-inference">variational Bayes</NoteLink> <Cite id="minka2001" />.
</Definition>

## Setup

Here the model is one-dimensional, so the exact posterior can be
computed on a grid and every approximation checked against it.

- **Factors.** The prior $t_0(\theta) = \Gauss(\theta \mid 0, 100)$ is already Gaussian and is kept exactly. Each data
  factor $t_i(\theta) = (1 - w)\,\Gauss(x_i \mid \theta, 1) + w\,\Gauss(x_i \mid 0, 10)$ is a Gaussian bump in $\theta$
  on a constant floor. The floor is the clutter term, which does not depend on $\theta$.
- **Why it is hard.** The exact posterior is a mixture of $2^n$ Gaussians, one for each assignment of the points to
  signal or clutter. For $n = 5$ that is 32 components; for $n = 50$ it is $10^{15}$.
- **Sites.** EP replaces each $t_i$ by $\tilde t_i(\theta) \propto \exp(-\frac12\tau_i\theta^2 + \nu_i\theta)$, a
  Gaussian shape with precision $\tau_i$ and precision-weighted mean $\nu_i$. The approximation is
  $q(\theta) \propto t_0(\theta)\prod_i \tilde t_i(\theta)$, a Gaussian with precision $0.01 + \sum_i\tau_i$ and
  $\nu = \sum_i \nu_i$.

## Solution

### The tilted moments of one clutter factor

A site update multiplies a Gaussian cavity $\Gauss(\theta \mid \mu, \sigma^2)$ by $t_i$ and matches the mean and
variance of the product. The normaliser is a two-term sum, because integrating $\Gauss(x_i \mid \theta, 1)$ against the
cavity gives $\Gauss(x_i \mid \mu, \sigma^2 + 1)$:

$$
Z_i(\mu) = (1 - w)\,\Gauss(x_i \mid \mu, \sigma^2 + 1) + w\,\Gauss(x_i \mid 0, 10).
$$

Let $r_i = (1 - w)\,\Gauss(x_i \mid \mu, \sigma^2 + 1)/Z_i$, the probability under the cavity that $x_i$ is signal. Only
the first term depends on $\mu$, and its <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> is itself times
$(x_i - \mu)/(\sigma^2 + 1)$, so

$$
\frac{\partial \log Z_i}{\partial \mu} = r_i\,\frac{x_i - \mu}{\sigma^2 + 1}.
$$

Differentiating again, with $\partial r_i/\partial\mu = r_i(1 - r_i)(x_i - \mu)/(\sigma^2 + 1)$, gives
$\partial^2_\mu \log Z_i = -\frac{r_i}{\sigma^2 + 1} + r_i(1 - r_i)\frac{(x_i - \mu)^2}{(\sigma^2 + 1)^2}$. For any
factor, the tilted mean is $\mu + \sigma^2\,\partial_\mu\log Z$ and the tilted variance is
$\sigma^2 + \sigma^4\,\partial_\mu^2\log Z$ (derived in <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-truncated-gaussian">a
Gaussian times a step function</NoteLink>). So

$$
\text{mean} = \mu + \sigma^2 r_i\,\frac{x_i - \mu}{\sigma^2 + 1}, \qquad
\text{variance} = \sigma^2 - \frac{\sigma^4 r_i}{\sigma^2 + 1} + \frac{\sigma^4\,r_i(1 - r_i)(x_i - \mu)^2}{(\sigma^2 + 1)^2}.
$$

The mean moves towards $x_i$ in proportion to how likely the point is to be signal. The variance has a shrinking term
and a growing term. The growing term is large when the point's role is uncertain ($r_i(1 - r_i)$ near $\tfrac14$) and
far from the cavity mean. When it wins, $(1 - r_i)(x_i - \mu)^2 > \sigma^2 + 1$, the tilted variance exceeds the cavity
variance, and the new site has **negative precision**. A negative-precision site is not a density: it curves upward, and
it widens $q$ to express doubt about where $\theta$ is.

### The first sweep

All sites start at $\tau_i = \nu_i = 0$, so the first cavity is the prior and the first sweep is
<NoteLink to="assumed-density-filtering">assumed density filtering</NoteLink>. For the first point,
$\Gauss(-2.5 \mid 0, 101) = 0.0385$ and $\Gauss(-2.5 \mid 0, 10) = 0.0923$, so
$r_1 = 0.8 \times 0.0385/(0.8 \times 0.0385 + 0.2 \times 0.0923) = 0.625$. The tilted mean is
$100 \times 0.625 \times (-2.5)/101 = -1.548$. The site precision is the tilted precision minus the cavity precision.
Computed with numpy, each update of the first sweep is:

| site | $x_i$ | cavity mean | cavity var | $r_i$ | tilted mean | tilted var | site precision | site mean |
| ---- | ----- | ----------- | ---------- | ----- | ----------- | ---------- | -------------- | --------- |
| 1    | −2.5  | 0.000       | 100.0      | 0.625 | −1.548      | 39.54      | 0.015          | −2.56     |
| 2    | 0.5   | −1.548      | 39.54      | 0.656 | −0.237      | 15.12      | 0.041          | 0.58      |
| 3    | 1.5   | −0.237      | 15.12      | 0.763 | 1.005       | 4.788      | 0.143          | 1.58      |
| 4    | 2.0   | 1.005       | 4.788      | 0.855 | 1.709       | 1.486      | 0.464          | 2.03      |
| 5    | 3.0   | 1.709       | 1.486      | 0.900 | 2.403       | 0.740      | 0.678          | 3.09      |

After one pass, $q = \Gauss(2.403, 0.740)$. The first point, $-2.5$, was absorbed when the approximation was the broad
prior and nothing marked it as an outlier; it was given a 62 per cent chance of being signal and dragged the early
estimate to $-1.5$. Processing the same points in reverse order ends at $\Gauss(0.066, 1.764)$.

### The second sweep

Now each cavity holds the other four sites. For the first point, the cavity is $\Gauss(2.460, 0.749)$: the other points
put $\theta$ near 2.5, where $-2.5$ is five units away and almost certainly clutter.

| site | $x_i$ | cavity mean | cavity var | $r_i$ | tilted mean | tilted var | site precision | site mean |
| ---- | ----- | ----------- | ---------- | ----- | ----------- | ---------- | -------------- | --------- |
| 1    | −2.5  | 2.460       | 0.749      | 0.011 | 2.436       | 0.796      | −0.079         | 2.84      |
| 2    | 0.5   | 2.499       | 0.822      | 0.760 | 1.813       | 0.688      | 0.237          | −1.71     |
| 3    | 1.5   | 1.838       | 0.764      | 0.912 | 1.705       | 0.464      | 0.846          | 1.50      |
| 4    | 2.0   | 1.617       | 0.591      | 0.921 | 1.748       | 0.390      | 0.871          | 2.00      |
| 5    | 3.0   | 1.264       | 0.531      | 0.857 | 1.780       | 0.418      | 0.511          | 3.68      |

The outlier's site now has precision $-0.079$. Its tilted variance, $0.796$, exceeds the cavity variance, $0.749$: the
point is almost surely clutter ($r_1 = 0.011$), but the small chance that it is signal, five units away, widens the
posterior. The second point, at $0.5$, gets a site whose mean is $-1.71$: the site is not an estimate of $\theta$ but a
quadratic in $\theta$ that, combined with the cavity, reproduces the tilted moments.

### Convergence and comparison

The posterior means after sweeps 1 to 4 are $2.403$, $1.780$, $1.750$ and $1.752$; after sweep 4 the sites change by
less than $10^{-4}$. EP converges to $q = \Gauss(1.752, 0.405)$, with one negative site (the outlier).

- **Exact.** The posterior on a grid of $\theta$ has mean $1.735$ and variance $0.555$. As $\abs\theta$ grows, every
  point becomes clutter and the likelihood tends to the constant $\prod_i w\,\Gauss(x_i \mid 0, 10)$, so the posterior's
  tails follow the broad $\Gauss(0, 100)$ prior. Only 0.4 per cent of the mass lies outside $[-1, 5]$, but it raises the
  variance from $0.401$ (inside that interval) to $0.555$. Inside $[-1, 5]$ the exact mean is $1.748$: EP matches the
  central bump to within $0.004$ in both mean and variance.
- **Laplace.** <NoteLink to="newtons-method">Newton's method</NoteLink> finds the mode at $1.760$. The curvature there,
  $\frac{1}{100} + \sum_i r_i - \sum_i r_i(1 - r_i)(x_i - \hat\theta)^2$ with $r_i$ evaluated at the mode, gives
  variance $0.307$. See <NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">the Laplace approximation</NoteLink>.
- **Variational Bayes.** A mean-field approximation $q(\theta)\prod_i q(c_i)$, with $c_i$ the indicator that $x_i$ is
  signal, alternates between $q(c_i = 1) \propto (1 - w)\exp\paren{\expect_q \log\Gauss(x_i \mid \theta, 1)}$ and a
  Gaussian $q(\theta)$ with precision $0.01 + \sum_i q(c_i = 1)$. It converges to $\Gauss(1.763, 0.279)$.

Laplace and variational Bayes both land near the mode and are too narrow: their variances are 23 and 30 per cent below
the variance of the central bump. Variational Bayes minimises $\KL(q \,\|\, p)$, which penalises $q$ for putting mass
where $p$ has little and so prefers narrow approximations. EP's local steps minimise $\KL(\hat p \,\|\, q)$, which
penalises $q$ for missing mass. See <NoteLink to="variational-inference">variational inference</NoteLink> for the two
directions.

## Simulation

<ClutterEp />

Stepping through the first five updates reproduces the first table. Update 6 shows the outlier's site turn into an
upward-curving parabola. Dragging the point at 3 to 6 gives the exact posterior a second mode near 6. EP then meets
cavities with negative precision, skips those updates and wanders between the modes without converging; a single
Gaussian cannot represent both modes.

## What it illustrates

- **Cavities carry context.** A site is recomputed against the cavity formed by the other sites' current values. The
  outlier looked like signal in the first sweep and like clutter in the second. See
  <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation">expectation propagation</NoteLink>.
- **Sites are not densities.** Site precisions can be negative and site means can lie far from the data. Only $q$ and
  the cavities must be proper Gaussians.
- **Order dependence of ADF.** The first sweep alone ends at $2.403$ in one order and $0.066$ in the other. See
  <NoteLink to="assumed-density-filtering">assumed density filtering</NoteLink>.
- **Failure on multimodal posteriors.** With two separated groups of points, EP can oscillate or reach a cavity with
  negative precision. See <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation-failure-modes">EP failure modes and fixes</NoteLink>.

## Variations

- **More data.** With $n = 20$ or more points, the central bump dominates the tails, the exact posterior is close to
  Gaussian, and EP's mean and evidence are more accurate than those of Laplace, variational Bayes,
  <NoteLink to="importance-sampling">importance sampling</NoteLink> or <NoteLink to="gibbs-sampling">Gibbs
  sampling</NoteLink> at equal cost <Cite id="minka2001" />.
- **Higher dimension.** In $d$ dimensions with an isotropic Gaussian $q$, the same updates hold with
  $(x_i - \mu)^2$ replaced by $\norm{\xvec_i - \muvec}^2/d$ in the variance term <Cite id="minka2001thesis" />.
- **Heavier clutter.** Raising $w$ widens the tails of the exact posterior and makes negative sites more common. At
  $w = 0.5$ with five points, the exact variance is dominated by the tails and no single Gaussian describes the
  posterior well.
