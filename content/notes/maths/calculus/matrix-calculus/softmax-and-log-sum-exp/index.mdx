<Definition>
For a vector of logits $\zvec \in \reals^K$, the log-sum-exp and the softmax are

$$
\operatorname{LSE}(\zvec) = \log\sum_{k=1}^{K} e^{z_k}, \qquad \operatorname{softmax}(\zvec)_i = p_i =
\frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{k} e^{z_k}}.
$$

The softmax maps any real vector to a probability vector: every $p_i$ is positive and they sum to 1
<Cite id="bridle1990" />.
</Definition>

## The gradient of log-sum-exp is the softmax

Differentiate the logarithm of the sum with the <NoteLink to="derivative">chain rule</NoteLink>:

$$
\frac{\partial\operatorname{LSE}}{\partial z_i} = \frac{1}{\sum_k e^{z_k}} \cdot e^{z_i} = p_i.
$$

So $\nabla\operatorname{LSE}(\zvec) = \operatorname{softmax}(\zvec)$. The softmax is the
<NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> of a scalar function, which is why its
<NoteLink to="jacobian">Jacobian</NoteLink> below is symmetric.

## The softmax Jacobian

Work with $\log p_i = z_i - \operatorname{LSE}(\zvec)$. Its derivative with respect to $z_j$ is

$$
\frac{\partial \log p_i}{\partial z_j} = \delta_{ij} - p_j,
$$

where $\delta_{ij}$ is 1 when $i = j$ and 0 otherwise. Since $\partial p_i / \partial z_j = p_i\,\partial\log
p_i/\partial z_j$,

$$
\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j} = p_i(\delta_{ij} - p_j), \qquad \Jmat = \diag(\pvec) - \pvec\pvec^\top.
$$

Three properties follow from this form.

- **Rows sum to zero.** $\Jmat\ones = \pvec - \pvec(\pvec^\top\ones) = \zeros$. Adding the same constant to every logit
  leaves the softmax unchanged, so the derivative in the direction $\ones$ is zero.
- **Symmetric and <NoteLink to="positive-definite-matrices">positive semidefinite</NoteLink>.** For any $\vvec$, $\vvec^\top\Jmat\vvec = \sum_i p_i v_i^2 - (\sum_i p_i
  v_i)^2$, the variance of $v$ under $\pvec$, which is never negative.
- **Log-sum-exp is convex.** $\Jmat$ is also the <NoteLink to="hessian">Hessian</NoteLink> of LSE, since it is the
  Jacobian of LSE's gradient. A positive semidefinite Hessian everywhere means LSE is convex <Cite id="boyd2004" />.

## The gradient of cross-entropy

For a one-hot label $\yvec$ (or any probability vector), the <NoteLink to="cross-entropy-and-perplexity">cross-entropy
loss</NoteLink> is $L = -\sum_i y_i\log p_i$. Substitute $\log p_i = z_i - \operatorname{LSE}(\zvec)$ and use
$\sum_i y_i = 1$:

$$
L = -\yvec^\top\zvec + \operatorname{LSE}(\zvec), \qquad \nabla_{\zvec} L = -\yvec + \pvec = \pvec - \yvec.
$$

The gradient is the predicted distribution minus the target. It is bounded, it vanishes exactly when the prediction
matches the label, and it needs no Jacobian at all. Libraries fuse softmax and cross-entropy into one operation for this
reason. <NoteLink to="logistic-regression">Logistic regression</NoteLink> is the case $K = 2$, where the gradient is
$\sigma(z) - y$.

## Worked example

Take $\zvec = (2, 1, 0)$ and true class 1, so $\yvec = (1, 0, 0)$.

- $\sum_k e^{z_k} = e^2 + e + 1 = 11.107$, so $\operatorname{LSE} = 2.4076$ and $\pvec = (0.665, 0.245, 0.090)$.
- The loss is $-\log 0.665 = 0.4076 = \operatorname{LSE} - z_1$.
- The gradient is $\pvec - \yvec = (-0.335, 0.245, 0.090)$: raise the correct logit, lower the others in proportion to
  their probability.

The softmax Jacobian at this point is

$$
\Jmat = \begin{bmatrix} 0.223 & -0.163 & -0.060 \\ -0.163 & 0.185 & -0.022 \\ -0.060 & -0.022 & 0.082 \end{bmatrix},
$$

and each of its rows sums to zero.

## A smooth maximum

Let $m = \max_k z_k$. The sum $\sum_k e^{z_k - m}$ contains the term $e^0 = 1$ and at most $K$ terms no larger than 1,
so it lies between 1 and $K$. Taking logarithms,

$$
\max_k z_k \le \operatorname{LSE}(\zvec) \le \max_k z_k + \log K.
$$

With a temperature $\tau > 0$, apply this to $\zvec/\tau$ and multiply by $\tau$: $\tau\operatorname{LSE}(\zvec/\tau)$
lies within $\tau\log K$ of the maximum, and converges to it as $\tau \to 0$. Its gradient,
$\operatorname{softmax}(\zvec/\tau)$, converges to the one-hot indicator of the largest logit (when it is unique). As
$\tau \to \infty$ the softmax tends to the uniform distribution. Sampling from a <Gloss name="language-model">language
model</Gloss> at temperature $\tau$ uses exactly this family.

## Computing it stably

Subtract the maximum logit before exponentiating: $\operatorname{LSE}(\zvec) = m + \log\sum_k e^{z_k - m}$ and
$\operatorname{softmax}(\zvec) = \operatorname{softmax}(\zvec - m\ones)$. No exponent is positive, so nothing overflows
<Cite id="blanchard2021" />. Compute $\log p_i = z_i - \operatorname{LSE}(\zvec)$ directly (log-softmax) rather than
taking the logarithm of a softmax that may have underflowed to 0. The
<NoteLink to="exponential-and-logarithm">exponential and logarithm</NoteLink> note works through the trick with numbers.

## Pitfalls

- **Softmax then log.** $\log(\operatorname{softmax}(\zvec))$ computed in two steps returns $-\infty$ when a probability
  underflows; the fused log-softmax does not.
- **Shift invariance.** Logits are only defined up to an additive constant, so the Jacobian is singular.
  Parameterisations that fix one logit at 0 remove the redundancy.
- **Saturation.** When one probability is near 1, every entry of $\Jmat$ is near 0 and gradients through the softmax
  vanish; attention layers divide logits by $\sqrt{d}$ partly to avoid this.
