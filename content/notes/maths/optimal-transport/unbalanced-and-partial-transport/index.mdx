<Definition>
Let $\avec \in \reals_{\ge 0}^n$ and $\bvec \in \reals_{\ge 0}^m$ be nonnegative measures, not necessarily of the same
total mass, and $\Cmat$ a cost matrix. **Unbalanced optimal transport** with marginal penalty $\tau > 0$ solves
<Cite id="liero2018" /> <Cite id="chizat2018" />

$$
\min_{\Pmat \ge 0}\ \inner{\Cmat}{\Pmat} + \tau\KL\paren{\Pmat\ones \,\|\, \avec} + \tau\KL\paren{\Pmat^\top\ones \,\|\, \bvec},
$$

where $\KL(\pvec \,\|\, \qvec) = \sum_i p_i\log(p_i / q_i) - p_i + q_i$ is the KL divergence between nonnegative
vectors. **Partial optimal transport** instead fixes the mass to move, $m \le \min\paren{\sum_i a_i, \sum_j b_j}$, and
solves $\min \inner{\Cmat}{\Pmat}$ subject to $\Pmat\ones \le \avec$, $\Pmat^\top\ones \le \bvec$ and $\ones^\top\Pmat\ones
= m$ <Cite id="figalli2010" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Balanced optimal transport must move every grain of $\avec$ and fill every hole of $\bvec$. That fails in two common
situations. The total masses may differ, as when comparing cell counts, image intensities or unnormalised histograms.
Or a few outliers may sit far from everything else, and the balanced plan must transport them anyway, at a cost that
dominates the total. Relaxing the marginals lets the plan leave some mass behind. Unbalanced transport charges for
every unit created or destroyed through a KL penalty; partial transport moves a chosen amount and leaves the rest free.

## The effect of $\tau$

For a single pair of points at distance $\ell$, each of mass 1, compare transporting the unit with deleting it and
recreating it. Transport costs $c(\ell)$. Destroying mass at the source and creating it at the target costs $\tau$
for each side in the limit where nothing is moved, since $\KL(0 \,\|\, 1) = 1$. So mass moves only between points whose
cost is below a threshold of order $2\tau$, and farther pairs are treated as unmatched. $\tau$ is a length scale in
units of the cost.

- **$\tau \to \infty$**: the penalties force $\Pmat\ones = \avec$ and $\Pmat^\top\ones = \bvec$, and balanced transport
  is recovered (when the masses are equal).
- **$\tau \to 0$**: moving anything costs more than the penalty saves, and $\Pmat \to 0$.

With $\tau = 1$ and the cost $c(x, y) = -\log\cos^2\paren{\min(d(x, y), \pi/2)}$, the square root of the optimal value defines a metric on
nonnegative measures, the Hellinger–Kantorovich or Wasserstein–Fisher–Rao distance. It interpolates between transport
for nearby mass and a Hellinger comparison for distant mass <Cite id="liero2018" />.

## Unbalanced Sinkhorn

Adding the entropy term $\varepsilon\KL(\Pmat \,\|\, \Kmat)$ with $K_{ij} = e^{-C_{ij}/\varepsilon}$ absorbs the cost
and gives a problem solved by scaling, as in the balanced case <Cite id="chizat2018" />:

$$
\min_{\Pmat \ge 0}\ \varepsilon\KL(\Pmat \,\|\, \Kmat) + \tau\KL\paren{\Pmat\ones \,\|\, \avec} + \tau\KL\paren{\Pmat^\top\ones
\,\|\, \bvec} .
$$

Write $\rvec = \Pmat\ones$ and $\cvec = \Pmat^\top\ones$. Setting the derivative with respect to $P_{ij}$ to zero gives

$$
\varepsilon\log\frac{P_{ij}}{K_{ij}} + \tau\log\frac{r_i}{a_i} + \tau\log\frac{c_j}{b_j} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P_{ij} = u_i K_{ij} v_j,\ \ u_i = \paren{\frac{a_i}{r_i}}^{\tau/\varepsilon},\ \ v_j = \paren{\frac{b_j}{c_j}}^{\tau/\varepsilon} .
$$

The plan has the same scaled-kernel form as in the balanced case. With $\vvec$ fixed, the row sums are $r_i = u_i
(\Kmat\vvec)_i$. Substituting into $u_i = (a_i / r_i)^{\tau/\varepsilon}$ gives $u_i^{1 + \tau/\varepsilon} =
\paren{a_i / (\Kmat\vvec)_i}^{\tau/\varepsilon}$, so the update is

$$
\uvec \leftarrow \paren{\avec \oslash \Kmat\vvec}^{\tau/(\tau + \varepsilon)}, \qquad
\vvec \leftarrow \paren{\bvec \oslash \Kmat^\top\uvec}^{\tau/(\tau + \varepsilon)},
$$

with powers taken elementwise. As $\tau \to \infty$ the exponent tends to 1 and Sinkhorn's balanced updates are
recovered. The exponent below 1 damps each update, so the marginals are matched only partly. The cost per iteration is
unchanged: two matrix-vector products.

## Partial transport as balanced transport

Partial transport reduces to a balanced problem with one dummy point on each side <Cite id="chapel2020" />. Write
$\abs{\avec} = \sum_i a_i$ and $\abs{\bvec} = \sum_j b_j$.

1. Add a dummy source with mass $\abs{\bvec} - m$ and a dummy target with mass $\abs{\avec} - m$. Both sides now have
   total mass $\abs{\avec} + \abs{\bvec} - m$.
2. Moving mass between a dummy and a real point costs 0. Moving mass from the dummy source to the dummy target is
   forbidden (infinite cost).
3. Solve the balanced problem and keep the real-to-real block.

The dummy target can absorb at most $\abs{\avec} - m$ of the real sources' mass, and it receives nothing from the dummy
source, so it receives exactly $\abs{\avec} - m$ from real sources. The rest of the real sources' mass, exactly $m$,
goes to real targets. Real mass sent to a dummy costs nothing, so the balanced optimum is the cheapest way to move mass
$m$ between real points.

## Worked example

Four source points at $0, 1, 2, 10$ and four target points at $0.2, 1.2, 2.2, -8$, each with mass $0.25$, and cost
$\abs{x - y}$. The first three points of each side are close pairs; $10$ and $-8$ are outliers.

- **Balanced.** The optimal plan pairs the sorted points: $-8$ with $0$, $0.2$ with $1$, $1.2$ with $2$, and $2.2$
  with $10$. Its cost is $0.25 \times (8 + 0.8 + 0.8 + 7.8) = 4.35$. The outliers dominate, and every inlier is matched
  to the wrong partner.
- **Partial, $m = 0.75$.** The plan matches $0 \to 0.2$, $1 \to 1.2$ and $2 \to 2.2$ and leaves both outliers
  untouched. Its cost is $0.75 \times 0.2 = 0.15$.

POT's `ot.partial.partial_wasserstein` <Cite id="flamary2021" /> and the dummy-point construction above both give
$0.15$.

## Uses

- **Measures of different mass.** Rubner et al. already allowed partial matching of image signatures with unequal
  weights <Cite id="rubner2000" />. Unnormalised histograms, intensity images and counts are compared without
  normalising.
- **Outlier-robust matching.** Point-cloud registration with partial overlap, and domain adaptation in which some
  classes are missing from one domain, need the plan to leave mass unmatched.
- **Positive–unlabelled learning.** Partial transport from the positive examples to the unlabelled set, moving only
  the known class prior's worth of mass, selects the unlabelled points most likely to be positive
  <Cite id="chapel2020" />.

## Pitfalls

- **$\tau$ and $m$ need choosing.** Both set how much mass is treated as unmatched. Too small a $\tau$ discards real
  structure; too large a $\tau$ brings back the outlier sensitivity.
- **Not a metric in general.** The unbalanced cost with arbitrary $\tau$ and cost does not satisfy the triangle
  inequality. The Hellinger–Kantorovich construction is the exception.
- **Different objectives.** Unbalanced transport penalises mass differences everywhere, while partial transport picks
  one amount to move. Results from the two are not interchangeable.
