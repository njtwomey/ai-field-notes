<Definition>
**Coordinate descent** minimises $f : \reals^n \to \reals$ by repeatedly choosing a coordinate $i$ and updating only
$x_i$, holding the others fixed. **Exact** coordinate descent sets $x_i$ to the minimiser of $f$ along coordinate $i$.
**Coordinate gradient descent** takes the step $x_i \leftarrow x_i - \tfrac{1}{L_i}\,\partial_i f(\xvec)$, where
$L_i$ bounds the curvature of $f$ along coordinate $i$. Coordinates are chosen in cyclic order, uniformly at random,
or greedily <Cite id="wright2015" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

A full <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> step changes every coordinate and needs every
<NoteLink to="partial-derivatives">partial derivative</NoteLink>. For some problems a single coordinate's
<NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink>, or even its exact minimiser, costs far less than $1/n$ of a full
gradient, because the rest of the computation can be cached and updated. Least squares with a maintained residual, the
<NoteLink to="lasso">lasso</NoteLink>, the <NoteLink to="support-vector-machine">support vector machine</NoteLink> dual
and many matrix factorisations have this property. Coordinate descent exploits it.

## Mechanism

### Exact minimisation on a quadratic

For $f(\xvec) = \tfrac12\xvec^\top\Amat\xvec - \bvec^\top\xvec$ with $\Amat$ symmetric positive definite, the partial
derivative in $x_i$ is $\sum_j A_{ij}x_j - b_i$. Setting it to zero gives

$$
x_i \leftarrow \frac{1}{A_{ii}}\LP b_i - \sum_{j \ne i} A_{ij}x_j \RP.
$$

A full cyclic sweep of this update is the **Gauss–Seidel** method for the linear system $\Amat\xvec = \bvec$. It
converges for every symmetric positive definite $\Amat$. Each update costs one row of $\Amat$.

### Why it converges on smooth functions

Assume the partial derivative $\partial_i f$ is $L_i$-Lipschitz along coordinate $i$, and let $L_{\max} = \max_i L_i$.
The one-dimensional <NoteLink to="mean-value-theorem">descent lemma</NoteLink>, applied along coordinate $i$ with step
$1/L_i$, gives

$$
f(\xvec^+) \le f(\xvec) - \frac{1}{2L_i}(\partial_i f(\xvec))^2 \le f(\xvec) - \frac{1}{2L_{\max}}(\partial_i f(\xvec))^2.
$$

Choose $i$ uniformly at random. Taking the <NoteLink to="expectation">expectation</NoteLink> over $i$ replaces
$(\partial_i f)^2$ by its average, $\norm{\nabla f}^2/n$:

$$
\expect\LB f(\xvec^+)\RB \le f(\xvec) - \frac{1}{2nL_{\max}}\norm{\nabla f(\xvec)}^2.
$$

This is the sufficient decrease of <NoteLink to="convergence-of-gradient-descent">gradient descent</NoteLink> with $L$
replaced by $nL_{\max}$. The rest of the analysis carries over. If $f$ is $\mu$-strongly convex, the
Polyak–Łojasiewicz inequality $\norm{\nabla f}^2 \ge 2\mu(f - f^*)$ gives

$$
\expect\LB f(\xvec_{k+1}) - f^*\RB \le \LP 1 - \frac{\mu}{nL_{\max}}\RP \expect\LB f(\xvec_k) - f^*\RB
$$

<Cite id="nesterov2012" />. After $n$ coordinate updates, about the cost of one full gradient, the error has shrunk by
roughly $1 - \mu/L_{\max}$. Full gradient descent shrinks it by $1 - \mu/L$ in the same cost. Since
$L_{\max} \le L \le nL_{\max}$, on problems where $n$ coordinate updates cost about one full gradient, coordinate
descent is never worse per unit of work. It is up to $n$ times better when the coordinates are strongly coupled and $L$
is much larger than every $L_i$. When the coordinates are weakly coupled, $L$ is close to $L_{\max}$ and the two rates
are similar.

### Separable nonsmooth penalties

For $F(\xvec) = f(\xvec) + \sum_i g_i(x_i)$, with $f$ smooth and each $g_i$ convex, the coordinate update minimises
$f$ along $x_i$ plus $g_i(x_i)$. For the lasso, $g_i = \lambda\abs{x_i}$, and the update is soft-thresholding of the
coordinate's least-squares value (derived in <NoteLink to="proximal-gradient-methods">proximal gradient
methods</NoteLink>). Coordinate descent converges to a minimiser in this setting, because at a point where no
coordinate can improve, $0 \in \partial_i F$ for each $i$, and separability makes the full subdifferential the product
of these coordinate sets. glmnet fits the whole lasso path this way <Cite id="friedman2010" />.

## Worked example: where coordinate descent stalls

Separability is essential. Consider

$$
f(x, y) = \abs{x - y} - \frac{x + y}{2} + \frac{(x + y)^2}{4}.
$$

The function is convex: it is a norm of a <NoteLink to="matrix-vector-product">linear map</NoteLink>, plus a linear
term, plus a convex quadratic of a linear map.

- Along the $x$ axis from the origin, $f(x, 0) = \abs{x} - x/2 + x^2/4$. For $x > 0$ this is $x/2 + x^2/4 > 0$, and for
  $x < 0$ it is $-3x/2 + x^2/4 > 0$. So $x = 0$ minimises $f$ along $x$. By symmetry $y = 0$ minimises $f$ along $y$.
- Along the diagonal, $f(t, t) = -t + t^2$, minimised at $t = 1/2$ with $f(1/2, 1/2) = -1/4 < 0 = f(0, 0)$.

Started at the origin, exact coordinate descent never moves, although the origin is not a minimum. A grid search
confirms that $-1/4$ is the global minimum. The kink of $\abs{x - y}$ lies along the diagonal, the only direction of
descent, and neither coordinate axis points along it. With a separable penalty such kinks lie along coordinate
directions, and the problem does not arise.

## Cost

Each update costs one partial derivative. For least squares with $\Amat \in \reals^{m \times n}$, keeping the residual
$\rvec = \Amat\xvec - \bvec$ up to date makes a coordinate update $O(m)$, and a sweep over all coordinates $O(mn)$, the
same as one full gradient. For sparse $\Amat$ the cost of an update is proportional to the nonzeros in one column.

## Trade-offs

- **No step size to tune** for exact minimisation, and no <NoteLink to="line-search">line search</NoteLink>.
- **Sequential.** Updates depend on each other, so parallelising needs care: simultaneous updates of coupled coordinates
  can diverge.
- **Coupled coordinates slow it down.** On a quadratic whose level sets are ellipses tilted at $45°$, each update moves
  along an axis and the path zig-zags. A <NoteLink to="change-of-variables">change of variables</NoteLink> that
  decouples the coordinates helps, as it does for gradient descent.
- **Cyclic versus random order.** Random order has the clean expected rate above. Cyclic order is often faster in
  practice but has worse worst-case bounds <Cite id="wright2015" />.
- **Related algorithms.** Block coordinate descent updates groups of variables. The
  <NoteLink to="expectation-maximisation">expectation–maximisation (EM) algorithm</NoteLink> and coordinate ascent
  <NoteLink to="mean-field-variational-inference">mean-field variational inference</NoteLink> are block coordinate
  ascent on a lower bound.
