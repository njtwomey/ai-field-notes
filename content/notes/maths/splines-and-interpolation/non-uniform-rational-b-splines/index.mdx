<Definition>
A **NURBS (non-uniform rational B-spline) curve** of degree $p$ has control points $\pvec_0, \dots, \pvec_n$, positive
weights $w_0, \dots, w_n$ and a knot vector $t_0 \le \dots \le t_{n+p+1}$. It is

$$
\cvec(u) = \frac{\sum_{i=0}^n w_i\,\pvec_i\,B_{i,p}(u)}{\sum_{i=0}^n w_i\,B_{i,p}(u)} = \sum_{i=0}^n \pvec_i\,R_{i,p}(u),
\qquad R_{i,p} = \frac{w_iB_{i,p}}{\sum_j w_jB_{j,p}},
$$

where $B_{i,p}$ are the <NoteLink to="b-splines">B-splines</NoteLink> on the knots. With all weights equal it is a
B-spline curve. With the knots of a single span it is a **rational Bézier curve** <Cite id="piegl1997" />.
</Definition>

## Properties of the rational basis

The functions $R_{i,p}$ inherit the useful properties of $B_{i,p}$ when all weights are positive. They are
non-negative, they sum to 1 by construction, and $R_{i,p}$ vanishes wherever $B_{i,p}$ does, so control stays local.
The curve lies in the convex hull of the control points active on each span, and it is as smooth as the B-splines
away from knots. A knot of multiplicity $r$ still gives $C^{p-r}$.

Each weight $w_i$ changes the shape of the curve:

- **Raising $w_i$** pulls the curve towards $\pvec_i$. As $w_i \to \infty$ the curve passes through $\pvec_i$.
- **Lowering $w_i$** pushes the curve away. At $w_i = 0$ the point has no influence.
- **Scaling every weight by the same factor** leaves the curve unchanged, because the factor cancels.

## The projective view

Lift each control point to **homogeneous coordinates** $\pvec_i^w = (w_ix_i, w_iy_i, w_i)$ in three dimensions. The
polynomial B-spline curve $\cvec^w(u) = \sum_i \pvec_i^w B_{i,p}(u)$ has third coordinate $W(u) = \sum_i w_iB_{i,p}(u)$.
Dividing by it, the central projection from the origin onto the plane $W = 1$, gives exactly the NURBS curve.

<NoteLink to="b-splines">De Boor's algorithm</NoteLink>, knot insertion and
<NoteLink to="bezier-curves">degree elevation</NoteLink> are applied to the homogeneous points, and the results are
projected. Projective transformations, the maps of <NoteLink to="homography">homographies</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="pinhole-camera-model">perspective cameras</NoteLink>, map a NURBS curve to a NURBS curve by acting on
the homogeneous control points. Polynomial curves are only closed under affine maps.

Derivatives follow from the <NoteLink to="derivative">quotient rule</NoteLink>. With
$\avec(u) = \sum_i w_i\pvec_iB_{i,p}(u)$, so that $\cvec = \avec/W$,

$$
\cvec' = \frac{\avec' - W'\cvec}{W}, \qquad \cvec'' = \frac{\avec'' - 2W'\cvec' - W''\cvec}{W}.
$$

## Exact conics

A polynomial curve cannot trace a circle: if $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ are polynomials with $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ for all $t$, both
must be constant. A rational quadratic can. Take the quadratic rational Bézier curve with control points
$\pvec_0 = (1, 0)$, $\pvec_1 = (1, 1)$, $\pvec_2 = (0, 1)$ and weights $1, w, 1$. With $a = (1 - t)^2$,
$b = 2wt(1 - t)$, $c = t^2$ and $D = a + b + c$,

$$
x(t) = \frac{a + b}{D}, \qquad y(t) = \frac{b + c}{D}.
$$

The curve lies on the unit circle iff $(a + b)^2 + (b + c)^2 = (a + b + c)^2$. Expanding both sides, all terms cancel
except $b^2 = 2ac$, that is

$$
4w^2t^2(1 - t)^2 = 2t^2(1 - t)^2 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w = \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}.
$$

So the weight $\sqrt2/2$ makes the curve a quarter of the unit circle. In general a circular arc of angle $\theta$ is
a rational quadratic whose middle control point is where the end tangents meet, with weight $\cos(\theta/2)$. Weights
below 1 give ellipses, 1 a parabola, above 1 hyperbolas <Cite id="piegl1997" />.

A full circle joins four quarter arcs: nine control points on a square, corner weights $\sqrt2/2$, and the knot vector
$(0, 0, 0, \tfrac14, \tfrac14, \tfrac12, \tfrac12, \tfrac34, \tfrac34, 1, 1, 1)$, whose double interior knots let
each quarter be its own rational Bézier piece.

**Worked example.** At $t = 1/2$ the quarter arc has $a = c = 1/4$ and $b = \sqrt2/4$, so

$$
\cvec(1/2) = \frac{\tfrac14(1, 0) + \tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}(1, 1) + \tfrac14(0, 1)}{\tfrac12 + \tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}}
= (0.7071, 0.7071),
$$

the point at 45°. At $t = 1/4$ the curve is at $(0.9298, 0.3681)$, an angle of 21.6°, not 22.5°. The curve is exactly
circular but its parameter is not proportional to angle. The playground's starting curve is a NURBS circle of radius 2.5
centred at $(5, 3)$, so its curvature is $1/2.5 = 0.4$ at every point.

## Trade-offs

- **Exact geometry.** Circles, cylinders, cones and surfaces of revolution are exact, which is why IGES, STEP and
  every major CAD kernel store geometry as NURBS.
- **Parameterisation.** Weights change the speed of the parameter along the curve. Two different sets of weights can
  give the same shape with different parameterisations.
- **Non-positive weights.** Zero or negative weights break the convex hull property and can put a pole on the curve.
- **Cost.** Every evaluation divides, and derivatives need the quotient rule. Rational curves have no simple closed
  form for arc length or offsets.
