<Definition>
Given data $(x_i, y_i)$, $i = 0, \dots, n$, with $x_0 < x_1 < \dots < x_n$, the **interpolating cubic spline** is the
function $S$ that is a cubic polynomial on each interval $[x_i, x_{i+1}]$, has continuous first and second <NoteLink to="derivative">derivatives</NoteLink>
at every interior node, and satisfies $S(x_i) = y_i$. These conditions leave two degrees of freedom, fixed by an
**end condition**: natural ($S'' = 0$ at both ends), clamped (given end slopes), not-a-knot (a single cubic across the
first two and the last two intervals) or periodic <Cite id="deboor1978" />.
</Definition>

## Counting the unknowns

There are $n$ intervals and $4n$ cubic coefficients. Interpolation at both ends of each interval gives $2n$
equations, and continuity of $S'$ and $S''$ at the $n - 1$ interior nodes gives $2(n - 1)$ more. That is $4n - 2$
equations for $4n$ unknowns, so two conditions remain, one at each end.

## The tridiagonal system

Parameterise the spline by its second derivatives at the nodes, the **moments** $M_i = S''(x_i)$. Let
$h_i = x_{i+1} - x_i$ and $\delta_i = (y_{i+1} - y_i)/h_i$. On $[x_i, x_{i+1}]$, $S$ is cubic, so $S''$ is linear and
interpolates the moments:

$$
S''(x) = M_i\frac{x_{i+1} - x}{h_i} + M_{i+1}\frac{x - x_i}{h_i}.
$$

This makes $S''$ continuous by construction. Integrating twice introduces a linear function, whose two constants are
fixed by $S(x_i) = y_i$ and $S(x_{i+1}) = y_{i+1}$:

$$
S(x) = M_i\frac{(x_{i+1} - x)^3}{6h_i} + M_{i+1}\frac{(x - x_i)^3}{6h_i}
+ \paren{y_i - \frac{M_i h_i^2}{6}}\frac{x_{i+1} - x}{h_i} + \paren{y_{i+1} - \frac{M_{i+1}h_i^2}{6}}\frac{x - x_i}{h_i}.
$$

Differentiating and evaluating at the two ends of the interval gives the one-sided slopes

$$
S'(x_i^+) = \delta_i - \frac{h_i}{6}(2M_i + M_{i+1}), \qquad S'(x_{i+1}^-) = \delta_i + \frac{h_i}{6}(M_i + 2M_{i+1}).
$$

The only condition left is continuity of $S'$ at each interior node, $S'(x_i^-) = S'(x_i^+)$. Using the second
formula on interval $i - 1$ and the first on interval $i$:

$$
h_{i-1}M_{i-1} + 2(h_{i-1} + h_i)M_i + h_iM_{i+1} = 6(\delta_i - \delta_{i-1}), \qquad i = 1, \dots, n - 1.
$$

These are $n - 1$ equations in $n + 1$ moments. Each involves three neighbouring moments, so the matrix is
tridiagonal.

## End conditions

- **Natural:** $M_0 = M_n = 0$. The spline continues as a straight line beyond the data. The system reduces to the
  $n - 1$ interior moments.
- **Clamped:** given slopes $S'(x_0) = s_0$ and $S'(x_n) = s_n$. From the slope formulas,
  $2h_0M_0 + h_0M_1 = 6(\delta_0 - s_0)$ and $h_{n-1}M_{n-1} + 2h_{n-1}M_n = 6(s_n - \delta_{n-1})$. scipy's
  `bc_type='clamped'` sets $s_0 = s_n = 0$.
- **Not-a-knot:** $S'''$ is continuous at $x_1$ and $x_{n-1}$, so the first two pieces are one cubic and so are the
  last two. Since $S''' = (M_{i+1} - M_i)/h_i$ on interval $i$, the condition at $x_1$ is
  $h_1M_0 - (h_0 + h_1)M_1 + h_0M_2 = 0$. Not-a-knot needs no information that the data does not contain, and is
  the default in scipy and MATLAB. With three points it gives the interpolating parabola.
- **Periodic:** $y_n = y_0$, $M_n = M_0$, and $S'$ continuous across the wrap-around. The first and last rows gain
  corner entries, and the matrix becomes cyclic tridiagonal.

**Worked example.** Take $x = 0, 1, 2, 3$ and $y = 0, 1, 0, 1$ with natural ends. Here $h_i = 1$,
$\delta = (1, -1, 1)$ and $M_0 = M_3 = 0$. The two interior equations are

$$
4M_1 + M_2 = 6(-1 - 1) = -12, \qquad M_1 + 4M_2 = 6(1 + 1) = 12,
$$

so $M_1 = -4$ and $M_2 = 4$. On $[0, 1]$, $S(x) = \tfrac{5}{3}x - \tfrac{2}{3}x^3$, and $S(0.5) = 0.75$. By symmetry
$S(1.5) = 0.5$. scipy's `CubicSpline(x, y, bc_type='natural')` returns the same moments and values.

## Solving in linear time: the Thomas algorithm

A tridiagonal system $a_iM_{i-1} + b_iM_i + c_iM_{i+1} = d_i$ is solved by <NoteLink to="lower-upper-decomposition">Gaussian elimination</NoteLink> that touches only
the three diagonals. The forward sweep eliminates $a_i$ with row $i - 1$:

$$
c_i' = \frac{c_i}{b_i - a_ic_{i-1}'}, \qquad d_i' = \frac{d_i - a_id_{i-1}'}{b_i - a_ic_{i-1}'},
$$

leaving an upper bidiagonal system $M_i + c_i'M_{i+1} = d_i'$, solved backwards by
$M_i = d_i' - c_i'M_{i+1}$. The cost is $O(n)$ operations and $O(n)$ memory, against $O(n^3)$ for dense elimination
<Cite id="press2007" />. No pivoting is needed when the matrix is strictly diagonally dominant, which the spline
system is: $2(h_{i-1} + h_i) > h_{i-1} + h_i$. Then every pivot satisfies $\abs{b_i - a_ic_{i-1}'} > \abs{c_i}$, so
$\abs{c_i'} < 1$ and errors do not grow.

## The smoothest interpolant

**Theorem (Holladay).** Among all functions $g$ with a square-integrable second derivative on $[x_0, x_n]$ that
interpolate the data, the natural cubic spline $S$ uniquely minimises $\int_{x_0}^{x_n} g''(x)^2\,dx$
<Cite id="holladay1957" />.

**Proof.** Let $e = g - S$, so $e(x_i) = 0$ at every node. Then

$$
\int g''^2 = \int S''^2 + 2\int S''e'' + \int e''^2.
$$

Integrate the middle term by parts on each interval:

$$
\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} S''e''\,dx = \brack{S''e'}_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} - \int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} S'''e'\,dx.
$$

Summed over intervals, the boundary terms telescope, because $S''$ and $e'$ are continuous, to
$S''(x_n)e'(x_n) - S''(x_0)e'(x_0)$, which is zero by the natural end conditions. On each interval $S'''$ is a constant
$c_i$, so $\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} S'''e' = c_i(e(x_{i+1}) - e(x_i)) = 0$. Hence $\int S''e'' = 0$ and

$$
\int g''^2 = \int S''^2 + \int e''^2 \ge \int S''^2,
$$

with equality only if $e'' = 0$, that is $e$ linear; $e$ vanishes at two or more nodes, so $e = 0$.

The same argument shows that a clamped spline minimises $\int g''^2$ among interpolants with the same end slopes: then
$e'(x_0) = e'(x_n) = 0$ kills the boundary terms. $\int g''^2$ approximates the bending energy of a thin elastic
strip with small slope, which is why a draughtsman's spline, a strip of wood held by weights at the data points,
takes this shape. For the worked example the natural spline has $\int S''^2 = 16$; the not-a-knot and zero-slope
clamped splines through the same points have 36.

## Accuracy

If $f$ has four continuous derivatives and the spline is clamped with the true slopes $f'(x_0)$, $f'(x_n)$, then

$$
\max\abs{f - S} \le \frac{5}{384}\,h^4\max\abs{f^{(4)}}, \qquad h = \max_i h_i,
$$

and the constant $5/384$ is the best possible <Cite id="hall1976" />. The derivatives converge too, with errors
$O(h^3)$ for $S'$ and $O(h^2)$ for $S''$. The rate is easy to see numerically. Interpolating $\sin$ on $[0, \pi]$ with
$n$ equal intervals and true end slopes gives maximum errors $6.32 \times 10^{-5}$, $3.89 \times 10^{-6}$,
$2.42 \times 10^{-7}$ and $1.51 \times 10^{-8}$ for $n = 8, 16, 32, 64$. Each halving of $h$ divides the error by 16.
The natural end condition is wrong whenever $f''$ is not zero at the ends. It then loses accuracy near the ends, to
$O(h^2)$, while the interior keeps $O(h^4)$.

Unlike polynomial interpolation, refining the nodes always helps. The bound holds for any spacing of the nodes, so the
clamped spline converges for every $f$ with four continuous derivatives as $h \to 0$, and there is no <NoteLink to="polynomial-interpolation-and-runge">Runge
phenomenon</NoteLink>.

## Pitfalls

- **Overshoot.** A cubic spline through step-like or monotone data overshoots. Through $y = 0, 0, 0, 1, 1, 1$ at
  $x = 0, \dots, 5$ the natural spline dips to $-0.109$ and rises to $1.109$. Use
  <NoteLink to="monotone-and-shape-preserving-interpolation">PCHIP</NoteLink> when the data's shape must be kept.
- **Noisy data.** An interpolant reproduces the noise. A <NoteLink to="smoothing-splines">smoothing spline</NoteLink>
  trades fidelity for smoothness.
- **Natural ends by default.** "Natural" sounds neutral but forces $S'' = 0$. Prefer not-a-knot unless the physics
  says the ends are straight.
