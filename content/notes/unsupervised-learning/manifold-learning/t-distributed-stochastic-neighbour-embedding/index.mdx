<Definition>
**t-distributed stochastic neighbour embedding** (t-SNE) maps points $\xvec_1, \dots, \xvec_n \in \reals^D$ to points
$\yvec_1, \dots, \yvec_n \in \reals^m$, usually $m = 2$ <Cite id="vandermaaten2008" />. It defines symmetric
affinities $p_{ij}$ from Gaussian neighbourhoods in the data and

$$
q_{ij} = \frac{\paren{1 + \norm{\yvec_i - \yvec_j}^2}^{-1}}{\sum_{k \ne l}\paren{1 + \norm{\yvec_k - \yvec_l}^2}^{-1}}
$$

in the embedding, and chooses the $\yvec_i$ to minimise $C = \KL(P \,\|\, Q) = \sum_{i \ne j} p_{ij}\log(p_{ij}/q_{ij})$.
</Definition>

## Affinities in the data

For each point $i$, t-SNE defines a distribution over the other points that favours near neighbours:

$$
p_{j \mid i} = \frac{\exp\paren{-\norm{\xvec_i - \xvec_j}^2/2\sigma_i^2}}{\sum_{k \ne i}\exp\paren{-\norm{\xvec_i -
\xvec_k}^2/2\sigma_i^2}}.
$$

The width $\sigma_i$ differs per point. It is set so that the distribution has a fixed **perplexity**
$2^{\entropy(P_i)}$, where $\entropy(P_i) = -\sum_j p_{j \mid i}\log_2 p_{j \mid i}$ is its
<NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink> in bits. Perplexity is an effective number of neighbours: a uniform
distribution over $k$ points has perplexity exactly $k$. A binary search on $\sigma_i$ reaches the target, because the
entropy increases with $\sigma_i$. Points in dense regions get small $\sigma_i$ and points in sparse regions large
ones, so every point has about the same number of effective neighbours. Typical perplexities are 5 to 50.

The conditional probabilities are symmetrised: $p_{ij} = (p_{j \mid i} + p_{i \mid j})/2n$. The $p_{ij}$ sum to 1 over
all pairs, and every point has $\sum_j p_{ij} > 1/2n$, so even an outlier influences the embedding.

## The crowding problem and the Student-t kernel

The original stochastic <NoteLink to="pacmap-trimap-and-largevis">neighbour embedding</NoteLink> used Gaussian affinities in the embedding as well. That fails because of
the **crowding problem**. In $D$ dimensions, the volume within distance $r$ of a point grows as $r^D$, so a point can
have many neighbours at a moderate distance, all nearly equidistant from it and from each other. In two dimensions
there is not enough room to place them all at that distance. With a Gaussian kernel in the embedding, moderately
distant points are then either crushed toward the centre or cost a great deal.

t-SNE uses a Student-t kernel with one degree of freedom, $(1 + d^2)^{-1}$, in the embedding
<Cite id="vandermaaten2008" />. Its tails are heavy: it decays as $d^{-2}$, not $e^{-d^2}$. A pair of points with a
small $p_{ij}$ can be placed far apart in the embedding without a large mismatch in $q_{ij}$. At distance 3 the
Student-t kernel is still $1/10$ of its peak, while $e^{-d^2}$ is $1.2 \times 10^{-4}$. The heavy tail lets
moderately dissimilar points spread out, which separates clusters and leaves space between them. The $d^{-2}$ decay
also means that far apart groups interact like an inverse-square law, independent of the scale of the map.

## The gradient

Write $d_{ij} = \norm{\yvec_i - \yvec_j}$, $w_{ij} = (1 + d_{ij}^2)^{-1}$ and $Z = \sum_{k \ne l} w_{kl}$, so that
$q_{ij} = w_{ij}/Z$. Since the $p_{ij}$ sum to 1,

$$
C = \sum_{k \ne l} p_{kl}\log p_{kl} - \sum_{k \ne l} p_{kl}\log w_{kl} + \log Z.
$$

The first term does not depend on the embedding. Only pairs that contain $i$ depend on $\yvec_i$, and
$\partial w_{ij}/\partial\yvec_i = -2w_{ij}^2(\yvec_i - \yvec_j)$.

- **Second term.** $-\partial\log w_{ij}/\partial\yvec_i = 2w_{ij}(\yvec_i - \yvec_j)$. Each unordered pair $\set{i, j}$
  appears twice, as $(i, j)$ and $(j, i)$, with $p_{ij} = p_{ji}$. The contribution is
  $4\sum_j p_{ij}w_{ij}(\yvec_i - \yvec_j)$.
- **Third term.** $\partial Z/\partial\yvec_i = 2\sum_j \partial w_{ij}/\partial\yvec_i = -4\sum_j w_{ij}^2(\yvec_i -
  \yvec_j)$, so $\partial\log Z/\partial\yvec_i = -4\sum_j q_{ij}w_{ij}(\yvec_i - \yvec_j)$.

Adding them,

$$
\frac{\partial C}{\partial\yvec_i} = 4\sum_{j}\paren{p_{ij} - q_{ij}}\frac{\yvec_i - \yvec_j}{1 + \norm{\yvec_i -
\yvec_j}^2}.
$$

Each other point acts on $\yvec_i$ like a spring along $\yvec_i - \yvec_j$. It attracts when the pair is more similar
in the data than in the map ($p_{ij} > q_{ij}$) and repels otherwise. The factor $w_{ij}$ weakens both forces with
distance.

## Optimisation

- **Initialisation.** Small random positions, or the first two <Gloss name="pca">principal components</Gloss> scaled down. PCA initialisation
  preserves more of the global arrangement <Cite id="kobak2021" />.
- **Early exaggeration.** For the first iterations, multiply every $p_{ij}$ by a factor such as 12. Attraction then
  dominates, clusters contract into tight groups, and they move past each other freely before the layout settles.
- **Momentum and adaptive gains** speed up gradient descent.
- **Cost.** The exact <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> costs $O(n^2)$ per iteration. Barnes–Hut t-SNE approximates the repulsive forces of
  distant groups by their centres of mass and uses only the nearest neighbours for the attractive forces, for
  $O(n\log n)$ <Cite id="vandermaaten2014" />.

## Worked example

**Calibrating one point.** A point has three neighbours at distances 1, 2 and 3, and the target perplexity is 2. The
binary search gives $\sigma = 1.156$. The conditional probabilities are $p = (0.727, 0.237, 0.036)$. Their entropy is
1 bit, so the perplexity is $2^1 = 2$: the point behaves as if it had two equally likely neighbours.

**Tails.** For a pair at embedding distance 1 the Student-t kernel is $1/2$ and the Gaussian $e^{-1} = 0.37$. At
distance 3 they are $1/10$ and $1.2 \times 10^{-4}$. Placing a weakly related pair at distance 3 instead of 1 reduces
its similarity 5-fold under the Student-t kernel, but about 3000-fold under the Gaussian.

## Reading a t-SNE plot

t-SNE preserves neighbourhoods. Most other features of the plot are unreliable <Cite id="wattenberg2016" />:

- **Cluster sizes mean little.** Each point has the same effective number of neighbours, so dense clusters expand and
  sparse ones contract.
- **Distances between clusters mean little.** The heavy tail stretches large distances by varying amounts.
- **Perplexity changes the picture.** Small perplexities break data into many small clumps, even pure noise. Compare
  several values.
- **Runs differ.** The cost is non-convex; different seeds give different layouts. Structure that appears across seeds
  and perplexities is trustworthy.

In practice, reduce very high-dimensional data to about 50 dimensions with PCA first, which removes noise and speeds up
the distance computations <Cite id="kobak2019" />.

## Pitfalls

- **No out-of-sample map.** t-SNE optimises positions for the given points. A new point requires rerunning or a
  separate method such as <NoteLink to="parametric-and-neural-embeddings">parametric t-SNE</NoteLink>.
- **Not for features.** The embedding axes have no meaning, and the embedding is for visualisation, not as input to
  downstream models.
