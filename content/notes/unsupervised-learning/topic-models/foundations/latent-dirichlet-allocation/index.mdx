<Definition>
**Latent Dirichlet allocation** (LDA) is a generative model of $D$ documents over a vocabulary of $V$ words with $K$
topics <Cite id="blei2003" />. With hyperparameters $\alpha > 0$ and $\beta > 0$:

1. For each topic $k = 1, \dots, K$, draw a distribution over words $\phivec_k \sim \Dir(\beta, \dots, \beta)$.
2. For each document $d$, draw a distribution over topics $\thetavec_d \sim \Dir(\alpha, \dots, \alpha)$.
3. For each word position $n$ in document $d$, draw a topic $z_{dn} \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$, then a word
   $w_{dn} \sim \Cat(\phivec_{z_{dn}})$.

Only the words $w_{dn}$ are observed. Inference recovers the topics $\phivec_k$, the document mixtures $\thetavec_d$
and the assignments $z_{dn}$.
</Definition>

## The model in words

A **topic** is a probability distribution over the vocabulary. A topic about genetics puts high probability on "gene",
"DNA" and "sequence". A **document** is a bag of words, with order ignored, and it is about several topics in different
proportions. Each word in it was produced by one of those topics. Documents share the same $K$ topics but mix them
differently. Fitting LDA to a corpus finds topics whose mixtures explain which words co-occur in documents.

In plate notation, an outer plate over documents $d$ contains $\thetavec_d$ and an inner plate over positions $n$,
which contains $z_{dn} \to w_{dn}$. A separate plate over topics $k$ contains $\phivec_k$, which points into every
$w_{dn}$. The hyperparameter $\alpha$ points into $\thetavec_d$ and $\beta$ into $\phivec_k$.

<LdaPlates />

The Dirichlet priors control sparsity. With $\alpha < 1$, draws of $\thetavec_d$ concentrate on few topics, so each
document is about a handful of subjects. With $\beta < 1$, each topic concentrates on few words. The
<NoteLink to="dirichlet-distribution">Dirichlet</NoteLink> is conjugate to the categorical distribution, which makes
the collapsed sampler below possible.

## Counts

Inference works with counts of the current topic assignments:

| Symbol | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| $n_{dk}$ | Number of words in document $d$ assigned to topic $k$. |
| $n_{kw}$ | Number of times word $w$ is assigned to topic $k$ across the corpus. |
| $n_k$ | Total number of words assigned to topic $k$: $n_k = \sum_w n_{kw}$. |
| $n^{\neg i}$ | Any count with the current word $i$ removed. |

## Collapsing the Dirichlets

The conditional distribution of one assignment needs the joint $p(\wvec, \zvec \mid \alpha, \beta)$ with $\thetavec$
and $\phivec$ integrated out. For one topic, the words assigned to it are independent draws from $\phivec_k$, so their
probability given $\phivec_k$ is $\prod_w\phi_{kw}^{n_{kw}}$. Integrating against the Dirichlet prior with density
$\frac{\Gamma(V\beta)}{\Gamma(\beta)^V}\prod_w\phi_{kw}^{\beta - 1}$ gives another Dirichlet integral:

$$
\int \prod_w \phi_{kw}^{n_{kw}}\,\Dir(\phivec_k \mid \beta)\,d\phivec_k = \frac{\Gamma(V\beta)}{\Gamma(\beta)^V}\,
\frac{\prod_w\Gamma(n_{kw} + \beta)}{\Gamma(n_k + V\beta)}.
$$

The integral of $\prod_w\phi_{kw}^{n_{kw} + \beta - 1}$ over the simplex is the inverse normalising constant of
$\Dir(n_{k1} + \beta, \dots, n_{kV} + \beta)$, which is $\prod_w\Gamma(n_{kw} + \beta)/\Gamma(n_k + V\beta)$. The same
argument for $\thetavec_d$ gives the factor $\prod_k\Gamma(n_{dk} + \alpha)/\Gamma(n_d + K\alpha)$ per document, up to
constants. So

$$
p(\wvec, \zvec \mid \alpha, \beta) \propto \prod_{k=1}^K\frac{\prod_w\Gamma(n_{kw} + \beta)}{\Gamma(n_k + V\beta)}
\;\prod_{d=1}^D\frac{\prod_k\Gamma(n_{dk} + \alpha)}{\Gamma(n_d + K\alpha)}.
$$

## The collapsed Gibbs update

Collapsed <NoteLink to="gibbs-sampling">Gibbs sampling</NoteLink> resamples one assignment $z_i$ at a time from its
conditional given all others
<Cite id="griffiths2004" />. Let word $i$ be word type $w$ in document $d$. Compare the joint with $z_i = k$ against the
joint with word $i$ removed. Only the factors for topic $k$ and document $d$ change, and each of their counts grows by
one. With $\Gamma(x + 1) = x\,\Gamma(x)$, each ratio of gamma functions reduces to a single count:

$$
\frac{\Gamma(n_{kw}^{\neg i} + 1 + \beta)}{\Gamma(n_{kw}^{\neg i} + \beta)} = n_{kw}^{\neg i} + \beta, \qquad
\frac{\Gamma(n_k^{\neg i} + V\beta)}{\Gamma(n_k^{\neg i} + 1 + V\beta)} = \frac{1}{n_k^{\neg i} + V\beta},
$$

and similarly $n_{dk}^{\neg i} + \alpha$ for the document. The document length $n_d$ does not depend on $k$. Hence

$$
p(z_i = k \mid \zvec^{\neg i}, \wvec) \propto \paren{n_{dk}^{\neg i} + \alpha}\,
\frac{n_{kw}^{\neg i} + \beta}{n_k^{\neg i} + V\beta}.
$$

The first factor favours topics already common in the document. The second favours topics that already use word $w$
often. One **sweep** applies the update to every word in the corpus, adjusting the counts as it goes. After burn-in, the
counts give point estimates of the collapsed parameters:

$$
\hat\phi_{kw} = \frac{n_{kw} + \beta}{n_k + V\beta}, \qquad \hat\theta_{dk} = \frac{n_{dk} + \alpha}{n_d + K\alpha}.
$$

## Variational EM

Blei, Ng and Jordan fitted LDA by variational inference instead <Cite id="blei2003" />. For each document they use the
<NoteLink to="mean-field-variational-inference">mean-field</NoteLink> family
$q(\thetavec_d, \zvec_d) = \Dir(\thetavec_d \mid \gammavec_d)\prod_n\Cat(z_{dn} \mid \rvec_{dn})$ and treat the topics
as parameters. Coordinate ascent on the evidence lower bound gives

$$
r_{dnk} \propto \phi_{k, w_{dn}}\exp\paren{\psi(\gamma_{dk})}, \qquad \gamma_{dk} = \alpha + \sum_n r_{dnk},
$$

where $\psi$ is the digamma function, since $\expect_q[\log\theta_{dk}] = \psi(\gamma_{dk}) - \psi(\sum_j\gamma_{dj})$.
The M-step sets $\phi_{kw} \propto \sum_{d,n} r_{dnk}\,\indicator[w_{dn} = w]$, the expected number of times topic $k$
produced word $w$. Variational EM is deterministic and parallelises across documents. Stochastic versions process the
corpus in mini-batches. <NoteLink to="neural-topic-models">Neural topic models</NoteLink> replace the per-document
updates of $\gammavec_d$ with an encoder network that outputs the approximate posterior in one pass.

## Worked example

**One Gibbs update.** A corpus has $V = 10$ word types and $K = 2$ topics, with $\alpha = 0.1$ and $\beta = 0.01$.
Word $i$ is type $w$ in document $d$. With word $i$ removed, the counts are $n_{d1} = 3$, $n_{d2} = 1$, $n_{1w} = 4$,
$n_{2w} = 2$, $n_1 = 20$ and $n_2 = 15$.

- Topic 1: $(3 + 0.1)(4 + 0.01)/(20 + 0.1) = 0.6185$.
- Topic 2: $(1 + 0.1)(2 + 0.01)/(15 + 0.1) = 0.1464$.

Normalising, $p(z_i = 1) = 0.809$ and $p(z_i = 2) = 0.191$. Topic 1 wins because the document already uses it more and
it already produces word $w$ more often.

**The bars corpus.** In the figure, the six true topics each put probability $1/3$ on one row or one column of a
$3 \times 3$ grid of words. Documents draw their topic mixtures from $\Dir(0.3)$. From random initial assignments, the
mean total variation distance between each learned topic and the closest true topic falls from $0.64$ to below $0.1$
after 20 sweeps and to about $0.02$ after 40 sweeps, for each of the first three seeds.

## Relatives

- <NoteLink to="probabilistic-latent-semantic-analysis">Probabilistic latent semantic analysis</NoteLink> is the same
  model without the Dirichlet priors, with $\thetavec_d$ a free parameter for each training document.
- A <NoteLink to="gaussian-mixture-model">mixture model</NoteLink> assigns a whole data point to one component. LDA
  assigns each word of a document separately, so one document can belong to several topics. This is a mixed-membership
  model.
- <NoteLink to="non-negative-matrix-factorisation">Non-negative matrix factorisation</NoteLink> of the document–word
  count matrix with the KL divergence gives topic-like factors without priors.

## Variants

Each variant changes one assumption of LDA. <NoteLink to="topic-model-family">The topic model family</NoteLink> maps them
all.

- <NoteLink to="dynamic-topic-model">Dynamic topic model</NoteLink>: topics drift across time slices by a Gaussian random
  walk on their natural parameters.
- <NoteLink to="topics-over-time">Topics over time</NoteLink>: each token also emits its timestamp from its topic's beta
  distribution, so topics have a lifespan.
- <NoteLink to="author-topic-model">Author-topic model</NoteLink>: topic mixtures belong to authors, and each word is
  attributed to one co-author.
- <NoteLink to="structural-topic-model">Structural topic model</NoteLink>: document covariates shift topic prevalence and
  topic vocabulary.
- <NoteLink to="supervised-latent-dirichlet-allocation">Supervised LDA</NoteLink>: a response is regressed on each document's topic frequencies.
- <NoteLink to="labelled-latent-dirichlet-allocation">Labelled LDA</NoteLink>: one topic per label, and each document uses only its own labels'
  topics.
- <NoteLink to="correlated-topic-model">Correlated topic model</NoteLink>: a logistic-normal prior lets topics co-occur.
- <NoteLink to="pachinko-allocation-model">Pachinko allocation</NoteLink>: super-topics over topics capture groups of
  co-occurring topics.
- <NoteLink to="hierarchical-dirichlet-process">HDP topic model</NoteLink>: an unbounded number of topics, shared through
  a hierarchical Dirichlet process.
- <NoteLink to="hierarchical-latent-dirichlet-allocation">Hierarchical LDA</NoteLink>: topics form a tree grown by the nested Chinese restaurant
  process.
- <NoteLink to="biterm-topic-model">Biterm topic model</NoteLink>: word pairs from short texts share one corpus-level
  mixture.
- Applications beyond text: <NoteLink to="smart-home-activity-discovery">smart-home sensor events</NoteLink>,
  <NoteLink to="activity-routines-from-wearables">wearable activity streams</NoteLink>,
  <NoteLink to="bag-of-visual-words-topic-models">visual words in images</NoteLink>,
  <NoteLink to="population-structure-admixture">genotypes</NoteLink>, which STRUCTURE models with the same generative
  process, and <NoteLink to="collaborative-topic-regression">recommendation of documents</NoteLink>.

## Pitfalls

- **Choosing K.** The number of topics is a modelling choice. Held-out perplexity, topic coherence and the use the
  topics are put to can disagree.
- **Label switching.** Topic numbers are arbitrary. Different runs or distant samples of one run give the same topics in
  a different order, so estimates must not be averaged across samples naively.
- **Stop words and rare words.** Very common words appear in every topic and very rare words cannot be assigned
  reliably. Remove both before fitting.
- **Bag of words.** Word order, syntax and negation are ignored.
