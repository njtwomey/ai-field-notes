<Definition>
The **hierarchical Dirichlet process** (HDP) topic model replaces LDA's fixed number of topics $K$ with a countably
infinite set <Cite id="teh2006hdp" />. Global topic weights $\betavec = (\beta_1, \beta_2, \dots)$ are drawn by
stick-breaking with concentration $\gamma$. Each document draws its own weights
$\pivec_d \sim \operatorname{DP}(\alpha_0, \betavec)$ over the same topics, each word draws a topic $z_{dn} \sim \pivec_d$,
and topic $k$ emits words from $\phivec_k \sim \Dir(\eta)$. Any finite corpus uses finitely many topics, and that number
is inferred.
</Definition>

## The problem it solves

<NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">LDA</NoteLink> needs the number of topics $K$ in advance. Too few merge
distinct subjects; too many split them or leave topics empty. Choosing $K$ by held-out likelihood means fitting many
models. A nonparametric prior puts probability on every number of topics and lets the posterior choose, with more
topics becoming available as the corpus grows.

The difficulty is sharing. Each document needs its own mixture, but all documents must mix the same topics. The HDP
achieves this with two levels of Dirichlet processes.

## The Dirichlet process

A **Dirichlet process** $\operatorname{DP}(\gamma, H)$ with concentration $\gamma > 0$ and base distribution $H$ is a
distribution over discrete distributions. The **stick-breaking construction** draws one:

$$
v_k \sim \Beta(1, \gamma), \qquad \beta_k = v_k\prod_{j<k}(1 - v_j), \qquad \phivec_k \sim H, \qquad
G = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \beta_k\,\delta_{\phivec_k}.
$$

Start with a stick of length one. Break off a fraction $v_1$; that piece is $\beta_1$. Break a fraction $v_2$ off the
remainder; that is $\beta_2$; and so on. The weights sum to one. Since $\expect[v_k] = 1/(1 + \gamma)$, the expected
weights decay geometrically, $\expect[\beta_k] = \gamma^{k-1}/(1 + \gamma)^k$: a small $\gamma$ puts most weight on the
first few atoms, a large $\gamma$ spreads it over many. The weight sequence is written $\betavec \sim \operatorname{GEM}(\gamma)$.

**The predictive rule.** Draws from $G$ repeat atoms. Integrating $G$ out, the $(n + 1)$-th draw equals an atom already
drawn $n_k$ times with probability $n_k/(n + \gamma)$ and is a fresh draw from $H$ with probability $\gamma/(n + \gamma)$.
This is the **Chinese restaurant process**: customer $n + 1$ joins a table in proportion to its occupancy or opens a new
one in proportion to $\gamma$.

<Derivation title="The predictive rule from a finite Dirichlet">
Approximate the DP by $L$ atoms with weights $\betavec \sim \Dir(\gamma/L, \dots, \gamma/L)$. After $n$ draws with counts
$n_1, \dots, n_L$, conjugacy gives the posterior $\Dir(\gamma/L + n_1, \dots, \gamma/L + n_L)$, whose mean gives the
predictive $p(\text{atom } k) = (n_k + \gamma/L)/(n + \gamma)$. As $L \to \infty$, an atom already used has probability
$n_k/(n + \gamma)$. The unused atoms, of which there are almost $L$, have total probability
$\sum_{\text{unused}}(\gamma/L)/(n + \gamma) \to \gamma/(n + \gamma)$.
</Derivation>

## Two levels

If every document drew its own $G_d \sim \operatorname{DP}(\alpha_0, H)$ with a continuous $H$, the atoms of different
documents would differ with probability one, and no topic would be shared. The HDP makes the base distribution itself a
draw from a Dirichlet process:

$$
G_0 \sim \operatorname{DP}(\gamma, H), \qquad G_d \mid G_0 \sim \operatorname{DP}(\alpha_0, G_0).
$$

$G_0 = \sum_k\beta_k\delta_{\phivec_k}$ is discrete, so every atom of $G_d$ is one of $G_0$'s atoms. Each document is a
reweighting of the same topics, $G_d = \sum_k\pi_{dk}\delta_{\phivec_k}$, with $\pivec_d \sim \operatorname{DP}(\alpha_0,
\betavec)$ and $\expect[\pivec_d] = \betavec$. The global weights $\betavec$ say how common each topic is in the corpus.
The concentration $\alpha_0$ says how closely each document follows the corpus-wide weights.

**The Chinese restaurant franchise.** Applying the predictive rule at both levels gives a sampling scheme with the
parameters integrated out. Each document is a restaurant, each word a customer, and each topic a dish on a menu shared by
the franchise. A customer sits at an occupied table in proportion to its occupancy, or at a new table in proportion to
$\alpha_0$. A new table orders an existing dish in proportion to the number of tables in the franchise already serving
it, or a new dish in proportion to $\gamma$. Popular topics are ordered by many tables in many restaurants.

## How fast the number of topics grows

In a Chinese restaurant process with concentration $\alpha$, customer $i$ opens a new table with probability
$\alpha/(\alpha + i - 1)$. By linearity of expectation the expected number of tables after $n$ customers is

$$
\expect[m \mid n] = \sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\alpha}{\alpha + i - 1} \approx \alpha\log\paren{1 + \frac{n}{\alpha}}.
$$

In the franchise, words open tables within documents at this rate with $\alpha_0$, and tables open topics across the
corpus at the same rate with $\gamma$. The number of topics therefore grows like $\gamma\log(1 + m/\gamma)$ in the
number of tables $m$, and so roughly logarithmically in the size of the corpus.

## Inference by direct assignment

Teh and co-authors give several Gibbs samplers <Cite id="teh2006hdp" />. The direct-assignment sampler keeps the global
weights $\betavec$ explicitly, for the $K$ topics in use plus the remainder $\beta_u = 1 - \sum_{k \le K}\beta_k$ for all
unused topics. Given $\betavec$, the document weights over the $K + 1$ groups are Dirichlet,
$\Dir(\alpha_0\beta_1, \dots, \alpha_0\beta_K, \alpha_0\beta_u)$, a finite Dirichlet with the same conjugacy as LDA.
Collapsing $\pivec_d$ and the topics as in the LDA sampler <Cite id="griffiths2004" /> gives

$$
p(z_i = k \mid \cdot) \propto
\begin{cases}
\paren{n_{dk}^{\neg i} + \alpha_0\beta_k}\,\dfrac{n_{kw}^{\neg i} + \eta}{n_k^{\neg i} + V\eta}, & k \le K,\\[2ex]
\alpha_0\beta_u\,\dfrac{1}{V}, & k = \text{new}.
\end{cases}
$$

LDA's symmetric $\alpha$ becomes $\alpha_0\beta_k$, so a topic common in the corpus is a priori common in every document.
A new topic has no words yet, and its predictive probability of word $w$ under $\Dir(\eta)$ is $\eta/(V\eta) = 1/V$. When
a new topic is chosen, $\beta_u$ is split by a fresh stick break, $v \sim \Beta(1, \gamma)$: the new topic takes
$v\beta_u$ and the remainder becomes $(1 - v)\beta_u$. After a sweep, the number of tables $m_{dk}$ serving topic $k$ in
each document is sampled, and the global weights are redrawn from
$(\beta_1, \dots, \beta_K, \beta_u) \sim \Dir(m_{\cdot 1}, \dots, m_{\cdot K}, \gamma)$, with $m_{\cdot k} = \sum_d m_{dk}$.

## Worked example

**Growth.** With $\alpha = 1$ and $n = 100$ customers, the expected number of tables is the harmonic number
$H_{100} = 5.19$; the approximation $\log 101 = 4.62$ underestimates it by about Euler's constant.

**One Gibbs step.** Two topics are in use, with $\betavec = (0.5, 0.3)$ and $\beta_u = 0.2$, and $\alpha_0 = 1$. In
document $d$, three other words are in topic 1 and none in topic 2. For the current word $w$, the topic factors are
$0.05$ and $0.2$, and $V = 1000$.

- Topic 1: $(3 + 0.5) \times 0.05 = 0.175$.
- Topic 2: $(0 + 0.3) \times 0.2 = 0.060$.
- New topic: $0.2 \times 0.001 = 0.0002$.

Normalised, $(0.744, 0.255, 0.0009)$. Topic 2 keeps a real chance although the document has not used it, because it is
common in the corpus and likes the word. A new topic is opened only when no existing topic explains a word.

## Assumptions

- **Exchangeable documents and words.** As in LDA, order is ignored.
- **Logarithmic growth.** The Dirichlet process implies a specific growth rate and a geometric decay of topic weights.
  Corpora whose topic frequencies follow a power law are better matched by the Pitman–Yor process.

## Pitfalls

- **$K$ is not free of assumptions.** The inferred number of topics depends strongly on $\gamma$, $\alpha_0$ and the
  topic prior $\eta$: a small $\eta$ demands sharp topics and yields more of them.
- **Slow mixing.** Topics are opened and closed one word at a time. Split–merge moves or variational methods help on
  large corpora.
- **Relatives.** <NoteLink to="hierarchical-latent-dirichlet-allocation">Hierarchical LDA</NoteLink> uses a nested Chinese restaurant process to
  arrange an unbounded number of topics in a tree.
