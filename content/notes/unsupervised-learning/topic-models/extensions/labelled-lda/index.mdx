<Definition>
**Labelled LDA** (L-LDA) is <NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">LDA</NoteLink> for a corpus in which each
document $d$ carries an observed set of labels $\Lambda_d \subseteq \set{1, \dots, K}$ <Cite id="ramage2009llda" />.
There is one topic per label. Document $d$'s topic proportions $\thetavec_d$ are drawn from a Dirichlet over the topics in
$\Lambda_d$ only, so every word of the document is assigned to one of its own labels.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A web page tagged "cooking" and "travel" contains words about both. The tags say what the page is about but not which
words belong to which tag. Labelled LDA answers that question, which Ramage and co-authors call **credit attribution**.
Each tag gets a topic, a distribution over words. Within a page, each word is assigned to whichever of the page's tags
explains it best, given how much of the page the tag already covers.

The labels fix the meaning and the number of topics. Unsupervised LDA returns topics that a person must name
afterwards; labelled LDA returns one named topic per label, estimated from the documents that carry the label.

## Generative process

With $K$ labels, vocabulary size $V$ and hyperparameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$:

1. For each label $k$, draw a topic $\phivec_k \sim \Dir(\beta)$.
2. For each document $d$ with label set $\Lambda_d$:
   1. Draw $\thetavec_d \sim \Dir(\alpha)$ over the $\abs{\Lambda_d}$ topics in $\Lambda_d$.
   2. For each word position $n$, draw $z_{dn} \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$, which is a label in $\Lambda_d$, then
      $w_{dn} \sim \Cat(\phivec_{z_{dn}})$.

Ramage and co-authors also generate the labels, each present with its own Bernoulli probability. The labels are
observed, so this part of the model does not affect the inference of topics.

## The Gibbs update

Restricting $\thetavec_d$ to $\Lambda_d$ means that the Dirichlet over the document's topics has dimension
$\abs{\Lambda_d}$. Collapsing it gives LDA's document factor over the labels in $\Lambda_d$, and topics outside
$\Lambda_d$ have probability zero. The collapsed Gibbs conditional of LDA <Cite id="griffiths2004" /> therefore becomes

$$
p(z_i = k \mid \zvec^{\neg i}, \wvec) \propto \indicator[k \in \Lambda_d]\,\paren{n_{dk}^{\neg i} + \alpha}\,
\frac{n_{kw}^{\neg i} + \beta}{n_k^{\neg i} + V\beta},
$$

with the same counts: $n_{dk}$ words of document $d$ in topic $k$, $n_{kw}$ occurrences of word $w$ in topic $k$ and
$n_k = \sum_w n_{kw}$. The sampler is LDA's with a mask, so it costs $O(\abs{\Lambda_d})$ per word instead of $O(K)$.

## The single-label case is naive Bayes

If every document has exactly one label, $\Lambda_d = \set{c_d}$, every $z_{dn}$ equals $c_d$ with probability one and
there is nothing to sample. The topic estimate is

$$
\hat\phi_{kw} = \frac{n_{kw} + \beta}{n_k + V\beta},
$$

where $n_{kw}$ counts occurrences of word $w$ in all documents labelled $k$. This is the class-conditional word
distribution of multinomial <NoteLink to="naive-bayes">naive Bayes</NoteLink> with additive smoothing $\beta$. Labelled
LDA is the extension of multinomial naive Bayes to documents with several labels, in which each word must be explained
by one of them.

## Worked example

A news article has labels $\Lambda_d = \set{\text{sport}, \text{politics}}$, with $\alpha = 0.1$, $\beta = 0.01$ and
$V = 1000$, so $V\beta = 10$. Word $i$ is "minister". With word $i$ removed, 6 of the article's words are assigned to
sport and 3 to politics. Corpus-wide, "minister" occurs 2 times in 500 sport words and 40 times in 400 politics words.

- Sport: $(6 + 0.1)(2 + 0.01)/(500 + 10) = 0.0240$.
- Politics: $(3 + 0.1)(40 + 0.01)/(400 + 10) = 0.3025$.
- Every other label: 0.

Normalised, $p(z_i = \text{politics}) = 0.926$. The word is credited to politics even though most of the article so
far is about sport, because "minister" is 25 times more frequent in the politics topic.

## Assumptions

- **One topic per label.** A label's topic is a single word distribution. A broad label such as "science" is forced
  into one distribution. Later partially labelled variants give each label several latent topics and add
  background topics shared by all documents.
- **Complete labels.** Every word must belong to one of the document's labels. Missing labels force their words onto the
  labels that are present.

## Pitfalls

- **Common words.** Without a background topic, function words are divided among labels and blur them. Remove stop
  words, or add a label carried by every document.
- **Rare labels.** A label on few documents gets a poorly estimated topic, as in naive Bayes.
- **Compared with <NoteLink to="supervised-lda">supervised LDA</NoteLink>.** Supervised LDA predicts a response from
  unsupervised topics. Labelled LDA uses labels to define the topics. Labelled LDA predicts labels for a new document
  by running inference over all $K$ topics and reading off the proportions.
