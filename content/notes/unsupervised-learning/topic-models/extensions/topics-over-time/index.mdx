<Definition>
**Topics over time** (TOT) is a topic model for timestamped documents <Cite id="wang2006tot" />. Each topic $k$ has a
word distribution $\phivec_k$ and a <NoteLink to="beta-distribution">beta distribution</NoteLink>
$\Beta(\psi_{k1}, \psi_{k2})$ over normalised time $t \in [0, 1]$. Each token draws a topic $z$ from its document's
mixture $\thetavec_d$, then a word from $\phivec_z$ and a copy of the document's timestamp from $\Beta(\psi_{z1}, \psi_{z2})$.
Time is an observed variable generated by the topics, not a chain that the topics follow.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A topic is used more in some periods than in others. A topic about a war peaks during the war and fades. A topic about
a technology rises and stays. In TOT a topic's meaning, its word distribution, is fixed for the whole corpus. What varies
with time is how often the topic is used, and TOT describes this with one smooth density per topic.

Because the timestamp is generated by the topic, time also informs the topic assignments. Two topics that use similar
words but belong to different periods are pulled apart by their timestamps. After fitting, the beta densities show each
topic's rise and fall, and the model can estimate the date of an undated document.

## Generative process

Timestamps are rescaled to $[0, 1]$ over the corpus's span. With $K$ topics, vocabulary size $V$ and hyperparameters
$\alpha$ and $\beta$:

1. For each topic $k$, draw $\phivec_k \sim \Dir(\beta)$. The time parameters $\psivec_k = (\psi_{k1}, \psi_{k2})$ are
   estimated, not drawn.
2. For each document $d$ with timestamp $t_d$, draw $\thetavec_d \sim \Dir(\alpha)$.
3. For each token $n$ of document $d$: draw $z_{dn} \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$, a word
   $w_{dn} \sim \Cat(\phivec_{z_{dn}})$ and a timestamp $t_{dn} \sim \Beta(\psi_{z_{dn}1}, \psi_{z_{dn}2})$.

Every token of document $d$ observes $t_{dn} = t_d$. The model is not consistent as a model of documents, since it
generates a separate timestamp per token, but it is consistent as a model of tokens, which is all that inference uses.

The beta distribution suits this role. On $[0, 1]$ it can be increasing, decreasing, bell-shaped or U-shaped with two
parameters, so it describes a topic that rises, fades, peaks once, or is used at both ends of the period.

## The Gibbs update

Collapsing $\thetavec$ and $\phivec$ exactly as in LDA leaves the joint of words, timestamps and assignments
<Cite id="griffiths2004" />:

$$
p(\wvec, \tvec, \zvec \mid \alpha, \beta, \psivec) = p(\wvec, \zvec \mid \alpha, \beta)
\prod_{d,n}\Beta\paren{t_{dn} \mid \psi_{z_{dn}1}, \psi_{z_{dn}2}}.
$$

The first factor is LDA's collapsed joint. The second is a product with one factor per token. Resampling token $i$ of
document $d$ with word type $w$ and timestamp $t$ changes only the LDA counts it touches and its own beta factor, so
the conditional is LDA's with one extra factor:

$$
p(z_i = k \mid \zvec^{\neg i}, \wvec, \tvec) \propto \paren{n_{dk}^{\neg i} + \alpha}\,
\frac{n_{kw}^{\neg i} + \beta}{n_k^{\neg i} + V\beta}\;
\frac{t^{\psi_{k1} - 1}(1 - t)^{\psi_{k2} - 1}}{B(\psi_{k1}, \psi_{k2})}.
$$

Here $n_{dk}$ counts tokens of document $d$ in topic $k$, $n_{kw}$ counts tokens of word $w$ in topic $k$,
$n_k = \sum_w n_{kw}$, and $B$ is the beta function.

## Updating the time parameters

After each sweep, the beta parameters of topic $k$ are refitted to the timestamps of the tokens now assigned to topic
$k$. Wang and McCallum use the method of moments. The beta distribution with parameters $(a, b)$ has mean
$m = a/(a + b)$ and variance $s^2 = m(1 - m)/(a + b + 1)$. Solving the variance equation for the sum gives
$a + b = m(1 - m)/s^2 - 1$, and the mean splits the sum:

$$
\psi_{k1} = \bar t_k\paren{\frac{\bar t_k(1 - \bar t_k)}{s_k^2} - 1}, \qquad
\psi_{k2} = (1 - \bar t_k)\paren{\frac{\bar t_k(1 - \bar t_k)}{s_k^2} - 1},
$$

where $\bar t_k$ and $s_k^2$ are the sample mean and variance of the timestamps assigned to topic $k$. The formula
needs $s_k^2 < \bar t_k(1 - \bar t_k)$, which holds for any sample inside $(0, 1)$ that is not concentrated at the two
ends.

## Worked example

**One Gibbs update.** Two topics have beta parameters $\psivec_1 = (2, 6)$, an early topic, and
$\psivec_2 = (6, 2)$, a late topic. A token with timestamp $t = 0.25$ has LDA factors
$(n_{dk} + \alpha)(n_{kw} + \beta)/(n_k + V\beta)$ equal to $0.2$ for topic 1 and $0.6$ for topic 2.

- LDA alone gives $p(z_i = 1) = 0.2/0.8 = 0.25$.
- The beta densities at $t = 0.25$ are $42 \times 0.25 \times 0.75^5 = 2.492$ for topic 1 and
  $42 \times 0.25^5 \times 0.75 = 0.0308$ for topic 2, since $1/B(2, 6) = 1/B(6, 2) = 42$.
- TOT gives $p(z_i = 1) = 0.498/(0.498 + 0.0185) = 0.964$.

The timestamp overturns the word evidence: the token is dated to the period where topic 1 is used.

**Method of moments.** If the tokens of a topic have mean timestamp $0.3$ and variance $0.01$, the sum is
$0.3 \times 0.7/0.01 - 1 = 20$, so $\psivec = (6, 14)$. This beta distribution has mean $0.3$ and variance $0.01$.

## Compared with the dynamic topic model

The <NoteLink to="dynamic-topic-model">dynamic topic model</NoteLink> is Markov in time: topics drift along a chain of
discrete slices, so a topic's words change and its neighbouring slices are dependent. TOT is non-Markov: time is a
continuous observed variable, there are no slices, and a topic's word distribution does not change. The DTM answers
"how did the language of this topic change?". TOT answers "when was this topic discussed?".

## Assumptions

- **Unimodal or U-shaped prevalence.** A beta density has at most one interior mode. A topic that recurs in several
  separated periods is forced into a broad density or split into several topics.
- **Fixed topic content.** Words do not change over time within a topic.
- **Timestamps as evidence.** Each token repeats its document's timestamp, so a long document's time is counted many
  times.

## Pitfalls

- **Time dominates in long documents.** The beta factor enters once per token, as the worked example shows it can
  outweigh word evidence. Wang and McCallum rescale the likelihood of one modality by a weighting hyperparameter, as
  speech recognisers balance acoustic and language models. A weight of $1/N_d$ on the time factor makes the per-token
  process equivalent to drawing one timestamp per document. They report the weight to be sensitive and set it by trial.
- **Degenerate beta fits.** A topic whose tokens all share one timestamp has zero variance, and the moment formula
  diverges. Cap the parameters or add a small variance floor.
