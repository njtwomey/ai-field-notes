<Definition>
The **topic model family** is the set of probabilistic models that extend
<NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">latent Dirichlet allocation</NoteLink> <Cite id="blei2003" />. LDA assumes
that a fixed number $K$ of topics, each a fixed distribution over words, are mixed in each document by independent
Dirichlet proportions, with nothing observed but the words. Each member of the family relaxes one of these assumptions or
adds an observed variable, and keeps the rest of LDA, including its inference machinery where it can.
</Definition>

## LDA's assumptions

LDA generates each document by drawing $\thetavec_d \sim \Dir(\alpha)$ and then, for each word, a topic
$z \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$ and a word $w \sim \Cat(\phivec_z)$. Six assumptions are packed into this:

1. **Topics are static.** $\phivec_k$ is the same in every document, whenever it was written.
2. **The mixture belongs to the document.** Nothing about the author or the source enters $\thetavec_d$.
3. **Only words are observed.** There is no label, rating or response.
4. **Topics are independent.** A Dirichlet draw is a set of independent gamma variables divided by their sum, so the
   only dependence between topic proportions is the negative correlation from summing to one, fixed by their means.
   The model cannot represent topics that tend to appear together more often than that.
5. **There are exactly $K$ topics, in a flat list.**
6. **Documents are long enough** to estimate a $K$-dimensional mixture from their words.

## What the changes have in common

**Conjugacy decides the inference.** Models that keep every Dirichlet–categorical pair conjugate (topics over time, the
author-topic model, labelled LDA, pachinko allocation, the biterm model) are fitted by collapsed Gibbs samplers whose
updates are LDA's update with one factor changed. The factor is always a ratio of counts, because collapsing a Dirichlet
turns $\Gamma(n + 1 + a)/\Gamma(n + a)$ into $n + a$. Models that replace a Dirichlet with a Gaussian (the dynamic,
correlated and structural topic models) lose conjugacy and use variational approximations with a bound or a Laplace
approximation for the log-sum-exp of the softmax.

**The logistic normal carries Gaussian structure into topic models.** Drift over time, correlation between topics and covariate effects
are all Gaussian ideas. Putting a Gaussian on $\etavec_d$ and mapping it through a softmax lets a topic model borrow
them: a random walk, a covariance matrix, a <NoteLink to="linear-regression">linear regression</NoteLink>.

**Where metadata enters matters.** Upstream metadata (authors, covariates, labels) shapes the prior of the mixture.
Downstream metadata (a response) is generated from the assignments. Upstream models describe how metadata explains
topics; downstream models predict metadata from text.

## Beyond this family

- **Applications outside text.** The same model applies whenever data are bags of discrete tokens grouped into units.
  <NoteLink to="smart-home-activity-discovery">Sensor events in a smart home</NoteLink>,
  <NoteLink to="bag-of-visual-words-topic-models">visual words in images</NoteLink>,
  <NoteLink to="population-structure-admixture">alleles in genomes</NoteLink> and
  <NoteLink to="collaborative-topic-regression">users' libraries of articles</NoteLink> all have topic models.
- **Neural topic models.** <NoteLink to="neural-topic-models">Neural topic models</NoteLink> keep the generative story
  but fit it with an encoder network that predicts each document's posterior, and some place topics in a word-embedding
  space.
