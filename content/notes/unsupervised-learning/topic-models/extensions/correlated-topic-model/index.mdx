<Definition>
The **correlated topic model** (CTM) is <NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">LDA</NoteLink> with a logistic-normal
prior on each document's topic proportions <Cite id="blei2005ctm" /> <Cite id="blei2007ctm" />. Each document draws
$\etavec_d \sim \Gauss(\muvec, \Sigmamat)$ in $\reals^K$ and sets $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\etavec_d)$,
with entries $\theta_{dk} = \exp(\eta_{dk})/\sum_j \exp(\eta_{dj})$. Words are then generated as in LDA. The covariance
$\Sigmamat$ encodes which topics tend to appear together.
</Definition>

## Why the Dirichlet cannot correlate topics

Under a Dirichlet with parameters $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_K$ and $\alpha_0 = \sum_k\alpha_k$, the moments are
$\expect[\theta_i] = \alpha_i/\alpha_0$ and $\expect[\theta_i\theta_j] = \alpha_i\alpha_j/(\alpha_0(\alpha_0 + 1))$ for
$i \ne j$. Subtracting the product of the means gives

$$
\cov(\theta_i, \theta_j) = \frac{\alpha_i\alpha_j}{\alpha_0(\alpha_0 + 1)} - \frac{\alpha_i\alpha_j}{\alpha_0^2}
= -\frac{\alpha_i\alpha_j}{\alpha_0^2(\alpha_0 + 1)} < 0.
$$

Every pair of proportions is negatively correlated, by an amount fixed by the means. With
$\var(\theta_i) = \alpha_i(\alpha_0 - \alpha_i)/(\alpha_0^2(\alpha_0 + 1))$, the correlation is
$-\sqrt{\alpha_i\alpha_j/((\alpha_0 - \alpha_i)(\alpha_0 - \alpha_j))}$. For a symmetric Dirichlet this is $-1/(K - 1)$
for every pair and every concentration. The Dirichlet has one degree of freedom per topic, and all of them are used to
set the means.

<Derivation title="The Dirichlet moments">
The Dirichlet density is $\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_0)}{\prod_k\Gamma(\alpha_k)}\prod_k\theta_k^{\alpha_k - 1}$. Multiplying by
$\theta_i\theta_j$ raises $\alpha_i$ and $\alpha_j$ by one, so the integral is a ratio of normalising constants:
$\expect[\theta_i\theta_j] = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha_0)}{\Gamma(\alpha_0 + 2)}\,\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_i + 1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_i)}
\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_j + 1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_j)} = \frac{\alpha_i\alpha_j}{\alpha_0(\alpha_0 + 1)}$. The same argument with
$\theta_i$ gives $\alpha_i/\alpha_0$, and with $\theta_i^2$ gives $\alpha_i(\alpha_i + 1)/(\alpha_0(\alpha_0 + 1))$, from
which the variance follows.
</Derivation>

In real corpora topics do co-occur. A document about genetics is more likely to also be about disease than about
astronomy. LDA has no parameter for this. The logistic normal has a full covariance matrix: a positive
$\Sigma_{ij}$ makes documents with a high $\eta_{di}$ also have a high $\eta_{dj}$.

## Generative process

With $K$ topics, mean $\muvec \in \reals^K$ and covariance $\Sigmamat \in \reals^{K \times K}$:

1. For each topic $k$, draw or fix $\phivec_k$, a distribution over the $V$ words.
2. For each document $d$, draw $\etavec_d \sim \Gauss(\muvec, \Sigmamat)$ and set
   $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\etavec_d)$.
3. For each word position $n$, draw $z_{dn} \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$ and $w_{dn} \sim \Cat(\phivec_{z_{dn}})$.

The softmax is unchanged by adding a constant to every entry of $\etavec_d$, so one coordinate is often fixed at zero.

## Inference

The logistic normal is not conjugate to the <NoteLink to="categorical-distribution">categorical distribution</NoteLink>, so neither the collapsed Gibbs sampler nor the
closed-form variational updates of LDA apply. Blei and Lafferty use a
<NoteLink to="mean-field-variational-inference">mean-field</NoteLink> approximation per document
<Cite id="blei2007ctm" />:

$$
q(\etavec_d, \zvec_d) = \prod_{k}\Gauss(\eta_{dk} \mid \lambda_k, \nu_k^2)\prod_n\Cat(z_{dn} \mid \rvec_n).
$$

The <NoteLink to="evidence-lower-bound">evidence lower bound</NoteLink> contains
$\expect_q[\log\theta_{dk}] = \lambda_k - \expect_q\brack{\log\sum_j\exp\eta_{dj}}$, which has no closed form. The
logarithm is concave, so it lies below each of its <NoteLink to="derivative">tangent lines</NoteLink>. The tangent at $\zeta > 0$ gives
$\log x \le x/\zeta - 1 + \log\zeta$ for all $x > 0$. The Gaussian <Gloss name="mgf">moment generating function</Gloss> gives
$\expect_q[\exp\eta_j] = \exp(\lambda_j + \nu_j^2/2)$. Together,

$$
\expect_q\brack{\log\sum_j e^{\eta_{dj}}} \le \frac{1}{\zeta}\sum_j\exp\paren{\lambda_j + \nu_j^2/2} - 1 + \log\zeta.
$$

Since the expectation enters the bound with a minus sign, this upper bound gives a lower bound on the evidence lower
bound, which is still a valid objective. Coordinate ascent then cycles through:

- **$\zeta$.** Setting the derivative $-\sum_j\exp(\lambda_j + \nu_j^2/2)/\zeta^2 + 1/\zeta$ to zero gives
  $\hat\zeta = \sum_j\exp(\lambda_j + \nu_j^2/2)$, where the bound touches.
- **$\rvec_n$.** As in LDA, $r_{nk} \propto \phi_{k,w_n}\exp(\lambda_k)$. The normalising term does not depend on $k$.
- **$\lambdavec$ and $\nuvec$.** No closed form. The objective is smooth and concave in $\lambdavec$; it is maximised by
  <Gloss name="cg">conjugate gradient</Gloss>, and each $\nu_k^2 > 0$ by <NoteLink to="newtons-method">Newton's method</NoteLink>.

The M-step fits the Gaussian prior to the variational posteriors of all $D$ documents:

$$
\hat\muvec = \frac{1}{D}\sum_d\lambdavec_d, \qquad
\hat\Sigmamat = \frac{1}{D}\sum_d\paren{\diag(\nuvec_d^2) + (\lambdavec_d - \hat\muvec)(\lambdavec_d - \hat\muvec)\transpose},
$$

and re-estimates the topics as in LDA, $\phi_{kw} \propto \sum_{d,n} r_{dnk}\,\indicator[w_{dn} = w]$. The covariance
update adds the posterior uncertainty $\diag(\nuvec_d^2)$ to the spread of the means, which is the expected <NoteLink to="matrix-multiplication">outer product</NoteLink>
under $q$.

## Worked example

**The Dirichlet correlation.** With $K = 5$ and a symmetric Dirichlet, every pair of proportions has correlation
$-1/(K - 1) = -0.25$, whatever the concentration. The left panel of the figure measures about this value for every
$\alpha$.

**The bound.** A document with $K = 3$ has variational parameters $\lambdavec = (0, 1, -1)$ and
$\nuvec^2 = (0.5, 0.5, 0.5)$. The optimal $\hat\zeta = e^{0.25}(1 + e + e^{-1}) = 5.247$, and the bound on
$\expect_q[\log\sum_j e^{\eta_j}]$ is $\log\hat\zeta = 1.658$. A Monte Carlo estimate of the expectation is $1.526$.
Plugging in the means, $\log\sum_j e^{\lambda_j} = 1.408$, underestimates it, as Jensen's inequality requires for a
<NoteLink to="convex-sets-and-functions">convex function</NoteLink>.

## Assumptions

- **Pairwise, Gaussian co-occurrence.** Correlation between topics is captured by one covariance matrix in <NoteLink to="exponential-and-logarithm">log space</NoteLink>.
- **Topic content fixed.** Only the prior on proportions changes; topics are as in LDA.

## Pitfalls

- **Cost.** $\Sigmamat$ has $K(K + 1)/2$ parameters, and inference needs numerical optimisation per document. The CTM is
  slower than LDA and harder to scale to hundreds of topics.
- **Sparsity.** A logistic normal does not produce the near-sparse proportions that a Dirichlet with $\alpha < 1$ does.
- **Reading $\Sigmamat$.** Correlations of the log-proportions are not correlations of the proportions. The softmax
  couples all topics, so even $\Sigma_{ij} = 0$ gives a negative correlation between $\theta_i$ and $\theta_j$, as the
  right panel shows at $\rho = 0$.
- **Relatives.** The <NoteLink to="pachinko-allocation-model">pachinko allocation model</NoteLink> captures correlation
  through super-topics instead of a covariance. The <NoteLink to="structural-topic-model">structural topic model</NoteLink>
  makes the mean $\muvec$ depend on document covariates.
