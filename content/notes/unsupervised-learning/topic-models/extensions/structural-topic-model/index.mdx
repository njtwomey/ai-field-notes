<Definition>
The **structural topic model** (STM) is a topic model in which observed document covariates affect topics
<Cite id="roberts2014stm" /> <Cite id="roberts2016stm" />. **Prevalence** covariates $\xvec_d \in \reals^P$ set the mean
of logistic-normal topic proportions: $\etavec_d \sim \Gauss(\Gammamat\transpose\xvec_d, \Sigmamat)$ and
$\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\etavec_d)$, with a $P \times K$ coefficient matrix $\Gammamat$. A categorical
**content** covariate $y_d$ changes the word distribution of every topic through additive deviations in log space. With
no covariates the STM is the <NoteLink to="correlated-topic-model">correlated topic model</NoteLink>.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Social scientists fit topic models to test hypotheses. Do Republican and Democratic senators write about the same issues
in the same proportions? Do respondents in a treatment group mention different reasons in open-ended answers? Fitting
LDA and then regressing the topic proportions on the covariates is the common two-stage approach, but the topics were
fitted without knowledge of the covariates, and the uncertainty of the proportions is ignored in the regression.

The STM puts the covariates inside the model. Two questions are separated:

- **How much** a document talks about each topic: prevalence, driven by $\xvec_d$.
- **How** a document talks about a topic: content. Liberal and conservative writers discuss immigration with different
  words, and a content covariate gives the topic a version for each group.

## Generative process

For each document $d$ with prevalence covariates $\xvec_d$ and content covariate $y_d \in \set{1, \dots, A}$:

1. Draw $\etavec_d \sim \Gauss(\Gammamat\transpose\xvec_d, \Sigmamat)$, with the $K$-th coordinate fixed at zero for
   identifiability, and set $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\etavec_d)$.
2. Form the document's topic–word distributions. For topic $k$ and word $v$,
   $$
   \beta_{d,k,v} \propto \exp\paren{m_v + \kappa^{(t)}_{k,v} + \kappa^{(c)}_{y_d,v} + \kappa^{(i)}_{y_d,k,v}},
   $$
   where $m_v$ is the baseline log frequency of word $v$, $\kappavec^{(t)}$ are topic deviations,
   $\kappavec^{(c)}$ covariate deviations and $\kappavec^{(i)}$ their interactions.
3. For each word position $n$, draw $z_{dn} \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$ and $w_{dn} \sim \Cat(\betavec_{d, z_{dn}})$.

The content model is a sparse additive generative model. Every topic, every covariate level and every interaction adds a
vector of log-frequency deviations to one shared baseline. Deviations have a sparsity-inducing prior, so most are zero:
a topic's vocabulary differs between groups only in the words that the data support. Without a content covariate,
$\beta_{k,v} \propto \exp(m_v + \kappa^{(t)}_{k,v})$, which is a reparameterised topic.

A related earlier model, Dirichlet-multinomial regression, lets covariates set the parameters of a Dirichlet prior,
$\alpha_{dk} = \exp(\xvec_d\transpose\lambdavec_k)$ <Cite id="mimno2008dmr" />. It keeps LDA's conjugacy but, like LDA,
cannot express correlations between topics.

## Inference

The STM is fitted by variational EM with a Laplace approximation for the logistic normal
<Cite id="roberts2016stm" />.

**E-step.** For each document, the topic assignments are summed out, which gives the log joint of $\etavec_d$ and the
words:

$$
f(\etavec_d) = -\tfrac12(\etavec_d - \muvec_d)\transpose\Sigmamat^{-1}(\etavec_d - \muvec_d) +
\sum_v c_{dv}\log\sum_k\theta_{dk}\,\beta_{d,k,v} + \text{const},
$$

with $\muvec_d = \Gammamat\transpose\xvec_d$ and $c_{dv}$ the count of word $v$ in document $d$. The
<NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">Laplace approximation</NoteLink> finds the mode $\hat\etavec_d$ by quasi-Newton
optimisation and sets $q(\etavec_d) = \Gauss(\hat\etavec_d, \Hmat_d^{-1})$, where $\Hmat_d$ is the negative Hessian of
$f$ at the mode. The responsibilities of the topics for word $v$ are
$r_{dvk} \propto \hat\theta_{dk}\,\beta_{d,k,v}$.

**M-step.**

- **Prevalence.** $\Gammamat$ is the solution of a regression of the modes $\hat\etavec_d$ on $\xvec_d$, one column per
  topic, with a Gaussian prior on the coefficients. It is linear regression because $\etavec_d$ is Gaussian with mean
  $\Gammamat\transpose\xvec_d$.
- **Covariance.** As in the CTM, $\hat\Sigmamat = \frac1D\sum_d\paren{\Hmat_d^{-1} + (\hat\etavec_d - \muvec_d)(\hat\etavec_d -
  \muvec_d)\transpose}$.
- **Content.** Each $\kappavec$ is fitted by penalised multinomial logistic regression of the expected word counts
  $\sum_d c_{dv}r_{dvk}$ on topic and covariate indicators.

**Estimating effects.** The quantity of interest is often the effect of a covariate on the expected proportion of a topic.
The R package stm estimates it by the method of composition: draw proportions from the approximate posterior of each
document, regress them on the covariates, and repeat, so that the uncertainty of the proportions enters the standard
errors <Cite id="roberts2019stm" />.

## Worked example

Three topics, with $\eta_3 = 0$. One binary covariate $x$ (0 or 1) and an intercept. The coefficients are: topic 1,
intercept $0$, slope $1$; topic 2, intercept $0.5$, slope $-0.5$.

- For $x = 0$, the prior mean is $\muvec = (0, 0.5, 0)$, and its softmax is $(0.274, 0.452, 0.274)$.
- For $x = 1$, $\muvec = (1, 0, 0)$, and its softmax is $(0.576, 0.212, 0.212)$.

Switching the covariate on doubles the share of topic 1 and halves topic 2 in a typical document. The expected
proportions differ somewhat from these values, since the softmax of the mean is not the mean of the softmax.

## Assumptions

- **Linear effects in log-ratio space.** Covariates act additively on $\etavec_d$. Nonlinear effects need spline bases in
  $\xvec_d$.
- **Categorical content covariate.** One discrete covariate changes the vocabulary.
- **Covariates are exogenous.** A covariate that is itself caused by the text violates the model's direction of
  dependence.

## Pitfalls

- **Causal claims.** A prevalence coefficient is an association, as in any regression. A causal reading needs a design,
  such as randomised treatment.
- **Topics shaped by the hypothesis.** Because covariates influence which topics are found, a significant effect can
  partly reflect topics that the covariate helped to create. Validating topics on held-out data, or across
  specifications, reduces this risk.
- **Relatives.** <NoteLink to="supervised-latent-dirichlet-allocation">Supervised LDA</NoteLink> puts metadata downstream as a response. The
  <NoteLink to="author-topic-model">author-topic model</NoteLink> is a special case in spirit, with authorship as the
  only prevalence covariate.
