<Definition>
**Hierarchical latent Dirichlet allocation** (hierarchical LDA, hLDA) is a topic model whose topics form a tree <Cite id="blei2010ncrp" />. Every node of the
tree is a topic $\phivec \sim \Dir(\eta)$. Each document draws a path $\cvec_d = (c_{d1}, \dots, c_{dL})$ from the root to
depth $L$ with the **nested Chinese restaurant process** (nCRP), draws proportions $\thetavec_d$ over the $L$ levels,
and generates each word from the topic at a level $z_{dn} \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$ on its path. The width of the tree is
unbounded and is inferred.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Topics in a corpus are not a flat list. "Neuroscience" contains "vision" and "memory", and every scientific paper uses
words such as "result" and "method". <NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">LDA</NoteLink> puts all of these side by
side, and the general words pollute every topic. hLDA places each document on one branch of a tree. The words a document
shares with all documents are explained by the root. The words it shares with documents on the same branch are explained
by the nodes they have in common. Its most specific words are explained by its leaf.

The tree is not fixed in advance. A new document can follow an existing branch or open a new one at any level, so the
number of children of each node grows with the data.

## The nested Chinese restaurant process

A **Chinese restaurant process** (CRP) with concentration $\gamma$ seats customers one at a time. A customer joins an
occupied table with probability proportional to the number of people there, or opens a new table with probability
proportional to $\gamma$. After $m$ customers, with $m_j$ at table $j$,

$$
p(\text{table } j) = \frac{m_j}{m + \gamma}, \qquad p(\text{new table}) = \frac{\gamma}{m + \gamma}.
$$

The **nested** CRP has a restaurant at every node of an infinite tree. Each table of a restaurant points to another
restaurant one level down. A document is a tourist who spends $L$ nights in the city. On the first night it eats at the
root restaurant and chooses a table by the CRP rule; the table sends it to a restaurant for the second night; and so on.
Its sequence of tables is a path of length $L$. Documents that choose the same tables share the upper part of their
paths and diverge below.

## Generative process

With depth $L$, nCRP concentration $\gamma$, level prior $\alpha$ and topic prior $\eta$:

1. For each node $k$ of the infinite tree, draw a topic $\phivec_k \sim \Dir(\eta)$.
2. For each document $d$:
   1. Draw a path $\cvec_d \sim \operatorname{nCRP}(\gamma)$ from the root to depth $L$, given the paths of the previous
      documents.
   2. Draw level proportions $\thetavec_d \sim \Dir(\alpha)$ over $L$ levels.
   3. For each word position $n$, draw a level $z_{dn} \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$ and a word
      $w_{dn} \sim \Cat(\phivec_{c_{d, z_{dn}}})$.

In the journal version the depth is also unbounded: the level proportions come from a stick-breaking prior over
infinitely many levels. The fixed-depth version is common in practice.

## Gibbs sampling

The topics and level proportions are integrated out, as in LDA's collapsed sampler <Cite id="griffiths2004" />.
Two kinds of variable are resampled.

**Levels.** Given the path, a word's level is resampled exactly as an LDA topic, with the $L$ nodes on the path as the
candidate topics. Let $n_{d\ell}$ be the number of words of document $d$ at level $\ell$, and let $n_{kw}$ and $n_k$ be
the word counts of node $k$. For word $i$ of type $w$,

$$
p(z_i = \ell \mid \cdot) \propto \paren{n_{d\ell}^{\neg i} + \alpha}\,
\frac{n_{c_{d\ell}, w}^{\neg i} + \eta}{n_{c_{d\ell}}^{\neg i} + V\eta}.
$$

**Paths.** A whole path is resampled at once. By <NoteLink to="bayes-theorem">Bayes' theorem</NoteLink>, its conditional is the nCRP prior times the likelihood
of the document's words:

$$
p(\cvec_d \mid \cdot) \propto p(\cvec_d \mid \cvec_{\neg d})\;p(\wvec_d \mid \cvec_d, \zvec_d, \wvec_{\neg d}, \zvec_{\neg d}).
$$

The prior is the product of the CRP probabilities of the table chosen at each level, computed from the other documents'
paths. The likelihood factorises over levels, because the words at level $\ell$ are generated by node $c_{d\ell}$ only.
The words at one level are a bag of draws from a categorical with a Dirichlet prior, whose posterior given the other
documents' words at that node is again Dirichlet. The Dirichlet-multinomial marginal gives, for each level,

$$
\frac{\Gamma\paren{n_{k}^{\neg d} + V\eta}}{\prod_w\Gamma\paren{n_{kw}^{\neg d} + \eta}}\;
\frac{\prod_w\Gamma\paren{n_{kw}^{\neg d} + n_{d\ell w} + \eta}}{\Gamma\paren{n_{k}^{\neg d} + n_{d\ell} + V\eta}},
\qquad k = c_{d\ell},
$$

where $n_{d\ell w}$ counts word $w$ among the document's words at level $\ell$. The factor for each level is the ratio
of the normalising constants of the Dirichlet posteriors after and before adding the document's words, which is the
integral used to collapse LDA. The product is evaluated for every existing path and for every way of branching off
into a new subtree; a new node has zero counts.

## Worked example

Nine documents have been placed as in the figure, and $\gamma = 1$. A new document chooses its first-level table:

- cell/gene: $5/(9 + 1) = 0.5$; neuron/brain: $0.3$; star/galaxy: $0.1$; a new branch: $0.1$.

Below cell/gene, five documents have been: four went to DNA/sequence and one to antigen/immune. The next choice has
probabilities $4/6$, $1/6$ and $1/6$ for a new branch. The prior probability of the path root → cell/gene → DNA/sequence
is $0.5 \times 4/6 = 0.333$. The prior of opening a new branch at the first level, which fixes a new second level too,
is $0.1$. The likelihood term then weighs these paths by how well the nodes on each path explain the document's words
at each level.

## Assumptions

- **One path per document.** A document mixes topics only along one branch. A paper that spans genetics and astronomy
  must explain one of them at a shared ancestor.
- **Rich get richer.** The CRP favours popular branches, which gives a few wide branches and many thin ones.
- **Level means generality.** The model assumes general words belong at shallow levels. With $\alpha$ badly set,
  specific words can drift upwards.

## Pitfalls

- **Slow mixing.** Moving a document to another branch changes many counts at once, and the sampler can stay in one tree
  for a long time. Several restarts with different seeds are advisable.
- **Hyperparameters shape the tree.** $\gamma$ controls branching, $\eta$ controls how sharp and hence how numerous
  topics are, and $\alpha$ controls how words divide between levels.
- **Relatives.** The <NoteLink to="hierarchical-dirichlet-process">hierarchical Dirichlet process (HDP) topic model</NoteLink> gives an unbounded flat set
  of topics. The <NoteLink to="pachinko-allocation-model">pachinko allocation model</NoteLink> uses a fixed <Gloss name="dag">directed
  acyclic graph</Gloss> of topics and lets each word take its own path.
