<Definition>
A **contextualised topic model** (CTM) is a <NoteLink to="prodlda">ProdLDA</NoteLink> <NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">topic model</NoteLink> whose encoder reads a
contextual embedding of the document's raw text, produced by a pretrained sentence encoder such as Sentence-BERT
(SBERT) <Cite id="bianchi2021ctm" /> <Cite id="reimers2019" />. Two variants differ only in the encoder's input:

- **CombinedTM** concatenates the <NoteLink to="bag-of-words">bag of words</NoteLink> $\wvec_d$ with a projection of the sentence embedding $\svec_d$.
- **ZeroShotTM** uses the sentence embedding $\svec_d$ alone <Cite id="bianchi2021zeroshot" />.

In both, the decoder is ProdLDA's and reconstructs the bag of words, so topics remain distributions over a fixed
vocabulary.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A bag of words discards word order, syntax and the senses that context gives a word. "The bank raised rates" and "the
river bank flooded" share the word "bank" and nothing else. Every earlier <NoteLink to="neural-topic-models">neural topic model</NoteLink>'s encoder reads only
counts, so it has to infer a document's topics from co-occurrence statistics of the training corpus alone. A sentence
encoder pretrained on large text collections maps a passage to a vector $\svec_d \in \reals^E$ that already encodes
what the passage is about, including word order and synonyms. Feeding $\svec_d$ to the topic model's encoder gives it a
better starting point for inferring $\thetavec_d$.

The encoder is the only part that changes. The topics are still read off the decoder, which must reproduce the
document's words, so a topic is still a list of vocabulary words. The pretrained encoder improves *inference*; the
generative model is untouched.

## The objective

The model is ProdLDA with a different conditioning variable in the approximate posterior. Let $\evec_d$ be the
encoder's input:

$$
\evec_d = \begin{cases} \brack{\wvec_d;\; \Wmat\svec_d + \bvec} & \text{CombinedTM,} \\ \svec_d & \text{ZeroShotTM,}
\end{cases}
$$

where $[\cdot\,;\cdot]$ is concatenation and $\Wmat \in \reals^{V \times E}$ projects the sentence embedding to the
vocabulary size. The encoder outputs $q_{\phivec}(\hvec_d \mid \evec_d) = \Gauss(\muvec_{\phivec}(\evec_d),
\diag\sigmavec^2_{\phivec}(\evec_d))$, and with $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$ the <Gloss name="elbo">evidence lower bound</Gloss>
is

$$
\Lcal_d = \expect_{q_{\phivec}}\brack{\sum_{v=1}^V w_{dv}\log\operatorname{softmax}\paren{\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat}_v} -
\KL\paren{q_{\phivec}(\hvec_d \mid \evec_d) \,\|\, \Gauss(\muvec_0, \Sigmamat_0)} ,
$$

with $\Gauss(\muvec_0, \Sigmamat_0)$ the <NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">Laplace approximation</NoteLink> of a Dirichlet prior in the softmax basis. It is a valid
lower bound on $\log p(\wvec_d)$ whatever $q$ is conditioned on, because the ELBO holds for every distribution over
$\hvec_d$. The true posterior depends on the document only through its counts $\wvec_d$. The sentence embedding helps
because it makes the amortised map from document to posterior easier to learn, not because the posterior needs the
information it adds. The sentence encoder is frozen; only the projection $\Wmat$, the encoder network and the topics
$\Bmat$ are trained.

## Zero-shot cross-lingual topic modelling

ZeroShotTM's encoder never sees the bag of words, so at test time it needs only a sentence embedding. If the sentence
encoder is **multilingual**, trained so that a passage and its translation map to nearby vectors, the topic model can
be trained on documents in one language and applied to documents in another <Cite id="bianchi2021zeroshot" />:

1. Train ZeroShotTM on English documents. The decoder learns topics over the English vocabulary.
2. Embed an Italian document with the same multilingual encoder and pass the embedding through the trained encoder to
   get $\thetavec_d$.
3. Describe the Italian document by its topic proportions, whose topics are lists of English words.

The bag of words is needed only to train the decoder. Bianchi et al. tested this on Wikipedia abstracts of the same
entities in five languages: they compared the topics predicted for an entity's abstracts in Portuguese, Italian, French
and German with those predicted for its English abstract, and found them coherent and stable across languages
<Cite id="bianchi2021zeroshot" />. The topic model never
saw a word of those languages.

## Assumptions

- **A frozen sentence encoder whose similarity matches topical similarity.** The encoder's notion of "about the same
  thing" is inherited from its training data and objective.
- **ProdLDA's generative model.** Documents are bags of words given $\thetavec_d$, and the prior is a logistic normal.
- **Cross-lingual alignment.** Zero-shot transfer assumes that the multilingual encoder maps translations to nearby
  vectors, and that the test language is one it was trained on.

## Pitfalls

- **Truncation.** Sentence encoders read a bounded number of tokens. The part of a long document beyond the limit is
  lost to the encoder, although it still counts in the bag of words the decoder reconstructs <Cite id="bianchi2021ctm" />.
  Split long documents or embed passages separately.
- **Topics stay in the training language.** ZeroShotTM describes a German document with English topic words. It does not
  learn German topics.
- **Preprocessing splits.** The sentence encoder should read the unprocessed text, with <NoteLink to="stop-words">stop words</NoteLink> and punctuation; the
  bag of words for the decoder should be preprocessed as for any topic model. Feeding the preprocessed text to both
  discards the context the encoder is there to use.
- **The encoder is not the model.** Better sentence embeddings improve the posterior approximation. They cannot make the
  decoder express a topic that a bag-of-words ProdLDA cannot express.
