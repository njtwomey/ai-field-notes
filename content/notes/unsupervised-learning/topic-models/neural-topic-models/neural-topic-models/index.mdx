<Definition>
A **neural topic model** is a topic model in which neural networks either define the generative model or perform
inference, or both. In the dominant family, an **encoder** network maps a document's <NoteLink to="bag-of-words">bag of words</NoteLink> $\wvec_d$ to an
approximate posterior over a latent vector, a softmax turns a sample of that vector into topic proportions
$\thetavec_d$ on the simplex, and a **decoder** turns $\thetavec_d$ into a distribution over the vocabulary. Encoder
and decoder are trained together by maximising the evidence lower bound, as in a
<NoteLink to="variational-autoencoder">variational autoencoder</NoteLink> <Cite id="miao2016nvdm" />
<Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" />. Other members of the family place topics in a word-embedding space, or obtain
topics by clustering <NoteLink to="document-embeddings">document embeddings</NoteLink> from a pretrained <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss> <Cite id="dieng2020etm" />
<Cite id="grootendorst2022bertopic" />.
</Definition>

## Why neural inference

<NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">Latent Dirichlet allocation</NoteLink> (LDA) is fitted by <NoteLink to="gibbs-sampling">collapsed Gibbs
sampling</NoteLink> or by mean-field <NoteLink to="expectation-maximisation-as-variational-inference">variational EM</NoteLink>. Both methods are derived by hand for one model. Mean-field EM gives every
document its own variational parameters $\gammavec_d$, found by an inner loop of coordinate updates, and repeats the
loop for every new document. A change to the model, such as a new likelihood or a new prior, needs new update
equations.

Neural inference replaces both the per-document inner loop and the hand-derived update equations.

- **Amortisation.** One encoder network $f_{\phivec}$ outputs the variational parameters of any document in a single
  forward pass. The per-document optimisation becomes a regression from $\wvec_d$ to the parameters, learnt once over
  the corpus (see <NoteLink to="amortised-inference">amortised inference</NoteLink>).
- **Black-box <NoteLink to="gradient">gradients</NoteLink>.** The evidence lower bound is estimated by Monte Carlo and differentiated by automatic
  differentiation through a <NoteLink to="reparameterisation-trick">reparameterised</NoteLink> sample. Changing the
  decoder changes only the code of the decoder.

Amortisation leaves an amortisation gap, a looser bound than per-document optimisation would give, and neural
training has pathologies that classical inference does not have, of which component collapse is the most important.

## The amortised variational view

LDA's topic assignments $z_{dn}$ can be summed out, because each word is independent of the others given
$\thetavec_d$. Write the $K \times V$ topic–word matrix as $\Bmat$, whose row $\betavec_k$ is topic $k$'s distribution
over the $V$ words. (The LDA note calls these rows $\phivec_k$; neural topic model papers call them $\betavec_k$, and
$\phivec$ here denotes the encoder's weights.) Summing $z_{dn}$ out gives

$$
p(w_{dn} = v \mid \thetavec_d, \Bmat) = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_{dk}\,\beta_{kv} = \paren{\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat}_v .
$$

The only remaining latent variable per document is $\thetavec_d$. For any approximate posterior $q_{\phivec}(\thetavec_d
\mid \wvec_d)$, the <NoteLink to="evidence-lower-bound">evidence lower bound</NoteLink> is

$$
\log p(\wvec_d \mid \alphavec, \Bmat) \;\ge\; \Lcal_d = \expect_{q_{\phivec}}\brack{\sum_{n=1}^{N_d}\log
\paren{\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat}_{w_{dn}}} - \KL\paren{q_{\phivec}(\thetavec_d \mid \wvec_d) \,\|\, p(\thetavec_d \mid
\alphavec)} .
$$

The first term rewards reconstructing the document's words. The second keeps the encoder's output close to the prior.
Training maximises $\sum_d \Lcal_d$ over the encoder weights $\phivec$ and the topics $\Bmat$ with minibatch stochastic
gradients.

Two obstacles separate this bound from a working model, and each member of the family is a different answer to them.

1. **The Dirichlet cannot be reparameterised by a location–scale transform.** The Gaussian can:
   $\hvec = \muvec + \sigmavec \odot \epsilonvec$. So most models replace the Dirichlet with a **logistic normal**:
   draw $\hvec_d$ from a Gaussian and set $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$. The prior is then chosen to
   imitate a Dirichlet (ProdLDA's <NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">Laplace approximation</NoteLink>), or it is dropped for a standard Gaussian (the neural variational document model and the embedded topic model). Other
   models keep a true Dirichlet or gamma posterior and use a different gradient estimator.
2. **The decoder need not be a mixture.** Once inference is a black box, the decoder can be any differentiable map from
   $\thetavec_d$ to word probabilities: a product of experts, a log-linear model on <NoteLink to="word-embeddings">word embeddings</NoteLink>, or a network
   conditioned on metadata.

## Components

Each model has its own note, with its derivation.

- <NoteLink to="neural-variational-document-model">Neural variational document model</NoteLink> (NVDM): a Gaussian
  latent and a softmax decoder, the first variational autoencoder for bags of words <Cite id="miao2016nvdm" />.
- <NoteLink to="prodlda">ProdLDA and AVITM</NoteLink>: amortised inference for LDA with a Laplace approximation of the
  Dirichlet prior in the softmax basis, and the product-of-experts decoder <Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" />.
- <NoteLink to="dirichlet-variational-topic-models">Dirichlet variational topic models</NoteLink>: keeping a Dirichlet
  or gamma posterior through <NoteLink to="rejection-sampling">rejection-sampling</NoteLink>, implicit or Weibull reparameterisation.
- <NoteLink to="embedded-topic-model">Embedded topic model</NoteLink> (ETM): topics as vectors in word-embedding
  space, and the dynamic ETM for topics that drift over time <Cite id="dieng2020etm" />.
- <NoteLink to="contextualised-topic-models">Contextualised topic models</NoteLink>: <NoteLink to="sentence-embeddings">sentence embeddings</NoteLink> from a
  pretrained transformer as encoder input, and zero-shot cross-lingual topic inference <Cite id="bianchi2021ctm" />.
- <NoteLink to="clustering-based-topic-models">Clustering-based topic models</NoteLink>: Top2Vec and BERTopic, which
  cluster document embeddings and describe each cluster by class-based TF-IDF <Cite id="grootendorst2022bertopic" />.
- <NoteLink to="wasserstein-topic-models">Wasserstein topic models</NoteLink>: W-LDA replaces the <Gloss name="kl" /> term by a <Gloss name="mmd">maximum
  mean discrepancy</Gloss> to a Dirichlet prior on the aggregate of all documents.
- <NoteLink to="docnade">DocNADE</NoteLink>: an autoregressive neural model of the words in a document, with no
  explicit topic proportions.
- <NoteLink to="topic-model-evaluation">Evaluating topic models</NoteLink>: perplexity, coherence (NPMI, $C_V$), topic
  diversity and human judgement, and why they disagree.

## The family at a glance

| Model | Latent per document | Prior | Gradient through the sample | Decoder $p(w \mid \cdot)$ | Topics are |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LDA (Gibbs, mean field) | $\thetavec_d$, $z_{dn}$ | $\Dir(\alphavec)$ | none: closed-form updates | $\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat$ | rows of $\Bmat$ on the simplex |
| NVDM | $\hvec_d \in \reals^K$ | $\Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$ | Gaussian reparameterisation | $\operatorname{softmax}(\Rmat^\top\hvec_d + \bvec)$ | rows of $\Rmat$ (logits) |
| AVITM (LDA) | $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$ | Laplace approximation of $\Dir(\alphavec)$ | Gaussian reparameterisation | $\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat$ | rows of $\Bmat$ on the simplex |
| ProdLDA | as AVITM | as AVITM | Gaussian reparameterisation | $\operatorname{softmax}(\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat)$ | rows of $\Bmat$ (logits) |
| Dirichlet VAE, WHAI | $\thetavec_d$ Dirichlet or gamma | $\Dir(\alphavec)$ or gamma | rejection, implicit or Weibull reparameterisation | $\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat$ or Poisson | rows of $\Bmat$ |
| ETM | $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$ | $\Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$ | Gaussian reparameterisation | $\sum_k\theta_{dk}\operatorname{softmax}(\rhovec^\top\alphavec_k)$ | vectors $\alphavec_k$ in embedding space |
| CTM, ZeroShotTM | as ProdLDA | as ProdLDA | Gaussian reparameterisation | as ProdLDA | as ProdLDA; encoder also reads SBERT |
| W-LDA | $\thetavec_d$, deterministic | $\Dir(\alphavec)$, matched on the aggregate | none needed: MMD on samples | $\operatorname{softmax}(\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat + \bvec^\top)$ | rows of $\Bmat$ (logits) |
| DocNADE | none: hidden state per position | none | exact likelihood | autoregressive $p(w_i \mid w_{1:i-1})$ | columns of the input weights |
| Top2Vec, BERTopic | one cluster per document | none | none: no likelihood | none | clusters of document embeddings |

The rows fall into three groups. The first seven are probabilistic topic models trained by <Gloss name="vi">variational inference</Gloss> and
differ in how they handle the simplex and in their decoders. W-LDA and DocNADE are neural generative models of
documents trained without a KL term to a per-document prior. Top2Vec and BERTopic are not generative models: they
cluster embeddings from a pretrained network and name each cluster afterwards.

## Data flow

A model of the variational family processes a minibatch of documents as follows.

1. **Input.** Each document becomes a bag-of-words count vector $\wvec_d \in \set{0, 1, 2, \dots}^V$, sometimes
   normalised or concatenated with a sentence embedding of the raw text.
2. **Encode.** A feed-forward network maps the input to a mean $\muvec_d$ and a log-variance $\log\sigmavec_d^2$, both
   in $\reals^K$.
3. **Sample.** Draw $\epsilonvec \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$ and set $\hvec_d = \muvec_d + \sigmavec_d \odot
   \epsilonvec$, then $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$.
4. **Decode.** Map $\thetavec_d$ to a distribution $\hat\wvec_d$ over the vocabulary.
5. **Loss.** The negative evidence lower bound is $-\sum_v w_{dv}\log\hat w_{dv}$ plus the KL divergence between two Gaussians, which
   has a closed form.
6. **Read out.** After training, topic $k$ is described by the $10$ or so words with the largest entries in row $k$ of
   $\Bmat$. A new document's topic proportions are one encoder pass: $\operatorname{softmax}(\muvec_d)$ or the mean of
   several samples.

## Variants

- **Discrete-latent and nonparametric variants.** Miao et al. derived three constructions of $\thetavec_d$ from a
  Gaussian draw: a Gaussian softmax, a Gaussian <NoteLink to="hierarchical-dirichlet-process">stick-breaking construction</NoteLink>, and a recurrent stick-breaking
  construction that can grow the number of topics <Cite id="miao2017gsm" />.
- **Metadata.** SCHOLAR adds document labels and covariates to the encoder and decoder, which gives supervised and
  covariate-dependent topics in the same framework <Cite id="card2018scholar" />.
- **Coherence regularisers.** NTM-R adds a differentiable surrogate of topic coherence, the similarity of each topic's
  word-embedding centroid to its words, to the evidence lower bound <Cite id="ding2018ntmr" />.

Surveys cover the wider family, including graph-based and pretrained-language-model variants
<Cite id="zhao2021ntmsurvey" /> <Cite id="wu2024ntmsurvey" />.
