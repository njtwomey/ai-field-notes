<Definition>
The **embedded topic model** (ETM) is a topic model in which every word $v$ has an embedding $\rhovec_v \in \reals^L$
and every topic $k$ has an embedding $\alphavec_k \in \reals^L$ in the same space <Cite id="dieng2020etm" />. Topic
$k$'s distribution over the $V$ words is

$$
\beta_{kv} = \frac{\exp(\rhovec_v^\top\alphavec_k)}{\sum_{u=1}^V\exp(\rhovec_u^\top\alphavec_k)} .
$$

A document $d$ draws $\hvec_d \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat_K)$ and sets $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$,
a logistic-normal draw of topic proportions. Each word draws a topic $z_{dn} \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$ and then a word
$w_{dn} \sim \Cat(\betavec_{z_{dn}})$. Inference is <NoteLink to="variational-autoencoder">amortised variational inference</NoteLink> with an encoder network, and the
word embeddings are either pretrained and fixed or learnt with the model.
</Definition>

## Intuition

In <NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA)</NoteLink> a topic is a free vector of $V$ probabilities. Two words
that mean the same thing are unrelated parameters, and a rare word's probability under each topic is estimated from its
few occurrences alone. In the ETM a topic is a single point $\alphavec_k$ in the embedding space, and its distribution
over words is a log-linear function of where each word sits. Words close to each other in the embedding space, which are
words used in similar contexts, receive similar probabilities under every topic. A rare word inherits a sensible
probability from its neighbours.

The ETM's likelihood is the continuous bag-of-words (CBOW) model of
<NoteLink to="word-embeddings">word embeddings</NoteLink> with a different context. CBOW draws a word from
$\operatorname{softmax}(\rhovec^\top\alphavec)$ with $\alphavec$ the sum of the embeddings of the surrounding words
<Cite id="mikolov2013" />. The ETM draws it with $\alphavec$ the embedding of the word's topic, chosen from the
document's topic proportions. The context is the document's subject, not a window of neighbouring words
<Cite id="dieng2020etm" />.

<EtmTopicVector />

The logit $\rhovec_v^\top\alphavec_k$ is the projection of the word's embedding onto the topic direction, scaled by the
length of $\alphavec_k$. The top words of a topic are the words furthest along its direction. The length of
$\alphavec_k$ is an inverse temperature: a long vector gives a sharp topic, a short one a flat topic.

## The objective

Summing out the topic assignments gives the per-word likelihood as a mixture, as in LDA:

$$
p(w_{dn} = v \mid \thetavec_d) = \sum_{k=1}^K\theta_{dk}\,\beta_{kv} .
$$

Let $\wvec_d$ be the vector of word counts, with $w_{dv}$ the count of word $v$. The encoder maps the normalised counts
$\wvec_d/N_d$ to a Gaussian $q_{\phivec}(\hvec_d \mid \wvec_d) = \Gauss(\muvec_d, \diag\sigmavec_d^2)$; normalising
lets documents of different lengths share one encoder. The <Gloss name="elbo">evidence lower bound</Gloss> is

$$
\Lcal_d = \expect_{q_{\phivec}}\brack{\sum_{v=1}^V w_{dv}\log\sum_{k=1}^K\operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)_k\,
\beta_{kv}} - \KL\paren{\Gauss(\muvec_d, \diag\sigmavec_d^2) \,\|\, \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)} ,
$$

with the expectation estimated by one reparameterised sample and the <Gloss name="kl" /> term in closed form. The <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> reaches the
topic embeddings $\alphavec_k$ through every $\beta_{kv}$, and reaches the word embeddings $\rhovec_v$ as well if they
are learnt. Each step computes the $K \times V$ matrix $\betavec$ from $K$ softmaxes over the vocabulary, once per
minibatch.

<NtmDiagram variants={['etm']} />

**Parameters.** LDA's topics have $K \times V$ free parameters. The ETM's have $K \times L$ for the topic embeddings plus
$L \times V$ for the word embeddings, which are shared by all topics and may be fixed. With $V = 10^5$, $K = 300$ and $L
= 300$, LDA's topics have $3 \times 10^7$ parameters and the ETM's topic embeddings have $9 \times 10^4$.

## Why the ETM tolerates large vocabularies and stop words

- **Rare words.** A word seen a handful of times has a pretrained embedding learnt from a large external corpus, or an
  embedding shaped by its contexts in the training corpus. Its probability under each topic follows from its position,
  so rare words need not be pruned from the vocabulary.
- **<NoteLink to="stop-words">Stop words</NoteLink>.** In LDA, words such as "the" occur in every document, so every topic must give them high probability
  to fit the data, and stop words fill every topic's top words. In the ETM, stop words occupy their own region of the
  embedding space. The model can dedicate a few topics to that region and leave the others free of stop words. Dieng et al. report
  that on a corpus with all stop words kept, the ETM's topics stay interpretable while LDA's fill with stop words
  <Cite id="dieng2020etm" />.

Dieng et al. measure **topic quality** as the product of topic coherence and topic diversity, the fraction of unique
words among the top 25 words of all topics (see <NoteLink to="topic-model-evaluation">evaluating topic models</NoteLink>).

## The dynamic embedded topic model

The **dynamic ETM** (D-ETM) models a corpus whose documents carry timestamps $t_d \in \set{1, \dots, T}$
<Cite id="dieng2019detm" />. Word embeddings $\rhovec_v$ are fixed across time, and each topic embedding follows a
Gaussian <NoteLink to="random-walk">random walk</NoteLink>:

$$
\alphavec_k^{(t)} \sim \Gauss\paren{\alphavec_k^{(t-1)}, \gamma^2\Imat}, \qquad \alphavec_k^{(0)} \sim \Gauss(\zeros,
\Imat) .
$$

The mean of the topic proportions also drifts, $\etavec_t \sim \Gauss(\etavec_{t-1}, \delta^2\Imat)$, and a document
at time $t_d$ draws $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$ with $\hvec_d \sim \Gauss(\etavec_{t_d},
a^2\Imat)$. A word then draws a topic and a word from $\operatorname{softmax}(\rhovec^\top\alphavec_{z_{dn}}^{(t_d)})$.
The random walk makes each topic's word distribution change smoothly: a topic about computing keeps its direction but
moves, over decades, towards the embeddings of newer words.

This is the <NoteLink to="dynamic-topic-model">dynamic topic model</NoteLink> of Blei and Lafferty with the random walk
moved from the $V$-dimensional <NoteLink to="exponential-family">natural parameters</NoteLink> of each topic to its
$L$-dimensional embedding <Cite id="blei2006dtm" />.

The variational family is structured: the proportions' means $\etavec_{1:T}$ have a Gaussian posterior whose parameters
are produced by an <Gloss name="lstm" /> that reads the average <NoteLink to="bag-of-words">bag of words</NoteLink> at
each time step, the topic embeddings have a mean-field Gaussian posterior, and each document's $\hvec_d$ has an
amortised Gaussian posterior <Cite id="dieng2019detm" />.

## Worked example

Take $L = 2$ and four words: "goal" at $(2.6, 1.9)$, "bank" at $(-2.4, 1.7)$, "the" at $(0.2, 0.1)$ and "transfer" at
$(0.3, 2.4)$. A topic has $\alphavec_k = (1, 0.5)$.

- The logits $\rhovec_v^\top\alphavec_k$ are $3.55$, $-1.55$, $0.25$ and $1.50$.
- The softmax gives $\betavec_k = (0.853, 0.005, 0.032, 0.110)$: a sport topic in which "transfer" is second.
- Doubling the topic vector to $(2, 1)$ doubles every logit, giving $(0.982, 0.000, 0.001, 0.016)$. The ranking is
  unchanged and the topic is sharper.

"The", near the origin, gets a logit near zero under every topic of moderate length. It is the top word only of a
topic whose direction gives every other word a smaller projection than the near-zero projection of "the".

## Assumptions

- **LDA's mixture likelihood** and bag-of-words documents.
- **A log-linear topic.** A topic's word distribution is the softmax of a linear function of the embeddings. A topic
  that needs two separate regions of the embedding space cannot be expressed by one $\alphavec_k$; it needs two topics.
- **Embeddings that encode the right similarity.** Pretrained embeddings carry the similarities of the corpus they were
  trained on. Domain-specific senses, such as "cell" in biology against telephony, follow the external corpus.
- **A logistic-normal prior with identity covariance**, which does not encourage sparse topic proportions the way
  $\Dir(\alpha)$ with $\alpha < 1$ does.

## Pitfalls

- **Hubs.** Words with large-norm embeddings get high logits under many topics and can dominate several topics' top
  words. Normalising embeddings, or learning them jointly, reduces this.
- **Fixed embeddings and new vocabulary.** With pretrained embeddings, a word missing from the embedding vocabulary has
  no $\rhovec_v$. Either drop it or learn embeddings for it.
- **Cost of the softmax.** Computing $\betavec$ needs $K$ softmaxes over $V$ words at every step. For very large
  vocabularies this dominates training.
