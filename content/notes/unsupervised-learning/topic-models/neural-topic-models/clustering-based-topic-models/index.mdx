<Definition>
A **clustering-based topic model** embeds each document with a pretrained neural encoder, clusters the embeddings, and
treats each cluster as a topic. A topic is then described by words chosen after clustering. **Top2Vec** describes a
cluster by the <NoteLink to="word-embeddings">word vectors</NoteLink> nearest its centroid, in a space where documents
and words are embedded jointly <Cite id="angelov2020top2vec" />. **BERTopic** describes it by **class-based TF-IDF**
(c-TF-IDF), which scores term $t$ for cluster $c$ as

$$
W_{t,c} = \mathrm{tf}_{t,c}\cdot\log\paren{1 + \frac{A}{f_t}},
$$

where $\mathrm{tf}_{t,c}$ is the frequency of $t$ in the cluster's documents, $f_t$ its frequency in all clusters, and
$A$ the average number of words per cluster <Cite id="grootendorst2022bertopic" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A probabilistic topic model such as <NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">LDA</NoteLink> learns topics and document
representations together, from word co-occurrence in the corpus alone. A pretrained sentence encoder already maps
documents about the same subject to nearby vectors, using what it learnt from far larger text collections. If documents
are already arranged by subject, finding topics reduces to finding dense regions among the document vectors, and
describing a topic reduces to asking which words distinguish one region from the others.

The two steps are decoupled. The embedding decides which documents belong together; the word statistics decide how the
group is labelled. Neither step is a likelihood, so there is no generative story, no posterior, and no perplexity.

## The pipeline

1. **Embed.** Each document $d$ becomes a vector $\svec_d$. BERTopic uses a
   <NoteLink to="sentence-embeddings">Sentence-BERT</NoteLink> model <Cite id="reimers2019" />. Top2Vec trains doc2vec,
   which learns document and word vectors in one space <Cite id="le2014" />, or uses a pretrained sentence encoder.
2. **Reduce.** <NoteLink to="uniform-manifold-approximation-and-projection">UMAP</NoteLink> maps the embeddings to a
   handful of dimensions <Cite id="mcinnes2018" />. Density-based clustering degrades in hundreds of dimensions, where
   distances concentrate, and UMAP preserves local neighbourhoods well enough for clusters to survive.
3. **Cluster.** HDBSCAN finds clusters of varying density and labels documents in sparse regions as outliers rather than
   forcing them into a topic <Cite id="campello2013" />. The number of topics is the
   <NoteLink to="choosing-the-number-of-clusters">number of clusters</NoteLink> found, not a parameter. See
   <NoteLink to="density-based-spatial-clustering">DBSCAN</NoteLink> for the density-based idea it extends.
4. **Describe.** Top2Vec computes each cluster's **topic vector**, the centroid of its documents' embeddings in the
   original space, and lists the words whose vectors are nearest to it <Cite id="angelov2020top2vec" />. BERTopic
   concatenates each cluster's documents into one <NoteLink to="bag-of-words">bag of words</NoteLink> and ranks the
   words by c-TF-IDF.
5. **Reduce the number of topics (optional).** BERTopic repeatedly merges the least frequent topic into the topic whose
   c-TF-IDF vector is most similar, until a requested number remains <Cite id="grootendorst2022bertopic" />.

## Deriving class-based TF-IDF

Classic <NoteLink to="term-frequency-inverse-document-frequency">TF-IDF</NoteLink> scores term $t$ in document $d$ as
$\mathrm{tf}_{t,d}\log(N/\mathrm{df}_t)$, with $N$ documents and $\mathrm{df}_t$ the number of documents that contain
$t$ <Cite id="salton1975" />. The first factor rewards terms frequent in $d$; the second penalises terms that appear
everywhere.

To describe a cluster, treat all of its documents as one long document. There are then only $C$ "documents", one per
cluster, and the classic inverse document frequency becomes $\log(C/\mathrm{df}_t)$ with $\mathrm{df}_t \le C$. With a
few dozen clusters this statistic is too coarse. A word that appears even once in every cluster gets weight zero,
however unevenly its occurrences are spread, and most moderately common words appear somewhere in every cluster.

The fix is to measure how common a term is by its total frequency, not by the number of clusters it touches. Let
$f_t = \sum_c\mathrm{tf}_{t,c}$ and let $A = \frac1C\sum_t f_t$ be the average number of words per cluster. Replace
$N/\mathrm{df}_t$, "how many documents per document containing $t$", by $A/f_t$, "how many words per occurrence of $t$,
at the scale of one cluster". Writing $p_t = f_t/\sum_u f_u$ for the corpus frequency of $t$,

$$
\frac{A}{f_t} = \frac{\sum_u f_u}{C\,f_t} = \frac{1}{C\,p_t} .
$$

A term that makes up a fraction $p_t$ of the corpus is expected $A p_t$ times in a cluster of average size. The
ratio $A/f_t$ is therefore large for a term that is rare relative to one cluster's worth of text and small for a term
that occurs many times in every cluster. Adding one inside the logarithm keeps every weight positive
<Cite id="grootendorst2022bertopic" />:

$$
W_{t,c} = \mathrm{tf}_{t,c}\,\log\paren{1 + \frac{A}{f_t}} .
$$

For a rare term, $A/f_t \gg 1$ and $\log(1 + A/f_t) \approx -\log p_t - \log C$: the weight is the term's count in the
cluster times its surprisal in the corpus, shifted by a constant. For a very frequent term, $A/f_t \ll 1$ and the
weight shrinks towards $\mathrm{tf}_{t,c}\,A/f_t$, which is small unless the term is extremely frequent in $c$.

The same weights give **dynamic topics** without re-clustering. BERTopic fits the clusters once on the whole corpus,
then recomputes $\mathrm{tf}_{t,c,i}$ from the documents of time slice $i$ and multiplies it by the global
$\log(1 + A/f_t)$ <Cite id="grootendorst2022bertopic" />. The same trick describes topics by author or source.

## Worked example

Three clusters, sport, finance and weather, have these word counts:

| term | sport | finance | weather | $f_t$ | $\log(1 + A/f_t)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the | 50 | 45 | 40 | 135 | 0.400 |
| goal | 12 | 0 | 0 | 12 | 1.876 |
| match | 8 | 1 | 1 | 10 | 2.033 |
| bank | 0 | 10 | 1 | 11 | 1.950 |
| rate | 1 | 9 | 2 | 12 | 1.876 |
| rain | 1 | 0 | 11 | 12 | 1.876 |
| transfer | 4 | 3 | 0 | 7 | 2.349 |

The clusters hold $199$ words in total, so $A = 199/3 = 66.33$.

- **Classic IDF, one document per cluster.** "match" and "rate" occur in all three clusters, so
  $\log(3/3) = 0$ and both vanish from every description, although "match" is plainly a sport word. "goal" gets
  $\log 3 = 1.099$.
- **c-TF-IDF.** Sport scores "goal" $12 \times 1.876 = 22.5$, "the" $50 \times 0.400 = 20.0$ and "match"
  $8 \times 2.033 = 16.3$. Finance scores "bank" $19.5$, "the" $18.0$ and "rate" $16.9$. Weather scores "rain" $20.6$
  and "the" $16.0$.

c-TF-IDF recovers "match" and "rate". It divides the weight of "the" by about five relative to a typical content word,
but "the" is so frequent that it still ranks second in every cluster. <NoteLink to="stop-words">Stop words</NoteLink>
should therefore be removed from the bag of words used for c-TF-IDF, while the sentence encoder still reads the raw
text.

## Assumptions

- **One topic per document.** Each document belongs to one cluster, or to none. A document about two subjects is placed
  in one or labelled an outlier; there are no mixed memberships unless HDBSCAN's soft cluster probabilities are used.
- **The embedding's similarity is topical similarity.** A sentence encoder may group documents by style, language or
  sentiment rather than subject.
- **Clusters are dense regions.** HDBSCAN finds regions denser than their surroundings; a broad, diffuse subject may
  split into several topics or dissolve into outliers.

## Pitfalls

- **Outliers.** HDBSCAN often labels a large share of documents as noise, which BERTopic reports as topic $-1$.
  Treating those documents as a topic, or silently dropping them, distorts topic sizes.
- **Randomness.** UMAP is stochastic, so topics change between runs unless its seed is fixed.
- **Truncation.** Sentence encoders read a bounded number of tokens; long documents are represented by their beginning.
- **Coherence is not directly comparable.** Topic words from c-TF-IDF are chosen to be distinctive, not to reconstruct
  the documents. Comparing their coherence with LDA's measures different things; see
  <NoteLink to="topic-model-evaluation">evaluating topic models</NoteLink>.
