<Definition>
**Autoencoding variational inference for topic models** (AVITM) fits
<NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">latent Dirichlet allocation</NoteLink> with an encoder network, as in a
variational autoencoder <Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" />. It makes three changes to LDA's inference:

1. The topic assignments $z_{dn}$ are summed out, leaving the topic proportions $\thetavec_d$ as the only latent
   variable per document.
2. The prior $\Dir(\alphavec)$ on $\thetavec_d$ is replaced by its Laplace approximation in the softmax basis: a
   Gaussian $\Gauss(\muvec_0, \Sigmamat_0)$ on $\hvec_d$ with $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$, a
   logistic-normal distribution.
3. The encoder outputs a diagonal Gaussian $q_{\phivec}(\hvec_d \mid \wvec_d)$, so a sample of $\thetavec_d$ is a
   differentiable function of the encoder weights.

**ProdLDA** is the model obtained by changing LDA's word distribution from the mixture $\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat$ to
$\operatorname{softmax}(\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat)$, with $\Bmat \in \reals^{K \times V}$ unconstrained. Each word
distribution is then a weighted product of the topics' distributions rather than a weighted average.
</Definition>

## The problem

LDA's evidence lower bound needs an expectation over $q(\thetavec_d)$ and a gradient of that expectation with respect to
the parameters of $q$. The <NoteLink to="reparameterisation-trick">reparameterisation trick</NoteLink> provides the
gradient when a sample can be written as a differentiable function of parameter-free noise. A Gaussian can be written
this way; a Dirichlet cannot be written as a location–scale transform of fixed noise. If $q$ is Dirichlet, the gradient
is hard to estimate. If $q$ is Gaussian or logistic normal, the KL divergence to a Dirichlet prior has no closed form.
AVITM resolves the mismatch by making the prior logistic normal too, with parameters chosen to imitate the Dirichlet.

## Summing out the topic assignments

Given $\thetavec_d$ and the topics, each word is drawn by choosing a topic $z$ with probability $\theta_{dz}$ and then a
word from $\betavec_z$. Summing over $z$:

$$
p(w_{dn} = v \mid \thetavec_d, \Bmat) = \sum_{k=1}^K \theta_{dk}\,\beta_{kv} = \paren{\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat}_v .
$$

The words of a document are independent given $\thetavec_d$, so $\log p(\wvec_d \mid \thetavec_d) = \sum_v w_{dv}
\log(\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat)_v$, where $w_{dv}$ counts word $v$ in document $d$. No discrete latent variable is left to
reparameterise.

## The Laplace approximation in the softmax basis

A Laplace approximation replaces a density by a Gaussian centred on its mode, with covariance equal to the inverse of
the Hessian of the negative log density at the mode (see
<NoteLink to="laplace-approximation">Laplace approximation</NoteLink>). The result depends on the coordinates. On the
simplex, $\Dir(\alphavec)$ has no interior mode when some $\alpha_k < 1$, and a Gaussian cannot respect the constraint
$\theta_k \ge 0$. MacKay proposed approximating the Dirichlet in the **softmax basis** instead
<Cite id="mackay1998basis" />.

**Change of variables.** Write $\thetavec = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec)$ with $\hvec \in \reals^K$. Adding a constant
to every $h_k$ leaves $\thetavec$ unchanged, so fix the redundant direction by $\sum_k h_k = 0$. The density of $\hvec$
on that $(K-1)$-dimensional plane is the Dirichlet density times the absolute Jacobian determinant of the map $\hvec
\mapsto \thetavec$, which is proportional to $\prod_k\theta_k$ (derivation below). The factors $\theta_k^{\alpha_k - 1}$
of the Dirichlet each gain one power:

$$
p(\hvec) \propto \prod_{k=1}^K \theta_k^{\alpha_k - 1} \cdot \prod_{k=1}^K \theta_k = \prod_{k=1}^K \theta_k(\hvec)^{\alpha_k},
\qquad \log p(\hvec) = \text{const} + \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k \log\operatorname{softmax}_k(\hvec) .
$$

Every exponent is now positive, so the density vanishes at the edges of the simplex, which lie at infinity in the
softmax basis. The log density is a sum of concave functions of $\hvec$, because $\log\operatorname{softmax}_k(\hvec) =
h_k - \log\sum_j e^{h_j}$ and <NoteLink to="softmax-and-log-sum-exp">log-sum-exp</NoteLink> is convex. So $p(\hvec)$
has exactly one mode for every $\alphavec > 0$.

<Derivation title="The Jacobian of the softmax on the plane sum(h) = 0">
Parameterise the plane by $\yvec = (h_1 - h_K, \dots, h_{K-1} - h_K)$, so that $y_k = \log(\theta_k/\theta_K)$ for
$k < K$, with $\theta_K = 1 - \sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\theta_j$. Differentiating,

$$
\frac{\partial y_k}{\partial\theta_j} = \frac{\delta_{kj}}{\theta_k} + \frac{1}{\theta_K}, \qquad j, k < K .
$$

This matrix is $\Dmat + \theta_K^{-1}\ones\ones^\top$ with $\Dmat = \diag(1/\theta_1, \dots, 1/\theta_{K-1})$. By the
matrix determinant lemma its determinant is

$$
\det(\Dmat)\paren{1 + \theta_K^{-1}\ones^\top\Dmat^{-1}\ones} = \prod_{k<K}\frac{1}{\theta_k}\cdot\frac{\theta_K +
\sum_{k<K}\theta_k}{\theta_K} = \frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^K\theta_k} .
$$

The inverse map $\yvec \mapsto (\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{K-1})$ therefore has Jacobian determinant $\prod_k\theta_k$,
and $p(\yvec) = \Dir(\thetavec(\yvec) \mid \alphavec)\prod_k\theta_k$. The coordinates $\yvec$ and $\hvec$ on the
plane differ by a fixed linear map, which changes the density only by a constant.
</Derivation>

**The mode.** Setting the gradient $\partial\log p/\partial h_j = \alpha_j - \alpha_0\theta_j$ to zero, with
$\alpha_0 = \sum_k\alpha_k$, gives $\theta_j = \alpha_j/\alpha_0$, the Dirichlet mean. In the softmax basis this is
$h_j = \log\alpha_j + c$, and the constraint $\sum_j h_j = 0$ fixes $c$:

$$
\mu_{0k} = \log\alpha_k - \frac1K\sum_{i=1}^K\log\alpha_i .
$$

**The curvature.** Differentiating again,

$$
-\frac{\partial^2\log p}{\partial h_i\,\partial h_j} = \alpha_0\paren{\theta_i\delta_{ij} - \theta_i\theta_j}, \qquad
\text{so at the mode}\quad \Hmat = \diag(\alphavec) - \frac{\alphavec\alphavec^\top}{\alpha_0} .
$$

$\Hmat\ones = \alphavec - \alphavec = \zeros$, so $\Hmat$ is singular along the redundant direction $\ones$ and the
covariance is its pseudo-inverse on the plane. With the centring matrix $\Pmat = \Imat - \frac1K\ones\ones^\top$, the
candidate $\Sigmamat_0 = \Pmat\,\diag(1/\alphavec)\,\Pmat$ satisfies $\Hmat\Sigmamat_0\Hmat = \Hmat$ and
$\Sigmamat_0\Hmat\Sigmamat_0 = \Sigmamat_0$, is symmetric, and has range orthogonal to $\ones$ like $\Hmat$, so it is the
Moore–Penrose inverse of $\Hmat$. Its entries are

$$
\Sigma_{0,ij} = \frac{\delta_{ij}}{\alpha_i} - \frac1K\paren{\frac1{\alpha_i} + \frac1{\alpha_j}} +
\frac1{K^2}\sum_{l=1}^K\frac1{\alpha_l}, \qquad \Sigma_{0,kk} = \frac1{\alpha_k}\paren{1 - \frac2K} +
\frac1{K^2}\sum_{l=1}^K\frac1{\alpha_l} .
$$

This is the approximation of Hennig et al. <Cite id="hennig2012ktm" />. AVITM keeps only the diagonal
$\Sigma_{0,kk}$ <Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" />. The off-diagonal entries are of order $1/(K\alpha)$, so the
diagonal approximation improves as the number of topics grows.

<Derivation title="Checking that the pseudo-inverse identities hold">
Let $\Dmat = \diag(1/\alphavec)$. Then $\Dmat\Hmat = \Imat - \ones\alphavec^\top/\alpha_0$ and $\Hmat\Dmat = \Imat -
\alphavec\ones^\top/\alpha_0$. Because $\ones^\top\Hmat = \zeros^\top$, $\Hmat\Dmat\Hmat = \Hmat - \alphavec
(\ones^\top\Hmat)/\alpha_0 = \Hmat$. Since $\Hmat\Pmat = \Pmat\Hmat = \Hmat$, it follows that $\Hmat(\Pmat\Dmat\Pmat)
\Hmat = \Hmat\Dmat\Hmat = \Hmat$. For the other identity, $\Dmat\Hmat\Dmat = \Dmat - \ones\ones^\top/\alpha_0$, and
$\Pmat\ones = \zeros$, so $\Pmat\Dmat\Pmat\,\Hmat\,\Pmat\Dmat\Pmat = \Pmat(\Dmat - \ones\ones^\top/\alpha_0)\Pmat =
\Pmat\Dmat\Pmat$.
</Derivation>

**Sparsity survives.** For a symmetric prior $\alpha_k = \alpha$, the formula gives $\mu_{0k} = 0$ and
$\Sigma_{0,kk} = (1 - 1/K)/\alpha$. A sparse Dirichlet with $\alpha = 0.02$ and $K = 50$ becomes a Gaussian with
variance $49$ per coordinate. Draws of $\hvec$ then differ by several units between coordinates, and the softmax of such
a draw puts almost all its mass on a few topics. A small $\alpha$ is a wide Gaussian in the softmax basis.

<LaplaceDirichlet />

In the figure, with the full covariance the total variation distance between the Dirichlet and its approximation falls
from $0.33$ at $\alphavec = (0.3, 0.3, 0.3)$ to $0.09$ at $(2, 2, 2)$ and $0.05$ at $(5, 5, 5)$. For small $\alpha$ the
Dirichlet's mass piles into the corners faster than a logistic normal's can. The diagonal covariance that ProdLDA uses
is worse on three topics, $0.21$ at $(2, 2, 2)$ and $0.17$ at $(5, 5, 5)$, because the dropped off-diagonal entries are
large when $K$ is small.

## The objective

The encoder maps the counts $\wvec_d$ to $\muvec_1 = \muvec_{\phivec}(\wvec_d)$ and $\sigmavec_1^2 =
\sigmavec^2_{\phivec}(\wvec_d)$ in $\reals^K$. A sample is $\hvec_d = \muvec_1 + \sigmavec_1 \odot \epsilonvec$ with
$\epsilonvec \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$, and $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$. Prior and posterior are
both Gaussians on $\hvec_d$, so the KL term is the KL divergence between two Gaussians:

$$
\Lcal_d = \expect_{\epsilonvec}\brack{\sum_{v=1}^V w_{dv}\log p(v \mid \thetavec_d)} - \frac12\paren{\trace(\Sigmamat_0^{-1}
\Sigmamat_1) + (\muvec_0 - \muvec_1)^\top\Sigmamat_0^{-1}(\muvec_0 - \muvec_1) - K + \log\frac{\abs{\Sigmamat_0}}{
\abs{\Sigmamat_1}}},
$$

with $\Sigmamat_1 = \diag\sigmavec_1^2$ and $\Sigmamat_0$ the diagonal prior covariance. With both covariances diagonal,
the KL term is a sum over topics of one-dimensional terms.

<NtmDiagram variants={['avitm', 'prodlda']} initial="prodlda" />

For LDA, $p(v \mid \thetavec_d) = (\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat)_v$ with every row of $\Bmat$ on the simplex, which AVITM
enforces by a softmax over each row. For ProdLDA, $p(v \mid \thetavec_d) = \operatorname{softmax}(\thetavec_d^\top
\Bmat)_v$. A minimal ProdLDA loss in PyTorch:

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def prodlda_loss(counts, encoder, beta, prior_mu, prior_var):
    mu, log_var = encoder(counts)                      # each (batch, K)
    h = mu + torch.exp(0.5 * log_var) * torch.randn_like(mu)
    theta = F.dropout(F.softmax(h, dim=1), p=0.2)      # dropout on θ against collapse
    log_p = F.log_softmax(theta @ beta, dim=1)         # (batch, V); beta is (K, V), unconstrained
    recon = -(counts * log_p).sum(1)
    var = log_var.exp()
    kl = 0.5 * ((var / prior_var) + (mu - prior_mu) ** 2 / prior_var - 1
                + prior_var.log() - log_var).sum(1)
    return (recon + kl).mean()
```

## Product of experts

A **mixture** averages probabilities; a **product of experts** multiplies them and renormalises
<Cite id="hinton2002poe" />. ProdLDA's decoder is a product of experts. Suppose each topic's unnormalised row is a
vector of log-probabilities, $\Bmat_{k\cdot} = \log\betavec_k$. Then

$$
\operatorname{softmax}\paren{\sum_k\theta_k\log\betavec_k}_v = \frac{\prod_k\beta_{kv}^{\theta_k}}{\sum_u
\prod_k\beta_{ku}^{\theta_k}} ,
$$

the weighted geometric mean of the topics' word distributions, renormalised. Mixing natural parameters of a
categorical distribution is the same as taking a weighted geometric mean of its mean parameters
<Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" />.

**Why a mixture cannot be sharp.** In a mixture, the probability of every word is an average,
$\sum_k\theta_k\beta_{kv} \le \max_k\beta_{kv}$. No word can be more probable under the mixture than under the best
topic for that word, and by concavity of entropy the mixture's entropy is at least $\sum_k\theta_k H(\betavec_k)$. A
document about two subjects gets a word distribution spread over the union of both topics' words. To fit a document
well, each topic must therefore already be a good model of documents on its own, and LDA's topics absorb frequent,
generic words that occur in many documents.

**Why a product can.** In a product, a word is probable only if every expert with weight gives it reasonable
probability. A word that one expert rules out is suppressed, whatever the others say, and the renormalisation hands the
freed mass to the words the experts agree on. The product can be sharper than every one of its experts. Each topic can
then specialise: it only needs to veto the words that do not fit, and the combination of topics, not any one topic,
picks out the document's words. Srivastava and Sutton found ProdLDA's topics markedly more coherent than LDA's, including
LDA trained by collapsed Gibbs sampling <Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" />.

<MixtureVsProduct />

In the figure the two topics are bumps of log-probability, $\log\beta_{kv} = -(v - c_k)^2/(2s^2) + \text{const}$. The
weighted sum of two such logits is again a bump of the same width $s$, centred at $\theta_1c_1 + \theta_2c_2$. The
product is about as sharp as a single topic and sits on the words where both topics overlap. (A single topic's
perplexity in the readout is slightly lower only because its bump is cut off at the end of the vocabulary.) The mixture has two peaks and
a perplexity close to twice that of one topic.

## Component collapse

AVITM with a standard training set-up often converges to a bad local optimum in which all $K$ topics are identical
<Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" />. Early in training, the KL term pulls each coordinate of $q(\hvec_d \mid \wvec_d)$
towards the prior. A coordinate that reaches the prior carries no information about the document, its decoder weights
receive no consistent gradient, and it stays there. Because the softmax couples all coordinates of $\hvec_d$, the
surviving coordinates are driven towards the same frequent words, and the topics collapse onto one another. This is
a form of the posterior collapse seen in text VAEs.

Srivastava and Sutton avoided it by changing the optimisation rather than the model:

- **High learning rate and momentum.** Adam with a first-moment decay rate above 0.8 and a high learning rate escapes the early optimum near the prior.
- **Batch normalisation.** High learning rates with momentum diverge; batch normalisation of the encoder and decoder
  outputs keeps them stable.
- **Dropout on $\thetavec_d$.** Randomly zeroing topic proportions forces the decoder to use many topics.

They also note that down-weighting the KL term early in training, a KL annealing schedule, serves the same purpose.

## Worked example

**Laplace approximation.** Take $K = 3$ and $\alphavec = (2, 1, 1)$. The mean of $\log\alphavec$ is $\log 2/3 = 0.231$,
so $\muvec_0 = (0.693 - 0.231, -0.231, -0.231) = (0.462, -0.231, -0.231)$. The sum $\sum_l 1/\alpha_l = 2.5$, so

$$
\Sigma_{0,11} = \tfrac12\paren{1 - \tfrac23} + \tfrac{2.5}{9} = 0.444, \qquad \Sigma_{0,22} = \Sigma_{0,33} =
1 \times \tfrac13 + \tfrac{2.5}{9} = 0.611 .
$$

The softmax of $\muvec_0$ is $(0.5, 0.25, 0.25)$, the Dirichlet mean $\alphavec/\alpha_0$. Topic 1 has the largest
$\alpha$ and the smallest variance in the softmax basis.

**Mixture against product.** Two topics over four words have $\betavec_1 = (0.49, 0.49, 0.01, 0.01)$ and $\betavec_2 =
(0.01, 0.49, 0.49, 0.01)$, each with entropy $1.141$ bits. With $\thetavec = (0.5, 0.5)$:

- Mixture: $(0.25, 0.49, 0.25, 0.01)$, entropy $1.571$ bits.
- Product: $\sqrt{\beta_{1v}\beta_{2v}} = (0.07, 0.49, 0.07, 0.01)$, which sums to $0.64$, so the renormalised product is
  $(0.109, 0.766, 0.109, 0.016)$, entropy $1.087$ bits.

The product gives word 2, the only word both topics favour, probability $0.766$, higher than either topic gives it, and
its entropy is below that of either expert.

## Assumptions

- **LDA's generative story, approximated.** AVITM-LDA targets the LDA posterior, but the prior is a logistic normal
  with diagonal covariance, not a Dirichlet. It is not a correlated topic model: the covariance is fixed by $\alphavec$,
  not learnt.
- **One encoder for all documents.** The approximate posterior is amortised, so it can be looser than per-document
  mean-field updates.
- **ProdLDA is a different model.** Its likelihood is not LDA's. Its topics are rows of unnormalised logits, and a
  topic's top words are the largest entries of its row, not a probability distribution.

## Pitfalls

- **A typo in the source.** Equation (6) of Srivastava and Sutton writes the last term of $\Sigma_{0,kk}$ as
  $\frac1{K^2}\sum_i\frac1{\alpha_k}$; the sum is over $\frac1{\alpha_i}$ <Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" />
  <Cite id="hennig2012ktm" />. The two agree only for a symmetric prior.
- **Small $\alpha$.** For $\alpha_k$ well below 1, the Dirichlet puts most of its mass extremely close to the simplex's
  faces, and the logistic normal matches it poorly. The model still produces sparse $\thetavec_d$, but it is no longer
  approximating the stated prior.
- **Collapse looks like success.** A collapsed model trains without error and reports a reasonable perplexity. Check that
  the topics' top words differ, for example with the topic diversity score in
  <NoteLink to="topic-model-evaluation">evaluating topic models</NoteLink>.
- **Normalising $\Bmat$ in ProdLDA.** Applying a softmax to each row of $\Bmat$ before mixing turns ProdLDA back into
  AVITM-LDA; the rows must stay unconstrained logits.
