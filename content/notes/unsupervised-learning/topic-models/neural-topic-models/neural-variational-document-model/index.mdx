<Definition>
The **neural variational document model** (NVDM) is a generative model of a document's <NoteLink to="bag-of-words">bag of words</NoteLink> with one
continuous latent vector per document <Cite id="miao2016nvdm" />. For a vocabulary of $V$ words and a latent dimension
$K$:

1. Draw $\hvec_d \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat_K)$.
2. For each word position $n = 1, \dots, N_d$, draw $w_{dn} \sim \Cat\paren{\operatorname{softmax}(\Rmat^\top\hvec_d +
   \bvec)}$, with $\Rmat \in \reals^{K \times V}$ and $\bvec \in \reals^V$.

An encoder network maps the word counts $\wvec_d$ to a diagonal Gaussian $q_{\phivec}(\hvec_d \mid \wvec_d)$. The
decoder parameters $\Rmat, \bvec$ and the encoder weights $\phivec$ are trained together by maximising the <Gloss name="elbo">evidence
lower bound</Gloss>.
</Definition>

## Intuition

NVDM is the <NoteLink to="variational-autoencoder">variational autoencoder</NoteLink> applied to text with the word order
thrown away. A document is summarised by a point $\hvec_d$ in $\reals^K$. The decoder is a
<NoteLink to="multinomial-logistic-regression">multinomial logistic regression</NoteLink> from that point to the
vocabulary: word $v$ gets the logit $\rvec_v^\top\hvec_d + b_v$, where $\rvec_v$ is column $v$ of $\Rmat$. Documents
that use similar words are pushed to nearby points, and words used in similar documents get similar columns $\rvec_v$.
This is <NoteLink to="latent-semantic-analysis">latent semantic analysis</NoteLink> with a probabilistic decoder and an
inference network in place of the <Gloss name="svd" />.

NVDM is a document model rather than a topic model. The coordinates of $\hvec_d$ can be negative and do not sum to one,
so they are not topic proportions, and one coordinate does not correspond to one coherent group of words. NVDM has low
held-out perplexity, but its "topics", read off the rows of $\Rmat$, are hard to interpret
<Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" /> <Cite id="dieng2020etm" />.

## The objective

Given $\hvec_d$, the words are independent draws from one <NoteLink to="categorical-distribution">categorical distribution</NoteLink>, so the likelihood depends on the
document only through its counts $w_{dv}$, the number of times word $v$ occurs:

$$
\log p(\wvec_d \mid \hvec_d) = \sum_{n=1}^{N_d}\log p(w_{dn} \mid \hvec_d) = \sum_{v=1}^V w_{dv}\paren{\rvec_v^\top\hvec_d
+ b_v} - N_d\log\sum_{u=1}^V\exp\paren{\rvec_u^\top\hvec_d + b_u} .
$$

The encoder is a <Gloss name="mlp">multilayer perceptron</Gloss> $\pivec_d = \text{MLP}_{\phivec}(\wvec_d)$ followed by two linear heads,
$\muvec_d = \Wmat_1\pivec_d + \cvec_1$ and $\log\sigmavec_d^2 = \Wmat_2\pivec_d + \cvec_2$. The evidence lower bound for one
document is

$$
\Lcal_d = \expect_{q_{\phivec}(\hvec_d \mid \wvec_d)}\brack{\log p(\wvec_d \mid \hvec_d)} - \KL\paren{\Gauss(\muvec_d,
\diag\sigmavec_d^2) \,\|\, \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)} .
$$

It is derived exactly as for any VAE: $\log p(\wvec_d) = \Lcal_d + \KL(q \,\|\, p(\hvec_d \mid \wvec_d))$ and the
second term is non-negative. The <Gloss name="kl" /> term between two Gaussians has the closed form $\frac12\sum_{k=1}^K\paren{\mu_{dk}^2 +
\sigma_{dk}^2 - 1 - \log\sigma_{dk}^2}$. The <NoteLink to="expectation">expectation</NoteLink> is estimated with one reparameterised sample $\hvec_d =
\muvec_d + \sigmavec_d \odot \epsilonvec$, $\epsilonvec \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$, which makes the estimate
differentiable in $\phivec$ (see the <NoteLink to="reparameterisation-trick">reparameterisation trick</NoteLink>).

The cost of one <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> step is dominated by the softmax normaliser, a sum over all $V$ words. It is computed once per
document, not once per word, because every word in the document shares the same $\hvec_d$.

## From NVDM to a topic model

Miao, Grefenstette and Blunsom turned NVDM into a topic model by inserting the simplex between the Gaussian and the
decoder <Cite id="miao2017gsm" />. The **Gaussian softmax** construction draws $\xvec_d \sim \Gauss(\muvec_d,
\diag\sigmavec_d^2)$ and sets $\thetavec_d = \operatorname{softmax}(\Wmat\xvec_d)$, a vector of $K$ topic proportions.
Each word is then drawn from the mixture $\thetavec_d^\top\Bmat$, with the topic–word matrix $\Bmat \in \reals^{K \times V}$
built from topic and word vectors, $\Bmat = \operatorname{softmax}(\Tmat\Umat^\top)$ row by row. The same paper derives
a **stick-breaking** construction, in which a Gaussian draw passed through a sigmoid gives break proportions, and a
**recurrent stick-breaking** construction whose recurrent network can generate an unbounded number of break
proportions, so that the number of topics need not be fixed in advance. These constructions keep the Gaussian
reparameterisation of NVDM and gain interpretable topic proportions.

<NoteLink to="prodlda">ProdLDA</NoteLink> takes the same step but chooses the Gaussian's prior so that
$\operatorname{softmax}(\hvec_d)$ approximates a Dirichlet.

## Worked example

Take $K = 2$ latent dimensions, $V = 3$ words, $\bvec = \zeros$ and

$$
\Rmat = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

A document has counts $\wvec_d = (2, 0, 1)$. The encoder outputs $\muvec_d = (0.5, -1)$ and $\sigmavec_d^2 = (0.25,
0.5)$.

- **Sample.** With $\epsilonvec = (0.4, -0.2)$, $\hvec_d = (0.5 + 0.5 \times 0.4,\; -1 + \sqrt{0.5} \times (-0.2)) =
  (0.700, -1.141)$.
- **Decode.** The logits $\Rmat^\top\hvec_d$ are $(1.400, -1.841, 2.283)$. The softmax gives $p = (0.289, 0.011,
  0.699)$.
- **Reconstruction term.** $2\log 0.289 + 1 \times \log 0.699 = -2.838$.
- **KL term.** $\frac12\brack{(0.25 + 0.25 - 1 - \log 0.25) + (1 + 0.5 - 1 - \log 0.5)} = 1.040$.
- **ELBO estimate.** $-2.838 - 1.040 = -3.878$ nats for this document and this sample.

The gradient of the reconstruction term with respect to the logit of word $v$ is $w_{dv} - N_d p_v$. Gradient ascent
on this estimate raises the logit of word 1, since $2 - 3 \times 0.289 > 0$, and lowers the logits of words 2 and 3,
since $0 - 3 \times 0.011 < 0$ and $1 - 3 \times 0.699 < 0$. It also moves $\muvec_d$ towards latent
vectors $\hvec_d$ that raise the reconstruction term, and shrinks the KL term.

## Assumptions

- **Bag of words.** Word order is discarded. All words of a document share one latent vector.
- **A Gaussian latent.** The prior $\Gauss(\zeros, \Imat)$ has no sparsity: a document tends to use all $K$ directions
  a little, unlike <Gloss name="latent-dirichlet-allocation" />'s Dirichlet with $\alpha < 1$, which concentrates each document on a few topics.
- **A log-linear decoder.** One linear layer and a softmax. Deeper decoders fit better but make the latent space harder
  to read.

## Pitfalls

- **Reading rows of $\Rmat$ as topics.** The largest entries of a row of $\Rmat$ are not a coherent topic in general;
  NVDM's topic coherence is well below LDA's <Cite id="srivastava2017prodlda" />.
- **Perplexity is not interpretability.** NVDM's low perplexity shows that it models word frequencies well, not that its
  latent dimensions mean anything (see <NoteLink to="topic-model-evaluation">evaluating topic models</NoteLink>).
- **Unused latent dimensions.** The KL term pushes coordinates that do not improve reconstruction back to the prior.
  With large $K$, many coordinates stay inactive.
