<Definition>
The **document neural autoregressive distribution estimator** (DocNADE) is a model of a document $\vvec = (v_1,
\dots, v_D)$, a sequence of word indices in $\set{1, \dots, V}$, that factorises the probability by the chain rule
<Cite id="larochelle2012docnade" />:

$$
p(\vvec) = \prod_{i=1}^D p(v_i \mid \vvec_{1:i-1}), \qquad p(v_i = w \mid \vvec_{1:i-1}) = \frac{\exp\paren{b_w +
\Umat_{w,:}\hvec_i}}{\sum_{u=1}^V\exp\paren{b_u + \Umat_{u,:}\hvec_i}}, \qquad \hvec_i = g\paren{\cvec +
\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\Wmat_{:,v_k}} .
$$

Here $\Wmat \in \reals^{H \times V}$ has one column per word, $\Umat \in \reals^{V \times H}$ and $\bvec \in \reals^V$
define the output softmax, $\cvec \in \reals^H$ is a hidden bias, and $g$ is an elementwise nonlinearity such as the
sigmoid. The hidden layer is shared by all positions, so every conditional reads the same summary of the words so far.
</Definition>

## Intuition

<NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">LDA</NoteLink> and the VAE-based topic models explain a document through a
latent variable, $\thetavec_d$, that must be inferred. DocNADE has no latent variable to infer. It predicts each word
from the words before it, like an <NoteLink to="autoregressive-generative-models">autoregressive model</NoteLink> of
text, but it summarises the previous words as a bag: $\hvec_i$ depends on $\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\Wmat_{:,v_k}$, the sum of the
previous words' columns, not on their order. The sum plays the role that $\thetavec_d$ plays in LDA: an estimate of
what the document is about, sharpened with every word read.

Because the model is a product of normalised conditionals, $\log p(\vvec)$ is computed exactly in one pass. There is no
lower bound, no sampling during training, and no amortisation gap.

## Where the architecture comes from

DocNADE is derived from the **Replicated Softmax**, an undirected model of documents with binary hidden units
$\hvec \in \set{0, 1}^H$ and energy $E(\vvec, \hvec) = -D\,\cvec^\top\hvec - \hvec^\top\Wmat\nvec(\vvec) -
\bvec^\top\nvec(\vvec)$, where $\nvec(\vvec)$ is the vector of word counts. Its conditionals factorise:

$$
p(h_j = 1 \mid \vvec) = \sigma\paren{Dc_j + \sum_{i=1}^D W_{j,v_i}}, \qquad p(v_i = w \mid \hvec) =
\operatorname{softmax}_w\paren{\bvec + \Wmat^\top\hvec} .
$$

The marginal $p(\vvec)$ needs the partition function, which is intractable, and training relies on contrastive
divergence. The autoregressive conditional $p(v_i \mid \vvec_{1:i-1})$ of the Replicated Softmax can be approximated by
mean-field inference, which alternates between updating the hidden units from the words and the unseen words from the
hidden units. Larochelle and Lauly run **one** such update: start with the unseen words set to zero, compute the hidden
means from the observed words $\vvec_{1:i-1}$ alone, then compute the distribution of $v_i$ from those hidden means
<Cite id="larochelle2012docnade" />. That single pass is the formula in the definition, after two further changes: the
input weights $\Wmat$ and output weights $\Umat$ are untied, and the factor $D$ on the hidden bias is dropped. The
truncated inference is then trained directly, by maximum likelihood, to be a good model on its own.

## Cost

The hidden pre-activations share their sums: $\avec_{i+1} = \avec_i + \Wmat_{:,v_i}$ with $\avec_1 = \cvec$. All $D$
hidden layers cost $O(DH)$ together. The softmax over the vocabulary costs $O(VH)$ per position, so a document costs
$O(DVH)$. Larochelle and Lauly replaced the flat softmax by a binary tree of logistic regressions over the vocabulary,
which costs $O(H\log V)$ per position <Cite id="larochelle2012docnade" />.

## Order

A bag of words has no order, but the chain rule needs one. Any fixed order gives a valid distribution over sequences,
and different orders give different probabilities for the same bag. DocNADE is therefore trained on documents whose
words are shuffled at random, which averages over orders and makes the model approximately invariant to them
<Cite id="lauly2017docnade" />. The document representation used downstream is the hidden layer after reading every
word, $\hvec(\vvec) = g(\cvec + \sum_{k=1}^D\Wmat_{:,v_k})$, which does not depend on the order.

## What plays the role of topics

DocNADE has no topic–word matrix. Each hidden unit $j$ responds to words through row $j$ of $\Wmat$, and the words with
the largest weights $W_{j,w}$ are those that most switch unit $j$ on. Reading those lists as topics works, but a hidden
unit is not a distribution over words and is not constrained to be sparse or coherent. DocNADE's strengths are its
exact likelihood, which makes held-out perplexity directly measurable, and its document representation, which works well
as input to classifiers and retrieval <Cite id="lauly2017docnade" />.

## Worked example

Take $H = 2$ hidden units, $V = 3$ words, the sigmoid for $g$, zero biases, and

$$
\Wmat = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \Umat = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ -1 &
2 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

The document is $\vvec = (1, 3)$.

- **First word.** $\hvec_1 = \sigma(\zeros) = (0.5, 0.5)$. The logits $\Umat\hvec_1 = (0.5, 0.5, 0.5)$ are equal, so
  $p(v_1 = 1) = 1/3$.
- **Second word.** $\hvec_2 = \sigma(\Wmat_{:,1}) = \sigma(2, 0) = (0.881, 0.5)$. The logits are $(0.881, 0.5,
  0.119)$, and the softmax gives $(0.465, 0.318, 0.217)$, so $p(v_2 = 3 \mid v_1 = 1) = 0.217$.
- **Document.** $p(\vvec) = 0.333 \times 0.217 = 0.0724$, a log-likelihood of $-2.63$ nats.

Reading the same two words in the order $(3, 1)$ gives $0.0534$. The two orders disagree, which is why training
averages over random orders.

## Assumptions

- **A bag-of-words summary of the context.** Each conditional sees only the counts of previous words.
- **A fixed vocabulary** with a softmax, or a tree, over all $V$ words.
- **Approximate order invariance** from training on shuffled documents, not by construction.

## Pitfalls

- **Comparing perplexities.** DocNADE's perplexity is exact. A VAE-based topic model reports a bound or an importance-
  sampled estimate. The numbers are comparable only if the VAE's likelihood is estimated tightly.
- **Interpreting hidden units as topics.** Rows of $\Wmat$ are not normalised, can take both signs, and are not tied
  to documents' proportions. They are features, not topics in LDA's sense.
- **Order at test time.** A test document's probability depends on the order in which its words are read. Average over
  several random orders when estimating it.
