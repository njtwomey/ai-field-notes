<Definition>
A **learning paradigm** is a setting for learning, defined by what the learner observes and what it must produce. The
paradigms differ in the feedback the data carry: a correct output for every input, outputs for only some inputs or only
approximately, no outputs at all, a stream that must be predicted as it arrives, data from a related but different
task, or a reward for actions taken. This Part covers each paradigm and the main methods within it.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The paradigm decides which methods apply before any model is chosen. A fraud model trained on confirmed cases alone is a
positive-unlabelled problem, not a standard classifier. A recommender that learns from what users clicked is learning
from rewards for its own actions. A model deployed to a new hospital faces <NoteLink to="dataset-shift">domain
shift</NoteLink>. Naming the paradigm first points to the right loss, the right evaluation and the right failure modes.
Part IV supplies the principles that every paradigm shares; this Part supplies the methods.

## Main ideas

**Labels are the main axis.** <NoteLink to="supervised-learning" /> has a correct output for every training input and
covers <NoteLink to="regression">regression</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="classification">classification</NoteLink>, kernel
machines, <Gloss name="gp">Gaussian processes</Gloss>, tree ensembles and structured outputs <Cite id="hastie2009" />.
Semi-supervised methods such as <NoteLink to="self-training">self-training</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="label-propagation">label propagation</NoteLink> add many unlabelled inputs to a few labelled ones.
<NoteLink to="weak-supervision" /> learns from labels that are incomplete, inexact or inaccurate: labels on bags,
proportions, candidate sets, crowds or programs. <NoteLink to="unsupervised-learning" /> has no labels and finds
clusters, low-dimensional coordinates, topics, densities and covariances.

**Representations can be learnt without labels.** <NoteLink to="representation-learning" /> trains encoders on pretext
tasks built from unlabelled data, contrastive or masked, and reuses the features for many downstream tasks with few
labels. This is how large pretrained models are built and adapted.

**The unusual case as the target.** <NoteLink to="anomaly-detection" /> finds the points that do not fit the rest, with
<NoteLink to="statistical-tests">statistical tests</NoteLink>, densities and distances, isolation, one-class boundaries,
reconstruction error and deep features. It is usually unsupervised, and its evaluation must handle very rare positives.

**Data that arrive over time or from elsewhere.** <NoteLink to="online-learning" /> predicts a sequence one round at a
time and bounds regret against the best fixed predictor on every sequence, including adversarial ones.
<NoteLink to="transfer-learning" /> reuses knowledge from a source domain or task, through
<NoteLink to="domain-adaptation">domain adaptation</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="multi-task-and-meta-learning">multi-task
and meta-learning</NoteLink>, and active and online transfer.

**Learning to act.** <NoteLink to="reinforcement-learning" /> learns a policy that maximises long-run reward in a
<Gloss name="mdp">Markov decision process</Gloss>, from interaction rather than labelled examples
<Cite id="sutton2018" />. Its methods learn values, policies or models of the environment, and its offline variant
learns from logged experience.

The Part assumes the mathematics of Part I, in particular <NoteLink to="linear-algebra">linear algebra</NoteLink>,

<NoteLink to="loss-functions">loss functions</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="model-selection">model selection</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="evaluation-metrics">evaluation metrics</NoteLink>. The <NoteLink to="principles-of-learning">principles of
learning</NoteLink> overview maps them.

Part VI builds the neural architectures that implement most of these paradigms at scale, from
<NoteLink to="feedforward-networks">feedforward networks</NoteLink> to <Gloss name="llm">large language models</Gloss>,
whose pretraining is representation learning and whose alignment uses
<NoteLink to="reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback">reinforcement learning from human feedback</NoteLink>. Part
VII applies the paradigms in domains such as recommendation, vision and language, and Part VIII covers deploying,
monitoring and auditing the resulting models.
