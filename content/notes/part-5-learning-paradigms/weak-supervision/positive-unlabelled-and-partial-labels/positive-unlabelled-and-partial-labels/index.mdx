<Definition>
This topic covers <NoteLink to="classification">classification</NoteLink> when each training instance carries less than
its true label. In **positive-unlabelled** (PU) learning, some positives are labelled and every other instance is
unlabelled, whether positive or negative. In **partial-label** learning, each instance has a set of candidate labels
that contains the true one. In **complementary-label** learning, each instance has one class it does not belong to. In
each case a model of how the weak label arises from the true label turns the weak data into an estimate of the ordinary
classification risk.
</Definition>

## What it is for

PU data are common wherever only one outcome is recorded: known disease genes among all genes, clicked items among all
shown items, confirmed fraud among all transactions, pages a user bookmarked. Partial labels arise when an image caption
names several people and the image shows one of them, or when annotators disagree and each candidate is kept.
Complementary labels are cheaper to collect than true labels when there are many classes, because saying "not this
one" is easy.

## Main ideas

**Model the labelling mechanism.** <NoteLink to="positive-unlabelled-learning" /> assumes that positives are labelled
at random with a constant probability $c$, independent of $\xvec$. A classifier trained to separate labelled from
unlabelled instances then learns $c\,\pr(y = 1 \mid \xvec)$, and dividing by $c$ recovers the posterior. The constant
$c$, or equivalently the class prior $\pi$, must be estimated or known <Cite id="bekker2020" />.

**Rewrite the risk in terms of what is observed.** The risk on the unseen negatives can be written as the risk on the
unlabelled data minus $\pi$ times the risk of treating labelled positives as negative. This gives an unbiased PU risk
for any loss and model. <NoteLink to="non-negative-positive-unlabelled-learning" /> shows that flexible models drive the
empirical version below zero and overfit, and that clipping the negative part at zero (nnPU) fixes it
<Cite id="kiryo2017" />.

**Learn from what the label rules out.** <NoteLink to="partial-label-learning" /> trains a classifier to put its
probability on the candidate set. If no wrong label accompanies the true one too often, a small risk on the candidate
sets bounds the true risk. <NoteLink to="complementary-labels" /> takes the extreme case of one ruled-out class per
instance. When that class is drawn uniformly from the wrong ones, a rewritten loss is again an unbiased estimate of the
true risk.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume <NoteLink to="empirical-risk-minimisation">empirical risk minimisation</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink> and the class of weak-supervision settings introduced
in <NoteLink to="weak-supervision">weak supervision</NoteLink>.

PU learning connects to several other topics. Estimating the class prior from unlabelled data is related to
<NoteLink to="prior-shift-correction">prior-shift correction</NoteLink>. Learning from positives alone, without
unlabelled data, is one-class classification, as in the <NoteLink to="one-class-support-vector-machine">one-class
support vector machine</NoteLink>. Labels that are present but wrong, rather than missing or ambiguous, belong to
<NoteLink to="learning-with-noisy-labels">learning with noisy labels</NoteLink>, and labels from many unreliable sources
to <NoteLink to="programmatic-weak-supervision">programmatic weak supervision</NoteLink>.
