<Definition>
**Programmatic weak supervision** replaces hand-labelling of a training set with labelling functions: short programs,
heuristics, pattern matches, knowledge-base lookups or model outputs that each vote for a label or abstain. A **label
model** estimates how accurate each function is, without any ground truth, and combines their votes into a
probabilistic label per example. An **end model** is then trained on those labels and generalises beyond the
functions' coverage <Cite id="ratner2016" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Large labelled training sets are slow and expensive to build, and experts' time is scarce. Domain experts can often
state their knowledge as rules faster than they can label thousands of examples. Programmatic supervision turns those
rules into training data, and the labels can be regenerated when the rules or the task change.

## Main ideas

**Many noisy voters, no ground truth.** <NoteLink to="data-programming" /> models each labelling function as a voter
with an unknown accuracy. If functions are conditionally independent given the true label, the agreement rate of two
functions is the product of their accuracies, so the accuracies can be recovered from agreements alone.

**Estimating the label model.** <NoteLink to="label-model-estimation" /> covers three ways to fit the accuracies and
correlations of the functions: pseudolikelihood with structure learning, matrix completion on the inverse covariance of
the votes (MeTaL), and a closed-form solution from triplets of functions (FlyingSquid). The same idea underlies the
<NoteLink to="dawid-skene-model" /> for crowd annotators.

**A system around the model.** <NoteLink to="snorkel" /> is the first end-to-end system: users write labelling
functions, Snorkel fits the label model and trains a discriminative end model on its probabilistic labels.
<NoteLink to="weak-supervision-in-production" /> describes later systems that generate labelling functions, learn
augmentations, monitor critical slices of data, and use prompted <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss> as
labelling functions.

**Measuring what works.** <NoteLink to="weak-supervision-benchmarks" /> reports the WRENCH benchmark: no label model
wins on every dataset, majority vote is a strong baseline, and the quality of the labelling functions matters more than
the choice of label model <Cite id="zhang2021wrench" />.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume the settings of <NoteLink to="weak-supervision">weak supervision</NoteLink>, basic probability, and
<NoteLink to="expectation-maximisation">expectation–maximisation</NoteLink> for the label models fitted by likelihood.

Programmatic labels are one kind of inaccurate supervision. <NoteLink to="dawid-skene-model" /> and
<NoteLink to="learning-from-crowds">learning from crowds</NoteLink> handle noisy human annotators, and
<NoteLink to="learning-with-noisy-labels">learning with noisy labels</NoteLink> handles labels corrupted by an unknown
noise process. <NoteLink to="positive-unlabelled-and-partial-labels" /> covers labels that are missing or ambiguous
rather than wrong.
