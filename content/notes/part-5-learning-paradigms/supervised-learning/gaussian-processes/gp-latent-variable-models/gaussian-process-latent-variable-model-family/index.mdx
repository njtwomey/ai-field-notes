<Definition>
The **Gaussian process latent variable model** (GP-LVM) explains each observation $\yvec_n \in \reals^D$ as a
Gaussian-process function of an unobserved latent point $\xvec_n \in \reals^Q$, with $Q$ much smaller than $D$. The
function is integrated out and the latent points are fitted. The family consists of the GP-LVM and the models that
change how the latent points are constrained, inferred or shared <Cite id="lawrence2005" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

GP-LVMs give low-dimensional embeddings for visualisation and generative models of high-dimensional data from few
examples. Each latent point maps back to a full observation, so the latent space can be sampled, interpolated and
edited. Uses include human motion capture, where dynamical variants model poses over time, paired data such as images
and their descriptions, and design spaces such as the <NoteLink to="manifold-of-fonts">manifold of fonts</NoteLink>.

## Main ideas

**A GP-LVM is a nonlinear probabilistic PCA.** Probabilistic PCA integrates out the latent points and fits a linear map.
The <NoteLink to="gaussian-process-latent-variable-model">GP-LVM</NoteLink> takes the dual view: it integrates out the
map under a Gaussian-process prior and fits the latent points. With a linear kernel the solution is PCA again. A
nonlinear kernel gives a smooth nonlinear embedding in which points far apart in the data stay far apart in the latent
space.

**Back-constraints keep neighbours together.** The GP-LVM preserves dissimilarity, not similarity: nearby data points
may land far apart. The <NoteLink to="back-constrained-gaussian-process-latent-variable-model">back-constrained
GP-LVM</NoteLink> writes each latent point as a smooth function of its observation, which keeps neighbours close and
embeds new data without optimisation.

**A Bayesian treatment selects the latent dimension.** The
<NoteLink to="bayesian-gaussian-process-latent-variable-model">Bayesian GP-LVM</NoteLink> integrates out the latent
points too, under a variational posterior and a lower bound built from inducing variables <Cite id="titsias2010" />.
With an <NoteLink to="automatic-relevance-determination">ARD</NoteLink> kernel, it switches off latent dimensions the
data do not need.

**Structure in the latent space.** A <NoteLink to="gaussian-process-dynamical-models">Gaussian process dynamical
model</NoteLink> puts a second Gaussian process on transitions between consecutive latent states, which favours
smooth trajectories for time series. <NoteLink to="shared-and-multi-view-gaussian-process-latent-variable-models">Shared
and multi-view GP-LVMs</NoteLink> explain several paired data sets with one latent space and learn which latent
dimensions each view shares and which are private to it.

## How the notes fit together

- Start with the <NoteLink to="gaussian-process-latent-variable-model" />. Every other note modifies it.
- The back-constrained and dynamical models each change one thing about the base model and can be read in any order.
- The Bayesian GP-LVM needs <NoteLink to="sparse-gaussian-processes">sparse Gaussian processes</NoteLink> for its
  inducing-variable bound. The multi-view models build on it.
- The <NoteLink to="manifold-of-fonts">manifold of fonts</NoteLink> applies the base GP-LVM to a real generative design
  problem.

## Prerequisites and next steps

Read the <NoteLink to="gaussian-process" />, <NoteLink to="probabilistic-principal-component-analysis">probabilistic
PCA</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="gaussian-process-hyperparameter-learning" /> first, since a GP-LVM is fitted by the
same marginal likelihood with the inputs made free. <NoteLink to="variational-inference" /> is needed for the Bayesian
variants.

The GP-LVM sits beside the <NoteLink to="dimensionality-reduction">embedding methods</NoteLink> of
<NoteLink to="manifold-learning">manifold learning</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="principal-component-analysis">principal
component analysis</NoteLink>, which map data to latent points rather than latent points to data. A
<NoteLink to="variational-autoencoder">variational autoencoder</NoteLink> replaces the Gaussian-process map with a
neural network and the fitted latent points with an encoder.
