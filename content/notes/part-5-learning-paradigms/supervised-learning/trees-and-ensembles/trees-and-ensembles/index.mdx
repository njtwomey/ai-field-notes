<Definition>
A **decision tree** predicts by routing an input through a sequence of threshold tests on single features to a leaf,
and returns the leaf's constant. An **ensemble** combines the predictions of many models, usually trees, into one.
Averaging independently grown trees reduces variance; adding trees one at a time, each correcting the errors of the
sum so far, reduces bias.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Tree ensembles are the strongest general-purpose models for tabular data: rows of mixed numeric and categorical
features, as in credit scoring, fraud detection, demand forecasting and ranking. On such data, gradient-boosted trees
still match or beat deep networks <Cite id="grinsztajn2022" />. Trees need no <NoteLink to="feature-scaling">feature
scaling</NoteLink>, handle missing values and interactions natively, and scale to millions of rows.

## Main ideas

**Greedy recursive splitting.** A <NoteLink to="decision-tree">decision tree</NoteLink> is grown by CART, which picks at
each node the feature and threshold that most reduce squared error, Gini impurity or entropy. A single deep tree has low
bias and high variance. <NoteLink to="tree-pruning" /> grows a large tree and cuts back branches whose gain does not pay
for their extra leaves.

**Averaging decorrelated trees.** <NoteLink to="bagging" /> fits one tree per bootstrap resample and averages them. The
variance of the average falls towards $\rho\sigma^2$, where $\sigma^2$ is the variance of one tree and $\rho$ the
correlation between trees. A <NoteLink to="random-forest">random forest</NoteLink> lowers $\rho$ by choosing each split
among a random subset of features <Cite id="breiman2001" />. <NoteLink to="extremely-randomised-trees" /> also randomise
the thresholds.

**Boosting fits the residual.** <NoteLink to="adaboost" /> reweights the training data towards the previous
mistakes, which is stagewise fitting of an additive model under the exponential loss.
<NoteLink to="gradient-boosting" /> generalises this to any differentiable loss: each new small tree fits the negative
gradient of the loss at the current predictions, so boosting is gradient descent in function space
<Cite id="friedman2001" />. <NoteLink to="xgboost-lightgbm-catboost">XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost</NoteLink> are
the libraries that made it fast and robust.

**Combining different models.** <NoteLink to="stacking" /> trains a meta-model on the out-of-fold predictions of
several base models. The out-of-fold step stops the meta-model from trusting whichever base model overfits most.

**Explaining ensembles.** An ensemble of hundreds of trees cannot be read directly.
<NoteLink to="shapley-additive-explanations-for-trees">SHAP values for trees</NoteLink> attribute each prediction to the
features, and TreeSHAP computes them exactly in polynomial time.
