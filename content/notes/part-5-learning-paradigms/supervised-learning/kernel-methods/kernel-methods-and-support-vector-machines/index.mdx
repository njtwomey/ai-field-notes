<Definition>
A **kernel method** fits a model that is linear in a feature space $\phivec(\xvec)$ but computes only
<NoteLink to="dot-product">inner products</NoteLink> $k(\xvec, \xvec') = \phivec(\xvec)^\top\phivec(\xvec')$, through a
positive semi-definite kernel $k$. The feature space may have very many or infinitely many dimensions, since the
features are never computed. The **support vector machine** (SVM) is the best-known kernel method: a classifier that
maximises the margin between classes.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Kernel methods give nonlinear classifiers and regressors whose training is a convex optimisation problem with a unique
optimum. SVMs were the leading general-purpose classifiers from the late 1990s until deep learning, and remain strong on
small and medium tabular, text and biological data. Kernels also define distances between distributions, tests of
independence and <NoteLink to="covariance-functions">covariance functions</NoteLink> for
<NoteLink to="gaussian-processes" />, and kernels on strings, graphs and sets let the same algorithms work on structured
data.

## Main ideas

**Maximise the margin.** A <NoteLink to="support-vector-machine">support vector machine</NoteLink> chooses the
separating hyperplane with the largest distance to the nearest training points, and allows violations at a cost $C$ per
unit of slack <Cite id="cortes1995" />. The solution depends only on the few points on or inside the margin, the support
vectors.

**Solve the dual.** The SVM is a quadratic programme. Its Lagrangian dual depends on the data only through inner
products $\xvec_i^\top\xvec_j$, and the <NoteLink to="karush-kuhn-tucker-conditions" /> say which points are support
vectors. <NoteLink to="solving-support-vector-machines" /> solves a six-point problem by hand, and
<NoteLink to="sequential-minimal-optimisation">sequential minimal optimisation (SMO)</NoteLink> solves the dual two
multipliers at a time, analytically.

**Replace inner products with a kernel.** Because the dual uses only inner products, substituting $k(\xvec_i, \xvec_j)$
fits a maximum-margin hyperplane in the kernel's feature space: the <NoteLink to="kernel-support-vector-machine">kernel
support vector machine</NoteLink>. The same substitution turns ridge regression into
<NoteLink to="kernel-ridge-regression">kernel ridge regression</NoteLink>, whose predictions equal the posterior mean of
a Gaussian process.

**The function-space view.** Each positive semi-definite kernel defines a
<NoteLink to="reproducing-kernel-hilbert-space">reproducing kernel Hilbert space</NoteLink> (RKHS) of functions, whose
norm measures roughness. The <NoteLink to="representer-theorem">representer theorem</NoteLink> shows that a regularised
fit over that space is a weighted sum of kernels centred on the $N$ training points. This explains why kernel methods
reduce infinite-dimensional problems to $N$ coefficients, and why they cost at least $O(N^2)$ in memory.

**Kernel choice matters.** A radial kernel's width sets how far each training point's influence reaches. The
<NoteLink to="median-heuristic">median heuristic for kernel width</NoteLink> sets it from typical pairwise distances, a
default that cross-validation then refines.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume the <NoteLink to="kernel-trick">kernel trick</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="lagrangian-duality" /> and
<NoteLink to="quadratic-programming">quadratic programming</NoteLink>. The RKHS note also needs
<NoteLink to="positive-definite-matrices">positive definite matrices</NoteLink>.

Kernels reach well beyond this topic. <NoteLink to="gaussian-processes" /> use the same kernels as prior covariances.
<NoteLink to="multiclass-support-vector-machines" /> extend the margin to $K$ classes, and
<NoteLink to="one-class-support-vector-machine">one-class SVMs</NoteLink> detect anomalies.
<NoteLink to="kernel-principal-component-analysis" /> applies the kernel trick to <Gloss name="pca" />, and
<NoteLink to="maximum-mean-discrepancy">maximum mean discrepancy</NoteLink> compares distributions through kernel mean
embeddings. <NoteLink to="random-fourier-features" /> approximate a kernel with explicit features, which removes the
$O(N^2)$ cost.
