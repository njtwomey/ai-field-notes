<Definition>
**Active and Bayesian transfer learning** reduces the number of labels a new target domain needs in two ways. A Bayesian
prior learnt on source domains gives the target model useful predictions before any target label arrives.
<Gloss name="active-learning">Active learning</Gloss> then spends a
labelling budget on the target examples whose labels would tell
the model most <Cite id="settles2009" />. The posterior uncertainty that the prior supplies is what makes the active
choice possible.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Labelling is often the expensive part of deploying a model to a new person, device or building. Activity recognition
is the running example in this topic: every new resident or home differs from the training data, and asking a person
to annotate their own activities is costly and intrusive. The same pattern applies to personalised medicine, per-user
recommendation and per-site sensor networks, wherever many related domains each have few labels.

## Main ideas

**Choose the most informative label.** <NoteLink to="bayesian-active-learning" /> keeps a posterior over model
parameters and queries the input whose label has the greatest expected information gain about them. That gain equals
the mutual information between label and parameters, which can be computed in label space as the predictive
<NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink> minus the expected entropy given the parameters, the form known as BALD
<Cite id="houlsby2011bald" />. Evidence-based selection compares Bayes factors instead.

**Start from the source, then query.** <NoteLink to="active-transfer-learning" /> initialises the target model with a
prior learnt from other people or other homes, using a community Bayes point machine, and then queries labels where
the transferred model is least certain <Cite id="diethe2016active" />. Transfer removes the cold start that makes early
active-learning queries poor, and active selection spends the budget where the model is least sure.

**Make the input spaces comparable.** Transfer between smart homes is heterogeneous: two homes rarely have the same
sensors. <NoteLink to="context-modulation-and-sensor-transfer" /> rewrites datasets into shared contexts by deleting,
merging and renaming sensors and labels, and learns sensor adjacency from unlabelled data to build layout-aware features
that carry across homes.
