<Definition>
**Domain adaptation** trains a predictor on labelled data from a source distribution $p_S(\xvec, y)$ for use on a
target distribution $p_T(\xvec, y)$ that differs from it. The task is the same in both domains. Usually the target
supplies unlabelled inputs only, which is unsupervised domain adaptation; sometimes it also supplies a few labels. The
methods assume a specific relation between the two distributions and exploit it.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Deployed models meet data unlike their training data. A classifier trained on product photos meets phone pictures, a
sentiment model trained on film reviews meets restaurant reviews, a medical model trained at one hospital meets
another hospital's scanners, and synthetic training images meet real ones. Labelling enough target data to retrain is
often too expensive. Domain adaptation uses the plentiful unlabelled target data instead.

## Main ideas

**Name the shift.** <NoteLink to="dataset-shift" /> classifies how $p(\xvec, y)$ can change. Covariate shift changes
$p(\xvec)$ and keeps $p(y \mid \xvec)$. Label shift changes $p(y)$ and keeps $p(\xvec \mid y)$. Concept shift changes
the relation between inputs and labels <Cite id="quinonero2009" />. Unlabelled target inputs alone cannot reveal a
change in $p(y \mid \xvec)$. Each method
in this topic is correct under one of these assumptions and can fail under the others.

**Bound the target error.** <NoteLink to="domain-adaptation-theory" /> bounds the target error of a classifier by its
source error, plus a divergence between the two input distributions, plus the error of the best classifier on both
domains together <Cite id="bendavid2010" />. The bound motivates two strategies: reweight the source so that it
resembles the target, or map both domains into a space where the divergence is small.

**Reweight the source.** Under covariate shift, weighting each source example by $p_T(\xvec)/p_S(\xvec)$ makes the
source risk unbiased for the target risk. <NoteLink to="importance-weighting-for-covariate-shift" /> estimates the
weights directly and shows that their variance limits the <Gloss name="ess">effective sample size</Gloss>.
<NoteLink to="label-shift-and-target-shift" /> estimates new class proportions from unlabelled target inputs and
reweights by class.

**Align the features.** <NoteLink to="feature-alignment-methods" /> learn a representation in which the source and
target distributions match, by kernel mean embeddings, covariances (CORAL) or an MMD loss.
<NoteLink to="domain-adversarial-training" /> trains features that defeat a
<NoteLink to="density-ratio-by-classification">domain classifier</NoteLink>, with a gradient reversal layer.
<NoteLink to="optimal-transport-for-domain-adaptation" /> moves source samples onto the target distribution along an
optimal transport plan.

**No target data at all.** <NoteLink to="domain-generalisation" /> trains on several source domains so that a predictor
works on an unseen one. Invariance across sources is the main tool, and pooled training is a strong baseline under fair
<NoteLink to="model-selection">model selection</NoteLink>.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume <NoteLink to="transfer-learning">transfer learning</NoteLink> for the vocabulary of domains and tasks,
and <NoteLink to="empirical-risk-minimisation">empirical risk minimisation</NoteLink>. The reweighting notes use
<NoteLink to="density-ratio-estimation">density ratio estimation</NoteLink>, and the alignment notes use
<NoteLink to="maximum-mean-discrepancy">maximum mean discrepancy</NoteLink> and <NoteLink to="optimal-transport">optimal
transport</NoteLink>.

The same problem appears elsewhere under other names. <NoteLink to="prior-shift-correction" /> handles label shift for a
fixed classifier. <NoteLink to="concept-drift-adaptation" /> handles shift that changes over time in a stream, and
<NoteLink to="test-time-adaptation">test-time adaptation</NoteLink> adapts a model to unlabelled test batches without
the source data. <NoteLink to="robustness-to-distribution-shift" /> covers shift from the point of view of deployment
and evaluation.
