<Definition>
A **domain-adversarial neural network** (DANN) has three parts: a feature extractor $G_f(\cdot; \thetavec_f)$, a label
predictor $G_y(\cdot; \thetavec_y)$ and a domain classifier $G_d(\cdot; \thetavec_d)$. The label predictor and the
domain classifier are trained to minimise their losses. The feature extractor is trained to minimise the label loss on
labelled source examples and to *maximise* the domain classifier's loss on source and target inputs. A
**<NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> reversal layer** between the features and the domain classifier makes this
possible with one backward pass <Cite id="ganin2015" /> <Cite id="ganin2016" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

The bound of <NoteLink to="domain-adaptation-theory" /> says a classifier transfers well if it has low source error and
works on a representation in which the domains are hard to tell apart. A domain classifier measures how easy they are to
tell apart: its best error $\epsilon$ gives the empirical divergence $2(1 - 2\epsilon)$. Fixed statistics such as the
<Gloss name="mmd" /> or covariance difference (see <NoteLink to="feature-alignment-methods" />) look for particular
differences. A domain classifier trained alongside the features looks for whatever difference remains, and the features
are trained to remove it.

## Mechanism

### The objective

Let $L_y$ be the classification loss on source examples $i = 1, \dots, n$, and $L_d$ the logistic loss of the domain
classifier on domain labels $d_i = 0$ for the $n$ source inputs and $d_i = 1$ for the $n'$ target inputs. DANN seeks a
saddle point of
$$
E(\thetavec_f, \thetavec_y, \thetavec_d) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} L_y^i(\thetavec_f, \thetavec_y) - \lambda
\paren{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} L_d^i(\thetavec_f, \thetavec_d) + \frac{1}{n'} \sum_{i=n+1}^{n+n'} L_d^i(\thetavec_f,
\thetavec_d)},
$$
minimised over $(\thetavec_f, \thetavec_y)$ and maximised over $\thetavec_d$ <Cite id="ganin2016" />. Maximising $E$
over $\thetavec_d$ is minimising the domain loss: the domain classifier gets as good as it can. Minimising $E$ over
$\thetavec_f$ trades label loss against domain loss: the features get as uninformative about the domain as the label
loss allows. The weight $\lambda > 0$ sets the trade.

### What the domain loss measures

Write $p_S(\zvec)$ and $p_T(\zvec)$ for the distributions of the features $\zvec = G_f(\xvec)$ in each domain, and take
equal domain sizes. A domain classifier outputs $D(\zvec) = \pr(d = 1 \mid \zvec)$, and its expected logistic loss is
$$
\ell(D) = -\expect_{p_S}\brack{\log(1 - D(\zvec))} - \expect_{p_T}\brack{\log D(\zvec)} .
$$
For each $\zvec$ the integrand $-p_S \log(1 - D) - p_T \log D$ is minimised at $D^*(\zvec) = p_T(\zvec) /
(p_S(\zvec) + p_T(\zvec))$. Substituting, with $m = (p_S + p_T)/2$,
$$
\ell(D^*) = -\expect_{p_S}\brack{\log \frac{p_S}{2m}} - \expect_{p_T}\brack{\log \frac{p_T}{2m}} = 2 \log 2 -
\KL(p_S \,\|\, m) - \KL(p_T \,\|\, m) = 2 \log 2 - 2\, \text{JS}(p_S, p_T),
$$
where $\text{JS}$ is the <Gloss name="js">Jensen–Shannon divergence</Gloss>. The feature extractor, by maximising the
loss of the best domain classifier, minimises the Jensen–Shannon divergence between the feature distributions. This is
the argument used for <Gloss name="gan">generative adversarial networks</Gloss>, with the source features in the role of
the generated samples <Cite id="goodfellow2014" />. With the 0–1 loss in place of the logistic loss, the best domain
classifier's error $\epsilon$ gives the $\Hcal$-divergence $2(1 - 2\epsilon)$ for the class of domain classifiers, the
quantity in the bound <Cite id="bendavid2010" />. Ganin et al. derive DANN from this second view
<Cite id="ganin2016" />.

### Gradient reversal

A saddle point of $E$ is sought by gradient steps with <Gloss name="learning-rate">learning rate</Gloss> $\eta$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\thetavec_f &\leftarrow \thetavec_f - \eta \paren{\frac{\partial L_y}{\partial \thetavec_f} - \lambda \frac{\partial
L_d}{\partial \thetavec_f}}, \\
\thetavec_y &\leftarrow \thetavec_y - \eta \frac{\partial L_y}{\partial \thetavec_y}, \\
\thetavec_d &\leftarrow \thetavec_d - \eta \lambda \frac{\partial L_d}{\partial \thetavec_d} .
\end{aligned}
$$
Only the first line is unusual: the feature extractor receives the domain gradient with its sign reversed. Standard
<NoteLink to="backpropagation" /> computes $\partial L_d / \partial \thetavec_f$ by passing
$\partial L_d / \partial \zvec$ back through $G_f$. Insert a layer $R$ between $G_f$ and $G_d$ that is the identity on
the forward pass and multiplies incoming gradients by $-\lambda$ on the backward pass:
$$
R(\zvec) = \zvec, \qquad \frac{\partial R}{\partial \zvec} := -\lambda \eye .
$$
Then minimise $L_y + L_d$ by ordinary backpropagation, with $L_d$ computed on $G_d(R(G_f(\xvec)))$. The domain
classifier sees an unmodified gradient and descends $L_d$. By the <NoteLink to="derivative">chain rule</NoteLink>, the
gradient that reaches $\thetavec_f$ from the domain branch is $\paren{\partial \zvec / \partial \thetavec_f}\transpose (-\lambda)\, \partial L_d / \partial \zvec = -\lambda\,
\partial L_d / \partial \thetavec_f$, which is the first update. The layer has no parameters and costs nothing
<Cite id="ganin2015" />.

### Schedule

Early in training the domain classifier's gradient is noisy and can wreck the features before they are useful for the
labels. Ganin et al. raise the reversal weight from $0$ to $1$ as $\lambda_p = 2 / (1 + \exp(-\gamma p)) - 1$, where $p$
is the fraction of training completed and $\gamma = 10$ <Cite id="ganin2016" />.

## Cost

One extra small network and its forward and backward passes per minibatch, and target inputs in every minibatch. No
target labels are used.

## Variants

- **Adversarial discriminative domain adaptation** trains a source encoder and classifier first, then trains a separate
  target encoder, initialised from the source one, to fool a domain classifier with the inverted GAN loss rather than
  a reversed gradient <Cite id="tzeng2017adda" />. Untying the encoders lets the target representation move further.
- **Weighting source examples.** Adding a discriminator that sees features and labels, and weighting each source example
  by the estimated joint density ratio, reduces negative transfer <Cite id="wang2019negative" />.

## Trade-offs

- **Aligned marginals can mean wrong labels.** The adversarial loss makes $p_S(\zvec)$ and $p_T(\zvec)$ match; it does
  not make $p(y \mid \zvec)$ match. If the class proportions differ between domains, matching the marginals must put
  some target inputs of one class where source inputs of another class are, and the joint error rises
  <Cite id="zhao2019invariant" />. See <NoteLink to="negative-transfer" />.
- **Saddle points are unstable.** The objective is a two-player game. Training can oscillate, and the domain classifier
  can win or lose completely. The schedule on $\lambda$ and a domain classifier of moderate capacity help.
- **Model selection needs target labels.** Choosing $\lambda$, the architecture and the
  <NoteLink to="martingale">stopping time</NoteLink> needs target labels, which the setting does not have. Reported
  gains that tuned on target labels overstate what is achievable.
