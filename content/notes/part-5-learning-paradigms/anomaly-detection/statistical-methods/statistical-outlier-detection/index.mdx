<Definition>
**Statistical outlier detection** fits a probability model to the data, usually a Gaussian or a model of its tail, and
flags points that the model makes improbable. Each method states its threshold as a probability, so the expected number
of false alarms on normal data is known in advance. The methods in this topic work on one variable or on a vector with
few dimensions, and need no labelled anomalies.
</Definition>

## What it is for

These methods clean measurement data before analysis, check sensor readings and transactions against their normal
range, and set alarm thresholds on the scores of any other detector. They are fast, need no training beyond a mean,
a spread or a tail fit, and give results that are easy to explain: a value is so many standard deviations from the
centre, or has a stated probability of being exceeded <Cite id="iglewicz1993" />.

## Main ideas

**Distance from the centre in units of spread.** The
<NoteLink to="z-score-and-robust-outlier-detection">z-score</NoteLink> $(x - \bar{x})/s$ counts standard deviations from
the mean. The robust z-score replaces the mean by the median and the standard deviation by the scaled median absolute
deviation, which a few outliers barely move.

**Masking.** Outliers inflate the mean and the standard deviation they are measured against, so they hide themselves
and each other. Robust estimates of location and scale, and of covariance in several dimensions, are the standard
remedy. Masking recurs in the z-score, Grubbs and Mahalanobis notes.

**A test for the most extreme value.** <NoteLink to="grubbs-test" /> asks whether the most extreme value in a Gaussian
sample is an outlier, with a critical value from a Student $t$ quantile. It is a hypothesis test with a stated
<NoteLink to="significance-level">significance level</NoteLink>, and repeated application needs care with
<NoteLink to="multiple-testing">multiple testing</NoteLink>.

**Several variables at once.** <NoteLink to="mahalanobis-distance-outliers" /> measures distance from the centre in
units of the covariance. For Gaussian data the squared distance follows a $\ChiSq(p)$ distribution with $p$ the number
of variables, which sets the threshold. A robust covariance estimate such as the minimum covariance determinant
prevents masking.

**Thresholds in the tail.** <NoteLink to="extreme-value-theory-for-anomalies" /> models only the excesses over a high
threshold, which follow a generalised Pareto distribution <Cite id="coles2001" />. It gives thresholds for very small
false-alarm rates, even beyond the largest value observed, and applies to the score of any detector.

## How the notes fit together

- Start with <NoteLink to="z-score-and-robust-outlier-detection">z-score and robust outlier detection</NoteLink>. It
  introduces masking and robust estimates.
- <NoteLink to="grubbs-test" /> and <NoteLink to="mahalanobis-distance-outliers" /> extend it to a formal test and to
  several variables. They are independent of each other.
- <NoteLink to="extreme-value-theory-for-anomalies" /> stands alone and is useful with every other family of
  detectors.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume the <NoteLink to="gaussian-distribution" />, the <NoteLink to="student-t-distribution" /> and the
<NoteLink to="chi-squared-distribution">chi-squared distribution</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="null-and-alternative-hypotheses">null and alternative hypotheses</NoteLink> for Grubbs' test, and the
<NoteLink to="mahalanobis-distance" />.

When the data do not fit a simple parametric model, other families of <NoteLink to="anomaly-detection">anomaly
detection</NoteLink> take over: <NoteLink to="local-outlier-factor" /> and neighbour distances for local density,
<NoteLink to="isolation-forest">isolation forest</NoteLink> for high-dimensional tabular data, and
<NoteLink to="deep-anomaly-detection">deep anomaly detection</NoteLink> for images and other raw signals.
