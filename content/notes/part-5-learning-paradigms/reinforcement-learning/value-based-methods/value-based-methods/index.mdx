<Definition>
A **value-based method** learns an estimate of the <Gloss name="value-function">action-value function</Gloss> $q(s, a)$,
the expected return of taking action $a$ in state $s$ and acting well afterwards, from sampled transitions rather than a
known model. The policy is derived from the values: act greedily, with some exploration. The methods replace the
expectations in the <NoteLink to="bellman-equations">Bellman equations</NoteLink> with single samples and move the
estimate a small step towards each sampled target <Cite id="sutton2018" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Value-based methods solve control problems with discrete actions when the dynamics are unknown or too complex to write
down: board and video games, routing, scheduling, and recommendation sequences. Tabular Q-learning solves small problems
exactly in the limit. Deep Q-networks learned to play dozens of Atari games from pixels with one architecture
<Cite id="mnih2015" />, and their stabilising devices are reused across deep <Gloss name="rl">reinforcement
learning</Gloss>.

## Main ideas

**Bootstrap from the next estimate.** <NoteLink to="temporal-difference-learning" /> moves $V(S_t)$ towards
$R_{t+1} + \gamma V(S_{t+1})$, a target built from the current estimate of the next state. It is stochastic
approximation of the Bellman equation: biased by its own estimates, but with far lower variance than waiting for the
full return.

**On-policy and off-policy control.** <NoteLink to="sarsa">SARSA</NoteLink> updates towards the value of the action the
agent actually takes next, so it learns the value of the exploring policy it follows.
<NoteLink to="q-learning" /> updates towards the best next action's value, $\max_{a'} Q(S', a')$, so it learns the
optimal values while following any policy that keeps exploring. The maximum over noisy estimates is biased upwards,
which double Q-learning corrects.

**Exploration.** An agent learns nothing about actions it never tries.
<NoteLink to="exploration-in-reinforcement-learning" /> shows that $\varepsilon$-greedy dithering can need time
exponential in how far away useful information lies, and covers directed alternatives: optimism in the face of
uncertainty, count-based bonuses and intrinsic rewards.

**Function approximation.** Large state spaces need a parameterised $Q_{\thetavec}$. The
<NoteLink to="deep-q-network">deep Q-network</NoteLink> fits a neural network by Q-learning on minibatches from a replay
buffer of past transitions, with targets from a frozen copy of the network. Both devices reduce the risk that
bootstrapping, off-policy data and function approximation together make the regression diverge.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume the <NoteLink to="markov-decision-processes-and-dynamic-programming">Markov decision process and
Bellman equations</NoteLink>, <NoteLink to="epsilon-greedy" /> action selection, and, for deep Q-networks, the
<NoteLink to="multilayer-perceptron">multilayer perceptron</NoteLink>.

Learned values reappear across the Part. <NoteLink to="actor-critic" /> methods in <NoteLink to="policy-methods">policy
methods</NoteLink> use a TD-learned critic. <NoteLink to="offline-reinforcement-learning" /> faces the overestimation of
Q-learning in its sharpest form, for actions absent from the data, and
<NoteLink to="model-based-reinforcement-learning">model-based reinforcement learning</NoteLink> mixes Q-learning with
simulated experience. The exploration problem in its simplest, one-state form is the
<NoteLink to="multi-armed-bandit">multi-armed bandit</NoteLink>.
