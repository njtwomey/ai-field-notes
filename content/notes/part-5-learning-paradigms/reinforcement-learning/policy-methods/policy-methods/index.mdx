<Definition>
A **policy method** represents the policy as a differentiable function $\pi_{\thetavec}(a \mid s)$ and adjusts its
parameters $\thetavec$ by gradient ascent on the expected return $J(\thetavec)$. The gradient is estimated from sampled
trajectories, often with the help of a learnt <Gloss name="value-function">value function</Gloss>. Unlike value-based
methods, the policy is the object being learnt, not something derived from values.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Policy methods handle continuous and high-dimensional action spaces, such as joint torques in robotics, where taking a
maximum over actions is impractical. They can represent stochastic policies, which matter in games and partially
observed problems, and they change the policy smoothly, which makes training more stable. Proximal policy optimisation
is the standard algorithm for fine-tuning <Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss> from human preferences.

## Main ideas

**The gradient needs no model.** The <NoteLink to="policy-gradient-theorem">policy gradient theorem</NoteLink> writes
$\nabla J(\thetavec)$ as an expectation of $q_\pi(S_t, A_t)\,\nabla \log \pi_{\thetavec}(A_t \mid S_t)$ over
trajectories, with no derivative of the environment. REINFORCE estimates it from sampled returns
<Cite id="williams1992" />. Subtracting a baseline that depends only on the state cuts the variance without adding bias.

**A critic reduces variance.** <NoteLink to="actor-critic" /> methods learn a value function, the critic, by
temporal-difference learning and use it to estimate the advantage of each action for the policy, the actor.
Generalised advantage estimation averages $n$-step advantages into a geometrically weighted sum of TD errors, trading
bias against variance with one parameter $\lambda$.

**Keep each update small.** A large policy step can collapse performance.
<NoteLink to="trust-region-and-proximal-policy-optimisation" /> maximises an importance-weighted surrogate objective
while keeping the new policy close to the old one: TRPO bounds their KL divergence, and PPO clips the probability ratio
<Cite id="schulman2017" />.

**Deterministic policies for continuous actions.** <NoteLink to="deterministic-policy-gradients" /> moves the action
uphill on a learnt critic, $\nabla_a Q(s, a)$, through the policy's Jacobian. DDPG and TD3 build on this, and soft
actor-critic adds an entropy bonus that keeps the policy exploring.

**Rewards from preferences.** <NoteLink to="reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback" /> fits a reward model to
pairwise human preferences and maximises it with PPO under a KL penalty to a reference policy. Direct preference
optimisation solves the same KL-regularised problem in closed form and fits the policy without a reward model.
