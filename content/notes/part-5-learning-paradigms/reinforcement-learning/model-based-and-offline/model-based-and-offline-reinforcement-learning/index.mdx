<Definition>
This topic covers two ways to reduce how much a reinforcement learner must interact with its environment. **Model-based
reinforcement learning** learns or is given a model of transitions and rewards, and uses it to plan. **Offline
reinforcement learning** learns a policy from a fixed dataset of past interactions, with no further
<NoteLink to="data-collection">data collection</NoteLink>. Both depend on evaluating a policy from data that it did not
generate, which is the subject of off-policy evaluation.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Interaction is expensive or risky in most real problems: a robot wears out, a patient cannot be given a random
treatment, a recommender cannot try arbitrary policies on live users. A model lets the agent rehearse in simulation, and
in games with known rules, planning with search reaches superhuman play. Offline methods turn the logs that
businesses and hospitals already hold into policies, and off-policy evaluation estimates how a new policy would perform
before it is deployed <Cite id="levine2020" />.

## Main ideas

**Plan with a model.** <NoteLink to="model-based-reinforcement-learning" /> uses a model in two ways. Dyna generates
simulated transitions from a learnt model and feeds them to an ordinary value-based learner. Monte Carlo tree search
looks ahead from the current state, and AlphaZero combines it with learnt policy and value networks. One-step model
errors compound over a long horizon, so MuZero learns only the quantities that planning needs.

**Evaluate without deploying.** <NoteLink to="off-policy-evaluation-in-reinforcement-learning" /> estimates a target
policy's return from trajectories of a different behaviour policy. Importance sampling reweights rewards by products
of probability ratios, which is unbiased but has variance that grows with the horizon. Doubly robust estimators
subtract a learnt value function as a control variate.

**Stay near the data.** <NoteLink to="offline-reinforcement-learning" /> explains why off-policy methods fail on a
fixed dataset: bootstrapped targets query actions that the data never contain, and the maximum in Q-learning picks out
exactly those overestimated values. Conservative Q-learning penalises the values of unseen actions, and behaviour
regularisation keeps the policy close to the one that collected the data.

<NoteLink to="counterfactual-evaluation-of-recommenders">counterfactual evaluation of recommenders</NoteLink> and other

policy's, which offline reinforcement learning must control.
