<Definition>
**Representation learning** trains a function $f : \Xcal \to \reals^m$, the encoder, whose output vectors make
downstream tasks easy to solve, often with a linear model or a few labelled examples. The features are learnt rather
than designed by hand. Most methods learn them from unlabelled data, by a pretext task whose targets come from the data
itself, and then reuse them across many tasks <Cite id="bengio2013" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Labelled data are scarce and expensive; raw text, images and audio are plentiful. A representation learnt once from the
plentiful data can be reused for search, <NoteLink to="classification">classification</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="clustering">clustering</NoteLink> and generation, which is how pretrained language and vision models are
used. Embeddings also turn discrete objects such as words, products and users into vectors that a distance can compare,
which <NoteLink to="nearest-neighbour-search">nearest-neighbour search</NoteLink> and recommendation rely on.

## Main ideas

**Similar contexts, similar vectors.** <NoteLink to="word-embeddings" /> give each word a vector so that words used in
similar contexts lie close together. Skip-gram with negative sampling and GloVe both factorise word co-occurrence
statistics.

**Self-supervision supplies the labels.** <NoteLink to="self-supervised-learning" /> trains an encoder on a pretext task
built from unlabelled data. Its three families each need a mechanism that stops the encoder collapsing to a constant
output.

- <NoteLink to="contrastive-learning" /> picks the matching view of an input out of many candidates, with the InfoNCE
  loss. Negatives prevent collapse.
- <NoteLink to="non-contrastive-learning" /> uses only positive pairs and prevents collapse by an asymmetric
  architecture or by penalising correlated features.
- <NoteLink to="masked-language-modelling" /> and <NoteLink to="masked-image-modelling">masked image
  modelling</NoteLink> hide part of the input and predict it from the rest.

**Align two modalities.** <NoteLink to="clip">CLIP</NoteLink> trains an image encoder and a text encoder contrastively
on image–caption pairs. The shared space classifies images zero-shot, by comparing them with an embedded caption for
each class.

**Reuse by probing or fine-tuning.** <NoteLink to="transfer-learning-and-fine-tuning" /> either freezes the encoder and
trains a new output head, or updates all the weights, or trains a small low-rank update such as LoRA.
<NoteLink to="embedding-evaluation" /> measures representations by linear and nearest-neighbour probes and by the
alignment and uniformity of the embedding.
