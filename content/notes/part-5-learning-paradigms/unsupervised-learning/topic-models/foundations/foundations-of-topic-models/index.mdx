<Definition>
A **topic model** describes a collection of documents by a small number of themes, called topics, and describes each
document by how much it uses each theme. The three foundational models share one input, a matrix of word counts per
document. Latent semantic analysis factorises it with <NoteLink to="linear-algebra">linear algebra</NoteLink>;
probabilistic latent semantic analysis and latent Dirichlet allocation explain it with a generative model in which each
word comes from one topic.
</Definition>

## What it is for

These models compress a corpus of $D$ documents over a vocabulary of $V$ words into $K$ topics, with $K$ typically
between 10 and a few hundred. The compressed representation supports retrieval, where a query matches documents that
share no words with it, exploration of a corpus by its themes, and features for downstream classifiers. Every later
topic model in this chapter modifies latent Dirichlet allocation, so these three notes are the base of the chapter.

## Main ideas

**Co-occurrence reveals meaning.** Words that appear in the same documents tend to share a theme. All three models turn
this into a low-rank structure: the $V \times D$ count matrix is approximated by a product of a $V \times K$
word-by-topic matrix and a $K \times D$ topic-by-document matrix.

**The linear-algebra view.** <NoteLink to="latent-semantic-analysis" /> (LSA) takes the truncated
<NoteLink to="singular-value-decomposition">singular value decomposition</NoteLink> of a weighted term–document matrix.
Documents and terms are compared in the $K$-dimensional space it spans. The factors can be negative, so they do not read
as distributions over words.

**The mixture view.** <NoteLink to="probabilistic-latent-semantic-analysis" /> (pLSA) models each word occurrence in
document $d$ as drawn from $P(w \mid d) = \sum_z P(z \mid d)\,P(w \mid z)$, a document-specific mixture of $K$ topics.
Topics are now distributions over words, fitted by
<NoteLink to="expectation-maximisation">expectation–maximisation</NoteLink> <Cite id="hofmann1999" />. pLSA has one
mixture parameter vector per training document, so it overfits and has no rule for new documents.

**The Bayesian view.** <NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation" /> (LDA) puts Dirichlet priors on the document
mixtures and on the topics <Cite id="blei2003" />. The prior makes the model a full generative model of new documents
and regularises sparse counts. Its posterior has no closed form; collapsed Gibbs sampling and variational EM
approximate it.

## How the notes fit together

- Read the three notes in order. Each one adds a single step to the previous model: a probabilistic reading of LSA,
  then priors on pLSA.
- LDA is the prerequisite for the <NoteLink to="topic-model-family">extensions of LDA</NoteLink>, the
  <NoteLink to="neural-topic-models">neural topic models</NoteLink> and the applications of topic models outside text.

## Prerequisites and next steps

LSA needs the <NoteLink to="singular-value-decomposition">singular value decomposition</NoteLink>. pLSA needs
<NoteLink to="expectation-maximisation">expectation–maximisation</NoteLink>. LDA needs the
<NoteLink to="dirichlet-distribution" />, and its inference uses <NoteLink to="gibbs-sampling" /> or
<NoteLink to="variational-inference">variational inference</NoteLink>.

pLSA fits the same model as <NoteLink to="non-negative-matrix-factorisation">non-negative matrix
factorisation</NoteLink> under the <Gloss name="kl">Kullback–Leibler divergence</Gloss> <Cite id="gaussier2005" />, so
LDA can be read as that factorisation with priors. <NoteLink to="word-embeddings">Word embeddings</NoteLink> replace the
document-level co-occurrence of these models with co-occurrence in a short window.
