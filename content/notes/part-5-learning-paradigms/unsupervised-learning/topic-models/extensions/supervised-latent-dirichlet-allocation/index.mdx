<Definition>
**Supervised LDA** (sLDA) is a topic model for documents paired with a response, such as a rating
<Cite id="blei2007slda" />. Documents are generated as in <NoteLink to="latent-dirichlet-allocation">LDA</NoteLink>.
The response $y_d$ is then drawn from a generalised linear model whose covariates are the empirical topic frequencies
$\bar\zvec_d = \frac{1}{N_d}\sum_n\zvec_{dn}$, where $\zvec_{dn}$ is the one-hot vector of word $n$'s topic. For a
real response, $y_d \sim \Gauss(\etavec\transpose\bar\zvec_d, \sigma^2)$ with coefficients $\etavec \in \reals^K$.
</Definition>

## Joint fitting versus two-stage regression

A two-stage approach fits LDA and then regresses the response on the inferred topic proportions. The topics of the first
stage explain word co-occurrence, and nothing forces them to align with the response. A corpus of film reviews splits
naturally into genres, while the rating depends on words of praise and complaint that cut across genres.

In sLDA the response is part of the likelihood. A topic assignment that makes the response easier to predict raises the
probability of the data, so the fitted topics trade off explaining the words against predicting the response. The
fitted model predicts the response of a new document from its words alone.

## Generative process

1. For each topic $k$, fix or draw a word distribution $\phivec_k$.
2. For each document $d$:
   1. Draw topic proportions $\thetavec_d \sim \Dir(\alpha)$.
   2. For each word position $n$: draw $z_{dn} \sim \Cat(\thetavec_d)$ and $w_{dn} \sim \Cat(\phivec_{z_{dn}})$.
   3. Draw the response $y_d \sim \Gauss(\etavec\transpose\bar\zvec_d, \sigma^2)$.

The response depends on the assignments $\zvec_d$, not on $\thetavec_d$. If $y_d$ depended on
$\thetavec_d$, the topic proportions could predict the response while the words were explained by other topics: the
response and the words would be conditionally independent given $\thetavec_d$, and the topics would not be forced to
carry the predictive words. Conditioning on $\bar\zvec_d$ ties the response to the same assignments that explain the
words.

The Gaussian response generalises to any <NoteLink to="exponential-family">exponential family</NoteLink>, $p(y \mid \bar\zvec) \propto h(y)\exp((\etavec\transpose
\bar\zvec)\,y - A(\etavec\transpose\bar\zvec))$: logistic for binary labels, Poisson for counts. This is the
<NoteLink to="generalised-linear-model">generalised linear model</NoteLink> with $\bar\zvec_d$ as its design row.

## Variational E-step

Blei and McAuliffe use the mean-field family of LDA,
$q(\thetavec_d, \zvec_d) = \Dir(\thetavec_d \mid \gammavec_d)\prod_n\Cat(\zvec_{dn} \mid \rvec_n)$
<Cite id="blei2007slda" />. The new term in the <Gloss name="elbo">evidence lower bound</Gloss> is the expected
log-likelihood of the response:

$$
\expect_q[\log p(y \mid \zvec, \etavec, \sigma^2)] = -\tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2) -
\frac{y^2 - 2y\,\etavec\transpose\expect_q[\bar\zvec] + \etavec\transpose\expect_q[\bar\zvec\bar\zvec\transpose]\etavec}{2\sigma^2}.
$$

Under $q$ the assignments are independent, with $\expect_q[\zvec_n] = \rvec_n$ and
$\zvec_n\zvec_n\transpose = \diag(\zvec_n)$ for a one-hot vector. So

$$
\expect_q[\bar\zvec] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_n\rvec_n, \qquad
\expect_q[\bar\zvec\bar\zvec\transpose] = \frac{1}{N^2}\paren{\sum_n\sum_{m \ne n}\rvec_n\rvec_m\transpose + \sum_n\diag(\rvec_n)}.
$$

Collect the terms that contain $\rvec_n$, writing $\rvec_{\neg n} = \sum_{m \ne n}\rvec_m$. They are linear in
$\rvec_n$, so the optimal $\rvec_n$ under the simplex constraint is a softmax of the coefficients:

$$
\rvec_n \propto \exp\paren{\expect_q[\log\thetavec] + \log\phivec_{\cdot, w_n} + \frac{y}{N\sigma^2}\etavec -
\frac{2(\etavec\transpose\rvec_{\neg n})\,\etavec + \etavec \circ \etavec}{2N^2\sigma^2}},
$$

where $\circ$ is the elementwise product and $\phivec_{\cdot, w_n}$ is the vector of probabilities of word $w_n$ under
each topic. The first two terms are LDA's update. The last two favour topics that move the predicted response
$\etavec\transpose\expect_q[\bar\zvec]$ towards $y$. The update for $\gammavec_d$ is LDA's.

## M-step

Stack the documents' frequency vectors as rows of a $D \times K$ matrix $\Amat$. Maximising the bound over $\etavec$ is
least squares with expected design matrices:

$$
\hat\etavec = \paren{\expect_q[\Amat\transpose\Amat]}^{-1}\expect_q[\Amat]\transpose\yvec, \qquad
\hat\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{D}\paren{\yvec\transpose\yvec - \yvec\transpose\expect_q[\Amat]\,\hat\etavec}.
$$

$\expect_q[\Amat\transpose\Amat] = \sum_d\expect_q[\bar\zvec_d\bar\zvec_d\transpose]$, so the posterior uncertainty in
the assignments acts like ridge regularisation. The topics are updated as in LDA.

**Prediction.** For a new document, run the E-step without the response terms, which is LDA inference, and predict
$\hat y = \hat\etavec\transpose\expect_q[\bar\zvec]$.

## Worked example

A document has $N = 10$ words, response $y = 2$, and $K = 2$ topics with $\etavec = (3, -1)$ and $\sigma^2 = 1$. The
other nine words have $\rvec_{\neg n} = (6, 3)$, so $\etavec\transpose\rvec_{\neg n} = 15$. Assume the LDA terms are
equal for both topics.

- Topic 1: $\frac{2 \times 3}{10} - \frac{2 \times 15 \times 3 + 9}{200} = 0.600 - 0.495 = 0.105$.
- Topic 2: $\frac{2 \times (-1)}{10} - \frac{2 \times 15 \times (-1) + 1}{200} = -0.200 + 0.145 = -0.055$.

So $r_{n1} = e^{0.105}/(e^{0.105} + e^{-0.055}) = 0.540$. The difference of the response terms between the two topics
equals the difference of $-(y - \hat y)^2/2$ between the corresponding predictions: with word $n$ in topic 1 the
prediction is $(15 + 3)/10 = 1.8$, and in topic 2 it is $(15 - 1)/10 = 1.4$, so the difference is
$-0.02 - (-0.18) = 0.16$, which equals $0.105 - (-0.055)$.

## Assumptions

- **The response depends on topic frequencies only.** Words affect the response only through their topics.
- **Linear predictor.** The response is a generalised linear function of $\bar\zvec_d$; interactions between topics are
  not modelled.

## Pitfalls

- **<NoteLink to="weak-supervision">Weak supervision</NoteLink> in long documents.** The words contribute $N_d$ terms to
  the likelihood and the response one. With long documents the response barely moves the topics. Scaling the response
  term, or conditioning on fewer words, is a common fix.
- **Two-stage baselines.** Where the words already cluster by the response, LDA followed by regression performs about
  as well.
- **Relatives.** <NoteLink to="labelled-latent-dirichlet-allocation">Labelled LDA</NoteLink> uses labels to restrict
  topics rather than predict a response. The <NoteLink to="structural-topic-model">structural topic model</NoteLink>
  puts metadata upstream, as covariates of the topic proportions, instead of downstream as a response.
