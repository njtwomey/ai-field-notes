<Definition>
**Manifold learning** maps points $\xvec_i \in \reals^D$ to coordinates $\yvec_i \in \reals^m$, with $m \ll D$, on the
assumption that the data lie near a curved surface of dimension $m$, a manifold, inside the high-dimensional space.
The methods are nonlinear and mostly nonparametric: they compute coordinates for the given points rather than a
formula. Each keeps one kind of structure: distances, local linear geometry, or neighbourhoods.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The main use is visualisation: two-dimensional maps of single-cell gene expression, word and image embeddings, and the
hidden states of neural networks. Manifold coordinates also serve as features for
<NoteLink to="clustering">clustering</NoteLink> and as a check on how many degrees of freedom the data have.
<NoteLink to="principal-component-analysis" /> keeps only linear structure and flattens a curved surface onto itself,
where these methods unroll it.

## Main ideas

**Keep distances.** <NoteLink to="multidimensional-scaling" /> places points so that their distances match a given
dissimilarity matrix. <NoteLink to="isomap" /> applies MDS to geodesic distances, estimated as shortest paths in a
nearest-neighbour graph, and so recovers the coordinates of a manifold that can be unrolled without stretching
<Cite id="tenenbaum2000" />.

**Keep local geometry.** <NoteLink to="locally-linear-embedding" /> reconstructs each point from its neighbours and
finds low-dimensional points that the same weights reconstruct.
<NoteLink to="local-tangent-space-alignment-and-hessian-eigenmaps" /> align local tangent spaces.
<NoteLink to="laplacian-eigenmaps" /> keep neighbours close by minimising a weighted sum of squared distances over the
graph, and <NoteLink to="diffusion-maps">diffusion maps</NoteLink> embed by random-walk distances. All four reduce to
the bottom eigenvectors of a sparse matrix.

**Keep neighbours.** <NoteLink to="t-distributed-stochastic-neighbour-embedding">t-SNE</NoteLink> matches neighbour
probabilities in the data with heavy-tailed ones in the embedding, which separates clusters clearly.
<NoteLink to="uniform-manifold-approximation-and-projection">UMAP</NoteLink> does the same with a fuzzy neighbour graph
and no global normalisation, so it scales further. <NoteLink to="pacmap-trimap-and-largevis" /> shows that these
methods differ mainly in the balance of attraction between neighbours and repulsion between other points, a single
spectrum that also contains Laplacian eigenmaps <Cite id="bohm2022" />.

**Topology on a grid.** <NoteLink to="self-organising-maps" /> place prototypes on a fixed lattice and pull lattice
neighbours together during training, so the lattice folds onto the data.

**Mapping new points and judging the result.** <NoteLink to="parametric-and-neural-embeddings" /> train a network to
produce the embedding, which maps unseen points directly. <NoteLink to="evaluating-embeddings" /> scores an embedding
by trustworthiness and continuity, read off the co-ranking matrix of neighbour ranks before and after.

## How the notes fit together

- Start with <NoteLink to="multidimensional-scaling">multidimensional scaling</NoteLink>, then <NoteLink to="isomap" />.
- The spectral methods follow in order: locally linear embedding, LTSA and Hessian eigenmaps, Laplacian eigenmaps,
  diffusion maps. Laplacian eigenmaps underlie both diffusion maps and the neighbour-embedding spectrum.
- Read <NoteLink to="t-distributed-stochastic-neighbour-embedding">t-SNE</NoteLink> before UMAP, and both before
  PaCMAP, TriMap and LargeVis.
- <NoteLink to="self-organising-maps" /> stand apart. The parametric-embedding and evaluation notes apply to every
  method and come last.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume <NoteLink to="principal-component-analysis">principal component analysis</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="eigendecomposition">eigendecomposition</NoteLink>, nearest-neighbour graphs from
<NoteLink to="k-nearest-neighbours" />, and, for t-SNE and UMAP, the <NoteLink to="kullback-leibler-divergence" />.

Manifold learning sits beside the linear methods of <NoteLink to="dimensionality-reduction">dimensionality
reduction</NoteLink> and shares its graph Laplacian with <NoteLink to="spectral-clustering">spectral
clustering</NoteLink>. The <NoteLink to="gaussian-process-latent-variable-model-family">GP-LVM family</NoteLink> learns
the reverse map, from latent points to data, and an <NoteLink to="autoencoder">autoencoder</NoteLink> learns both
directions. Neighbour embeddings are also the standard way to inspect the representations in
<NoteLink to="representation-learning">representation learning</NoteLink>.
