<Definition>
**Unsupervised learning** finds structure in a set of inputs $\xvec_1, \dots, \xvec_n$ that carry no labels. The
structure may be groups of similar points, a few directions or coordinates that explain most of the variation, a
probability density, or a set of latent themes. With no labels to predict, each method defines the structure it looks
for, and its results are judged by fit to held-out data, stability, or usefulness downstream <Cite id="hastie2009" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Most data are unlabelled. Unsupervised methods explore and visualise them, compress them, find segments and themes, and
estimate densities for anomaly detection and sampling. They also prepare data for
<NoteLink to="supervised-learning">supervised learning</NoteLink>: <Gloss name="pca">principal components</Gloss> and
cluster memberships serve as features, density ratios correct for shift between training and test data, and shrunk
covariance estimates make linear discriminants and portfolio weights stable.

## Main ideas

**Groups.** <NoteLink to="clustering" /> partitions points by closeness to centres, by probability components, by a
merge tree, by density or by graph connectivity. Each definition of a cluster finds different groups in the same data,
and choosing the number of clusters needs a penalty or a reference distribution.

**Directions and coordinates.** <NoteLink to="dimensionality-reduction" /> maps data to a few coordinates while keeping
variance, independence, correlation with another view, or non-negative parts: PCA, probabilistic PCA, factor analysis,
ICA, CCA, NMF, kernel PCA and random projections. <NoteLink to="manifold-learning" /> keeps distances along a curved
surface or keeps neighbourhoods, and produces the t-SNE and UMAP maps used to visualise embeddings.

**Latent themes.** <NoteLink to="topic-models" /> describe documents, or any grouped counts, as mixtures of topics.
Latent Dirichlet allocation and its extensions are probabilistic latent variable models fitted by approximate
inference.

**Densities and their ratios.** Density estimation fits $p(\xvec)$ directly, by
<NoteLink to="histogram-density-estimation">histograms</NoteLink> or by <NoteLink to="kernel-density-estimation">kernel
density estimation</NoteLink>, with a bandwidth that trades bias against variance.
<NoteLink to="density-ratio-estimation" /> estimates $p(\xvec)/q(\xvec)$ from samples of each without estimating either
density, by classification, KLIEP or least-squares fitting.

**Second moments in high dimension.** The sample covariance is noisy when the dimension is comparable to the sample
size. <NoteLink to="shrinkage-covariance-estimation" /> pulls its eigenvalues towards their mean, and the
<NoteLink to="graphical-lasso">graphical lasso</NoteLink> estimates a sparse inverse covariance whose zeros encode
conditional independence.

## How the notes fit together

- Start with <NoteLink to="clustering">clustering</NoteLink> or <NoteLink to="dimensionality-reduction">dimensionality
  reduction</NoteLink>; both need only linear algebra and basic probability. Principal component analysis in the second
  is a prerequisite for much of the rest.
- <NoteLink to="manifold-learning" /> follows dimensionality reduction.
- <NoteLink to="topic-models" /> need the Dirichlet distribution and approximate inference, and come later.
- The density, density-ratio and covariance topics are short and independent. Kernel density estimation is the
  natural first step towards density ratios.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The chapter assumes <NoteLink to="eigendecomposition">eigendecomposition</NoteLink>, the
<NoteLink to="singular-value-decomposition">singular value decomposition</NoteLink>, the
<NoteLink to="multivariate-normal-distribution">multivariate normal distribution</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="expectation-maximisation">expectation–maximisation</NoteLink>. <NoteLink to="clustering-metrics" />
evaluate clusterings.

Unsupervised learning feeds other paradigms. <NoteLink to="anomaly-detection" /> scores points by density, distance or
reconstruction error. <NoteLink to="representation-learning" /> learns features from unlabelled data with neural
networks, and the <NoteLink to="gaussian-process-latent-variable-model-family">GP-LVM family</NoteLink> and the
<NoteLink to="variational-autoencoder">variational autoencoder</NoteLink> turn dimensionality reduction into generative
models. Density ratios supply the weights in <NoteLink to="importance-weighting-for-covariate-shift">importance
weighting for covariate shift</NoteLink>.
