<Definition>
**OPTICS** (Ordering Points To Identify the Clustering Structure) computes an augmented linear ordering of points
alongside two distances per point: the **core distance** and the **reachability distance** <Cite id="ankerst1999" />.

Given generating distance $\varepsilon$ and minimum points $\text{minPts}$:

1. The **core distance** of $\pvec$ is the smallest distance such that $\pvec$ qualifies as a core point:
   $$
   \operatorname{core-dist}_\varepsilon(\pvec) = \begin{cases}
   \text{undefined} & \text{if } |N_\varepsilon(\pvec)| < \text{minPts} \\
   d(\pvec, N_\varepsilon^{\text{minPts}}(\pvec)) & \text{otherwise}
   \end{cases}
   $$
   where $N_\varepsilon^{\text{minPts}}(\pvec)$ is the $\text{minPts}$-th nearest neighbour of $\pvec$.

2. The **reachability distance** of point $\pvec$ with respect to core object $\qvec$ is:
   $$
   \operatorname{reachability-dist}_\varepsilon(\pvec, \qvec) = \begin{cases}
   \text{undefined} & \text{if } |N_\varepsilon(\qvec)| < \text{minPts} \\
   \max\paren{\operatorname{core-dist}_\varepsilon(\qvec), d(\qvec, \pvec)} & \text{otherwise}
   \end{cases}
   $$
</Definition>

## Why DBSCAN needs varying density

In <NoteLink to="density-based-spatial-clustering">DBSCAN</NoteLink>, a single global radius $\varepsilon$ determines
whether points are density-connected. When a dataset contains multiple clusters of differing densities—such as a compact
dense core surrounded by a diffuse outer ring—any choice of a single $\varepsilon$ fails:

- Setting $\varepsilon$ small enough to separate dense clusters isolates sparse clusters as pure noise.
- Setting $\varepsilon$ large enough to capture sparse clusters merges the dense clusters into a single blob.

OPTICS avoids committing to a single partition at fit time. Instead, it visits points in order of density connectedness,
recording each point's reachability distance from its densest predecessor.

## The reachability plot

When the points are arranged along the horizontal axis in their OPTICS visit order, and their reachability distances are
plotted as vertical bars, the resulting graph is the **reachability plot**:

- **Valleys** correspond to clusters: points within a dense region have low reachability distances from one another.
- **Deep valleys** correspond to dense clusters; **shallow valleys** correspond to sparser clusters.
- **Valleys within valleys** reveal hierarchical cluster structure: dense cores nested inside broader clusters.
- **Peaks** (bars with high reachability or undefined values) mark transitions between clusters or isolated noise
  points.

## Extracting DBSCAN clusters from the reachability plot

A primary theorem proved by Ankerst et al. <Cite id="ankerst1999" /> is that the OPTICS ordering contains the DBSCAN
clustering for every radius $\varepsilon' \le \varepsilon$:

1. Draw a horizontal threshold line at height $\varepsilon'$.
2. Scan the reachability plot from left to right.
3. Every continuous sequence of points whose reachability falls below $\varepsilon'$ constitutes a DBSCAN cluster
   (starting after a point with $\operatorname{core-dist} \le \varepsilon'$).
4. Points whose reachability remains above $\varepsilon'$ are classified as noise.

Adjusting $\varepsilon'$ in the interactive figure above corresponds directly to shifting this horizontal slice.

## Comparison: DBSCAN, OPTICS, and HDBSCAN

| Property | DBSCAN <Cite id="ester1996" /> | OPTICS <Cite id="ankerst1999" /> | HDBSCAN <Cite id="campello2013" /> |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Output** | Hard flat partition | Ordering + reachability distances | Condensed cluster hierarchy + labels |
| **Varying density** | No (single global $\varepsilon$) | Yes (full multiscale spectrum) | Yes (stability-optimised selection) |
| **Parameters** | $\varepsilon, \text{minPts}$ | $\text{minPts}$ (and upper bound $\varepsilon$) | $\text{minPts}, \text{minClusterSize}$ |
| **Hierarchical view** | None | Visual reachability valleys / $\xi$-steep | Condensed tree & persistence |
| **Computational cost** | $O(n \log n)$ spatial, $O(n^2)$ | $O(n \log n)$ spatial, $O(n^2)$ | $O(n^2)$ or $O(n \log n)$ via dual-tree MST |
