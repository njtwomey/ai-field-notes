<Definition>
**Clustering** partitions a set of unlabelled points into groups, called clusters, so that points in the same cluster
are more similar to each other than to points in other clusters. Each method makes "similar" precise in its own way:
closeness to a centre, membership of a probability component, linkage in a tree, density, or connection in a graph.
There is no single correct clustering of a data set; the method's definition of a cluster decides what it finds
<Cite id="jain2010" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Clustering segments customers, groups documents and images, finds cell types in gene-expression data, and compresses
data by replacing each point with its cluster centre. It also serves as a step inside other methods: codebooks for
<NoteLink to="bag-of-visual-words-topic-models">visual words</NoteLink> and vector quantisation, initialisation of
mixture models, and the cell structure of <Gloss name="ivf">inverted-file indexes</Gloss> for
<NoteLink to="nearest-neighbour-search">nearest-neighbour search</NoteLink>.

## Main ideas

**Centres and squared distance.** <NoteLink to="k-means" /> chooses $k$ centres to minimise the summed squared distance
from each point to its nearest centre. Lloyd's algorithm alternates assigning points and recomputing means, and
reaches a local minimum. It finds compact, roughly spherical clusters of similar size.

**Soft assignments from a probability model.** A <NoteLink to="gaussian-mixture-model" /> treats each cluster as a
Gaussian component and gives every point a probability of belonging to each. Expectation-maximisation fits it, and
$k$-means is its limit when all components share an isotropic variance that shrinks to zero. The likelihood makes model
selection by BIC possible.

**A hierarchy instead of one partition.** <NoteLink to="hierarchical-clustering" /> merges the two closest clusters
repeatedly, from single points up to one cluster. The linkage, single, complete, average or Ward, defines closeness
between clusters. The merges form a dendrogram, and cutting it at a height gives a flat clustering.

**Clusters as dense regions.** <NoteLink to="density-based-spatial-clustering">DBSCAN</NoteLink> grows clusters from
points with many neighbours within a radius $\varepsilon$, finds clusters of any shape, and labels isolated points as
noise. <NoteLink to="optics" /> removes the single global radius by ordering points by reachability.

**Clusters as graph components.** <NoteLink to="spectral-clustering" /> builds a similarity graph and clusters the
bottom eigenvectors of its normalised Laplacian, a relaxation of the normalised cut <Cite id="vonluxburg2007" />. It
separates connected, non-convex clusters that $k$-means splits.

**Choosing $k$.** A clustering objective always improves as $k$ grows.
<NoteLink to="choosing-the-number-of-clusters" /> covers the elbow method, the silhouette, the gap statistic, BIC and
the eigengap, each of which adds a penalty or a reference distribution so that a best $k$ exists.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume distances, <NoteLink to="expectation-maximisation">expectation–maximisation</NoteLink> for mixtures,
and <NoteLink to="eigendecomposition">eigendecomposition</NoteLink> for spectral clustering.
<NoteLink to="clustering-metrics" /> evaluate the results, with external measures such as the
<NoteLink to="rand-index-and-adjusted-rand-index">adjusted Rand index</NoteLink> when labels exist and internal indices
when they do not.

Clustering connects to the rest of <NoteLink to="unsupervised-learning">unsupervised learning</NoteLink>.
<NoteLink to="topic-models" /> are soft clusterings in which each document belongs to several topics.
<NoteLink to="manifold-learning" /> embeddings, such as those from <Gloss name="umap" />, are often clustered, and
spectral clustering shares its eigenvectors with <NoteLink to="laplacian-eigenmaps">Laplacian eigenmaps</NoteLink>. A
<NoteLink to="hidden-markov-model" /> clusters the observations of a sequence with a <NoteLink to="markov-chain">Markov
chain</NoteLink> over cluster labels.
