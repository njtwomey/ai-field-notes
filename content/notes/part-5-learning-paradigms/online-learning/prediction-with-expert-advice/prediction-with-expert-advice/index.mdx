<Definition>
**Prediction with expert advice** is an online game. In each of $T$ rounds, $N$ experts each make a prediction, the
learner combines them into its own, the outcome is revealed, and every expert and the learner suffer a loss. The
learner's goal is small **regret**: its cumulative loss minus that of the best expert in hindsight, on every possible
sequence of outcomes <Cite id="cesabianchi2006" />. Nothing is assumed about how the outcomes or the experts'
predictions are generated.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The experts can be anything that predicts: forecasters, trained models, trading strategies, hyperparameter settings or
routing choices. The setting gives guarantees that hold against drift and even against an adversary, so it suits
ensembling under nonstationarity, portfolio selection, and <NoteLink to="model-selection">model selection</NoteLink> on
a stream. Its algorithms also appear inside other methods: AdaBoost's reweighting of examples and the multiplicative
weights method for solving games and linear programs are exponential-weights updates.

## Main ideas

**Multiply the weight of those who err.** <NoteLink to="weighted-majority" /> predicts a binary label by a weighted
vote and multiplies the weight of every expert that errs by $\beta \in (0, 1)$. Its number of mistakes is at most a
constant times the best expert's mistakes plus $\log_2 N$ <Cite id="littlestone1994" />.

**Randomise and use exponential weights.** A deterministic learner can be forced to err every round. Hedge plays a
distribution over experts with weights $\propto \exp(-\eta L_i)$, where $L_i$ is expert $i$'s cumulative loss.
<NoteLink to="hedge-and-exponential-weights" /> shows that its regret is at most $\sqrt{(T\ln N)/2}$ for losses in
$[0, 1]$. The regret grows with $T$ but its average per round vanishes, and the dependence on $N$ is only logarithmic.

**Some losses allow constant regret.** If the loss is mixable, as log loss and squared loss on a bounded range are,
<NoteLink to="aggregating-algorithm-and-mixability">Vovk's aggregating algorithm</NoteLink> has regret at most
$(\ln N)/\eta$, independent of $T$. For log loss the algorithm is exactly the Bayesian mixture of the experts.

**Compete with a changing best expert.** <NoteLink to="tracking-the-best-expert" /> compares the learner with the best
sequence of experts that switches a few times. Fixed share mixes a little uniform weight back in every round, so that
an expert that was poor earlier can recover quickly.

**The rates cannot be improved.** <NoteLink to="lower-bounds-for-online-learning" /> draws losses at random to show
that no algorithm has regret below $(1 - o(1))\sqrt{(T\ln N)/2}$ on every sequence, so Hedge is optimal including its
constant.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume the protocol and regret of <NoteLink to="online-learning">online learning</NoteLink>, the
<NoteLink to="softmax-and-log-sum-exp">softmax and log-sum-exp</NoteLink> for exponential weights, and
<NoteLink to="bayes-theorem" /> for the aggregating algorithm.

Expert advice is the finite case of <NoteLink to="online-convex-optimisation">online convex optimisation</NoteLink>:
Hedge is <NoteLink to="follow-the-regularised-leader">follow the regularised leader</NoteLink> with an entropy
regulariser over the simplex. When the learner sees only the loss of the expert it followed, the problem becomes the
adversarial <NoteLink to="multi-armed-bandit">multi-armed bandit</NoteLink> and Hedge becomes
<NoteLink to="exp3">EXP3</NoteLink>. <NoteLink to="adaboost" /> applies the same multiplicative update to training
examples rather than experts.
