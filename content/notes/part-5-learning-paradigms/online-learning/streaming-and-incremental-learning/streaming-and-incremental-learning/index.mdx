<Definition>
**Streaming and incremental learning** updates a model as each example arrives, without revisiting old data. The memory
and the time per update must not grow with the length of the stream. Incremental learners keep a summary of the past,
such as a posterior, a set of <NoteLink to="sufficient-statistic">sufficient statistics</NoteLink>, a sample or a
sketch, and fold each new example into it. When the data distribution changes over time, they must also forget.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Streams arise wherever data arrive continuously: clicks, sensor readings, transactions, network traffic and log
events. Storing the whole stream and retraining from scratch is too slow or impossible, and the model must be current.
Incremental methods also train on data sets too large for memory, by making a single pass over them.

## Main ideas

**The posterior is a sufficient summary.** <NoteLink to="sequential-bayesian-updating" /> uses each posterior as the
prior for the next observation. With a conjugate prior the update only adds to a few sufficient statistics. Without one,
<Gloss name="assumed-density-filtering">assumed density filtering</Gloss> projects the updated posterior back to a
tractable family by matching moments.

**Exact and approximate updates for <NoteLink to="linear-models">linear models</NoteLink>.**
<NoteLink to="online-learning-for-linear-models" /> keeps the exact ridge solution with
<NoteLink to="recursive-least-squares-filter">recursive least squares</NoteLink>, at $O(d^2)$ per example, or updates at
$O(d)$ with the <NoteLink to="perceptron">perceptron</NoteLink> and passive-aggressive rules, which correct only the
latest mistake or margin violation.

**Sketches and samples in fixed memory.** <NoteLink to="streaming-algorithms-for-learning" /> covers reservoir
sampling, which keeps a uniform sample of any stream, the count-min sketch, which estimates counts within a fixed
error, and Hoeffding trees, which split a leaf once a concentration bound says enough examples have been seen to choose
the split.

**Drift needs forgetting.** <NoteLink to="concept-drift-adaptation" /> treats the distribution $p_t(\xvec, y)$ as
changing over time. Learners forget old data with a sliding window or a decay factor, detect change and reset, as
ADWIN does, or reweight an ensemble of models of different ages <Cite id="gama2014" />.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The notes assume the vocabulary of <NoteLink to="online-learning">online learning</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="bayes-theorem" /> and <NoteLink to="conjugate-priors">conjugate priors</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="linear-regression">linear regression</NoteLink>, and <NoteLink to="hoeffdings-inequality" />.

Sequential updating meets several other topics. The <NoteLink to="kalman-filter" /> is sequential Bayesian updating
for a linear Gaussian state that drifts. <NoteLink to="online-gradient-descent" /> gives the regret view of
incremental gradient updates. <NoteLink to="continual-learning" /> studies a stream of whole tasks rather than of
examples, where forgetting is a failure rather than a remedy. <NoteLink to="dataset-shift" /> classifies the kinds of
change that drift detectors look for.
