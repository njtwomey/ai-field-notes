<Definition>
In **stream-based active learning** unlabelled examples $\xvec_1, \xvec_2, \dots$ arrive one at a time, usually drawn
independently from a distribution $\Dcal$. For each example the learner decides immediately whether to request its
label; examples not queried are not stored. The aim is a classifier with small error on $\Dcal$ using as few labels
as possible. It differs from **pool-based** active learning, where the learner may rank a stored pool of unlabelled
examples and query the best one <Cite id="settles2009" />.
</Definition>

## Notation

$\Hcal$ is the hypothesis class. The **version space** $V_t \subseteq \Hcal$ is the set of hypotheses consistent with
the labels queried before round $t$. The **region of disagreement** of $V_t$ is the set of $\xvec$ on which two
hypotheses in $V_t$ predict differently. $B \in (0, 1]$ is a labelling budget, the fraction of the stream that may be
labelled.

## Query rules

Three rules cover most stream-based methods.

- **Uncertainty sampling.** Query when the current model is unsure, for example when the largest predicted class
  probability is below a threshold $\theta$, or the margin of a linear model is small <Cite id="lewis1994" />.
- **Query by committee.** Keep several models consistent with the labels so far, or trained on
  <NoteLink to="bootstrap">bootstrap</NoteLink> samples, and query when they disagree <Cite id="seung1992" />.
- **Disagreement-based querying.** Query exactly when $\xvec_t$ lies in the region of disagreement of the version
  space. Outside it, every consistent hypothesis gives the same label, so the label can be inferred without asking
  <Cite id="cohn1994" />.

<StreamQueryDiagram />

The last rule, due to Cohn, Atlas and Ladner and called CAL, never loses information in the
<NoteLink to="probably-approximately-correct-learning">realisable case</NoteLink>: an unqueried label is determined by
the queried ones, so the learner ends with a labelled sample as informative as the passive one.

## Exponential savings on thresholds

Let $\xvec_t = x_t$ be uniform on $[0, 1]$ and let the label be $+1$ if $x_t \ge \theta$ and $-1$ otherwise, for an
unknown $\theta$. The consistent thresholds after $t$ queries form an interval between the largest point labelled $-1$
and the smallest point labelled $+1$. This interval is the region of disagreement.

**How often CAL queries.** Every example that falls outside the interval has an inferred label, so after $t$ rounds the
interval is the same as if all $t$ labels had been seen. It is the gap in $[0, 1]$ between the two sample points on
either side of $\theta$, which is made of two adjacent spacings of $t$ uniform points. Each spacing has expected length
about $1/(t + 1)$, so the interval has expected length about $2/(t + 1)$. Example $t + 1$ is queried if it falls inside,
which has probability equal to the length. The expected number of queries over $T$ rounds is therefore about

$$
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{2}{t + 1} \approx 2\ln T.
$$

**Label complexity.** Any threshold in the final interval has error at most the interval's length, about $2/T$. To reach
error $\epsilon$ passively takes about $2/\epsilon$ labels; CAL takes about $2\ln(2/\epsilon)$. The saving is
exponential.

**Worked example.** Simulating CAL with $\theta = 0.37$ over 300 streams gives 9.0 queries for $T = 100$, 13.7 for
$T = 1000$ and 18.5 for $T = 10\,000$. The approximation $2\ln T$ gives 9.2, 13.8 and 18.4. A passive learner with
the same final accuracy labels all $T$.

For homogeneous linear separators in $\reals^d$ under the uniform distribution on the
sphere, query by committee needs $O(d\log(1/\epsilon))$ labels, again exponentially fewer than passive learning
<Cite id="freund1997b" />. The saving is not universal. For other classes and distributions the region of disagreement
shrinks slowly and active learning gives little gain. With label noise the version space may become empty, and the rule
must be relaxed to a confidence-interval version that keeps hypotheses whose error is not provably worse than the best.

## Committees and information

Query by committee with two members, each drawn at random from the version space, queries with probability equal to
the probability that they disagree on $\xvec_t$. For homogeneous linear separators under the uniform distribution on
the sphere, each query then removes a constant fraction of the version space's volume in
<NoteLink to="expectation">expectation</NoteLink>, which gives the logarithmic label
complexity <Cite id="freund1997b" />.

Practical committees replace sampling from the version space with an ensemble trained on the queried labels, and measure
disagreement by vote entropy or by the average divergence between members' predicted
distributions <Cite id="settles2009" />.

## Budgets and drift

A fixed uncertainty threshold spends labels at a rate that depends on the data. If the stream becomes easier the
learner stops asking; if it becomes harder it exhausts the budget. On a drifting stream a fixed threshold has a worse
failure: after a change in a region where the model is confident, the model is confidently wrong there, never queries,
and never notices.

Žliobaitė and co-authors combine two fixes <Cite id="zliobaite2014" />.

- **A variable threshold.** Keep a running estimate of the fraction of labels spent. When a label is requested, lower
  $\theta$ by a factor $1 - s$; when not, raise it by $1 + s$. The query rate settles near the budget $B$ whatever the
  data.
- **Randomisation.** Multiply the threshold by a random factor drawn around 1 before each decision, so that confident
  examples are occasionally queried. This lets the learner detect drift anywhere in the input space, at the cost of
  some labels on uninformative examples.

Randomised querying is also what gives <NoteLink to="selective-sampling" /> its worst-case guarantee.

## Assumptions

- The label oracle is available at the moment of the decision, and the example is discarded if not queried.
- The savings above assume a realisable, stationary problem. Noise and drift reduce them and require the relaxed or
  randomised rules.

## Pitfalls

- **Sampling bias.** Queried examples are concentrated near the current boundary. A model trained on them without
  correction can be biased, and an error estimate computed from them is not an estimate of error on $\Dcal$. Record
  each query probability and weight each labelled example by its inverse.
- **Cold start.** With few labels the uncertainty estimates are unreliable. Query at a fixed rate until the model is
  reasonable.
- **Comparing against the wrong baseline.** Report accuracy against the number of labels, and compare with passive
  learning at the same number of labels drawn at random from the stream.
