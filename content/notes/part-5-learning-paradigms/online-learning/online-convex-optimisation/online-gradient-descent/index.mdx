<Definition>
**Online gradient descent** (OGD) is an algorithm for <NoteLink to="online-convex-optimisation" />. It starts from any
$\wvec_1 \in \Wcal$. After playing $\wvec_t$ and observing a subgradient $\gvec_t \in \partial f_t(\wvec_t)$, it sets

$$
\wvec_{t+1} = \Pi_\Wcal\paren{\wvec_t - \eta_t\gvec_t}, \qquad \Pi_\Wcal(\vvec) = \argmin_{\wvec \in \Wcal}\norm{\wvec - \vvec}_2,
$$

where $\eta_t > 0$ is the step size and $\Pi_\Wcal$ is the Euclidean projection onto the closed convex set $\Wcal$
<Cite id="zinkevich2003" />.
</Definition>

## Notation

$D$ is the diameter of $\Wcal$, $\max_{\wvec, \wvec' \in \Wcal}\norm{\wvec - \wvec'}_2$. $G$ bounds every subgradient,
$\norm{\gvec_t}_2 \le G$. The regret against a comparator $\uvec \in \Wcal$ is
$R_T(\uvec) = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\paren{f_t(\wvec_t) - f_t(\uvec)}$. All norms are Euclidean.

## Intuition

The update is gradient descent, but the function changes every round. OGD does not converge to a point, because there
is no fixed objective to converge to. The guarantee is instead that the distance to any comparator $\uvec$ cannot keep
falling while the learner keeps losing to it. Each round in which $\wvec_t$ does worse than $\uvec$ on $f_t$, the step
moves $\wvec_t$ towards $\uvec$. The squared distance $\norm{\wvec_t - \uvec}^2$ is at most $D^2$ and at least $0$, so
the total progress, and hence the total excess loss, is bounded.

## The regret bound

**Projection does not increase distances to points in $\Wcal$.** Let $\vvec$ be any point and
$\pvec = \Pi_\Wcal(\vvec)$. The projection minimises $\norm{\wvec - \vvec}^2$ over the convex set, so its first-order
optimality condition is $\inner{\vvec - \pvec}{\uvec - \pvec} \le 0$ for every $\uvec \in \Wcal$. Expanding
$\norm{\vvec - \uvec}^2 = \norm{\vvec - \pvec}^2 + \norm{\pvec - \uvec}^2 + 2\inner{\vvec - \pvec}{\pvec - \uvec}$, the
cross term is non-negative, so $\norm{\pvec - \uvec} \le \norm{\vvec - \uvec}$.

**One round.** Apply this with $\vvec = \wvec_t - \eta_t\gvec_t$:

$$
\norm{\wvec_{t+1} - \uvec}^2 \le \norm{\wvec_t - \eta_t\gvec_t - \uvec}^2
= \norm{\wvec_t - \uvec}^2 - 2\eta_t\inner{\gvec_t}{\wvec_t - \uvec} + \eta_t^2\norm{\gvec_t}^2.
$$

Rearranging, and writing $a_t = \norm{\wvec_t - \uvec}^2$,

$$
\inner{\gvec_t}{\wvec_t - \uvec} \le \frac{a_t - a_{t+1}}{2\eta_t} + \frac{\eta_t}{2}\norm{\gvec_t}^2.
$$

By convexity $f_t(\wvec_t) - f_t(\uvec) \le \inner{\gvec_t}{\wvec_t - \uvec}$, so the left side bounds the regret in
round $t$.

**Constant step.** With $\eta_t = \eta$, summing over $t$ telescopes the first term to
$(a_1 - a_{T+1})/(2\eta) \le D^2/(2\eta)$:

$$
R_T(\uvec) \le \frac{D^2}{2\eta} + \frac{\eta G^2 T}{2}.
$$

The first term penalises small steps, which are slow to reach $\uvec$. The second penalises large steps, which
overshoot. Their sum is smallest at $\eta = D/(G\sqrt{T})$, where

$$
R_T(\uvec) \le DG\sqrt{T}.
$$

**Steps that do not need $T$.** With $\eta_t = D/(G\sqrt{t})$ the sum of the first terms is

$$
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{a_t - a_{t+1}}{2\eta_t} \le \frac{a_1}{2\eta_1} + \sum_{t=2}^{T}\frac{a_t}{2}\paren{\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}}}
\le \frac{D^2}{2}\paren{\frac{1}{\eta_1} + \sum_{t=2}^{T}\paren{\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}}}} = \frac{D^2}{2\eta_T} = \frac{DG\sqrt{T}}{2}.
$$

The first inequality regroups the sum by $a_t$ and drops $-a_{T+1}/(2\eta_T) \le 0$. The second uses $a_t \le D^2$ and
$1/\eta_t \ge 1/\eta_{t-1}$. The second terms sum to $\tfrac{DG}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-1/2} \le DG\sqrt{T}$, because
$\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-1/2} \le 2\sqrt{T}$. Together, $R_T(\uvec) \le \tfrac32 DG\sqrt{T}$ for every $T$ at once
<Cite id="zinkevich2003" />.

The rate matches the lower bound $DG\sqrt{T/(2\pi)}$ for random linear losses, derived in <NoteLink
to="online-convex-optimisation" />, up to a constant.

## Strongly convex losses

A function is **$\lambda$-strongly convex** if
$f(\uvec) \ge f(\wvec) + \inner{\gvec}{\uvec - \wvec} + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\norm{\uvec - \wvec}^2$ for every subgradient
$\gvec \in \partial f(\wvec)$. Regularised losses such as
$\ell(\inner{\wvec}{\xvec}, y) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\norm{\wvec}^2$ have this property. For such losses the per-round
bound gains a negative term:

$$
f_t(\wvec_t) - f_t(\uvec) \le \inner{\gvec_t}{\wvec_t - \uvec} - \frac{\lambda}{2}a_t
\le \frac{a_t - a_{t+1}}{2\eta_t} - \frac{\lambda}{2}a_t + \frac{\eta_t}{2}G^2.
$$

Summing and collecting the coefficient of each $a_t$,

$$
R_T(\uvec) \le \sum_{t=1}^{T}a_t\paren{\frac{1}{2\eta_t} - \frac{1}{2\eta_{t-1}} - \frac{\lambda}{2}} + \frac{G^2}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eta_t,
$$

with the convention $1/\eta_0 = 0$. The step $\eta_t = 1/(\lambda t)$ makes every coefficient zero:
$\lambda t/2 - \lambda(t - 1)/2 - \lambda/2 = 0$. What remains is

$$
R_T(\uvec) \le \frac{G^2}{2\lambda}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{t} \le \frac{G^2}{2\lambda}(1 + \ln T).
$$

The curvature term $-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}a_t$ cancels the distance terms exactly, so no $D^2/\eta$ penalty remains and
the steps can shrink as $1/t$ instead of $1/\sqrt{t}$ <Cite id="hazan2007" />. The step size uses $\lambda$ and not $D$.

## Worked example

Take $\Wcal$ a ball of radius 1, so $D = 2$, with $G = 1$ and $T = 10\,000$ rounds.

- Constant step $\eta = D/(G\sqrt{T}) = 0.02$: $R_T \le DG\sqrt{T} = 200$. The average regret per round is at most
  $0.02$.
- Anytime steps $\eta_t = 2/\sqrt{t}$: $R_T \le \tfrac32 \cdot 200 = 300$ at $T = 10\,000$, and the same formula
  holds at every earlier $T$.
- If each loss is also $1$-strongly convex: $R_T \le \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + \ln 10\,000) = 5.1$. The average regret per round
  is $5 \times 10^{-4}$, forty times smaller than the convex bound.

## Relation to stochastic gradient descent

If $f_t(\wvec) = \ell(\wvec; \zvec_t)$ with $\zvec_t$ drawn independently from a distribution, OGD is
<NoteLink to="stochastic-gradient-descent-theory">projected stochastic gradient descent</NoteLink> on the risk
$L(\wvec) = \expect_{\zvec}\ell(\wvec; \zvec)$. <NoteLink to="online-to-batch-conversion" /> shows that the average
iterate $\bar\wvec_T$ then has excess risk at most $\expect[R_T]/T$, which gives <Gloss name="sgd" />'s $O(DG/\sqrt{T})$
rate as a corollary of the regret bound. The online analysis is stronger: it needs no distribution at all.

OGD is also <NoteLink to="follow-the-regularised-leader" /> with the regulariser $\norm{\wvec}^2/(2\eta)$ on linearised
losses, when $\Wcal = \reals^d$, and it is <NoteLink to="mirror-descent" /> with the Euclidean mirror map.

## Assumptions

- $\Wcal$ is closed and convex, and projection onto it is cheap. For a ball, projection rescales; for a box, it clips
  each coordinate.
- The <NoteLink to="subgradients">subgradients</NoteLink> are bounded by $G$ on $\Wcal$. Squared loss on an unbounded
  domain violates this.
- Tuned constants need $D$ and $G$. A wrong $G$ scales the bound by at most $\max(G/G', G'/G)$, where $G'$ is the value
  used.

## Pitfalls

- **Geometry mismatch.** OGD measures distance in the <NoteLink to="vector-norms">Euclidean norm</NoteLink>. On the
  probability simplex with $N$ experts, $D = \sqrt{2}$ but $G$ can be $\sqrt{N}$, which gives regret $O(\sqrt{NT})$.
  Exponential weights, which is mirror descent with the <NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink>, gets
  $O(\sqrt{T\log N})$ on the same problem.
- **Strong convexity with the wrong step.** $\eta_t = 1/(\lambda t)$ with an overestimated $\lambda$ can make early
  steps too small and the bound invalid. With $\lambda$ underestimated, the bound holds but is loose.
- **Last iterate versus average.** The regret bound is about the sequence of decisions. For a single output model, use
  the average iterate, as in online-to-batch conversion. The last iterate carries no such guarantee without further
  assumptions.
