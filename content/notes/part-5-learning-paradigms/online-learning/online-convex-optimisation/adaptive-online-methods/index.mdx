<Definition>
An **adaptive online method** replaces the fixed step size of <NoteLink to="online-gradient-descent" /> with a
positive definite matrix $\Hmat_t$ built from past gradients, and steps
$\wvec_{t+1} = \Pi_\Wcal^{\Hmat_t}\paren{\wvec_t - \eta\Hmat_t^{-1}\gvec_t}$, projecting in the norm
$\norm{\vvec}_{\Hmat_t} = \sqrt{\vvec^\top\Hmat_t\vvec}$. **Diagonal AdaGrad** uses
$\Hmat_t = \diag(h_{t,1}, \dots, h_{t,d})$ with $h_{t,i} = \sqrt{\sum_{s=1}^{t}g_{s,i}^2}$, so each coordinate gets
its own step size $\eta/h_{t,i}$ <Cite id="duchi2011" />.
</Definition>

## Notation

$\gvec_t \in \reals^d$ is the subgradient at round $t$, $g_{t,i}$ its $i$-th coordinate, and
$\gvec_{1:T,i} = (g_{1,i}, \dots, g_{T,i})$ the history of coordinate $i$.
$D_\infty = \max_{\wvec, \wvec' \in \Wcal}\norm{\wvec - \wvec'}_\infty$ and $D_2$ are the diameters of $\Wcal$ in the
max and Euclidean norms. $R_T(\uvec)$ is the regret against $\uvec \in \Wcal$.

## Why adapt

The bound $D_2G\sqrt{T}$ of online gradient descent uses one step size for every coordinate and every round. Two
common situations make it loose.

- **Sparse features.** In text or click data most features are zero in most examples. A rare feature receives few
  gradient updates, so it needs larger steps than a frequent one. A single step size tuned for the frequent features
  leaves the rare ones barely trained.
- **Unknown scale.** The optimal fixed step $D_2/(G\sqrt{T})$ needs $G$ and $T$. A method that measures the gradients
  as they arrive needs neither.

AdaGrad sets each coordinate's step from that coordinate's own history. The reasoning below shows this is the best
diagonal choice in hindsight, up to a factor of 2.

## The regret bound

**One round in a changing norm.** The argument of <NoteLink to="online-gradient-descent" />, with the Euclidean norm
replaced by $\norm{\cdot}_{\Hmat_t}$ (projection in that norm is non-expansive in the same norm), gives

$$
\inner{\gvec_t}{\wvec_t - \uvec} \le \frac{1}{2\eta}\paren{\norm{\wvec_t - \uvec}_{\Hmat_t}^2 - \norm{\wvec_{t+1} - \uvec}_{\Hmat_t}^2}
+ \frac{\eta}{2}\gvec_t^\top\Hmat_t^{-1}\gvec_t.
$$

**Distance terms.** Summing over $t$ and regrouping by $\wvec_t$,

$$
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\paren{\norm{\wvec_t - \uvec}_{\Hmat_t}^2 - \norm{\wvec_{t+1} - \uvec}_{\Hmat_t}^2}
\le \norm{\wvec_1 - \uvec}_{\Hmat_1}^2 + \sum_{t=2}^{T}(\wvec_t - \uvec)^\top(\Hmat_t - \Hmat_{t-1})(\wvec_t - \uvec).
$$

$\Hmat_t - \Hmat_{t-1}$ is diagonal and non-negative, because each $h_{t,i}$ can only grow. Each squared coordinate
difference $(w_{t,i} - u_i)^2$ is at most $D_\infty^2$. So the sum is at most
$D_\infty^2\sum_i h_{T,i} = D_\infty^2\sum_i\norm{\gvec_{1:T,i}}_2$: the telescoping sum of the diagonal entries is
their final value.

**Gradient terms.** $\gvec_t^\top\Hmat_t^{-1}\gvec_t = \sum_i g_{t,i}^2/h_{t,i}$. For any non-negative $a_1, \dots, a_T$
with partial sums $S_t = \sum_{s \le t}a_s$,

$$
\frac{a_t}{\sqrt{S_t}} \le \frac{2a_t}{\sqrt{S_t} + \sqrt{S_{t-1}}} = 2\paren{\sqrt{S_t} - \sqrt{S_{t-1}}},
$$

so $\sum_t a_t/\sqrt{S_t} \le 2\sqrt{S_T}$. With $a_t = g_{t,i}^2$, the gradient terms sum to at most
$2\sum_i\norm{\gvec_{1:T,i}}_2$.

**Combine.** Linearising the convex losses as in <NoteLink to="online-convex-optimisation" />,

$$
R_T(\uvec) \le \paren{\frac{D_\infty^2}{2\eta} + \eta}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\norm{\gvec_{1:T,i}}_2,
$$

which at $\eta = D_\infty/\sqrt2$ is

$$
R_T(\uvec) \le \sqrt2\,D_\infty\sum_{i=1}^{d}\norm{\gvec_{1:T,i}}_2
$$

<Cite id="duchi2011" />. The step size $\eta$ needs only $D_\infty$, a property of the decision set. The gradient scale
and $T$ enter through the data.

**Never much worse than online gradient descent (OGD).** By Cauchy–Schwarz,
$\sum_i\norm{\gvec_{1:T,i}}_2 \le \sqrt{d\sum_t\norm{\gvec_t}_2^2} \le \sqrt{d}\,G\sqrt{T}$. On a box,
$D_2 = \sqrt{d}D_\infty$, so the AdaGrad bound is at most $\sqrt2 D_2G\sqrt{T}$, the OGD bound up to $\sqrt2$. When a
few coordinates carry most of the gradient mass, the sum of per-coordinate norms is much smaller than $\sqrt{d}$ times
the total.

## Why the square root of the sum of squares

Consider choosing, in hindsight, a fixed diagonal preconditioner $\diag(\svec)$ with $\svec > \zeros$ and a budget
$\sum_i s_i \le c$, to minimise the gradient term $\sum_t\gvec_t^\top\diag(\svec)^{-1}\gvec_t = \sum_i A_i/s_i$, where
$A_i = \sum_t g_{t,i}^2$. The Lagrangian $\sum_i A_i/s_i + \mu(\sum_i s_i - c)$ is stationary at
$-A_i/s_i^2 + \mu = 0$, so $s_i \propto \sqrt{A_i} = \norm{\gvec_{1:T,i}}_2$, and the minimum is
$(\sum_i\sqrt{A_i})^2/c$. AdaGrad's $h_{t,i}$ is this optimal choice computed from the gradients seen so far, and
the lemma above shows that learning it online costs at most a factor of 2 <Cite id="duchi2011" />.

## Worked example

Take $d = 1000$ binary features on the box $\Wcal = [-1, 1]^d$, so $D_\infty = 2$ and $D_2 = 2\sqrt{1000}$. Feature $i$
is active in a round with probability $1/i^2$, and each gradient coordinate is $\pm 1$ when active and $0$ otherwise.
Over $T = 10\,000$ rounds, replace each sum of squares by its expectation.

- Coordinate $i$ has $\norm{\gvec_{1:T,i}}_2 \approx \sqrt{T/i^2} = 100/i$, so
  $\sum_i\norm{\gvec_{1:T,i}}_2 \approx 100\sum_{i=1}^{1000}1/i = 748.5$. AdaGrad's bound is
  $\sqrt2 \cdot 2 \cdot 748.5 = 2117$.
- The whole gradient has $\expect\norm{\gvec_t}^2 = \sum_i 1/i^2 = 1.644$. OGD with the best fixed step in hindsight
  has bound $D_2\sqrt{\sum_t\norm{\gvec_t}^2} \approx 2\sqrt{1000} \cdot \sqrt{16\,440} = 8109$.

AdaGrad's bound is about a quarter of the best fixed-step bound. The gain grows with the dimension: frequent features
set the Euclidean gradient norm, while AdaGrad's bound adds each feature's own gradient norm $\norm{\gvec_{1:T,i}}_2$.

## Variants

- **Full-matrix AdaGrad** uses $\Hmat_t = (\sum_{s \le t}\gvec_s\gvec_s^\top)^{1/2}$ and adapts to correlated
  coordinates, at $O(d^2)$ memory and a matrix square root per step. The <NoteLink
  to="online-newton-step-and-exp-concavity">online Newton step</NoteLink> uses the same matrix without the square
  root, because exp-concave losses supply curvature.
- **FTRL-Proximal** is the lazy, <NoteLink to="follow-the-regularised-leader" /> form of the same adaptive regulariser,
  with an $\ell_1$ term for sparse weights. It was built for large-scale
  <Gloss name="click-through-rate">click-through-rate</Gloss> prediction, and its analysis covers regularisers chosen
  from the data <Cite id="mcmahan2017b" />.
- **RMSProp and Adam** replace the running sum of squares by an exponential moving average, so the step sizes stop
  shrinking. That suits non-stationary deep-learning objectives. The regret analysis above no longer applies, because
  $\Hmat_t - \Hmat_{t-1}$ can be negative. See <NoteLink to="adagrad-and-rmsprop" /> and <NoteLink to="adam" />.
- **Parameter-free methods** remove the need for $D_\infty$ as well, with regret close to $\norm{\uvec}G\sqrt{T}$ up to
  logarithmic factors for any comparator, from coin-betting arguments <Cite id="orabona2019" />.

## Assumptions

- The decision set is bounded in $\norm{\cdot}_\infty$. With an unbounded set, the bound is stated with the largest
  distance the iterates actually reach.
- The step $\eta = D_\infty/\sqrt2$ needs the box size; a wrong value scales the bound by a constant factor.
- A small constant $\delta$ is usually added to $h_{t,i}$ so the first steps are defined; it adds a small term to the
  bound.

## Pitfalls

- **Monotone step decay.** AdaGrad's steps only shrink. After a change in the data, a coordinate with a long history
  moves slowly. On drifting streams this is the same weakness as a learning rate $1/\sqrt{t}$; see
  <NoteLink to="concept-drift-adaptation" />.
- **Dense, isotropic <NoteLink to="gradient">gradients</NoteLink> gain nothing.** When all coordinates are equally
  active, the bound equals OGD's up to $\sqrt2$.
- **Coordinate dependence.** The diagonal version depends on the basis. A rotation of the features can turn sparse
  gradients into dense ones.
