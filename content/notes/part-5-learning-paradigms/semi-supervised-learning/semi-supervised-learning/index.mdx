<Definition>
**Semi-supervised learning** fits a predictor to $\ell$ labelled pairs $(\xvec_i, y_i)$ and $u$ unlabelled inputs
$\xvec_j$, with $u$ usually much larger than $\ell$. The labels say how $y$ depends on $\xvec$ at a few places; the
unlabelled inputs say only where the inputs lie, that is, they inform the input density $p(\xvec)$. They improve the
predictor only through an assumption that links $p(\xvec)$ to $p(y \mid \xvec)$, and each family of methods builds one
such assumption into its model or its loss <Cite id="chapelle2006" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Labels cost expert time, while inputs are often collected automatically: a hospital stores thousands of scans and has a
radiologist read a hundred, a crawler gathers millions of web pages and annotators label a few thousand. Semi-supervised
learning uses the unread scans and the unlabelled pages to place the decision boundary better than the labels alone
can. The classical setting is classification; the same ideas apply to regression, clustering with side information and
the labelling of nodes in a network.

The field sits between <NoteLink to="supervised-learning">supervised learning</NoteLink>, which uses only the labelled
pairs, and <NoteLink to="unsupervised-learning">unsupervised learning</NoteLink>, which uses only the inputs. A
semi-supervised result is a gain over the best supervised model trained on the same labels, and the gain can be
negative.

## Main ideas

**Unlabelled data need an assumption.** If the parameters of $p(\xvec)$ and of $p(y \mid \xvec)$ are independent a
priori, the unlabelled inputs leave the posterior over the <NoteLink to="data-programming">label model</NoteLink>
unchanged. Every method therefore assumes a link, and the four standard ones are the smoothness, cluster, low-density
separation and manifold assumptions (<NoteLink to="semi-supervised-assumptions">semi-supervised assumptions</NoteLink>).
When the link holds, unlabelled data can cut the labels needed by a large factor; with no link, the saving for
thresholds on the line is at most a factor of 2 (<NoteLink to="when-unlabelled-data-helps">when unlabelled data
helps</NoteLink>). A method may label only the given unlabelled points or return a function for any new input
(<NoteLink to="transductive-and-inductive-learning">transductive and inductive learning</NoteLink>).

**Model the inputs and the labels together.** A generative model of $p(\xvec, y)$ ties the two by construction: the
class-conditional densities that set $p(y \mid \xvec)$ also make up the mixture $p(\xvec)$. Unlabelled points enter
through their marginal likelihood, and expectation-maximisation treats their labels as latent
(<NoteLink to="generative-semi-supervised-learning">generative semi-supervised learning</NoteLink>). Deep versions put
the label inside a <Gloss name="vae">variational autoencoder</Gloss>
(<NoteLink to="semi-supervised-variational-autoencoders">semi-supervised variational autoencoders</NoteLink>) or give a
discriminator a $(K+1)$-th "generated" class
(<NoteLink to="semi-supervised-generative-adversarial-networks">semi-supervised GANs</NoteLink>). A model of the wrong
form makes the unlabelled data pull the boundary to the wrong place.

**Keep the boundary out of dense regions.** <NoteLink to="low-density-separation">Low-density separation</NoteLink>
penalises unlabelled points that lie near the decision boundary, so the boundary slides into gaps between clusters.
<NoteLink to="entropy-minimisation">Entropy minimisation</NoteLink> penalises uncertain predictions on unlabelled
points. The <NoteLink to="transductive-support-vector-machine">transductive support vector machine</NoteLink> maximises
the margin over labelled and unlabelled points together, at the cost of a non-convex objective.

**Let models label the data.** <NoteLink to="self-training">Self-training</NoteLink> adds a model's confident
predictions on unlabelled points as labels and refits. <NoteLink to="pseudo-labelling">Pseudo-labelling</NoteLink> does
the same inside one training run of a network, and <NoteLink to="noisy-student">Noisy Student</NoteLink> trains a
larger, noised student on a teacher's labels. <NoteLink to="co-training">Co-training</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="tri-training-and-disagreement-methods">tri-training</NoteLink> let several learners label data for each
other. All of them risk confirmation bias: a confident mistake becomes a training label and is reinforced
(<NoteLink to="self-training-and-co-training">self-training and co-training</NoteLink>).

**Ask predictions to agree under perturbation.** <NoteLink to="consistency-regularisation">Consistency
regularisation</NoteLink> penalises the change in a prediction on an unlabelled input when the input is augmented or
the network is perturbed by dropout. The target can come from a moving-average teacher
(<NoteLink to="mean-teacher">mean teacher</NoteLink>), and the perturbation can be the most damaging direction
(<NoteLink to="virtual-adversarial-training">virtual adversarial training</NoteLink>) or a strong augmentation
(<NoteLink to="unsupervised-data-augmentation">unsupervised data augmentation</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="mixmatch">MixMatch</NoteLink>). <NoteLink to="fixmatch">FixMatch</NoteLink> joins this idea with
pseudo-labelling: a confident hard label from a weak view is the target for a strong view.
<NoteLink to="adaptive-confidence-thresholds">Adaptive confidence thresholds</NoteLink> set FixMatch's threshold per
class or from the model's current confidence.

**Spread labels along a graph.** <NoteLink to="graph-based-semi-supervised-learning">Graph-based
methods</NoteLink> join similar inputs by weighted edges and choose labels that change little across strong edges.
<NoteLink to="label-propagation">Label propagation</NoteLink> fixes the labelled nodes and makes each unlabelled score
the weighted average of its neighbours. <NoteLink to="manifold-regularisation">Manifold regularisation</NoteLink> adds
the same penalty to a kernel method and so predicts new inputs. The choice of graph decides the result more than the
propagation rule does.

## How the notes fit together

- Start with <NoteLink to="semi-supervised-assumptions">semi-supervised assumptions</NoteLink>, which every method
  chapter requires. The sample-complexity and transductive notes of the foundations chapter can wait.
- <NoteLink to="generative-semi-supervised-learning">Generative semi-supervised learning</NoteLink> is the oldest family
  and the clearest case of an assumption that can fail.
- <NoteLink to="low-density-separation">Low-density separation</NoteLink> states the boundary assumption that
  self-training and consistency methods share.
- <NoteLink to="self-training-and-co-training">Self-training and co-training</NoteLink> and
  <NoteLink to="consistency-regularisation">consistency regularisation</NoteLink> are the two deep-learning chapters;
  FixMatch is where they meet.
- <NoteLink to="graph-based-semi-supervised-learning">Graph-based semi-supervised learning</NoteLink> is independent of
  the other method chapters and leads to graph neural networks.
- <NoteLink to="semi-supervised-learning-in-practice">Semi-supervised learning in practice</NoteLink> comes last. Under
  an equally tuned supervised baseline and a validation set drawn from the same small label budget, many reported gains
  shrink <Cite id="oliver2018realistic" />. The chapter also covers the ways unlabelled data hurt, unlabelled data from
  unseen classes, self-supervised pretraining as the alternative, and regression and clustering.

## Prerequisites and next steps

The topic assumes <NoteLink to="classification">classification</NoteLink> with a probabilistic classifier such as
<NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink>. The generative chapter uses
<NoteLink to="expectation-maximisation">expectation-maximisation</NoteLink>, and the graph chapter uses
<NoteLink to="k-nearest-neighbours">nearest neighbours</NoteLink>. Chapelle, Schölkopf and Zien's book and Zhu and
Goldberg's introduction cover the classical methods <Cite id="chapelle2006" /> <Cite id="zhu2009intro" />; van Engelen
and Hoos survey the deep ones by assumption <Cite id="vanengelen2020survey" />.

<NoteLink to="weak-supervision">Weak supervision</NoteLink> is the neighbouring setting with many inexact or noisy
labels instead of few exact ones; <NoteLink to="weak-supervision-and-semi-supervised-learning">weak supervision and
semi-supervised learning</NoteLink> compares the two, and
<NoteLink to="positive-unlabelled-learning">positive-unlabelled learning</NoteLink> belongs to both.
<NoteLink to="representation-learning">Representation learning</NoteLink> uses unlabelled data without any labels,
through <NoteLink to="self-supervised-learning">self-supervised learning</NoteLink>.
<NoteLink to="bayesian-active-learning">Active learning</NoteLink> chooses which unlabelled points to send for
labelling. Pseudo-labels are noisy labels, so <NoteLink to="learning-with-noisy-labels">learning with noisy
labels</NoteLink> applies to them. <NoteLink to="graph-neural-networks">Graph neural networks</NoteLink>, in Part VI,
replace fixed propagation with learnt message passing.
