<Definition>
**Graph-based semi-supervised learning** represents the labelled and unlabelled inputs as the nodes of a weighted graph,
with strong edges between similar inputs, and predicts labels that change little across strong edges. The methods
differ in how the labels move along the graph (clamped averages, spreading with a fit penalty, a penalty added to a
supervised model, a neural network conditioned on the graph) and in whether they predict only the graph's nodes or any
new input <Cite id="zhu2009intro" />.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The methods fit problems with few labels and many unlabelled inputs whose structure is visible in a neighbourhood graph:
images and documents that form clusters in an embedding space, sensor readings on a manifold, nodes of a citation or
social network. They implement the cluster and manifold assumptions of
<NoteLink to="semi-supervised-assumptions">semi-supervised learning</NoteLink> directly. A class boundary is cheap where
the graph has few, weak edges, so the predicted boundary falls in the gaps between dense regions.

The same machinery appears outside classical semi-supervised learning. Label spreading over the embeddings of a deep
network produces pseudo-labels for <NoteLink to="self-training">self-training</NoteLink>. Spreading a model's
predictions over a graph is a standard post-processing step for node classification. The penalty $\fvec^\top\Lmat\fvec$
is a regulariser that any loss can take.

## Main ideas

**One energy.** All the methods measure how much a labelling $\fvec$ varies across the graph by the energy
$\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}W_{ij}(f_i - f_j)^2 = \fvec^\top\Lmat\fvec$, with $\Wmat$ the edge weights and
$\Lmat = \Dmat - \Wmat$ the graph Laplacian. <NoteLink to="label-propagation">Label propagation</NoteLink> minimises it
with the labelled scores fixed, which makes every unlabelled score the weighted average of its neighbours: the harmonic
solution, also the absorption probability of a random walk and the voltage of a resistor network
<Cite id="zhu2003" />.

**Clamped against spread.** <NoteLink to="label-spreading">Label spreading</NoteLink> replaces the fixed labels with a
penalty for leaving them and normalises each edge by the degrees at both ends. Its closed form
$(1 - \alpha)(\I - \alpha\Smat)^{-1}\Ymat$ is a geometric sum over walks, and with $\alpha$ near 1 a mislabelled point
can be overruled by its neighbours <Cite id="zhou2004lgc" />.

**The graph matters more than the rule.** The neighbourhood rule ($k$ nearest neighbours, mutual neighbours, an
$\varepsilon$-ball, b-matching) and the edge weights decide which edges join the classes. A few strong edges across a
gap carry labels across it under any propagation rule <Cite id="zhu2005survey" />. This is the subject of
<NoteLink to="graph-construction-for-semi-supervised-learning">graph construction for semi-supervised
learning</NoteLink>.

**From nodes to functions.** Label propagation and label spreading are transductive: they score the graph's nodes
only. <NoteLink to="manifold-regularisation">Manifold regularisation</NoteLink> adds the energy of a kernel function's
values to a supervised kernel objective. A representer theorem over the labelled and unlabelled points gives Laplacian
regularised least squares and the Laplacian support vector machine, which predict any new input
<Cite id="belkin2006manifold" />.

**Features and edges together.** When the graph is given, as in a citation network, and every node carries features,
<NoteLink to="semi-supervised-node-classification">semi-supervised node classification</NoteLink> trains a
<NoteLink to="graph-convolutional-network">graph convolutional network</NoteLink> on the labelled nodes. It smooths
the features over the graph instead of penalising the labels, and it beats propagation when the features are
informative <Cite id="kipf2017" />. Propagation methods remain strong baselines there, alone or as a correction step
<Cite id="huang2021correctsmooth" />.
