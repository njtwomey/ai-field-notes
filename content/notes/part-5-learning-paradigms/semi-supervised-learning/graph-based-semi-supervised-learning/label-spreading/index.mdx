<Definition>
Given $n$ points with a symmetric non-negative affinity matrix $\Wmat$ ($n \times n$, zero diagonal), degrees
$d_i = \sum_j W_{ij}$ and a one-hot label matrix $\Ymat$ ($n \times C$, zero rows for unlabelled points), **label
spreading** computes class scores $\Fmat$ ($n \times C$) by iterating

$$
\Fmat^{(t+1)} = \alpha\,\Smat\,\Fmat^{(t)} + (1 - \alpha)\,\Ymat, \qquad \Smat = \Dmat^{-1/2}\Wmat\Dmat^{-1/2},
$$

from $\Fmat^{(0)} = \Ymat$, with $\alpha \in (0, 1)$. The iteration converges to
$\Fmat^* = (1 - \alpha)(\I - \alpha\Smat)^{-1}\Ymat$, and each point takes the class of its largest score. No row is
clamped, so a given label can be overruled <Cite id="zhou2004lgc" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

<NoteLink to="label-propagation">Label propagation</NoteLink> fixes every labelled score to its given label and makes
each unlabelled score the weighted average of its neighbours. Two weaknesses follow from that rule.

- **A wrong label is permanent.** A mislabelled point keeps its wrong label, and the harmonic solution spreads that
  label to every point that is closer to it, along the graph, than to a correctly labelled point. Labels gathered by
  annotators or by a heuristic contain such errors.
- **Normalisation at one end.** The average $f_i = \sum_j W_{ij} f_j / d_i$ divides by the degree of the receiving
  point only. The weight of point $j$ in that average does not depend on $d_j$, so a hub inside a dense region passes
  its score to each of its many neighbours as strongly as an isolated point passes its score to its few.

Label spreading, proposed by Zhou, Bousquet, Lal, Weston and Schölkopf as "learning with local and global consistency",
changes both <Cite id="zhou2004lgc" />. It keeps the graph and the idea that scores should vary little across strong
edges. It replaces clamping by a penalty for moving away from the given labels, and it normalises every edge by the
degrees at both of its ends.

## Mechanism

### The objective

Label spreading minimises

$$
Q(\Fmat) = \frac{1}{2}\paren{\sum_{i, j} W_{ij}\norm{\frac{\fvec_i}{\sqrt{d_i}} - \frac{\fvec_j}{\sqrt{d_j}}}^2
+ \mu \sum_i \norm{\fvec_i - \yvec_i}^2},
$$

where $\fvec_i$ and $\yvec_i$ are the rows of $\Fmat$ and $\Ymat$ (length $C$) and $\mu > 0$ weighs the fit to the
labels <Cite id="zhou2004lgc" />. The first term is the **smoothness** term: it is small when strongly linked points
have similar scores after each score is divided by the square root of its point's degree. The second is the **fitting**
term. It applies to unlabelled points too, where $\yvec_i = \zeros$ pulls the scores towards zero, so a point far from
every label ends with small scores.

**The smoothness term is a quadratic form.** Expanding the square and using the symmetry of $\Wmat$,

$$
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i, j} W_{ij}\norm{\frac{\fvec_i}{\sqrt{d_i}} - \frac{\fvec_j}{\sqrt{d_j}}}^2
= \sum_i \norm{\fvec_i}^2 - \sum_{i, j}\frac{W_{ij}}{\sqrt{d_id_j}}\fvec_i^\top\fvec_j
= \trace\paren{\Fmat^\top(\I - \Smat)\Fmat},
$$

because $\sum_j W_{ij} = d_i$ turns the two squared terms into $\norm{\fvec_i}^2$ each. The matrix $\I - \Smat$ is the
**normalised graph Laplacian**, the operator of <NoteLink to="spectral-clustering">spectral clustering</NoteLink>.

**The minimiser.** $Q$ is a convex quadratic in $\Fmat$, so its minimiser is where the gradient vanishes:

$$
\frac{\partial Q}{\partial \Fmat} = (\I - \Smat)\Fmat + \mu(\Fmat - \Ymat) = \zeros
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Fmat = \frac{1}{1 + \mu}\Smat\Fmat + \frac{\mu}{1 + \mu}\Ymat.
$$

Write $\alpha = 1/(1 + \mu)$, so $1 - \alpha = \mu/(1 + \mu)$. The condition reads
$(\I - \alpha\Smat)\Fmat = (1 - \alpha)\Ymat$, and the minimiser is

$$
\Fmat^* = (1 - \alpha)(\I - \alpha\Smat)^{-1}\Ymat.
$$

The inverse exists because the eigenvalues of $\Smat$ lie in $[-1, 1]$ (derivation below), so those of
$\I - \alpha\Smat$ lie in $[1 - \alpha, 1 + \alpha]$ and are positive. A large $\alpha$ (small $\mu$) trusts the graph;
a small $\alpha$ (large $\mu$) trusts the given labels.

<Derivation title="Why the eigenvalues of S lie in [−1, 1], and why the iteration converges">
$\Smat = \Dmat^{-1/2}\Wmat\Dmat^{-1/2} = \Dmat^{1/2}(\Dmat^{-1}\Wmat)\Dmat^{-1/2}$ is similar to the random-walk
matrix $\Pmat = \Dmat^{-1}\Wmat$, whose rows are non-negative and sum to 1. Similar matrices share eigenvalues. For an
eigenvector $\vvec$ of $\Pmat$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$, take the entry $i$ of largest magnitude:
$\abs{\lambda}\abs{v_i} = \abs{\sum_j P_{ij}v_j} \le \sum_j P_{ij}\abs{v_j} \le \abs{v_i}$, so $\abs{\lambda} \le 1$.
The eigenvalue 1 is attained by the constant vector, $\Pmat\ones = \ones$, and for $\Smat$ by the vector
$\sqrt{\dvec}$ with entries $\sqrt{d_i}$.

Unrolling the iteration from $\Fmat^{(0)} = \Ymat$ gives

$$
\Fmat^{(t)} = (\alpha\Smat)^t\Ymat + (1 - \alpha)\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}(\alpha\Smat)^k\Ymat.
$$

Every eigenvalue of $\alpha\Smat$ has magnitude at most $\alpha < 1$, so $(\alpha\Smat)^t \to \zeros$ and the geometric
series converges to $(\I - \alpha\Smat)^{-1}$. The limit is $\Fmat^*$ <Cite id="zhou2004lgc" />. The error after $t$
steps shrinks at least as fast as $\alpha^t$.
</Derivation>

### What the iteration does

Each step gives every point the normalised sum of its neighbours' current scores, weighted by $\alpha$, plus a share
$1 - \alpha$ of its own given label. The diagonal of $\Wmat$ is zero, so a point receives nothing from its own previous
score. A labelled point is therefore pulled towards its neighbours' consensus at every step.

The series form gives a random-walk reading. $\Fmat^* = (1 - \alpha)\sum_k \alpha^k\Smat^k\Ymat$ adds the label mass
that reaches each point along walks of every length $k$, with weight $(1 - \alpha)\alpha^k$. These weights are a
geometric distribution on the walk length with mean $\alpha/(1 - \alpha)$: 1 step at $\alpha = 0.5$, 9 steps at
$\alpha = 0.9$, 99 steps at $\alpha = 0.99$. Replacing $\Smat$ by the random-walk matrix $\Pmat = \Dmat^{-1}\Wmat$ gives
personalised PageRank with restart probability $1 - \alpha$, the propagation used by APPNP to spread a neural
network's predictions over a graph <Cite id="gasteiger2019appnp" />.

### Relation to the harmonic solution

The two methods share the graph and differ in three choices.

| | Harmonic label propagation | Label spreading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labelled scores | clamped to the labels | penalised for leaving them, weight $\mu = (1 - \alpha)/\alpha$ |
| Edge normalisation | $W_{ij}/d_i$ (receiving end) | $W_{ij}/\sqrt{d_id_j}$ (both ends) |
| Unlabelled scores | weighted average of neighbours, between 0 and 1 | shrunk towards zero far from labels |

As $\mu \to \infty$ ($\alpha \to 0$), label spreading returns $\Ymat$ itself and gives unlabelled points no score. As
$\alpha \to 1$ on a connected graph, every eigenvalue of $\alpha\Smat$ except the top one stays bounded below 1, and the
top eigenvector term $\sqrt{\dvec}\,\sqrt{\dvec}^\top\Ymat / \sum_i d_i$ dominates. Every row of $\Fmat^*$ then becomes
proportional to the same vector, and every point takes the same class. The useful range of $\alpha$ lies between these
limits, close to 1 but not so close that this single term takes over.

## Worked example

Four points are all joined to each other with weight 1, so every degree is 3 and $\Smat = \Wmat/3$. Points 1 and 2 are
labelled A, point 3 is labelled B by mistake, and point 4 is unlabelled. The label matrix has columns
$\yvec_A = (1, 1, 0, 0)$ and $\yvec_B = (0, 0, 1, 0)$.

**Harmonic solution.** Points 1 to 3 keep their labels. Point 4 averages its three neighbours: score
$(1 + 1 + 0)/3 = 2/3$ for A and $1/3$ for B. Point 3 stays B.

**First step of label spreading, $\alpha = 0.9$.** Point 3 receives $\alpha$ times the sum of its neighbours' labels
divided by 3: $0.9 \times (1 + 1 + 0)/3 = 0.6$ for A and $0.9 \times 0/3 = 0$ for B. It adds $(1 - \alpha) = 0.1$ of
its own label, giving $(0.6, 0.1)$. After one step point 3 already leans to A.

**The fixed point.** The matrix $\Smat = (\ones\ones^\top - \I)/3$ has eigenvalue 1 on the constant vector and $-1/3$ on
every vector orthogonal to it. Split each label column into its mean part and the rest:
$\yvec_A = \frac{1}{2}\ones + (0.5, 0.5, -0.5, -0.5)$ and $\yvec_B = \frac{1}{4}\ones + (-0.25, -0.25, 0.75, -0.25)$.
The operator $(1 - \alpha)(\I - \alpha\Smat)^{-1}$ leaves the mean part unchanged and multiplies the rest by
$r = (1 - \alpha)/(1 + \alpha/3)$. Point 3 therefore scores

$$
F_{3A} = 0.5 - 0.5\,r, \qquad F_{3B} = 0.25 + 0.75\,r.
$$

Point 3 switches to A when $0.5 - 0.5r > 0.25 + 0.75r$, that is when $r < 0.2$. Solving
$(1 - \alpha) < 0.2(1 + \alpha/3)$ gives $\alpha > 0.75$.

- At $\alpha = 0.5$, $r = 0.5/(7/6) = 0.429$, so $F_3 = (0.286, 0.571)$: the wrong label survives.
- At $\alpha = 0.9$, $r = 0.1/1.3 = 0.077$, so $F_3 = (0.462, 0.308)$: point 3 is relabelled A, with normalised scores
  $(0.6, 0.4)$.

Point 4 scores $(0.5 - 0.5r, 0.25 - 0.25r)$, always in the ratio 2 to 1, which is the harmonic answer after
normalisation. The scores of a labelled point stay below 1: at $\alpha = 0.9$ point 1 scores $(0.538, 0.231)$, and the
row sum below 1 is the shrinkage of the fitting term.

## Cost

- **Direct solve.** $\Fmat^* = (1 - \alpha)(\I - \alpha\Smat)^{-1}\Ymat$ costs $O(n^3)$ for a dense factorisation, the
  same as the harmonic solution.
- **Iteration.** One step costs $O(\abs{E}C)$ on a sparse graph with $\abs{E}$ edges. The error shrinks like
  $\alpha^t$, so reaching a tolerance $\varepsilon$ needs about $\ln\varepsilon / \ln\alpha$ steps: about 690 steps for
  $\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$ at $\alpha = 0.99$.
- **<Gloss name="cg" />.** $\I - \alpha\Smat$ is symmetric positive definite with condition number at most
  $(1 + \alpha)/(1 - \alpha)$, 199 at $\alpha = 0.99$. Conjugate gradient converges in a number of steps that grows with
  the square root of the condition number, a few dozen here, which is why large-scale implementations solve the
  system with it <Cite id="iscen2019labelprop" />.

## Trade-offs

- **Robustness against fidelity.** Clamping, in the harmonic solution, is right when the labels are exact. Label
  spreading is right when some labels are wrong: on the two moons above, with four of twenty labels flipped, harmonic
  propagation reaches 0.88 accuracy on average over six draws and label spreading at $\alpha = 0.99$ reaches 1.00,
  overruling most flipped labels.
- **The choice of $\alpha$.** Zhou et al. fixed $\alpha = 0.99$ in all their experiments <Cite id="zhou2004lgc" />, and
  scikit-learn's `LabelSpreading` defaults to $\alpha = 0.2$. A small $\alpha$ keeps the given labels and spreads them
  only a step or two, which leaves distant points with little mass. An $\alpha$ very close to 1 drifts towards the
  single-class limit above. Tuning $\alpha$ needs labelled validation data, which semi-supervised problems rarely have.
- **Symmetric normalisation.** Dividing by $\sqrt{d_id_j}$ makes the operator symmetric, so the theory of symmetric
  matrices and conjugate gradient apply. The scores of low-degree points are smaller than those of hubs before
  <NoteLink to="row-normalisation">row normalisation</NoteLink>, which can bias the arg max when classes differ in
  density.

**Use today.** Label spreading is the propagation step in several current methods. Iscen et al. build a
nearest-neighbour graph on a deep network's embeddings, spread the few labels over it to produce pseudo-labels for the
unlabelled images, retrain the network on them and repeat <Cite id="iscen2019labelprop" />. Correct and Smooth fits a
simple model to node features, then spreads its residual errors and its predictions over the graph with the
label-spreading iteration; on several node-classification benchmarks it matches or exceeds <Gloss name="gnn">graph
neural networks</Gloss> with far fewer parameters <Cite id="huang2021correctsmooth" />.

## Pitfalls

- **Too few labels to outvote a wrong one.** A label is overruled only when the mass spread from correctly labelled
  points outweighs the point's own share $1 - \alpha$. With one or two labels per class, a wrong label usually has no
  correct label near it and survives.
- **Disconnected components.** As with the harmonic solution, a component with no labelled point gets zero scores
  and no class.
- **The graph still decides.** Label spreading changes how labels move, not where the graph's edges are. An edge that
  joins two classes carries labels across whatever the propagation rule; see
  <NoteLink to="graph-construction-for-semi-supervised-learning">graph construction</NoteLink>.
- **Transductive only.** The scores exist only for the points in the graph. A new point must be added to the graph,
  or a classifier that extends to new points must be used, such as
  <NoteLink to="manifold-regularisation">manifold regularisation</NoteLink>.
- **Class proportions.** The arg max of $\Fmat^*$ ignores the class priors. Class mass normalisation, as described for
  <NoteLink to="label-propagation">label propagation</NoteLink>, applies here too <Cite id="zhu2003" />.
