<Definition>
**MixMatch** is a semi-supervised training procedure that builds a target for every unlabelled example by **label
guessing**: average the model's predictions over $K'$ augmentations of the example, then **sharpen** the average with
a temperature $T$. Labelled examples with their labels and unlabelled examples with their guesses are shuffled together
and combined pairwise by <NoteLink to="mixup-and-cutmix">mixup</NoteLink>. The model is trained with cross-entropy on
the mixed labelled examples and squared error on the mixed unlabelled ones <Cite id="berthelot2019mixmatch" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

By 2019 three ideas each improved semi-supervised <NoteLink to="image-classification">image classification</NoteLink>. <NoteLink
to="consistency-regularisation">Consistency regularisation</NoteLink> made predictions stable under augmentation.
<NoteLink to="entropy-minimisation">Entropy minimisation</NoteLink> made predictions on unlabelled data confident, so
the boundary avoided them <Cite id="grandvalet2005" />. Generic regularisation such as <Gloss name="weight-decay">weight
decay</Gloss> and mixup kept the network from memorising the few labels <Cite id="zhang2018mixup" />. Each was used
separately. MixMatch puts all three into one target and one loss: averaging over augmentations gives consistency,
sharpening gives low entropy, and mixup regularises both terms. Berthelot et al. showed that the combination did better
than any part, with the largest gains when labels were fewest <Cite id="berthelot2019mixmatch" />.

## Mechanism

### Label guessing

For each unlabelled example $\uvec_b$ in a batch, draw $K'$ augmentations $\hat\uvec_{b,1}, \dots, \hat\uvec_{b,K'}$
and average the model's predictions over them:

$$
\bar\qvec_b = \frac{1}{K'} \sum_{k=1}^{K'} p_{\thetavec}(\cdot \mid \hat\uvec_{b,k}) \in [0, 1]^C ,
$$

with $C$ the number of classes. The average is held fixed: no gradient flows through it. An average over several
views is less noisy than one prediction, and it already enforces agreement between the views, since all $K'$ of them
are trained towards the same target.

### Sharpening

The average is pushed towards a one-hot vector with temperature $T \in (0, 1]$:

$$
\operatorname{Sharpen}(\bar\qvec, T)_c = \frac{\bar q_c^{1/T}}{\sum_{j=1}^{C} \bar q_j^{1/T}} .
$$

$T = 1$ leaves the average unchanged; as $T \to 0$ the result tends to the one-hot vector of the arg max. Training on a
sharpened target lowers the entropy of the model's predictions on unlabelled data, which is entropy minimisation done
through the target rather than through an extra loss term. The paper fixes $T = 0.5$ and $K' = 2$ for every
experiment <Cite id="berthelot2019mixmatch" />.

### Mixup across both sets

Collect the batch's $B$ augmented labelled examples with one-hot labels into $\hat\Xcal$, and the $K'B$ augmented
unlabelled copies, each with its sharpened guess, into $\hat\Ucal$. Shuffle their union into $\Wcal$. Mixup of two
examples $(\xvec_1, \pvec_1)$ and $(\xvec_2, \pvec_2)$ draws $\lambda \sim \Beta(\alpha, \alpha)$, sets
$\lambda' = \max(\lambda, 1 - \lambda)$, and returns

$$
\xvec' = \lambda'\xvec_1 + (1 - \lambda')\xvec_2, \qquad \pvec' = \lambda'\pvec_1 + (1 - \lambda')\pvec_2 .
$$

The $\max$ keeps the mixed example closer to its first argument, so a labelled example stays mostly labelled and an
unlabelled one mostly unlabelled; plain mixup omits it. The $i$-th labelled example is mixed with $\Wcal_i$, and the
$i$-th unlabelled copy with $\Wcal_{B+i}$, so that every mixed example has a partner drawn from either set. The paper
uses $\alpha = 0.75$ as a starting point.

### The loss

With the mixed sets $\Xcal'$ (labelled) and $\Ucal'$ (unlabelled),

$$
\Lcal = \frac{1}{|\Xcal'|} \sum_{(\xvec, \pvec) \in \Xcal'} \mathrm{CE}\big(\pvec, p_{\thetavec}(\cdot \mid \xvec)\big)
+ \lambda_U \frac{1}{C\,|\Ucal'|} \sum_{(\uvec, \qvec) \in \Ucal'} \norm{\qvec - p_{\thetavec}(\cdot \mid \uvec)}^2 .
$$

The unlabelled term is the squared error (the multiclass Brier score), which is bounded and less sensitive than
cross-entropy to a confidently wrong guess. Its weight $\lambda_U$ is large (75 for <Gloss name="cifar-10" />, 100 as a
starting point) and is ramped up linearly over the first 16,000 steps <Cite id="berthelot2019mixmatch" />.

### What each part buys

The paper's ablation on CIFAR-10 with 250 labels, where full MixMatch has an error of 11.80%, removes one part at a
time <Cite id="berthelot2019mixmatch" />:

- one augmentation instead of two ($K' = 1$): 17.09%;
- no sharpening ($T = 1$): 27.83%;
- no mixup: 39.11%;
- an EMA teacher of the weights for guessing, as in <NoteLink to="mean-teacher">mean teacher</NoteLink>: 11.86%,
  slightly worse.

On those images every part helps, and mixup most of all.

### ReMixMatch

ReMixMatch adds two parts <Cite id="berthelot2020remixmatch" />. **Distribution alignment** rescales each guess so
that the model's average prediction on unlabelled data matches the class distribution of the labelled data: the
guess $\bar\qvec$ is multiplied elementwise by the ratio of the labelled class frequencies to a running average of the
model's predictions, then renormalised. This stops the guesses from collapsing onto a few classes. **Augmentation
anchoring** takes the guess from a weakly augmented view only and uses it as the target for several strongly
augmented views, with a learned augmentation policy (CTAugment). Anchoring a strong view to a weak view's prediction
is the step that <NoteLink to="fixmatch">FixMatch</NoteLink> then simplified, replacing the sharpened guess by a
thresholded hard label <Cite id="sohn2020" />.

## Worked example

Three classes, one unlabelled example, $K' = 2$, $T = 0.5$.

**Step 1: guessing.** The model predicts $(0.6, 0.3, 0.1)$ on the first augmentation and $(0.4, 0.4, 0.2)$ on the
second. The average is $\bar\qvec = (0.5, 0.35, 0.15)$.

**Step 2: sharpening.** With $1/T = 2$ the entries are squared: $(0.25, 0.1225, 0.0225)$, summing to $0.395$. The
target is $(0.633, 0.310, 0.057)$. Its entropy is $0.816$ nats, against $0.999$ for the average.

**Step 3: mixing.** The shuffle pairs one copy of the example with a labelled example of class 2, target $(0, 1,
0)$. The draw $\lambda = 0.3$ gives $\lambda' = \max(0.3, 0.7) = 0.7$. The mixed input is $0.7$ of the unlabelled copy
plus $0.3$ of the labelled input, and the mixed target is $0.7 \times (0.633, 0.310, 0.057) + 0.3 \times (0, 1, 0) =
(0.443, 0.517, 0.040)$.

**Step 4: the unlabelled loss.** The model predicts $(0.5, 0.4, 0.1)$ on the mixed input. The squared differences
are $0.0032$, $0.0137$ and $0.0036$; their sum divided by $C = 3$ is $0.0069$, which enters the loss multiplied by
$\lambda_U$.

The figure runs MixMatch on two moons with eight labels, where it behaves differently from the image benchmarks. On the
default data seed, with $\lambda_U = 30$ and 600 steps, the accuracy on the 192 unlabelled points is 87.5% with the
paper's $T = 0.5$ and $\alpha = 0.75$, against 84.4% for the labelled points alone. Turning mixup off ($\alpha =
0$) raises it to 91.1%, and turning sharpening off as well ($T = 1$) to 99.5%. Across data seeds 1 to 3, mixup lowered
the final accuracy at $T = 1$ on every seed (86.5%, 87.0% and 86.5% with mixup against 90.6%, 99.5% and 96.4% without).
Two properties of the toy explain it. Mixed points between two moons fall in the gap with intermediate targets, so mixup
asks for a gradual change across the gap, the opposite of the <NoteLink to="low-density-separation">low-density
separation</NoteLink> the unlabelled data support; image mixtures fall far from any real image and do not compete with
the data in this way. And the labelled-only network starts with whole regions of a moon on the wrong side, so sharpening
makes those wrong guesses confident before they are corrected.

## Cost

A step costs $K'$ forward passes without gradients per unlabelled example for guessing, and a forward and backward
pass over $B + K'B$ mixed examples. With $K' = 2$ this is about three times a supervised step on the same number of
labelled examples. Mixup itself is a weighted sum of inputs and costs nothing next to the network.

## Trade-offs

- **Soft targets with sharpening against hard thresholded labels.** Sharpening keeps some uncertainty and uses every
  unlabelled example; FixMatch's threshold discards uncertain examples and uses a hard label. FixMatch reached lower
  error with fewer hyperparameters <Cite id="sohn2020" />.
- **Many hyperparameters.** $K'$, $T$, $\alpha$ and $\lambda_U$ interact. The paper fixes $K'$ and $T$ and tunes only
  $\alpha$ and $\lambda_U$ per dataset, which still needs labelled validation data.
- **Mixup's assumption.** Mixup asks the prediction to change linearly along the segment between two examples. For
  images the segment runs through inputs far from any real image, and the requirement acts as a regulariser. Where the
  segment crosses a gap between classes inside the data, as on two moons, it conflicts with low-density separation.

## Pitfalls

- **Weights that do not transfer.** The paper's $\lambda_U$ ranged from 50 to 250 across its datasets (75 for
  CIFAR-10, 150 for CIFAR-100) <Cite id="berthelot2019mixmatch" />, and a value tuned on one dataset is a guess on the
  next.
- **Order of the mixed sets.** The $\lambda' = \max(\lambda, 1 - \lambda)$ step matters: without it a "labelled"
  mixed example can be mostly an unlabelled input carrying a guessed label, and the two loss terms lose their roles.
- **Class collapse with few labels.** Sharpened guesses can drift towards the classes the model learnt first.
  Distribution alignment in ReMixMatch is the remedy <Cite id="berthelot2020remixmatch" />.
