<Definition>
**Weight decay** multiplies the parameters by $1 - \eta\lambda$ at every step, with decay rate $\lambda \ge 0$ and step
size $\eta$, in addition to the optimiser's gradient step. **Decoupled** weight decay applies this shrinkage outside
the optimiser's gradient processing. For Adam, the decoupled method is **AdamW**:

$$
\thetavec_t = \thetavec_{t-1} - \eta_t\paren{\alpha\,\frac{\hat\mvec_t}{\sqrt{\hat\vvec_t} + \epsilon} + \lambda\,\thetavec_{t-1}},
$$

where $\hat\mvec_t$ and $\hat\vvec_t$ are Adam's bias-corrected moment estimates, computed from the loss gradient only,
and $\eta_t$ is a schedule multiplier <Cite id="loshchilov2019" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

An L2 penalty $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\norm{\thetavec}^2$ added to the loss pulls every weight towards zero, as in
<NoteLink to="ridge-regression">ridge regression</NoteLink>. Deep-learning libraries long implemented "weight decay" in
every optimiser by adding $\lambda\thetavec$ to the gradient, which is exactly the gradient of that penalty. For
gradient descent the two are the same. For adaptive optimisers they are not, and the L2 version regularises poorly
<Cite id="loshchilov2019" />.

## Mechanism

### Gradient descent: the two coincide

With the penalty, the gradient is $\gvec_t + \lambda\thetavec_{t-1}$, where $\gvec_t$ is the gradient of the unpenalised
loss. One step of gradient descent is

$$
\thetavec_t = \thetavec_{t-1} - \eta\paren{\gvec_t + \lambda\thetavec_{t-1}} = (1 - \eta\lambda)\,\thetavec_{t-1} - \eta\,\gvec_t.
$$

This is a gradient step on the unpenalised loss plus a shrinkage by the factor $1 - \eta\lambda$. An L2 penalty with
coefficient $\lambda$ and a weight decay of $\eta\lambda$ per step are the same algorithm. The same holds with momentum,
up to the momentum's averaging of the penalty term.

### Adam: the penalty is rescaled per coordinate

Adam divides its averaged gradient by $\sqrt{\hat\vvec_t}$ coordinatewise; see <NoteLink to="adam">Adam</NoteLink>.
With the penalty folded into the gradient, the averaged gradient includes $\lambda\thetavec$, and since the averaging
is linear the penalty's share of the step in coordinate $k$ is approximately

$$
\alpha\,\frac{\lambda\,\theta_k}{\sqrt{\hat v_k} + \epsilon}.
$$

The effective decay rate of coordinate $k$ is $\alpha\lambda/\sqrt{\hat v_k}$. It depends on the history of that
coordinate's gradients. Weights whose loss gradients are large, or have been large, have a large $\hat v_k$ and are
barely decayed. Weights whose gradients are small are decayed strongly. No choice of $\lambda$ gives a uniform
shrinkage, so the L2 penalty in Adam is not equivalent to weight decay of any rate <Cite id="loshchilov2019" />. The
same argument applies to AdaGrad and RMSProp.

### AdamW

AdamW computes $\hat\mvec_t$ and $\hat\vvec_t$ from the loss gradient alone and subtracts $\eta_t\lambda\thetavec_{t-1}$
separately. Every coordinate shrinks by the same factor $1 - \eta_t\lambda$ per step. The decay is multiplied by the
schedule $\eta_t$, so it decays with the learning rate; see
<NoteLink to="learning-rate-schedules">learning-rate schedules</NoteLink>. Loshchilov and Hutter found that with
decoupled decay, the best $\lambda$ depends much less on the step size, so the two can be tuned separately, and that
decoupled decay let Adam compete with SGD with momentum in test error on image classification
<Cite id="loshchilov2019" />.

## Worked example

Take two weights, both equal to 1, in a model trained with Adam at $\alpha = 10^{-3}$ and $\lambda = 10^{-2}$. For
AdamW, use the library convention in which the decay per step is $\alpha\lambda = 10^{-5}$.
Suppose the running root mean square of the loss gradient is $\sqrt{\hat v_1} = 10$ for the first weight and
$\sqrt{\hat v_2} = 0.01$ for the second, and that the penalty is too small to change these values.

| Method              | Decay of weight 1 per step            | Decay of weight 2 per step               |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| L2 penalty in Adam  | $10^{-3}\cdot10^{-2}/10 = 10^{-6}$    | $10^{-3}\cdot10^{-2}/0.01 = 10^{-3}$     |
| AdamW, decay $\alpha\lambda$ | $10^{-3}\cdot10^{-2} = 10^{-5}$ | $10^{-5}$                          |

Under the L2 penalty the second weight decays 1000 times faster than the first, and 100 times faster than intended. After
1000 steps the first weight has lost $0.1\%$ of its value to the penalty and the second about $63\%$, since
$(1 - 10^{-3})^{1000} = 0.368$. Under AdamW both have lost $1 - (1 - 10^{-5})^{1000} = 1.0\%$.

## Weight decay with normalisation layers

A weight matrix followed by <NoteLink to="batch-normalisation">batch normalisation</NoteLink> or layer normalisation
can be scaled by any $c > 0$ without changing the network's output. For such weights, weight decay does not constrain
the function the network computes. It does change training: the gradient of a scale-invariant loss is orthogonal to the
weights and scales as $1/\norm{\thetavec}$, so the change in direction per step is proportional to
$\eta/\norm{\thetavec}^2$. Shrinking $\norm{\thetavec}$ raises this effective learning rate. Weight decay on normalised
layers acts through the learning rate, not as a regulariser of the function <Cite id="vanlaarhoven2017" />.

## Cost

One multiply-add per parameter per step, the same as the L2 gradient term.

## Trade-offs

- **Which parameters to decay.** Biases and the gains of normalisation layers have few degrees of freedom and do not
  benefit from shrinkage towards zero. Many training recipes exclude them from decay.
- **Two conventions for the rate.** In the formula above the decay per step is $\eta_t\lambda$, with $\eta_t$ a
  multiplier that is 1 at the schedule's peak. Libraries such as PyTorch multiply $\lambda$ by the learning rate itself,
  so $\lambda$ values do not transfer between conventions without rescaling.
- **Interpretation.** For gradient descent, weight decay is the L2 penalty of ridge regression and has a Bayesian reading
  as a Gaussian prior on the weights. For AdamW it has no such reading: it is a shrinkage step, not the gradient of a
  term in the loss that Adam minimises.
