<Definition>
Causal masking adds a matrix $\Mmat$ to the attention scores before the softmax, with $M_{ij} = 0$ for $j \le i$ and
$M_{ij} = -\infty$ for $j > i$:
$$
\operatorname{softmax}\paren{\frac{\Qmat \Kmat^{\tr}}{\sqrt{d_k}} + \Mmat} \Vmat.
$$
Query position $i$ then puts zero weight on every later key position <Cite id="vaswani2017" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

An autoregressive model factorises the probability of a sequence as

$$
p(x_1, \dots, x_T) = \prod_{t=1}^{T} p(x_t \mid x_1, \dots, x_{t-1}).
$$

The prediction for position $t$ must not see $x_t$ or anything after it. Otherwise training is trivial, since the
answer is in the input, and the trained model is useless at generation time, when later tokens do not exist yet.

Self-attention lets every position read every other position. A decoder built from it needs a way to block the future
without giving up the parallel computation that makes attention fast. A recurrent network is causal by construction
but must process positions one after another.

## Mechanism

Add $\Mmat$ to the score matrix $\Smat = \Qmat \Kmat^{\tr} / \sqrt{d_k}$. For $j > i$ the softmax numerator becomes
$\exp(S_{ij} - \infty) = 0$, so

$$
\alpha_{ij} = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{\exp(S_{ij})}{\sum_{l \le i} \exp(S_{il})} & j \le i, \\[8pt]
0 & j > i.
\end{cases}
$$

Each row is renormalised over the allowed keys, so it still sums to 1. The output at position $i$ is
$\ovec_i = \sum_{j \le i} \alpha_{ij} \vvec_j$.

**Causality holds through every layer.** Queries, keys and values at position $j$ are linear functions of the input at
position $j$ alone. So $\ovec_i$ depends only on inputs at positions $j \le i$. The feed-forward sublayer, residual
connections and layer normalisation act on each position separately, so they preserve this. By induction over layers,
the final representation at position $i$ depends only on $x_1, \dots, x_i$.

## Training in parallel

Feed the sequence $x_1, \dots, x_T$ and ask position $t$ to predict $x_{t+1}$. The loss is

$$
\Lcal = -\sum_{t=1}^{T-1} \log p_\theta(x_{t+1} \mid x_1, \dots, x_t).
$$

With the mask, the prediction at position $t$ uses only $x_{\le t}$, which is the conditional the factorisation needs.
All $T - 1$ predictions come out of one forward pass, as matrix products over the whole sequence. A sequence of 1000
tokens gives 999 training targets for the cost of one pass. This is teacher forcing, done for all positions at once.

## Generation

At inference, tokens are produced one at a time: predict $x_{t+1}$, append it, repeat. Position $t$'s keys and values
depend only on $x_{\le t}$, so they do not change when later tokens are appended. They can be stored and reused, which
is <NoteLink to="kv-cache">KV caching</NoteLink>. The new token then attends to the cache with one query row, and the
mask is not needed because no future keys exist.

## Worked example

Take three positions with scores

$$
\Smat = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.
$$

Without the mask, row 1 is $\operatorname{softmax}(2, 1, 0) = (0.665, 0.245, 0.090)$: position 1 reads positions 2 and 3.
With the mask:

- Row 1 keeps only $S_{11}$: weights $(1, 0, 0)$.
- Row 2 keeps $S_{21} = 1$ and $S_{22} = 3$: $\operatorname{softmax}(1, 3) = (0.119, 0.881)$, so weights
  $(0.119, 0.881, 0)$.
- Row 3 keeps all three: $\operatorname{softmax}(0, 1, 1) = (0.155, 0.422, 0.422)$.

The first position always attends only to itself.

## Cost

The mask removes no computation in a plain implementation: the full $T \times T$ score matrix is formed and half of it
is discarded. Block-wise implementations skip blocks that lie entirely above the diagonal, which roughly halves the
work.

## Trade-offs

- **Decoder-only models** such as GPT use causal self-attention in every layer. They generate text but each position
  sees only the left context.
- **Encoder-only models** such as BERT use no mask. Every position sees the whole input, which suits classification
  and tagging, but they cannot generate left to right <Cite id="devlin2019" />.
- **Encoder–decoder models** use unmasked self-attention in the encoder, causal self-attention in the decoder, and
  unmasked cross-attention from decoder to encoder; see <NoteLink to="transformer">transformer</NoteLink>.
- **Padding masks** use the same mechanism to give zero weight to padding tokens in a batch of sequences of different
  lengths.

## Pitfalls

- **Fully masked rows.** A row whose entries are all $-\infty$ gives $0/0$. This cannot happen with a causal mask
  alone, since the diagonal is always kept, but it can when combined with a padding mask. Implementations often use a
  large finite negative number instead of $-\infty$ and handle such rows explicitly.
- **Off-by-one in the targets.** The output at position $t$ predicts $x_{t+1}$. Shifting the targets the wrong way
  trains the model to copy its input.
- **Mask order.** The mask must be applied before the softmax. Zeroing weights after the softmax leaves rows that do
  not sum to 1.
