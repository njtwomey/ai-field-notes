<Definition>
A transformer maps a sequence of $n$ token vectors in $\reals^d$ to a sequence of $n$ vectors in $\reals^d$ through a
stack of identical blocks. Each block has two sublayers, multi-head self-attention and a position-wise feed-forward
network. Each sublayer has a residual connection and layer normalisation. The original encoder–decoder transformer
stacks $N$ encoder blocks over the input and $N$ decoder blocks over the output generated so far; decoder blocks add a
causally masked self-attention and a cross-attention to the encoder's output <Cite id="vaswani2017" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

A recurrent network passes information between positions one step at a time. Relating the first and last words of a
sentence takes $n$ steps, and the steps cannot run in parallel. A transformer relates every pair of positions directly,
in one layer, with <NoteLink to="scaled-dot-product-attention">attention</NoteLink>. All positions are processed at
once with matrix products.

The architecture alternates two operations. **Attention mixes information across positions**: each token gathers what
it needs from the others. **The feed-forward network processes each position on its own**: it transforms what the token
gathered. Residual connections let each sublayer add a correction to a running representation instead of replacing it.

## The block

Let $\Xmat \in \reals^{n \times d}$ hold one row per position. An encoder block computes

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Zmat &= \operatorname{LN}\paren{\Xmat + \operatorname{MultiHead}(\Xmat, \Xmat, \Xmat)}, \\
\Ymat &= \operatorname{LN}\paren{\Zmat + \operatorname{FFN}(\Zmat)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The pieces:

- **<NoteLink to="multi-head-attention">Multi-head self-attention</NoteLink>.** Queries, keys and values are all
  projections of $\Xmat$.
- **Position-wise feed-forward network.** The same two-layer network is applied to each row:
  $\operatorname{FFN}(\xvec) = \max(0, \xvec \Wmat_1 + \bvec_1) \Wmat_2 + \bvec_2$, with $\Wmat_1 \in \reals^{d \times d_{ff}}$
  and $\Wmat_2 \in \reals^{d_{ff} \times d}$. The original uses $d_{ff} = 4d$.
- **Residual connections.** Each sublayer's output is added to its input, as in a
  <NoteLink to="residual-network">residual network</NoteLink>, so gradients have an identity path through every block.
- **Layer normalisation.** For each position separately, $\operatorname{LN}(\xvec) = \gammavec \odot (\xvec - \mu) /
  \sigma + \betavec$, where $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the mean and standard deviation of the $d$ entries of $\xvec$ and
  $\gammavec, \betavec \in \reals^d$ are learned <Cite id="ba2016" />. It keeps the scale of the residual stream stable.

A decoder block has three sublayers: causally masked self-attention over the decoder's inputs, cross-attention whose
queries come from the decoder and whose keys and values are the encoder's final output, then the feed-forward network.
The <NoteLink to="causal-masking">causal mask</NoteLink> keeps position $t$ from seeing later outputs.

## The full model

1. **Embedding.** Each input token is mapped to a learned vector in $\reals^d$ and multiplied by $\sqrt{d}$.
2. **Position.** Attention is unchanged by permuting its inputs, so the model adds a
   <NoteLink to="positional-encoding">positional encoding</NoteLink> to give each position a distinct signature.
3. **Encoder.** $N$ encoder blocks transform the input sequence.
4. **Decoder.** $N$ decoder blocks transform the shifted output sequence, reading the encoder output through
   cross-attention.
5. **Output.** A linear map to vocabulary size and a softmax give $p(y_t \mid y_{<t}, \xvec)$. The output matrix shares
   its weights with the embedding.

The base model has $N = 6$, $d = 512$, $h = 8$ heads and $d_{ff} = 2048$ <Cite id="vaswani2017" />.

## Why attention rather than recurrence

Compare one layer of each on a sequence of length $n$ and width $d$ <Cite id="vaswani2017" />:

- **Sequential operations.** A recurrent layer needs $n$ steps in sequence. A self-attention layer needs a constant
  number, since every row of $\Qmat \Kmat^{\tr}$ is computed at once.
- **Path length.** A signal from position 1 reaches position $n$ through $n - 1$ recurrent steps, and gradients along
  that path decay as described in <NoteLink to="backpropagation-through-time">backpropagation through time</NoteLink>.
  In self-attention the path has length 1.
- **Cost per layer.** A recurrent layer costs $O(n d^2)$. Self-attention costs $O(n^2 d)$.

**When attention dominates the cost.** Count multiply-adds in one encoder block. The four projections of multi-head
attention cost $4nd^2$. The scores $\Qmat \Kmat^{\tr}$ and the weighted sum of values cost $n^2 d$ each, $2n^2 d$ in
total. The feed-forward network costs $2 n d\, d_{ff} = 8nd^2$. The quadratic term exceeds the linear ones when

$$
2n^2 d > 12 n d^2 \iff n > 6d.
$$

For $d = 512$ that is $n > 3072$ tokens. Shorter sequences spend most of their compute in the projections and the
feed-forward network, not in attention.

## Worked example

Count the parameters of the base model, with biases and the two layer-norm vectors $\gammavec, \betavec$ per sublayer.

- **Multi-head attention:** $4d^2 + 4d = 4 \cdot 512^2 + 4 \cdot 512 = 1{,}050{,}624$.
- **Feed-forward network:** $2 d\, d_{ff} + d_{ff} + d = 2 \cdot 512 \cdot 2048 + 2048 + 512 = 2{,}099{,}712$.
- **Encoder block:** one of each plus two layer norms of $2d$: $1{,}050{,}624 + 2{,}099{,}712 + 2048 = 3{,}152{,}384$.
- **Decoder block:** an encoder block plus a cross-attention and its layer norm:
  $3{,}152{,}384 + 1{,}050{,}624 + 1024 = 4{,}204{,}032$.
- **Six of each:** $6 \cdot 3{,}152{,}384 + 6 \cdot 4{,}204{,}032 = 44{,}138{,}496$.
- **Shared embedding** over a vocabulary of about 37,000 tokens: $37{,}000 \cdot 512 = 18{,}944{,}000$.

The total is about 63 million; the paper reports 65 million. Two thirds of each encoder block's weights are in the
feed-forward network.

## Training and results

The model was trained with Adam and a learning rate that rises linearly for 4000 warm-up steps and then decays as the
inverse square root of the step number. Dropout of 0.1 and label smoothing of 0.1 regularised it. The large model
reached 28.4 BLEU on WMT'14 English to German and 41.8 on English to French, better than earlier recurrent and
convolutional models at a fraction of their training cost <Cite id="vaswani2017" />.

## Variants

- **Pre-LN.** Most later models normalise before each sublayer: $\Xmat + \operatorname{Sublayer}(\operatorname{LN}(\Xmat))$.
  The residual path is then an exact identity from input to output. Pre-LN transformers train stably without the
  learning-rate warm-up that the original post-LN arrangement needs <Cite id="xiong2020" />.
- **Encoder-only** models, such as BERT, keep the encoder stack and drop the decoder. Every position sees the whole
  input <Cite id="devlin2019" />.
- **Decoder-only** models, such as GPT, keep only causally masked self-attention blocks and train on next-token
  prediction. They are the standard design for language models.
- **Other domains.** Splitting an image into patches and treating them as tokens gives the vision transformer
  <Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />. Restricting attention to the edges of a graph gives a
  <NoteLink to="graph-attention-network">graph attention network</NoteLink>.

## Assumptions

- Relations between positions can be expressed as weighted averages chosen by content. Order enters only through the
  positional encoding.
- The whole sequence fits in memory: attention stores $n^2$ weights per head per layer during training.

## Pitfalls

- **Quadratic cost in length.** Doubling the context quadruples attention time and memory. Long contexts need
  memory-efficient attention kernels or sparse and linear variants.
- **No inherent order.** Without a positional encoding the model is equivariant to permutations and cannot tell "dog
  bites man" from "man bites dog".
- **Post-LN instability.** The original arrangement can diverge early in training without a warm-up of the learning
  rate <Cite id="xiong2020" />.
