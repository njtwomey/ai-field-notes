<Definition>
**ItemSage** is Pinterest's embedding for shopping products. A product's up to 20 images, each represented by its
PinSage embedding, and 12 text features are turned into a sequence of 32 vectors, prefixed by a global `[CLS]` token and
passed through a one-layer <NoteLink to="transformer">transformer</NoteLink> encoder. The `[CLS]` output goes through a two-layer <Gloss name="mlp" /> to a unit-length
256-dimensional embedding. Training contrasts query–product pairs from several engagement types in the same batch, so
that one embedding serves image queries, text queries and user-activity queries <Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

Products differ from ordinary pins. A product has several images, from different angles or in different settings, and
structured text from the merchant: title, description, brand, colours, materials, sizes and more. Shopping surfaces
must also optimise for add-to-cart actions and checkouts, which are far rarer than clicks and saves
<Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.

Three surfaces recommend products, each with a query in a different modality. Home uses the user's past activity,
Closeup uses the pin being viewed, and Search uses a text query. Separate embeddings per surface would mean separate
models, indexes and upgrade cycles. ItemSage is one embedding for all three.

## Compatibility

ItemSage embeddings must be **compatible** with two existing embeddings: PinSage for images and SearchSage, a search
query model fine-tuned from DistilBERT. Compatible means that the <NoteLink to="dot-product">cosine similarity</NoteLink> between a query embedding and a
product embedding is directly a relevance score. That lets product candidates be retrieved by <Gloss name="ann-search">approximate
nearest-neighbour search</Gloss> (<NoteLink to="hnsw">HNSW</NoteLink>) without a transformation at query time. PinSage and
SearchSage are frozen during ItemSage training <Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.

## Architecture

**Image inputs.** Up to 20 images, each represented by its pretrained
<NoteLink to="pinsage">PinSage</NoteLink> embedding. Missing slots are zero vectors. 99.7% of products
have at most 20 images <Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.

**Text inputs.** Each of 12 text fields becomes a bag of word unigrams, word bigrams and character trigrams, drawn from a
vocabulary of the most frequent 200,000 unigrams, 1,000,000 bigrams and 64,000 trigrams. A **hash embedding** keeps the
table small: an embedding table $\Emat$ of size $100{,}000 \times 256$, an importance table $\Wmat$ of size
$\abs{\Vcal} \times 2$, and two hash functions $h_1, h_2$. Token $i$ has embedding
$W_{i1}\Emat_{h_1(i)} + W_{i2}\Emat_{h_2(i)}$. A field's embedding is the sum of its tokens' embeddings
<Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.

**Encoder.** Each group of features gets its own <NoteLink to="matrix-vector-product">linear map</NoteLink> to 512 dimensions, which removes the need for equal input
sizes. The 32 projected vectors and the `[CLS]` token go through one transformer layer with 8 attention heads and a
feed-forward module with one hidden layer. The `[CLS]` output passes through a two-layer MLP to 256 dimensions and is
normalised to unit length. Encoders with 2 or 3 layers were worse offline, and 4 layers gave mixed results
<Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.

## Loss

Given a batch of query–product pairs $\set{(x_i, y_i)}_{i=1}^{\abs{\Bcal}}$ with frozen query embeddings $\qvec_{x_i}$,
the ideal objective is a softmax over the whole catalogue $\Ccal$,
$-\frac{1}{\abs{\Bcal}}\sum_i \log \frac{e^{\qvec_{x_i}\transpose \pvec_{y_i}}}{\sum_{y \in \Ccal} e^{\qvec_{x_i}\transpose\pvec_y}}$.
The sum over the catalogue is too expensive, so ItemSage uses two sampled terms <Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />:

$$
L_{S_{\mathrm{pos}}} = -\frac{1}{\abs{\Bcal}}\sum_{i=1}^{\abs{\Bcal}}\log
\frac{e^{\qvec_{x_i}\transpose \pvec_{y_i} - \log Q_p(y_i \mid x_i)}}{\sum_{y \in \Bcal} e^{\qvec_{x_i}\transpose \pvec_y - \log Q_p(y \mid x_i)}},
\qquad
L_{S_{\mathrm{neg}}} = -\frac{1}{\abs{\Ncal}}\sum_{i=1}^{\abs{\Ncal}}\log
\frac{e^{\qvec_{x_i}\transpose \pvec_{y_i} - \log Q_n(y_i)}}{\sum_{y \in \Ncal} e^{\qvec_{x_i}\transpose \pvec_y - \log Q_n(y)}},
$$

and minimises $L_{S_{\mathrm{pos}}} + L_{S_{\mathrm{neg}}}$. The first uses the other positives in the batch as
negatives, with the <NoteLink to="two-tower-model">logQ correction</NoteLink> for how often each product is a positive, estimated with a count-min sketch
<Cite id="yi2019sampling" /> <Cite id="cormode2005" />. In-batch negatives alone never include products that nobody
engaged with and over-penalise popular products. The second term uses $\abs{\Ncal} = \abs{\Bcal}$ products sampled
from the catalogue, with $Q_n$ their sampling probability. Mixed negative sampling puts both sets in one denominator
<Cite id="yang2020mns" />. ItemSage keeps two separate terms and reports at least 3.5% better recall on every task
with the separation <Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.

The logQ term divides each sampled exponential by its inclusion probability, which makes the sampled denominator an
unbiased estimate of the catalogue-wide sum. The derivation is in <NoteLink to="pinnerformer">PinnerFormer</NoteLink>.

## Multi-task batches

ItemSage learns four engagement types: clicks, saves, add-to-cart actions and checkouts, with queries from Closeup
(images) and Search (text). <Gloss name="multi-task-learning">Multi-task learning</Gloss> here means that one batch of size $\abs{\Bcal}$ holds $T_k$ positives
from each task $k$, with $\sum_k T_k = \abs{\Bcal}$. There are no task-specific heads, because the output must be one
embedding. The allocation $T_k$ sets the trade-off between tasks. The paper reports that, with $K$ tasks, $T_k = \abs{\Bcal}/K$ is a good
starting point when adding a task, and attributes the lack of harm to existing tasks to correlation between them: a
purchase is usually preceded by a click <Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.

For Home, which queries with user activity, the product retrieval uses the <NoteLink to="pinnersage">PinnerSage</NoteLink> approach: cluster the user's
activity and sample pins from several clusters, which reduces the problem to Closeup-style image queries
<Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" /> <Cite id="pal2020pinnersage" />.

## Serving

A daily batch job embeds all products, builds one HNSW index and writes the embeddings to a feature store. All three
surfaces share the index. Home and Closeup query with stored PinSage embeddings; Search computes the SearchSage query
embedding on the fly, for unseen queries. One workflow and one index instead of three cut infrastructure and maintenance
cost by a factor of three. An MLP on ItemSage embeddings also improved top-1 accuracy of a product gender-affinity
classifier by 3.6% <Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.

## Reported results

In <Gloss name="ab-test" plural /> where ItemSage generated candidates, against the previous embeddings (sum-pooling baselines), the relative
changes were <Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />:

| surface | clicks  | purchases | gross merchandise value per user |
| ------- | ------- | --------- | -------------------------------- |
| Home    | +11.34% | +2.61%    | +2.94%                           |
| Closeup | +9.97%  | +5.13%    | +6.85%                           |
| Search  | +2.3%   | +1.5%     | +3.7%                            |

Offline, the transformer beat sum-pooling and MLP baselines on all tasks, with the largest gains on search clicks and
saves. Image-only models were stronger than text-only ones, and the multi-modal model beat both. Training on shopping
tasks together with clicks and saves improved the sparse shopping tasks with small losses on clicks and saves
<Cite id="baltescu2022itemsage" />.

## Pitfalls

- **Frozen query spaces.** Compatibility ties ItemSage to specific versions of PinSage and SearchSage. Upgrading either
  requires retraining.
- **Bag-of-words text.** Text uses n-gram bags, not a pretrained <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss>; the authors list this as future work.
- **No graph.** Despite the name, ItemSage uses no neighbourhood aggregation of its own. Graph information enters only
  through the PinSage embeddings of its images.
