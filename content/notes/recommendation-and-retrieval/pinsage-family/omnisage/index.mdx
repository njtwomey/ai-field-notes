<Definition>
**OmniSage** is a representation-learning framework that produces one embedding per entity (pins, boards and other
entity types) at Pinterest. It builds a heterogeneous graph of entities and interactions, samples each node's
neighbourhood by random walks with restart per node type, and encodes the node's content features together with its
neighbours' features with a transformer, one per entity type. It is trained on three families of contrastive tasks:
entity–entity pairs from graph edges and engagement logs, entity–feature pairs, and next and future entities in user
activity sequences <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

Before OmniSage, Pinterest learned representations with separate models for separate signals: PinSage for the pin–board graph,
PinnerFormer for user sequences, content models for images and text, and task-specific models such as ItemSage and
OmniSearchSage <Cite id="ying2018pinsage" /> <Cite id="pancha2022pinnerformer" /> <Cite id="agarwal2024omnisearchsage" />.
MultiBiSage had already shown that pin embeddings improve when trained on several bipartite graphs (with users, idea
pins, creators and interactions such as add-to-cart and follow) instead of the pin–board graph alone
<Cite id="gurukar2022multibisage" />. OmniSage combines graphs, content and sequences in one model whose embeddings
serve many applications <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

## Heterogeneous graph

The graph $\Hcal = (\Vcal, \Ecal, \Tcal_v, \Tcal_e)$ has node types $\Tcal_v$ (users, queries, pins, boards and others)
and edge types $\Tcal_e$ (searches, saves, clicks, categorisations and others). Popular nodes are pruned by keeping a
uniform sample of $d^{\alpha}$ of their $d$ edges, with $\alpha = 0.86$; low-degree nodes are not pruned. After pruning
the graph has 5.6 billion nodes and 63.5 billion edges <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

## Neighbourhood sampling

OmniSage keeps PinSage's importance-based neighbourhoods: the proximity $s(u, v, G)$ of $v$ to $u$ is the visit
probability of a random walk with restart from $u$ <Cite id="ying2018pinsage" />. It extends them to several types. For a
subset of relations $R \subseteq \Tcal_e$, let $\Hcal_R$ be the subgraph with only those edge types. For each node type
$t$, it keeps the $k_t$ nodes of type $t$ with the highest proximity in $\Hcal_R$. The neighbourhood is the union over
chosen subsets $R_1, \dots, R_n$:

$$
N(u, \Hcal) = \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} N(u, \Hcal_{R_i}).
$$

Per-type quotas $k_t$ stop one abundant type from filling the neighbourhood. The proximity scores are approximated with
the forward-push algorithm <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

## Encoder

Each entity type $t$ has its own embedder $f_t$. Images are encoded by an in-house pretrained vision transformer and text
by a shared hash embedding of n-grams. The neighbours of $u$ are sorted by proximity score, their features pass through
an MLP, and the resulting sequence goes through a transformer encoder with a learned `[CLS]` token. The `[CLS]` output
passes through an MLP and is normalised to unit length <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

Compared with <NoteLink to="pinsage">PinSage</NoteLink>, the aggregator changes from a weighted mean with
weights fixed by visit counts to self-attention over neighbours ordered by those counts. Attention weights can then
depend on the content of the node and each neighbour.

## Objectives

All tasks use the same sampled softmax with in-batch and random negatives, for a query $\qvec$, positive $\pvec$ and
negatives $\Ncal$: $L(\qvec, \pvec, \Ncal) = -\log \frac{e^{s(\qvec, \pvec)}}{e^{s(\qvec, \pvec)} +
\sum_{\nvec \in \Ncal} e^{s(\qvec, \nvec)}}$ <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

- **Entity–entity.** Positive pairs come from graph edges (a node and a sampled neighbour of a chosen type) and from
  engagement logs such as pin–pin and board–pin. Each data source $i$ has a weight $w_i$ in $L_{\mathrm{pair}}$.
- **Entity–feature.** For each feature type $x$ of node $u$, an MLP encoder $g_x$ maps the feature to a query, and the
  positive is $u$'s own embedding. This keeps content information in the embedding, which matters for cold-start nodes.
- **User–entity.** A causal transformer decoder reads a user's sequence of entity embeddings $\pvec_1, \dots, \pvec_t$
  and outputs user embeddings $\uvec_t$. The next-action loss contrasts $\uvec_t$ with $\pvec_{t+1}$. The future-action
  loss contrasts it with an entity sampled from a future window, following PinnerFormer
  <Cite id="pancha2022pinnerformer" />.

The total loss is
$L = \lambda_p L_{\mathrm{pair}} + \lambda_f L_{\mathrm{feat}} + \lambda_s (L_{\mathrm{next}} + L_{\mathrm{fut}})$.
Because the sequence losses backpropagate into the entity embedders, the graph embeddings are trained end to end on user
activity <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

## Training and inference systems

**Graph engine.** An in-house engine, Grogu, holds the graph as memory-mapped adjacency lists on high-memory machines,
samples neighbours on the fly in C++, and fetches features from a RocksDB key-value store partitioned across machines
<Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

**Chunked sampled softmax.** With $b$ queries and $k$ negatives pooled across devices, the logits matrix
$\Qmat\Nmat\transpose$ has $b \times k$ entries per task and is held until the backward pass. OmniSage detaches the
query, positive and negative embeddings, computes the loss and its gradients with respect to those embeddings chunk by
chunk, frees each chunk's logits, averages the accumulated gradients, and then backpropagates once from the embeddings
into the model. Peak memory for logits falls from $b \times k$ to (chunk size) $\times k$ without extra backward passes
through the model, which permits larger batches <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

**Inference.** A daily batch job embeds over 6 billion entities. Nodes pruned from the graph or without edges get zero
neighbour features <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

## Reported results

Offline, the unified model improved recall over PinSage by 32.1% and over PinnerFormer by 89.5% on their respective
tasks <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

In A/B tests that replaced PinSage representations with OmniSage in homefeed ranking, related-pins retrieval and
related-pins ranking, sitewide repins rose by 0.92%, 0.39% and 0.85% respectively, over 1.7% in total. OmniSage board
embeddings in the Board More Ideas surface raised sitewide repins by 1.05% and that surface's repins by 9.7%. Across
five applications the paper reports about a 2.5% increase in sitewide repins <Cite id="badrinath2025omnisage" />.

## Pitfalls

- **Many knobs.** Relation subsets, per-type quotas $k_t$, task weights $\lambda$ and data-source weights $w_i$ all
  change the embedding; the paper ablates several of them.
- **Daily inference.** Embeddings are refreshed once a day, like PinnerFormer, so they miss same-day activity.
- **Infrastructure.** On-the-fly sampling over billions of nodes needs a dedicated graph engine and feature store.
