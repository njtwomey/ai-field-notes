<Definition>
**BERT4Rec** encodes a user's item sequence with a bidirectional <NoteLink to="transformer">transformer</NoteLink>, in which every position attends to every
other. During training a proportion $\rho$ of items is replaced by a special token $[\text{mask}]$, and the model
predicts the original item at each masked position from the whole sequence. At inference $[\text{mask}]$ is appended
after the last real item, and the output at that position scores candidates for the next item <Cite id="sun2019bert4rec"
/>.
</Definition>

## Motivation

<NoteLink to="sasrec">SASRec</NoteLink> lets each position see only its past. Sun et al. argued that user behaviour is
not strictly ordered. A user might view an item, browse related products, and come back to buy the first one. The
context of the purchase lies both before and after the item in the clickstream, and a representation that uses both
sides should be richer <Cite id="sun2019bert4rec" />.

## The Cloze objective

A bidirectional model cannot be trained to predict the next item: position $t$ would attend to $i_{t+1}$ and copy it.
BERT4Rec adopts the masked-language-model objective of <Gloss name="bert" /> <Cite id="devlin2019" />, known as the Cloze task.

1. Replace a random proportion $\rho$ of the items in the sequence with $[\text{mask}]$.
2. Run the <NoteLink to="encoder-only-transformers">bidirectional encoder</NoteLink> over the corrupted sequence.
3. At each masked position, predict the original item with a softmax over the whole catalogue, and minimise the <NoteLink to="cross-entropy-and-perplexity">negative
   log-likelihood</NoteLink> of the true items:

$$
\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{\abs{\Mcal}}\sum_{t \in \Mcal}\ln p\paren{i_t \mid S_u'},
$$

where $\Mcal$ is the set of masked positions and $S_u'$ the masked sequence. Each masked position is predicted from
items on both sides, and no position sees its own answer. Random masking also creates many training examples from one
sequence <Cite id="sun2019bert4rec" />.

**Inference.** Append $[\text{mask}]$ to the end of the history. The output at that position is a representation of the
user's current intent; its softmax scores the next item. Because the only masked position
at inference is the last one, Sun et al. also trained on sequences in which only the last item is masked, to reduce the
mismatch between training and use.

## Does bidirectionality help?

BERT4Rec reported gains over SASRec on several benchmarks. Later work separated the architecture from the loss.
BERT4Rec's objective is a full softmax over all items; the original SASRec used binary cross-entropy with one sampled
negative. When SASRec is trained with the same full-softmax cross-entropy, it outperforms BERT4Rec and trains faster
<Cite id="klenitskiy2023" />. The causal architecture fits <NoteLink to="sequential-recommendation-models">next-item prediction</NoteLink> more naturally
than masked pre-training, which was designed for a different objective.

The comparison is a case of the evaluation problem described in
<NoteLink to="progress-and-baselines-in-recommendation">progress and baselines</NoteLink>: a difference in training
setup was credited to an architectural idea.

## Pitfalls

- **Training–inference mismatch.** Masks are scattered in training and at the end at inference. Include last-item
  masking in training.
- **Slow convergence.** Only the masked fraction of positions gives a training signal per pass, against every
  position for a causal model. BERT4Rec needs longer training.
- **Full softmax cost.** The output layer over millions of items dominates computation; large catalogues need <NoteLink to="two-tower-model">sampled
  softmax</NoteLink>.
