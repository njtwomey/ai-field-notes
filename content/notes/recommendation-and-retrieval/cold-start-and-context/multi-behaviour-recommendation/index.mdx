<Definition>
A **multi-behaviour recommender** models several kinds of user action on items, such as view, click, add-to-cart and
purchase, instead of one. It is usually a **multi-task** model: one network with a shared part and one output head per
behaviour, trained on the sum of the tasks' losses, $\mathcal{L} = \sum_k \lambda_k\mathcal{L}_k$.
</Definition>

## Behaviours differ in strength and density

A purchase says more than a view. A system trained on one behaviour ignores the rest.
The behaviours also differ in volume: views may outnumber purchases a hundred to one. Modelling them jointly keeps each
behaviour distinct, for example as separate sequences or graphs per behaviour, while sharing what they have in common.

<Gloss name="multi-task-learning">Multi-task learning</Gloss> offers two gains.

1. **Efficiency.** One model to train and serve instead of several.
2. **Positive transfer.** Representations learned from a data-rich task, such as clicks, improve a data-poor one, such
   as purchases.

The risk is <NoteLink to="negative-transfer">negative transfer</NoteLink>: optimising one task harms another when their
needs conflict.

## Architectures

- **Shared bottom.** Embeddings and lower layers are shared; each task has its own tower on top. Simple, but because
  all tasks must use the same representation, tasks whose needs conflict cause negative transfer.
- **MMoE.** Multi-gate mixture of experts replaces the shared bottom with $E$ expert networks $f_1, \ldots, f_E$. Each
  task $k$ has a gating network that weights the experts for each input:
  $$
  y_k = h_k\paren{\sum_{e=1}^{E} g_{k,e}(\xvec)f_e(\xvec)}, \qquad \gvec_k(\xvec) = \operatorname{softmax}\paren{\Wmat_k\xvec}.
  $$
  Tasks that are related learn to use the same experts; tasks that are not learn to use different ones, which limits
  negative transfer <Cite id="ma2018mmoe" />. See <NoteLink to="mixture-of-experts">mixture of experts</NoteLink>.
- **PLE.** Progressive layered extraction splits experts into shared experts and task-specific experts at each layer.
  Each task's gate mixes the shared experts with its own, and stacking such layers separates shared and task-specific
  knowledge step by step <Cite id="tang2020ple" />.
- **STEM.** The architectures above still share one embedding per user and item across all tasks, which assumes a
  user's preferences are the same whatever the behaviour. Shared and task-specific embeddings (STEM) give each user and
  item a shared embedding plus one embedding per task; a task's prediction uses the shared embedding and its own. This
  captures both the common ground and where behaviours diverge <Cite id="su2024stem" />.

**Cascaded tasks.** Some behaviours are conditional on others: a conversion follows a click. Training a conversion
model only on clicked impressions creates a sample-selection bias, because it is served on all impressions. The entire space multi-task model (ESMM) models
$p(\text{click and convert}) = p(\text{click}) \cdot p(\text{convert} \mid \text{click})$ over the entire impression
space, so both factors are trained on the data they are applied to <Cite id="ma2018esmm" />.

## Warming up cold users

A user is often cold only for the target behaviour. A new shopper may have no purchases and dozens of views and clicks
from the first session. A purchase-only model has nothing for this user. A multi-task model learns the user's
representation from the dense auxiliary behaviours, and the purchase head reads that representation, so it can predict
purchases before the first one. See the <NoteLink to="cold-start-problem">cold-start
problem</NoteLink>.

## Pitfalls

- **Task weights are business decisions.** The weights $\lambda_k$ trade clicks against purchases. Multi-task models
  force an organisation to quantify the relative value of its objectives.
- **<NoteLink to="gradient">Gradient</NoteLink> conflict.** Tasks with very different loss scales or data volumes dominate shared parameters. Normalise
  losses and monitor each task.
- **Behaviours can mislead.** A click on a clickbait title is dense and weakly related to satisfaction. Transfer from it
  carries its biases into sparse tasks.
