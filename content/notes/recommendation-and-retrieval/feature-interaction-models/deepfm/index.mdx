<Definition>
**DeepFM** predicts a click probability as $\hat{y} = \sigma(y_{\text{FM}} + y_{\text{DNN}})$. Both terms read the same
embedding $\evec_f$ of each input field $f$. $y_{\text{FM}}$ is a
<NoteLink to="factorisation-machines">factorisation machine</NoteLink>: first-order weights plus <NoteLink to="dot-product">inner products</NoteLink> of
pairs of embeddings. $y_{\text{DNN}}$ is a feed-forward network applied to the concatenated embeddings. The model is
trained end to end <Cite id="guo2017deepfm" />.
</Definition>

## From Wide & Deep to DeepFM

<NoteLink to="wide-and-deep">Wide & Deep</NoteLink> needs engineers to choose which features to cross in its wide
part. DeepFM puts a factorisation machine there. The FM learns a weight for every pair of features through their
embeddings, so no crosses are designed by hand. The FM captures first- and second-order
interactions explicitly; the network captures higher orders implicitly.

The two components share one embedding table. This differs from earlier hybrids such as FNN, which pre-trained an FM
and used its embeddings to initialise a network, and from Wide & Deep, whose wide inputs are separate raw and crossed
features. Sharing means the FM's pairwise signal shapes the embeddings the network uses, and the model needs no
pre-training <Cite id="guo2017deepfm" />. DeepFM became a standard baseline for <Gloss name="click-through-rate">click-through-rate</Gloss> prediction.

## Relatives

Several relatives differ in how they build interactions.

- **Deep & Cross Network (DCN).** A cross network replaces the wide part. Each layer computes $\xvec_{l+1} =
  \xvec_0\xvec_l\transpose\wvec_l + \bvec_l + \xvec_l$ from the input embedding vector $\xvec_0$. Layer $l$ adds
  interactions of degree one higher, so $L$ layers give bounded-degree polynomial crosses with only $O(d)$ parameters
  per layer for input dimension $d$ <Cite id="wang2017dcn" />.
- **xDeepFM.** A compressed interaction network (CIN) builds interactions at the level of whole embedding vectors
  rather than individual bits, layer by layer, alongside a plain network. This makes the order of each learned
  interaction explicit <Cite id="lian2018xdeepfm" />.
- **DLRM.** Facebook's deep learning recommendation model looks up an embedding for each sparse feature, passes dense
  features through a bottom network to a vector of the same size, takes the dot products of all pairs of these vectors,
  and feeds them with the dense vector to a top network. The paper also addresses training at scale: embedding tables
  too large for one device are split across devices (<NoteLink to="model-parallelism">model parallelism</NoteLink>) while the networks are replicated (<NoteLink to="data-parallelism">data
  parallelism</NoteLink>) <Cite id="naumov2019dlrm" />.

All four share one design: embeddings for sparse features, an explicit interaction component, and a network. They are
**ranking** models. Their power comes from combining user, item and context features before scoring, which is exactly
what a retrieval model over a large catalogue cannot afford (see <NoteLink to="multi-stage-recommendation">multi-stage
recommendation</NoteLink>).

## Pitfalls

- **Embedding tables dominate.** Parameters are mostly embeddings. Memory, hashing of rare IDs and the sharding of
  tables matter more than the network.
- **Gains over tuned baselines are small.** Improvements of a few thousandths of AUC are typical in CTR benchmarks.
  Compare against a well-tuned FM or DeepFM under the same preprocessing.
