<Definition>
Zachary observed a university karate club of 34 members and recorded 78 friendships outside the club. A dispute between
the instructor ("Mr. Hi", node 0) and an officer (node 33) split the club into two factions of 17 members each
<Cite id="zachary1977" />. Given only the friendship graph, embed the members in two dimensions **without training
anything**, by propagating random features over the graph, and measure how well the embedding separates the two
factions.
</Definition>

## Setup

Let $\Amat \in \set{0, 1}^{34 \times 34}$ be the adjacency matrix, $\tilde\Amat = \Amat + \eye$ the graph with
self-loops, $\tilde\Dmat$ its diagonal degree matrix, and

$$
\hat\Amat = \tilde\Dmat^{-1/2}\tilde\Amat\tilde\Dmat^{-1/2}
$$

the propagation matrix of a <NoteLink to="graph-convolutional-network">graph convolutional network</NoteLink>
<Cite id="kipf2017" />. There are no node features, so the input is the identity, $\Xmat = \eye$: every node is its own
one-hot feature. Two untrained maps produce 2-D embeddings.

- **Untrained GCN.** $\Hmat_{l+1} = \tanh(\hat\Amat\Hmat_l\Wmat_l)$ with $\Hmat_0 = \Xmat$ and weights $\Wmat_l$ drawn
  from a Gaussian and never updated, with widths $34 \to 4 \to \cdots \to 2$ for $K$ layers. Kipf's demonstration of
  GCNs used exactly this setting with three layers <Cite id="kipf2016blog" />.
- **Linear propagation.** $\Zmat = \hat\Amat^K\Rmat$ with a random Gaussian projection $\Rmat \in \reals^{34 \times 2}$:
  $K$ rounds of neighbourhood averaging and no non-linearity, as in <NoteLink to="lightgcn">LightGCN</NoteLink> <Cite
  id="he2020lightgcn" />.

**Measure of separation.** Fit a logistic regression that predicts the faction from the 2-D point and report its
accuracy on the 34 members (a linear probe), and the mean silhouette of the two factions.

## Solution

Averaged over 200 random seeds (computed with numpy and scikit-learn):

| layers $K$ | probe accuracy, tanh GCN | probe accuracy, linear | silhouette, tanh GCN | silhouette, linear |
| ---------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------ |
| 0          | 0.60                     | 0.60                   | 0.00                 | 0.00               |
| 1          | 0.70                     | 0.70                   | 0.09                 | 0.09               |
| 2          | 0.78                     | 0.80                   | 0.18                 | 0.21               |
| 3          | 0.84                     | 0.86                   | 0.25                 | 0.30               |
| 4          | 0.88                     | 0.90                   | 0.29                 | 0.35               |

With $K = 0$ the points are random, and 0.60 is what a linear separator scores on 34 random points by chance fit. Each
layer adds separation. The median seed at $K = 4$ classifies 32 of 34 members correctly (0.94). Nothing was learned: the
weights are random, and the linear version has no weights beyond one random projection.

## Why propagation finds the communities

$\hat\Amat$ is symmetric, so it has an eigendecomposition $\hat\Amat = \sum_{j=1}^{34}\lambda_j\uvec_j\uvec_j\transpose$
with real eigenvalues $1 = \lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \cdots > -1$ and orthonormal eigenvectors $\uvec_j$. Then

$$
\hat\Amat^K\Rmat = \sum_{j=1}^{34}\lambda_j^K\,\uvec_j\paren{\uvec_j\transpose\Rmat}.
$$

The random projection gives every eigenvector a random coefficient $\uvec_j\transpose\Rmat$. Powering multiplies each by
$\lambda_j^K$, which shrinks components with $\abs{\lambda_j} < 1$ geometrically, the smallest fastest. For the karate
club the leading eigenvalues are $1$, $0.896$, $0.774$ and $0.701$; after $K = 4$ the second component keeps
$0.896^4 = 0.64$ of its weight and the fourth only $0.701^4 = 0.24$. The embedding is increasingly spanned by the first
few eigenvectors. Repeated averaging over neighbours is a **low-pass filter** on the graph: it keeps signals that vary
smoothly along edges and removes those that alternate between neighbours.

The smooth eigenvectors are the ones spectral clustering uses. $\hat\Amat = \eye - \Lmat_{\text{sym}}$, where
$\Lmat_{\text{sym}} = \eye - \tilde\Dmat^{-1/2}\tilde\Amat\tilde\Dmat^{-1/2}$ is the normalised Laplacian of the graph
with self-loops, so the largest eigenvalues of $\hat\Amat$ belong to the smallest eigenvalues of $\Lmat_{\text{sym}}$.
The top eigenvector is proportional to $\sqrt{\tilde{d}_i}$ and carries only degree. The second varies slowly within
densely connected groups and changes sign across the sparse cut between them. On the karate club, the sign of $\uvec_2$
alone assigns 32 of 34 members to their faction. See <NoteLink to="spectral-clustering">spectral clustering</NoteLink>
and <NoteLink to="laplacian-eigenmaps">Laplacian eigenmaps</NoteLink>.

The tanh network behaves similarly while its activations are small and tanh is nearly linear; the random weight matrices
mix the propagated components without undoing the smoothing. Kipf gives a complementary reading: a GCN layer is a
differentiable relative of the Weisfeiler–Lehman colour-refinement algorithm, which labels nodes by their neighbourhoods
<Cite id="kipf2016blog" />.

## What it illustrates

- **The graph does most of the work.** A good part of what a graph network "learns" about community structure is given
  by the propagation itself. Training adjusts which smooth components matter for the task.
- **Why LightGCN works.** <NoteLink to="lightgcn">LightGCN</NoteLink> keeps only linear propagation on the user–item
  graph and trains the input embeddings. The collaborative signal is smoothness over the interaction graph, which
  propagation supplies. See <NoteLink to="graph-collaborative-filtering">graph collaborative filtering</NoteLink>.
- **Over-smoothing.** As $K \to \infty$, $\hat\Amat^K\Rmat$ converges to the component along $\uvec_1$ alone, which
  encodes only degree, and the communities merge again. Useful depth is a few layers.

## Variations

- **Without self-loops** the adjacency of a bipartite graph has eigenvalue $-1$, and powers oscillate between the two
  sides; self-loops, or LightGCN's averaging over layers, remove the oscillation.
- **Trained weights.** Training the same GCN with the faction label of only one member per faction, the semi-supervised
  setting of Kipf and Welling, pulls the two groups apart further <Cite id="kipf2017" /> <Cite id="kipf2016blog" />.
