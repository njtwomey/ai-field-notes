<Definition>
**Weighted matrix factorisation** (WMF) fits user vectors $\wvec_u$ and item vectors $\vvec_i$ to all $U \times M$ cells
of an implicit-feedback matrix. Each cell has a binary target $p_{ui}$, 1 if user $u$ interacted with item $i$ and 0
otherwise, and a weight $c_{ui} > 0$ that is large for observed interactions and small for unobserved ones:

$$
\min_{\Wmat, \Vmat}\sum_{u, i} c_{ui}\paren{p_{ui} - \wvec_u\transpose\vvec_i}^2 + \lambda\paren{\norm{\Wmat}_F^2 + \norm{\Vmat}_F^2}.
$$

<Cite id="hu2008implicit" />
</Definition>

## Problem

Implicit feedback records only positive events. Fitting <NoteLink to="matrix-factorisation-for-recommendation">matrix
factorisation</NoteLink> to the observed cells alone fits a matrix of ones: the model predicts 1 everywhere and ranks
nothing. The unobserved cells must enter the objective. They cannot be treated as firm negatives either, because most
of them are items the user never saw.

## Mechanism

A general form puts a weight $w_{ui}$ on observed pairs and a small uniform weight $w_0$ on the rest:

$$
\min_{\Wmat, \Vmat}\sum_{(u, i) \in \Ocal} w_{ui}\paren{r_{ui} - \wvec_u\transpose\vvec_i}^2 + \sum_{(u, i) \notin \Ocal} w_0\paren{\wvec_u\transpose\vvec_i}^2.
$$

The second sum pulls every unobserved prediction towards 0, softly. The weights express confidence: $w_{ui}$ grows with
how often or how long the user engaged, and $w_0$ says how much an absence is worth as a negative.

Hu, Koren and Volinsky's version, the usual reference implementation, separates the two roles of the raw count
$r_{ui}$, such as the number of plays. The **preference** $p_{ui} = \indicator[r_{ui} > 0]$ is the target. The
**confidence** $c_{ui} = 1 + \alpha r_{ui}$ is the weight, with $\alpha$ typically around 40 in their experiments.
Unobserved cells get $p_{ui} = 0$ with confidence 1; a film watched ten times gets $p_{ui} = 1$ with confidence
$1 + 10\alpha$ <Cite id="hu2008implicit" />.

**Fitting by weighted ALS.** With $\Vmat$ fixed, user $u$'s terms are a weighted least-squares problem over all $M$
items. Let $\Cmat_u = \diag(c_{u1}, \ldots, c_{uM})$ and $\pvec_u = (p_{u1}, \ldots, p_{uM})\transpose$. Setting the
gradient $-2\Vmat\transpose\Cmat_u(\pvec_u - \Vmat\wvec_u) + 2\lambda\wvec_u$ to zero gives

$$
\wvec_u = \paren{\Vmat\transpose\Cmat_u\Vmat + \lambda\eye}^{-1}\Vmat\transpose\Cmat_u\pvec_u.
$$

Only the $n_u$ observed items have $c_{ui} \ne 1$ or $p_{ui} \ne 0$. Writing $\Vmat\transpose\Cmat_u\Vmat =
\Vmat\transpose\Vmat + \Vmat\transpose(\Cmat_u - \eye)\Vmat$ reuses $\Vmat\transpose\Vmat$ across users, and
$\Vmat\transpose\Cmat_u\pvec_u$ sums only over observed items. Each user step costs $O(n_uK^2 + K^3)$ even though it
fits all $M$ cells. Item steps are symmetric. See <NoteLink to="alternating-least-squares">alternating least
squares</NoteLink> <Cite id="hu2008implicit" />.

## Worked example

A user played item A 5 times and item B once, and never played item C. With $\alpha = 40$ the targets and weights
are:

| item | plays $r_{ui}$ | target $p_{ui}$ | weight $c_{ui} = 1 + 40r_{ui}$ |
| ---- | -------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------ |
| A    | 5              | 1               | 201                            |
| B    | 1              | 1               | 41                             |
| C    | 0              | 0               | 1                              |

A prediction error of 0.5 on A costs $201 \times 0.25 = 50.25$, on B $41 \times 0.25 = 10.25$, and on C $1 \times 0.25 =
0.25$. The fit bends to explain A before B, and bends very little for C. C still counts: summed over the hundreds of
thousands of unobserved items, the weak zeros stop the model from predicting 1 everywhere.

## Cost

- The objective has $UM$ terms. Without the rewriting above, fitting it would be infeasible. With it, a sweep costs
  about as much as ALS on the observed cells alone.
- SGD cannot use the trick directly; SGD versions sample unobserved cells instead, which approximates the objective.

## Trade-offs

- **Uniform weight on absences.** A constant $w_0$ treats an item the user never saw like one they saw and skipped.
  Exposure-aware variants weight unobserved cells by an estimated probability of exposure, which connects WMF to
  <NoteLink to="propensity-in-recommendation">propensity</NoteLink> methods.
- **Pointwise, not pairwise.** WMF regresses on 0 and 1. <NoteLink to="bayesian-personalised-ranking">BPR</NoteLink>
  instead asks only that observed items outrank unobserved ones, which targets the ranking directly.
- **Hyperparameters matter.** The confidence scale $\alpha$ and the regularisation $\lambda$ change results
  substantially and must be tuned on held-out interactions.
