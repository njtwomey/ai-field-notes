<Definition>
Let $\xvec_1, \dots, \xvec_\nu$ be independent $\MvGauss(\zeros, \Vmat)$ vectors in $\reals^d$, with $\nu \ge d$. The
$d \times d$ scatter matrix

$$
\Wmat = \sum_{i=1}^{\nu} \xvec_i \xvec_i^\top
$$

has a Wishart distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom and scale matrix $\Vmat$, written
$\Wmat \sim \Wishart(\nu, \Vmat)$. For real $\nu > d - 1$ its density on positive-definite matrices is

$$
p(\Wmat \mid \nu, \Vmat) = \frac{\det(\Wmat)^{(\nu - d - 1)/2} \exp\paren{-\tfrac12 \trace(\Vmat^{-1}\Wmat)}}{2^{\nu
d/2}
\det(\Vmat)^{\nu/2}\, \Gamma_d(\nu/2)},
$$

where $\Gamma_d$ is the multivariate gamma function.
</Definition>

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Parameters | Degrees of freedom $\nu > d - 1$, scale $\Vmat \in \reals^{d \times d}$ positive definite |
| Support | Symmetric positive-definite $d \times d$ matrices |
| Mean | $\nu\Vmat$ |
| Variance of entries | $\var(W_{ij}) = \nu\paren{V_{ij}^2 + V_{ii}V_{jj}}$ |
| Mode | $(\nu - d - 1)\Vmat$ for $\nu \ge d + 1$ |

## Where it comes from

John Wishart derived the distribution of the sample covariance of multivariate Gaussian data <Cite id="wishart1928" />.
If $\xvec_1, \dots, \xvec_n \sim \MvGauss(\muvec, \Sigmamat)$ with sample mean $\bar\xvec$, then

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} (\xvec_i - \bar\xvec)(\xvec_i - \bar\xvec)^\top \sim \Wishart(n - 1, \Sigmamat).
$$

It is the matrix counterpart of the result that $(n - 1)S^2/\sigma^2 \sim \ChiSq_{n-1}$ for scalar data. The proof is
the same rotation: an orthogonal matrix whose first row is $\ones^\top/\sqrt n$ turns the $n$ centred vectors into
$n - 1$ independent $\MvGauss(\zeros, \Sigmamat)$ vectors whose outer products sum to the scatter matrix (see
<NoteLink to="chi-squared-distribution">χ² distribution</NoteLink>).

## Properties

**Mean and variance.** From the definition,
$\expect[\Wmat] = \sum_{i=1}^{\nu} \expect[\xvec_i\xvec_i^\top] = \nu \Vmat$, because each $\xvec_i$ has mean $\zeros$
and covariance $\Vmat$. Each entry $W_{jk} = \sum_i x_{ij}x_{ik}$ is a sum of $\nu$ independent products. For a
zero-mean Gaussian pair, $\expect[x_j^2x_k^2] = V_{jj}V_{kk} + 2V_{jk}^2$ (Isserlis' theorem), so
$\var(x_jx_k) = V_{jj}V_{kk} + 2V_{jk}^2 - V_{jk}^2 = V_{jk}^2 + V_{jj}V_{kk}$, and the $\nu$ terms add to the variance
in the table.

**Diagonal entries.** Each diagonal entry is a scaled χ²: $W_{ii}/V_{ii} \sim \ChiSq_\nu$, because
$W_{ii} = \sum_{j=1}^\nu x_{ji}^2$ and each $x_{ji}/\sqrt{V_{ii}}$ is an independent standard normal. With $d = 1$ and
$V = 1$, $\Wishart(\nu, 1) = \ChiSq_\nu$. The Wishart is therefore a multivariate χ², and a multivariate gamma.

**Linear maps.** For a full-rank $\Amat \in \reals^{m \times d}$,
$\Amat\Wmat\Amat^\top \sim \Wishart(\nu, \Amat\Vmat\Amat^\top)$:
$\Amat\Wmat\Amat^\top = \sum_i (\Amat\xvec_i)(\Amat\xvec_i)^\top$, and each $\Amat\xvec_i$ is
$\MvGauss(\zeros, \Amat\Vmat\Amat^\top)$.

**Conjugacy.** For data $\xvec_1, \dots, \xvec_n \sim \MvGauss(\muvec, \Lambdamat^{-1})$ with known mean and a prior
$\Lambdamat \sim \Wishart(\nu, \Vmat)$ on the precision matrix, the posterior is

$$
\Lambdamat \mid \xvec_{1:n} \sim \Wishart\paren{\nu + n,\ \paren{\Vmat^{-1} + \textstyle\sum_i (\xvec_i -
\muvec)(\xvec_i -
\muvec)^\top}^{-1}}
$$

<Cite id="bishop2006" />. Each observation adds one degree of freedom and its outer product to the scatter.

<Derivation title="Conjugacy">
Write $\Smat = \sum_i (\xvec_i - \muvec)(\xvec_i - \muvec)^\top$. Using
$(\xvec - \muvec)^\top\Lambdamat(\xvec - \muvec) = \trace\paren{\Lambdamat(\xvec - \muvec)(\xvec - \muvec)^\top}$, the
likelihood is

$$
\prod_{i=1}^n \det(\Lambdamat)^{1/2}e^{-\frac12(\xvec_i - \muvec)^\top\Lambdamat(\xvec_i - \muvec)}
= \det(\Lambdamat)^{n/2}\exp\paren{-\tfrac12\trace(\Smat\Lambdamat)}.
$$

Multiplying by the prior density $\det(\Lambdamat)^{(\nu - d - 1)/2}\exp\paren{-\tfrac12\trace(\Vmat^{-1}\Lambdamat)}$
gives $\det(\Lambdamat)^{(\nu + n - d - 1)/2}\exp\paren{-\tfrac12\trace\paren{(\Vmat^{-1} + \Smat)\Lambdamat}}$, the
unnormalised $\Wishart\paren{\nu + n, (\Vmat^{-1} + \Smat)^{-1}}$ density.
</Derivation>

## Relations to other distributions

- **χ² and gamma.** The case $d = 1$. See the <NoteLink to="chi-squared-distribution">χ² distribution</NoteLink> and the
  <NoteLink to="gamma-distribution">gamma distribution</NoteLink>.
- **Inverse Wishart.** If $\Wmat \sim \Wishart(\nu, \Vmat)$, then $\Wmat^{-1}$ has an inverse Wishart distribution. It
  is the conjugate prior for a covariance matrix, as the inverse gamma is for a variance.
- **Multivariate normal.** Scatter matrices of <NoteLink to="multivariate-normal-distribution">multivariate
  normal</NoteLink> vectors are Wishart. The normal-Wishart distribution is the joint conjugate prior for a mean and a
  precision matrix.

## In machine learning

- **Bayesian Gaussian mixtures.** Variational Bayesian Gaussian mixture models place a Wishart prior on each
  component's precision, which regularises covariances and lets unused components shrink away <Cite id="bishop2006" />.
- **Covariance estimation.** A Wishart or inverse-Wishart prior shrinks a sample covariance towards the prior scale,
  which keeps it well conditioned when data are scarce.

## Pitfalls

- **Coupled variances and correlations.** A single $\nu$ controls the uncertainty of every entry, so the inverse Wishart
  links the variances to the correlations. Hierarchical models often use a separation prior, such as an LKJ prior on the
  correlation matrix with separate priors on the scales, instead <Cite id="gelman2013" />.
- **Parameter conventions.** Some texts parameterise by $\Vmat$ and others by $\Vmat^{-1}$. Check whether the mean is
  $\nu\Vmat$ or $\nu\Vmat^{-1}$ before using a library's Wishart.
