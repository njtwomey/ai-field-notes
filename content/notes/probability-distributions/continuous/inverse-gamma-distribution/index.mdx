<Definition>
A <NoteLink to="random-variable">random variable</NoteLink> $X$ has an inverse-gamma distribution with shape $\alpha > 0$ and scale $\beta > 0$, written
$X \sim \InvGamma(\alpha, \beta)$, if its density is

$$
p(x \mid \alpha, \beta) = \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\, x^{-\alpha - 1} \exp\paren{-\frac{\beta}{x}}, \qquad x >
0.
$$

Equivalently, $X \sim \InvGamma(\alpha, \beta)$ if and only if $1/X \sim \GammaD(\alpha, \beta)$ with rate $\beta$.
</Definition>

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Parameters | Shape $\alpha > 0$, scale $\beta > 0$ |
| Support | $x > 0$ |
| pdf | $\beta^\alpha x^{-\alpha - 1} e^{-\beta/x} / \Gamma(\alpha)$ |
| cdf | $Q(\alpha, \beta/x)$, the regularised upper incomplete <NoteLink to="gamma-function">gamma function</NoteLink> |
| Mean | $\beta/(\alpha - 1)$ for $\alpha > 1$, otherwise infinite |
| Variance | $\beta^2 / \paren{(\alpha - 1)^2(\alpha - 2)}$ for $\alpha > 2$, otherwise infinite or undefined |
| Mode | $\beta/(\alpha + 1)$ |

## Where it comes from

The density follows from the <NoteLink to="gamma-distribution">gamma</NoteLink> density by the <NoteLink to="change-of-variables">change of variables</NoteLink>
$x = 1/y$. If $Y \sim \GammaD(\alpha, \beta)$ has
density $p_Y(y)$, then $X = 1/Y$ has density $p_Y(1/x) \cdot \abs{d(1/x)/dx} = p_Y(1/x)/x^2$, which is the formula
above.

The distribution arises as the posterior of a Gaussian variance. Let $y_1, \dots, y_n \sim \Gauss(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with
$\mu$ known and prior $\sigma^2 \sim \InvGamma(\alpha, \beta)$. The likelihood is proportional to
$(\sigma^2)^{-n/2} \exp\paren{-\sum_i (y_i - \mu)^2 / (2\sigma^2)}$, which has the same form in $\sigma^2$ as the
prior. Write $R = \sum_i (y_i - \mu)^2$. Multiplying prior by likelihood,

$$
p(\sigma^2 \mid y_{1:n}) \propto (\sigma^2)^{-\alpha - 1}e^{-\beta/\sigma^2}\cdot(\sigma^2)^{-n/2}e^{-R/(2\sigma^2)}
= (\sigma^2)^{-(\alpha + n/2) - 1}\exp\paren{-\frac{\beta + R/2}{\sigma^2}},
$$

which is the unnormalised inverse-gamma density with updated parameters <Cite id="gelman2013" />:

$$
\sigma^2 \mid y_{1:n} \sim \InvGamma\paren{\alpha + \frac{n}{2},\ \beta + \frac12 \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \mu)^2}.
$$

For example, a prior $\InvGamma(3, 2)$ has mean $2/(3 - 1) = 1$. Ten observations with $R = 18$ give the posterior
$\InvGamma(8, 11)$, with mean $11/7 \approx 1.57$. The data alone suggest $R/n = 1.8$; the prior pulls the estimate
towards 1.

## Properties

**Shape.** The density vanishes as $x \to 0^+$, because the factor $e^{-\beta/x}$ goes to zero faster than any power.
The right tail decays like $x^{-\alpha - 1}$, a power law. The inverse gamma therefore rules out variances near zero
while keeping very large variances plausible.

**Mean and variance.** Only moments of order below $\alpha$ exist. The mean is infinite for $\alpha \le 1$ and the
variance for $\alpha \le 2$. Write $X = 1/Y$ with $Y \sim \GammaD(\alpha, \beta)$. The gamma moment formula
$\expect[Y^r] = \Gamma(\alpha + r)/(\Gamma(\alpha)\beta^r)$ holds for any $r > -\alpha$. With $r = -1$ and $r = -2$:

$$
\expect[X] = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha - 1)\,\beta}{\Gamma(\alpha)} = \frac{\beta}{\alpha - 1}, \qquad
\expect[X^2] = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha - 2)\,\beta^2}{\Gamma(\alpha)} = \frac{\beta^2}{(\alpha - 1)(\alpha - 2)}.
$$

Then

$$
\var(X) = \frac{\beta^2}{(\alpha - 1)(\alpha - 2)} - \frac{\beta^2}{(\alpha - 1)^2} = \frac{\beta^2}{(\alpha -
1)^2(\alpha - 2)}.
$$

The mode maximises the log-density $-(\alpha + 1)\log x - \beta/x$, whose <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> $-(\alpha + 1)/x + \beta/x^2$ is
zero at $x = \beta/(\alpha + 1)$.

**Scaling.** If $X \sim \InvGamma(\alpha, \beta)$ and $c > 0$, then $cX \sim \InvGamma(\alpha, c\beta)$, because
$1/(cX) = Y/c \sim \GammaD(\alpha, c\beta)$.

## Relations to other distributions

- **Gamma.** $X \sim \InvGamma(\alpha, \beta) \iff 1/X \sim \GammaD(\alpha, \beta)$.
- **Scaled inverse χ².** $\InvGamma(\nu/2, \nu s^2/2)$ is the scaled inverse-χ² distribution with $\nu$ degrees of
  freedom and scale $s^2$, the parameterisation used in many Bayesian texts <Cite id="gelman2013" />.
- **Student t.** A Gaussian whose variance is inverse-gamma is Student t. If
  $X \mid \tau^2 \sim \Gauss(0, \tau^2)$ and $\tau^2 \sim \InvGamma(\nu/2, \nu/2)$, then $X \sim \StudentT_\nu$. See the
  <NoteLink to="student-t-distribution">Student t distribution</NoteLink>.
- **<NoteLink to="wishart-distribution">Inverse Wishart</NoteLink>.** The inverse Wishart distribution generalises the inverse gamma to covariance matrices.

## In machine learning

- **Priors on noise variance.** <NoteLink to="bayesian-linear-regression">Bayesian linear regression</NoteLink> places an inverse-gamma prior on the noise variance, which
  keeps the joint posterior of weights and variance in closed form <Cite id="bishop2006" />.
- **Robust likelihoods.** Writing a Student t likelihood as a Gaussian with an inverse-gamma variance per data point
  lets EM and Gibbs samplers fit heavy-tailed models with Gaussian updates.

## Pitfalls

- **Near-flat priors.** $\InvGamma(\epsilon, \epsilon)$ with small $\epsilon$ is often used as a vague prior on a
  variance. In <NoteLink to="hierarchical-models">hierarchical models</NoteLink> with few groups the posterior depends strongly on $\epsilon$. A half-Cauchy or
  half-normal prior on the <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink> is usually more stable <Cite id="gelman2013" />.
- **Scale conventions.** SciPy's `invgamma(a, scale=β)` uses the scale $\beta$ above. Check that the second parameter is
  not a rate.
