<Definition>
A <NoteLink to="random-variable">random variable</NoteLink> $X$ has a geometric distribution with success probability $p \in (0, 1]$, written $X \sim \Geom(p)$,
if

$$
P(X = k) = (1 - p)^{k - 1} p, \qquad k = 1, 2, 3, \dots
$$

$X$ is the number of independent $\Bern(p)$ trials up to and including the first success.
</Definition>

Some texts count the failures before the first success instead. That variable is $X - 1$, with support
$\{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$ and mean $(1 - p)/p$. This note counts trials.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Parameter | Success probability $p \in (0, 1]$ |
| Support | $k \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ |
| pmf | $(1 - p)^{k - 1} p$ |
| cdf | $1 - (1 - p)^k$ |
| Mean | $1/p$ |
| Variance | $(1 - p)/p^2$ |
| Mode | 1 |
| Median | $\lceil -1/\log_2(1 - p) \rceil$ for $p < 1$ |

## Where it comes from

The first success happens on trial $k$ when the first $k - 1$ trials fail and trial $k$ succeeds. <NoteLink to="independence">Independence</NoteLink> gives
probability $(1 - p)^{k - 1} p$. The tail $P(X > k) = (1 - p)^k$ is the probability that the first $k$ trials all fail
<Cite id="wasserman2004" />. It decays exponentially in $k$. The cdf is $1 - (1 - p)^k$, and the probabilities sum to 1
by the geometric series, $\sum_{k \ge 1} (1 - p)^{k - 1} p = p / \paren{1 - (1 - p)} = 1$.

For example, a trial that succeeds with probability $p = 0.2$ first succeeds on the third attempt with probability
$0.8^2 \times 0.2 = 0.128$. The probability that more than five attempts are needed is $0.8^5 \approx 0.33$.

## Properties

**Mean and variance.** The expected number of trials is $1/p$. A success probability of 1% needs 100 trials on average.
The derivation conditions on the first trial, which is called <NoteLink to="gamblers-ruin">first-step analysis</NoteLink>. With probability $p$ the first trial
succeeds and $X = 1$. With probability $1 - p$ it fails, and the process starts afresh with one trial used, so $X$ is
$1 + X'$ with $X'$ distributed as $X$. Therefore

$$
\expect[X] = 1 + (1 - p)\,\expect[X], \qquad \expect[X] = \frac{1}{p}.
$$

The same argument for the second moment gives
$\expect[X^2] = p + (1 - p)\,\expect[(1 + X)^2] = 1 + 2(1 - p)/p + (1 - p)\,\expect[X^2]$, so
$\expect[X^2] = (2 - p)/p^2$ and $\var(X) = (2 - p)/p^2 - 1/p^2 = (1 - p)/p^2$.

**Memorylessness.** After $m$ failures, the number of further trials needed has the same distribution as at the start:

$$
P(X > m + k \mid X > m) = \frac{(1 - p)^{m + k}}{(1 - p)^m} = (1 - p)^k = P(X > k).
$$

The geometric is the only distribution on
$\{1, 2, \dots\}$ with this property.

<Derivation title="Only the geometric is memoryless">
Let $X$ take values in $\{1, 2, \dots\}$ with $P(X > m + k) = P(X > m)\,P(X > k)$ for all $m, k \ge 0$, and write
$q = P(X > 1)$. Applying the identity repeatedly with $m = 1$ gives $P(X > k) = q^k$. Then
$P(X = k) = q^{k-1} - q^k = q^{k-1}(1 - q)$, which is $\Geom(1 - q)$.
</Derivation>

**Maximum likelihood.** For independent observations $x_1, \dots, x_n$ the log-likelihood is
$\ell(p) = n \log p + \paren{\sum_i x_i - n}\log(1 - p)$. Setting $\ell'(p) = n/p - (\sum_i x_i - n)/(1 - p)$ to zero
gives $\hat p = n / \sum_i x_i = 1/\bar x$, the reciprocal of the mean number of trials.

## Relations to other distributions

- **Bernoulli.** The geometric is the waiting time in a sequence of independent
  <NoteLink to="bernoulli-distribution">Bernoulli</NoteLink> trials.
- **Negative binomial.** The number of failures before the $r$-th success is a sum of $r$ independent copies of
  $X - 1$. That sum is <NoteLink to="negative-binomial-distribution">negative binomial</NoteLink>, and $r = 1$ gives
  $X - 1$ itself.
- **Exponential.** The <NoteLink to="exponential-distribution">exponential distribution</NoteLink> is the continuous
  memoryless distribution. If $T \sim \Exp(\lambda)$,
  then $\lceil T \rceil \sim \Geom(1 - e^{-\lambda})$, since $P(\lceil T \rceil > k) = P(T > k) = e^{-\lambda k}$.

## In machine learning

- **Rejection sampling.** In <NoteLink to="rejection-sampling">rejection sampling</NoteLink>, each proposal is accepted
  independently with probability $a$, so the number of proposals per accepted sample is $\Geom(a)$. The expected cost is
  $1/a$ proposals, and a poor proposal distribution with small $a$ makes sampling slow.
- **Random restarts.** If each run of a non-convex method such as <NoteLink to="k-means">k-means</NoteLink> finds a good
  solution independently with probability $p$, the expected number of runs until one does is $1/p$. The probability that
  at least one of $m$ runs succeeds is $1 - (1 - p)^m$.

## Pitfalls

- **Two conventions.** SciPy's `geom` and NumPy's `geometric` count trials, with support starting at 1. TensorFlow
  Probability's `Geometric` counts failures, with support starting at 0. The means differ by 1.
- **Heavy tails for small p.** The <NoteLink to="variance-and-covariance">standard deviation</NoteLink> $\sqrt{1 - p}/p$ is close to the mean when $p$ is small. A wait
  several times longer than $1/p$ is common, not an anomaly.
