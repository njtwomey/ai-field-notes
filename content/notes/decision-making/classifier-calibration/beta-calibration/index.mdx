<Definition>
For a binary classifier whose score $s \in (0, 1)$ estimates $P(y = 1 \mid \xvec)$, the **beta calibration map** is

$$
\mu(s; a, b, c) = \frac{1}{1 + 1 \big/ \paren{e^c\, s^a / (1 - s)^b}} = \sigma\paren{a \ln s - b \ln(1 - s) + c},
$$

where $\sigma$ is the logistic function and $a, b \ge 0$. The parameters are fitted by
<NoteLink to="logistic-regression">logistic regression</NoteLink> of the label on the two features $\ln s$ and
$-\ln(1 - s)$ over a calibration set. Kull, Silva Filho and Flach derived it from beta-distributed class-conditional
scores <Cite id="kull2017" /> <Cite id="kull2017b" />.
</Definition>

## Problem

<NoteLink to="platt-scaling">Logistic calibration</NoteLink> fits $\sigma(\gamma s + \delta)$ to a probability score.
Its derivation assumes that the scores of each class are Gaussian with a shared variance, which cannot be right for
numbers confined to $[0, 1]$. Its shapes are all sigmoids in $s$. In particular, the identity map $s \mapsto s$ is not a
member, so logistic calibration of an already calibrated classifier makes it worse <Cite id="kull2017" />. An
overconfident model needs an inverse-sigmoid correction, which the logistic family also lacks.

## Mechanism

**Derivation from beta-distributed scores.** Model the score of each class with a beta distribution, the standard family
on $[0, 1]$: $s \mid y = 1 \sim \Beta(\alpha_1, \beta_1)$ and $s \mid y = 0 \sim \Beta(\alpha_0, \beta_0)$, with prior
$\pi_1 = P(y = 1)$ and $\pi_0 = 1 - \pi_1$. The beta density is $s^{\alpha - 1}(1 - s)^{\beta - 1} / B(\alpha, \beta)$,
where $B$ is the <NoteLink to="gamma-function">beta function</NoteLink>. The likelihood ratio is

$$
\frac{p(s \mid 1)}{p(s \mid 0)} = \frac{B(\alpha_0, \beta_0)}{B(\alpha_1, \beta_1)}\, s^{\alpha_1 - \alpha_0}\,(1 -
s)^{\beta_1 - \beta_0}.
$$

The posterior odds are the prior odds times this ratio. Taking logs,

$$
\log\frac{P(y = 1 \mid s)}{P(y = 0 \mid s)} = a \ln s - b \ln(1 - s) + c,
$$

with $a = \alpha_1 - \alpha_0$, $b = \beta_0 - \beta_1$ and $c = \ln\frac{B(\alpha_0, \beta_0)}{B(\alpha_1, \beta_1)} +
\ln\frac{\pi_1}{\pi_0}$. Applying the logistic function gives the map in the definition <Cite id="kull2017" />.

**Which shapes it contains.**

- **The identity.** At $a = b = 1$, $c = 0$ the log-odds is $\ln s - \ln(1 - s)$, the log-odds of $s$ itself, so
  $\mu(s) = s$. A calibrated classifier stays calibrated. It arises, for instance, when positives are $\Beta(2, 1)$ and
  negatives $\Beta(1, 2)$ with equal priors.
- **Logistic in the log-odds.** When $a = b$, $a \ln s - a\ln(1 - s) = a \operatorname{logit}(s)$, so the map is
  $\sigma(a \operatorname{logit}(s) + c)$: Platt scaling applied to the log-odds of the score. $a > 1$ gives sigmoids,
  which sharpen an underconfident model; $a < 1$ gives inverse sigmoids, which soften an overconfident one.
- **Asymmetric maps.** $a \ne b$ bends the two ends of the curve differently, which fits models that are overconfident
  about one class only.

**Monotonicity.** The <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> of the log-odds is $a/s + b/(1 - s)$. It is
non-negative for all $s$ exactly when $a \ge 0$ and $b \ge 0$, and then the map is non-decreasing and preserves the
classifier's ranking. An unconstrained fit can in principle return a negative coefficient, although this never happened
in the authors' experiments <Cite id="kull2017" />. If an unconstrained fit ever returns $a < 0$ or $b < 0$, the authors
suggest fixing that coefficient at zero and refitting.

**Fitting.** Compute the features $u = \ln s$ and $v = -\ln(1 - s)$ for each calibration case and fit an ordinary
logistic regression of $y$ on $(u, v)$ with an intercept. The coefficients are $a$, $b$ and $c$.

**Midpoint parametrisation.** The score $m$ at which $\mu(m) = 1/2$ satisfies $c = b\ln(1 - m) - a\ln m$. Writing the
family in terms of $(a, b, m)$ separates the steepness of the two ends from the location of the decision threshold.

## Cost

Three parameters, fitted by a logistic regression with two features. Like logistic calibration it needs little
calibration data.

On 41 UCI datasets, with <NoteLink to="naive-bayes">naive Bayes</NoteLink> and two <Gloss name="adaboost" /> variants
as the base classifiers, beta calibration was significantly better than logistic calibration for naive Bayes and for
the AdaBoost variant that pushes probabilities to the extremes, in both log loss and Brier score. In
<NoteLink to="cross-entropy-and-perplexity">log loss</NoteLink> it was also significantly better than isotonic
calibration on those learners <Cite id="kull2017" />.

## Trade-offs

- **Still parametric.** Beta calibration cannot fit a non-monotone or wavy distortion. With a large calibration set,
  <NoteLink to="isotonic-calibration">isotonic calibration</NoteLink> can do better.
- **Scores at 0 or 1.** $\ln s$ or $\ln(1 - s)$ is infinite. Clip scores to $[\varepsilon, 1 - \varepsilon]$ before
  computing the features.
- **Binary only.** The multiclass extension replaces the two beta distributions by $K$
  <NoteLink to="dirichlet-distribution">Dirichlet distributions</NoteLink>, giving
  <NoteLink to="dirichlet-calibration">Dirichlet calibration</NoteLink>.
- **Beyond classification.** Song, Diethe, Kull and Flach use beta calibration maps to transform the predicted
  <Gloss name="cdf">cumulative distribution functions</Gloss> of a regressor (see
  <NoteLink to="distribution-calibration">distribution calibration</NoteLink>) <Cite id="songhao2019" />.

## Worked example

**A calibrated model.** Positives have scores $\Beta(2, 1)$, with density $2s$, and negatives $\Beta(1, 2)$, with
density $2(1 - s)$. With equal priors, $a = 2 - 1 = 1$, $b = 2 - 1 = 1$ and
$c = \ln\frac{B(1, 2)}{B(2, 1)} = \ln 1 = 0$. The map is the identity: the posterior is $2s / (2s + 2(1 - s)) = s$.

**A skewed model.** Positives have scores $\Beta(3, 1)$ and negatives $\Beta(1, 2)$, with equal priors. Then $a = 3 -
1 = 2$, $b = 2 - 1 = 1$, and $c = \ln\frac{B(1, 2)}{B(3, 1)} = \ln\frac{1/2}{1/3} = \ln 1.5 = 0.405$. The map is
$\mu(s) = \sigma(2\ln s - \ln(1 - s) + 0.405)$:

| score $s$ | $0.2$ | $0.5$ | $0.8$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mu(s)$ | $0.070$ | $0.429$ | $0.828$ |

Each value equals the posterior computed directly from the two beta densities. At $s = 0.5$, for example, the densities
are $3 \times 0.5^2 = 0.75$ and $2 \times 0.5 = 1$, so the posterior is $0.75 / 1.75 = 0.429$. The model is
underconfident at both ends, far more at the low end ($0.2 \mapsto 0.070$) than at the high end ($0.8 \mapsto 0.828$).
The log-odds $2\ln s - \ln(1 - s) + 0.405$ is not affine in $s$, so no logistic calibration map $\sigma(\gamma s +
\delta)$ reproduces it.
