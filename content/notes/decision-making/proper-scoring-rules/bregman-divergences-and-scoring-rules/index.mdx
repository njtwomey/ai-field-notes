<Definition>
Let $\phi$ be a strictly convex, differentiable function on a convex set. The **Bregman divergence** of $\phi$ is

$$
D_\phi(\pvec, \qvec) = \phi(\pvec) - \phi(\qvec) - \inner{\nabla\phi(\qvec)}{\pvec - \qvec},
$$

the gap at $\pvec$ between $\phi$ and its tangent plane at $\qvec$ <Cite id="bregman1967" />. A scoring rule on
probability vectors is proper if and only if it has the **Savage representation**

$$
S(\qvec, y) = H(\qvec) + \inner{\nabla H(\qvec)}{\evec_y - \qvec}
$$

for a concave function $H$, its generalised entropy, and then $S(\qvec, \pvec) - S(\pvec, \pvec) = D_{-H}(\pvec,
\qvec)$ <Cite id="savage1971" /> <Cite id="gneiting2007" />.
</Definition>

## Bregman divergences

The tangent plane of a convex function lies below it everywhere, so $D_\phi(\pvec, \qvec) \ge 0$. If $\phi$ is strictly
convex the plane touches only at $\qvec$, so $D_\phi(\pvec, \qvec) = 0$ only when $\pvec = \qvec$. A Bregman divergence
is not symmetric in general and does not satisfy the triangle inequality. Two examples cover most of practice.

- **Squared Euclidean distance.** With $\phi(\pvec) = \norm{\pvec}^2$, $\nabla\phi(\qvec) = 2\qvec$ and
  $D_\phi(\pvec, \qvec) = \norm{\pvec}^2 - \norm{\qvec}^2 - 2\inner{\qvec}{\pvec - \qvec} = \norm{\pvec - \qvec}^2$.
- **Kullback–Leibler divergence.** With the negative entropy $\phi(\pvec) = \sum_k p_k \log p_k$ on the simplex,
  $\partial\phi/\partial q_k = \log q_k + 1$, and
  $D_\phi(\pvec, \qvec) = \sum_k p_k \log p_k - \sum_k q_k \log q_k - \sum_k (\log q_k + 1)(p_k - q_k)$. The terms in
  $q_k \log q_k$ cancel, and $\sum_k (p_k - q_k) = 0$, leaving $\sum_k p_k \log(p_k / q_k) = \KL(\pvec \,\Vert\, \qvec)$.

## From an entropy to a proper scoring rule

Let $H$ be concave on the probability simplex and set $\phi = -H$. Define $S$ by the Savage form. Because
$\expect_{y \sim \pvec}[\evec_y] = \pvec$ and $S(\qvec, y)$ is affine in $\evec_y$,

$$
S(\qvec, \pvec) = H(\qvec) + \inner{\nabla H(\qvec)}{\pvec - \qvec}.
$$

At $\qvec = \pvec$ this is $H(\pvec)$, so $H$ is the minimum expected score, the entropy of the rule. The excess expected
loss of reporting $\qvec$ is

$$
S(\qvec, \pvec) - S(\pvec, \pvec) = -\brack{H(\pvec) - H(\qvec) - \inner{\nabla H(\qvec)}{\pvec - \qvec}} = D_{-H}(\pvec,
\qvec) \ge 0.
$$

$S$ is proper, and strictly proper when $H$ is strictly concave. Geometrically, the expected score of a fixed report
$\qvec$ is an affine function of the true distribution $\pvec$: the tangent plane to $H$ at $\qvec$. The loss from
misreporting is the height of that plane above $H$ at $\pvec$ <Cite id="dawid2007" />.

## From a proper scoring rule to an entropy

The converse also holds. Let $S$ be proper and define $H(\pvec) = S(\pvec, \pvec)$. For each fixed $\qvec$, the
function $\pvec \mapsto S(\qvec, \pvec) = \sum_y p_y S(\qvec, y)$ is linear in $\pvec$. Propriety says
$S(\qvec, \pvec) \ge H(\pvec)$ for all $\pvec$, with equality at $\pvec = \qvec$. So each such linear function lies above
$H$ and touches it at $\qvec$. $H$ is the lower envelope of linear functions, hence concave, and $S(\qvec, \cdot)$ is a
supporting plane at $\qvec$, with gradient a supergradient of $H$ there. That is the Savage form, with $\nabla H$
replaced by a supergradient where $H$ is not differentiable <Cite id="gneiting2007" />. Proper scoring rules and
concave entropies are two descriptions of the same object.

## The standard rules as entropies

- **Log loss.** $H(\pvec) = -\sum_k p_k \log p_k$, the Shannon entropy. The Savage form gives
  $-\sum_k q_k \log q_k - \sum_k (\log q_k + 1)(\indicator[y = k] - q_k) = -\log q_y$, since the sums of
  $q_k \log q_k$ cancel and $\sum_k(\indicator[y = k] - q_k) = 0$. The divergence is KL.
- **Brier score.** $H(\pvec) = 1 - \norm{\pvec}^2$, with $\nabla H(\qvec) = -2\qvec$. The Savage form gives
  $1 - \norm{\qvec}^2 - 2\inner{\qvec}{\evec_y - \qvec} = 1 - 2q_y + \norm{\qvec}^2 = \norm{\qvec - \evec_y}^2$. The
  divergence is squared Euclidean distance. For two classes $H(p) = 2p(1 - p)$, twice the Bernoulli variance.
- **Zero-one loss of the argmax.** $H(\pvec) = 1 - \max_k p_k$ is concave but piecewise linear. At a report $\qvec$ with
  unique largest entry $m$, a supergradient is $-\evec_m$, and the Savage form gives
  $1 - q_m - (\indicator[y = m] - q_m) = 1 - \indicator[y = m]$, the error of predicting the most probable class. It is
  proper but not strictly proper: any report with the same argmax scores the same.

The entropy is the <NoteLink to="bayes-decision-rule">Bayes risk</NoteLink> of a decision problem. The zero-one entropy
is the Bayes risk of classification, and the Brier and Shannon entropies are Bayes risks averaged over a range of
decision problems (see <NoteLink to="proper-scoring-rule">proper scoring rules</NoteLink>).

## The mean minimises every Bregman divergence

Let $\yvec$ be a random vector with mean $\muvec$. For any fixed $\avec$,

$$
\expect\brack{D_\phi(\yvec, \avec)} - \expect\brack{D_\phi(\yvec, \muvec)} = \phi(\muvec) - \phi(\avec) -
\inner{\nabla\phi(\avec)}{\muvec - \avec} = D_\phi(\muvec, \avec).
$$

Expanding both divergences, the terms in $\phi(\yvec)$ cancel, and $\expect[\yvec - \muvec] = \zeros$ removes
$\inner{\nabla\phi(\muvec)}{\yvec - \muvec}$, leaving the right-hand side. So the mean is the unique minimiser of expected
Bregman divergence, for every strictly convex $\phi$. Banerjee, Guo and Wang proved the converse under mild conditions:
if the mean minimises the expected loss for every distribution, the loss is a Bregman divergence
<Cite id="banerjee2005b" />. The same identity is why k-means generalises to any Bregman divergence with the centroid
still the cluster mean <Cite id="banerjee2005" />.

For classification the outcome is the one-hot vector $\evec_y$, its conditional mean is the class distribution, and
$S(\qvec, y) = D_{-H}(\evec_y, \qvec) + H(\evec_y)$. Applying the identity conditionally on the model output $\svec$,
with $\cvec = \expect[\evec_y \mid \svec]$, gives $\expect[D(\evec_y, \svec)] = \expect[D(\cvec, \svec)] +
\expect[D(\evec_y, \cvec)]$: the calibration-refinement split of <NoteLink to="scoring-rule-decompositions">proper
scoring rule decompositions</NoteLink>.

## Worked example

Take $\pvec = (0.7, 0.3)$ and $\qvec = (0.5, 0.5)$.

- Shannon entropy: $\KL(\pvec \,\Vert\, \qvec) = 0.7\log 1.4 + 0.3\log 0.6 = 0.2355 - 0.1532 = 0.0823$ nats. This is
  the excess expected log loss of reporting $\qvec$.
- Brier entropy: $\norm{\pvec - \qvec}^2 = 0.2^2 + 0.2^2 = 0.08$, the excess expected Brier score.

A binary label with $P(y = 1) = 0.3$ has mean $0.3$. The expected squared error $\expect[(y - a)^2]$ and the expected
log loss $-\expect[y\log a + (1 - y)\log(1 - a)]$ are both minimised at $a = 0.3$, the mean, as the identity predicts.

## Pitfalls

- **Argument order matters.** In the scoring-rule form the outcome goes first and the forecast second,
  $D(\evec_y, \qvec)$. Reversing the arguments gives a different loss; for KL, $\KL(\qvec \,\Vert\, \evec_y)$ is infinite
  unless the forecast is certain.
- **Only proper rules have this form.** A loss that is not a Bregman divergence of the outcome, such as absolute error
  of probabilities, is minimised by the median or a mode, not the mean, and does not elicit probabilities.
