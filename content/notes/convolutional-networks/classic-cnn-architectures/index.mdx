## Architecture

All four networks follow one pattern. Convolutional layers extract local features. Downsampling reduces the spatial size
and enlarges the receptive field. The number of channels grows as the resolution falls. A classifier maps the final
features to class scores.

**LeNet-5** <Cite id="lecun1998b" /> reads a $32 \times 32$ digit. Two stages of $5 \times 5$ convolution and
subsampling produce 16 maps of $5 \times 5$. A $5 \times 5$ convolution to 120 units, a dense layer of 84 units and 10
radial-basis output units follow. Its subsampling layers average $2 \times 2$ windows and apply a trainable scale and
bias. LeNet-5 established the template of alternating convolution and pooling trained end to end by backpropagation.

**AlexNet** <Cite id="krizhevsky2012" /> scaled the template to ImageNet. The first layer applies 96 kernels of
$11 \times 11 \times 3$ with stride 4. Four more convolutional layers and three dense layers of 4096, 4096 and 1000
units follow. The network was split across two GPUs with 3 GB of memory each.

**VGG-16** <Cite id="simonyan2015" /> uses only $3 \times 3$ convolutions with stride 1 and padding 1, in five stages
of 64, 128, 256, 512 and 512 channels. Each stage ends with a $2 \times 2$ max pool. Three dense layers classify. Its
uniformity made it a standard feature extractor.

**ResNet** <Cite id="he2016" /> adds an identity shortcut around every pair or triple of layers. A $7 \times 7$
stride-2 convolution and a $3 \times 3$ max pool reduce a $224$-pixel input to $56 \times 56$. Four stages of
bottleneck blocks follow, each stage halving the resolution with a stride-2 convolution. Global average pooling replaces
the large dense layers, so ResNet-152 has fewer parameters than VGG-16 despite being nine times deeper. See
<NoteLink to="residual-network">residual network</NoteLink> for why the shortcut matters.

## Training

LeNet-5 was trained on 60,000 MNIST digits. The later three were trained on the 1.2 million images of the ImageNet
ILSVRC training set with stochastic gradient descent with momentum and weight decay.

AlexNet introduced most of the methods that made large networks trainable on this data <Cite id="krizhevsky2012" />:

- **ReLU.** $\max(0, z)$ does not saturate for positive inputs. The paper reports six times fewer iterations than tanh
  to reach 25% training error on CIFAR-10 with a four-layer network.
- **Dropout** in the first two dense layers, which hold most of the 60 million parameters.
- **Data augmentation** with random $224 \times 224$ crops of $256 \times 256$ images, horizontal flips and colour
  perturbation.
- **GPU training.** Convolutions ran on two GPUs for five to six days.

VGG initialised its deeper configurations from the weights of a trained shallower one. ResNet used batch normalisation
after every convolution and trained all depths from random initialisation.

## What is distinctive

**Stacks of small kernels.** VGG replaced large kernels with stacks of $3 \times 3$ ones. Three $3 \times 3$ layers with
stride 1 have a receptive field of $1 + 3 \cdot 2 = 7$ pixels, the same as one $7 \times 7$ layer. With $C$ channels in
and out, the stack has $3 \cdot 9C^2 = 27C^2$ weights and the single layer has $49C^2$, which is 81% more
<Cite id="simonyan2015" />. The stack also applies three nonlinearities instead of one, so it can represent more
functions of the same region.

**Depth through shortcuts.** Plain networks deeper than about 20 layers trained worse, not better. Residual connections
removed that limit and took ImageNet networks from 19 to 152 layers.

**From dense to global pooling.** In VGG-16, the first dense layer alone maps $7 \cdot 7 \cdot 512 = 25{,}088$ inputs
to 4096 outputs, about 103 million weights, three quarters of the model. ResNet's global average pooling and single
1000-way dense layer remove almost all of that.

## Worked example: AlexNet's first layer

The output width of a convolution is $\lfloor (n + 2p - k)/s \rfloor + 1$. AlexNet's first layer has $k = 11$, $s = 4$
and $p = 0$. For the stated $224$-pixel input, $(224 - 11)/4 + 1 = 54.25$, which is not an integer. The paper's
$55 \times 55$ output requires $n = 227$: $(227 - 11)/4 + 1 = 55$. Reimplementations use a $227$-pixel input or pad
the $224$-pixel image. The layer has $11 \cdot 11 \cdot 3 \cdot 96 + 96 = 34{,}944$ parameters and produces
$55 \cdot 55 \cdot 96 = 290{,}400$ values.

## Comparison and later designs

Accuracy on ImageNet improved with depth, and the cost per unit of accuracy fell. The four networks form a line of
descent. Later designs changed specific parts of the template:

- **Inception (GoogLeNet)** runs $1 \times 1$, $3 \times 3$ and $5 \times 5$ convolutions in parallel within each block
  and concatenates them <Cite id="szegedy2015" />.
- **DenseNet** concatenates each layer's output to the inputs of all later layers in a block instead of adding
  <Cite id="huang2017" />.
- **EfficientNet** scales depth, width and input resolution together by a fixed ratio <Cite id="tan2019" />.
- **Vision transformers** split an image into $16 \times 16$ patches and replace convolutions with self-attention
  <Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />.
- **ConvNeXt** updates a ResNet with design choices borrowed from vision transformers and matches their accuracy with
  convolutions alone <Cite id="liu2022" />.
