<Definition>
For the linear system $\xvec_{k+1} = \Amat\xvec_k + \Bmat\uvec_k$ with $\xvec_k \in \reals^n$ and
$\uvec_k \in \reals^m$, the **linear-quadratic regulator** (LQR) chooses the inputs that minimise

$$
J = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\paren{\xvec_k^\top\Qmat\xvec_k + \uvec_k^\top\Rmat\uvec_k} + \xvec_N^\top\Qmat_f\xvec_N,
$$

with $\Qmat, \Qmat_f \succeq 0$ weighting state deviations and $\Rmat \succ 0$ weighting control effort. The optimal
input is linear <NoteLink to="pole-placement">state feedback</NoteLink> $\uvec_k = -\Kmat_k\xvec_k$, with gains computed from a backward **Riccati recursion**.
When $(\Amat, \Bmat)$ is stabilisable and $(\Amat, \Qmat^{1/2})$ is detectable, the gain becomes constant as
$N \to \infty$ <Cite id="kalman1960b" />.
</Definition>

## Why a quadratic cost

Pole placement asks the designer to choose closed-loop eigenvalues, and it gives no guidance about which are good. The
LQR asks instead for weights that express what matters: <NoteLink to="chernoff-bounds">large deviations</NoteLink> of some states are costly, large inputs are
costly. The <NoteLink to="positive-definite-matrices">quadratic form</NoteLink> has three advantages. It penalises large errors more than proportionally. It makes the
problem exactly solvable, with a linear controller. And the resulting loop has guaranteed robustness margins, which
arbitrary pole placement does not.

## Derivation by dynamic programming

Define the **cost-to-go** $V_k(\xvec)$ as the smallest cost that can still be incurred from time $k$ onwards,
starting at state $\xvec$. At the final time nothing remains to choose: $V_N(\xvec) = \xvec^\top\Qmat_f\xvec$. The
**principle of optimality** <Cite id="bellman1957" /> says an optimal sequence of inputs, cut at time $k$, is still
optimal from there on. So the cost-to-go at time $k$ is the best one-step cost plus the cost-to-go from wherever that
step leads:

$$
V_k(\xvec) = \min_{\uvec}\brack{\xvec^\top\Qmat\xvec + \uvec^\top\Rmat\uvec + V_{k+1}(\Amat\xvec + \Bmat\uvec)}.
$$

**Claim: every $V_k$ is quadratic,** $V_k(\xvec) = \xvec^\top\Pmat_k\xvec$ with $\Pmat_k \succeq 0$. It holds at
$k = N$ with $\Pmat_N = \Qmat_f$. Suppose it holds at $k + 1$ and write $\Pmat = \Pmat_{k+1}$. The expression to
minimise is

$$
\xvec^\top\Qmat\xvec + \uvec^\top\Rmat\uvec + (\Amat\xvec + \Bmat\uvec)^\top\Pmat(\Amat\xvec + \Bmat\uvec),
$$

a quadratic in $\uvec$ with <NoteLink to="hessian">Hessian</NoteLink> $2(\Rmat + \Bmat^\top\Pmat\Bmat) \succ 0$. It is strictly convex, so its unique
minimiser is where the <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> vanishes, $2\Rmat\uvec + 2\Bmat^\top\Pmat(\Amat\xvec + \Bmat\uvec) = \zeros$:

$$
\uvec^* = -\Kmat_k\xvec, \qquad \Kmat_k = \paren{\Rmat + \Bmat^\top\Pmat\Bmat}^{-1}\Bmat^\top\Pmat\Amat.
$$

Substituting back, with $\uvec^* = -\Kmat\xvec$, the minimum is
$\xvec^\top\brack{\Qmat + \Kmat^\top\Rmat\Kmat + (\Amat - \Bmat\Kmat)^\top\Pmat(\Amat - \Bmat\Kmat)}\xvec$. Expanding the
last product and grouping $\Kmat^\top(\Rmat + \Bmat^\top\Pmat\Bmat)\Kmat = \Amat^\top\Pmat\Bmat\Kmat$ (from the
definition of $\Kmat$) leaves

$$
\Pmat_k = \Qmat + \Amat^\top\Pmat_{k+1}\Amat
- \Amat^\top\Pmat_{k+1}\Bmat\paren{\Rmat + \Bmat^\top\Pmat_{k+1}\Bmat}^{-1}\Bmat^\top\Pmat_{k+1}\Amat.
$$

This is the **discrete-time Riccati recursion**. It runs backwards from $\Pmat_N = \Qmat_f$. $\Pmat_k$ is positive
semidefinite because it is a minimum of nonnegative costs. The claim follows by induction, and so does the form of the
optimal controller: linear state feedback with a gain that depends on time but not on the state. The minimal cost
from $\xvec_0$ is $\xvec_0^\top\Pmat_0\xvec_0$.

The whole solution is computed offline. The recursion never looks at the state, only at $\Amat$, $\Bmat$, $\Qmat$ and
$\Rmat$.

## The infinite horizon

As $N \to \infty$, the recursion, run backwards, converges to a fixed point $\Pmat$ that satisfies the **discrete
algebraic Riccati equation** (DARE)

$$
\Pmat = \Qmat + \Amat^\top\Pmat\Amat - \Amat^\top\Pmat\Bmat\paren{\Rmat + \Bmat^\top\Pmat\Bmat}^{-1}\Bmat^\top\Pmat\Amat,
$$

provided $(\Amat, \Bmat)$ is stabilisable and $(\Amat, \Qmat^{1/2})$ is detectable. Then $\Kmat$ is constant and the
closed loop $\Amat - \Bmat\Kmat$ is stable <Cite id="anderson2007" />. Stability follows from using the cost-to-go as a
<NoteLink to="lyapunov-stability">Lyapunov function</NoteLink>. With $V(\xvec) = \xvec^\top\Pmat\xvec$ and the
optimal input, the <NoteLink to="bellman-equations">Bellman equation</NoteLink> at the fixed point says

$$
V(\xvec_{k+1}) - V(\xvec_k) = -\paren{\xvec_k^\top\Qmat\xvec_k + \uvec_k^\top\Rmat\uvec_k} \le 0.
$$

The cost-to-go never increases, and detectability rules out a state that moves forever while the stage cost stays
zero.

In continuous time, for $\dot{\xvec} = \Amat\xvec + \Bmat\uvec$ and $J = \int_0^\infty(\xvec^\top\Qmat\xvec +
\uvec^\top\Rmat\uvec)\,dt$, the same argument in the limit of small time steps (the
<NoteLink to="hamilton-jacobi-bellman-equation">Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation</NoteLink>) gives
$\Kmat = \Rmat^{-1}\Bmat^\top\Pmat$ with $\Pmat$ solving the **continuous algebraic Riccati equation** (CARE)

$$
\Amat^\top\Pmat + \Pmat\Amat - \Pmat\Bmat\Rmat^{-1}\Bmat^\top\Pmat + \Qmat = \zeros.
$$

## Worked example

Take the scalar system $x_{k+1} = x_k + u_k$ with $Q = R = 1$. The DARE is
$p = 1 + p - p^2/(1 + p)$, so $p^2 = 1 + p$ and $p = (1 + \sqrt5)/2 = 1.618$, the golden ratio $\varphi$. The gain is
$k = p/(1 + p) = 1/\varphi = 0.618$ and the closed loop is $x_{k+1} = (1 - 0.618)x_k = 0.382\,x_k$. The cost from
$x_0 = 1$ is $1.618$: the first step costs $1 + 0.618^2 = 1.382$ and the remaining steps $0.382^2 \times 1.618 = 0.236$.

The same numbers appear in the steady-state <NoteLink to="kalman-filter">Kalman filter</NoteLink> for a <NoteLink to="random-walk">random walk</NoteLink>
with unit process and measurement noise. The filter's Riccati recursion is this one with
$\Amat$ replaced by $\Amat^\top$, $\Bmat$ by $\Cmat^\top$, $\Qmat$ and $\Rmat$ by the noise covariances, and time
reversed. Estimation and control are **dual** problems <Cite id="kalman1960b" />.

## Guaranteed margins

Continuous-time LQR with a single input has guaranteed robustness margins. Write $L(s) = \Kmat(s\eye - \Amat)^{-1}\Bmat$ for
the <NoteLink to="transfer-function-and-block-diagrams">loop transfer function</NoteLink> obtained by breaking the loop at the input. Then

$$
\abs{1 + L(i\omega)} \ge 1 \quad\text{for all } \omega,
$$

so the Nyquist curve of $L$ stays outside the unit disc centred at $-1$ <Cite id="kalman1964" />. Geometrically this
forces a <NoteLink to="bode-plots-and-stability-margins">gain margin</NoteLink> from $1/2$ to $\infty$ and a phase margin of at least $60°$. With several inputs and a diagonal
$\Rmat$ the same margins hold in each input channel <Cite id="safonov1977" />.

<Derivation title="The return-difference inequality">
Write $\Phimat(s) = (s\eye - \Amat)^{-1}$ and use $\Rmat\Kmat = \Bmat^\top\Pmat$. Add and subtract $s\Pmat$ in the
CARE to write $-\Amat^\top\Pmat - \Pmat\Amat = (-s\eye - \Amat^\top)\Pmat + \Pmat(s\eye - \Amat)$, so

$$
(-s\eye - \Amat^\top)\Pmat + \Pmat(s\eye - \Amat) + \Kmat^\top\Rmat\Kmat = \Qmat.
$$

Multiply on the left by $\Bmat^\top\Phimat(-s)^\top$ and on the right by $\Phimat(s)\Bmat$:

$$
\Rmat\Kmat\Phimat(s)\Bmat + \paren{\Kmat\Phimat(-s)\Bmat}^\top\Rmat
+ \paren{\Kmat\Phimat(-s)\Bmat}^\top\Rmat\,\Kmat\Phimat(s)\Bmat = \Bmat^\top\Phimat(-s)^\top\Qmat\Phimat(s)\Bmat.
$$

Adding $\Rmat$ to both sides factors the left side:

$$
\paren{\eye + \Kmat\Phimat(-s)\Bmat}^\top\Rmat\paren{\eye + \Kmat\Phimat(s)\Bmat}
= \Rmat + \Bmat^\top\Phimat(-s)^\top\Qmat\Phimat(s)\Bmat.
$$

At $s = i\omega$ with one input, $\Phimat(-i\omega)$ is the complex conjugate of $\Phimat(i\omega)$, so this reads
$r\abs{1 + L(i\omega)}^2 = r + \norm{\Qmat^{1/2}\Phimat(i\omega)\Bmat}^2 \ge r$.
</Derivation>

The margins belong to state feedback with the true state. With an observer or a Kalman filter in the loop they can
vanish entirely, which is the subject of <NoteLink to="linear-quadratic-gaussian-control">linear-quadratic-Gaussian (LQG) control</NoteLink>.

## Assumptions

- The model is linear and known, the full state is measured, and there are no constraints on inputs or states.
  Constraints are handled by <NoteLink to="model-predictive-control">model predictive control</NoteLink>, which
  solves a constrained version of the same problem online.
- $\Rmat \succ 0$. With $\Rmat$ singular, some inputs are free, the minimiser can be unbounded, and the problem
  becomes singular.

## Pitfalls

- **Choosing weights.** The weights are a design language, not physical quantities. A common start is Bryson's rule:
  diagonal entries $1/(\text{largest acceptable value})^2$ for each state and input. The result is then tuned by
  simulation.
- **Only the ratio matters.** Multiplying $\Qmat$ and $\Rmat$ by the same constant scales $\Pmat$ and leaves $\Kmat$
  unchanged. The figure's sliders act through the ratios $q_1/r$ and $q_2/r$.
- **Detectability.** If a state that $\Qmat$ does not penalise is unstable and influences nothing penalised, the
  regulator ignores it, and the DARE has no stabilising solution.
