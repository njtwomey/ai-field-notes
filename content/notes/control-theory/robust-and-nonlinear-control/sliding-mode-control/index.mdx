<Definition>
For a system $x^{(n)} = f(\xvec) + b(\xvec)u + d(t)$ with tracking error $e = x - x_d$, a **sliding surface** is
$s = \paren{\frac{d}{dt} + \lambda}^{n-1}e = 0$ with $\lambda > 0$. **Sliding-mode control** chooses the input so that
the **reaching condition**

$$
\tfrac12\frac{d}{dt}s^2 \le -\eta\abs{s}, \qquad \eta > 0,
$$

holds for every admissible model error and disturbance <Cite id="utkin1977" />. The state then reaches $s = 0$ in
finite time and slides along it, where the error obeys the stable linear equation
$\paren{\frac{d}{dt} + \lambda}^{n-1}e = 0$.
</Definition>

## Problem

Model-based cancellation, as in <NoteLink to="feedback-linearisation">feedback linearisation</NoteLink>, needs an
exact model. Real models have bounded but unknown errors: an uncertain mass, unmodelled friction, a load disturbance.
Sliding-mode control trades smoothness for a guarantee: as long as the uncertainty enters through the same channel as
the input and has a known bound, the closed loop behaves exactly like a chosen first-order system after a finite
reaching time.

## Mechanism

Take $n = 2$, the common case of a mechanical system $\ddot{x} = f(\xvec) + u + d$ with $b = 1$. The model knows an
estimate $\hat{f}$ with $\abs{f - \hat{f} + d} \le F$. Choose

$$
s = \dot{e} + \lambda e.
$$

On $s = 0$, $\dot{e} = -\lambda e$, so the error decays as $e^{-\lambda t}$ and the dynamics on the surface do not
depend on $f$ or $d$ at all. The problem of order 2 has become a problem of order 1: keep $s$ at zero.

Differentiate $s$ along the dynamics: $\dot{s} = \ddot{x} - \ddot{x}_d + \lambda\dot{e} = f + u + d - \ddot{x}_d +
\lambda\dot{e}$. The input that would hold $\dot{s} = 0$ with the nominal model is the **equivalent control**
$u_{\mathrm{eq}} = -\hat{f} + \ddot{x}_d - \lambda\dot{e}$. Add a switching term:

$$
u = u_{\mathrm{eq}} - k\,\sgn(s) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dot{s} = (f - \hat{f} + d) - k\,\sgn(s).
$$

Then

$$
s\dot{s} = s\,(f - \hat{f} + d) - k\abs{s} \le (F - k)\abs{s}.
$$

With $k = F + \eta$ the reaching condition $s\dot{s} \le -\eta\abs{s}$ holds. $V = \tfrac12 s^2$ is a
<NoteLink to="lyapunov-stability">Lyapunov function</NoteLink> for the scalar $s$, and more: since
$\frac{d}{dt}\abs{s} \le -\eta$ away from zero, $\abs{s}$ falls at least at rate $\eta$ and reaches zero no later than
$t_r = \abs{s(0)}/\eta$. Once on the surface, the switching term flips sign every time $s$ strays, and the state is
held there.

## Worked example

A unit mass with an unknown force $\abs{d(t)} \le 1$, $\ddot{x} = u + d$, must be brought to $x = 0$. Take
$\lambda = 2$, $\hat{f} = 0$, $F = 1$ and $\eta = 0.5$, so $k = 1.5$ and

$$
u = -2\dot{x} - 1.5\,\sgn(\dot{x} + 2x).
$$

From $x(0) = 1$, $\dot{x}(0) = 0$: $s(0) = 2$, so the state reaches the surface within $2/0.5 = 4$ s whatever $d$ does.
From then on $\dot{x} = -2x$ and $x$ halves every $0.35$ s. Neither result needed $d$, only its bound. A linear
controller with the same stiffness would leave a steady-state error for a constant $d$.

## Chattering and the boundary layer

The discontinuous $\sgn(s)$ needs an actuator that switches infinitely fast. Real actuators have delays and finite
bandwidth, so the state overshoots the surface, the sign flips, and the input oscillates at high frequency with
amplitude $2k$. This **chattering** wears actuators and excites unmodelled resonances. The standard fix replaces
$\sgn(s)$ by the saturation $\operatorname{sat}(s/\phi)$, linear inside a **boundary layer** $\abs{s} \le \phi$. The
input is then continuous. Outside the layer the reaching argument holds unchanged; inside it the state is confined to
$\abs{s} \le \phi$, which bounds the tracking error by $\phi/\lambda$ in steady state rather than zero
<Cite id="slotine1991" />. Higher-order sliding modes (such as the super-twisting algorithm) keep finite-time
convergence with a continuous input.

## Cost

The law costs the same as a feedback-linearising one plus a sign evaluation. The analysis needs only a bound $F$ on the
uncertainty.

## Trade-offs

- **Robustness against effort.** $k$ must exceed the uncertainty bound. A conservative bound means large switching
  amplitude, more chattering and more actuator wear.
- **Matched uncertainty only.** The guarantee covers disturbances that enter through the input channel. Unmatched
  uncertainty, acting on states the input does not reach directly, changes the dynamics on the surface too.
- **Precision against smoothness.** A thicker boundary layer means less chattering and a larger residual error.
- **Relation to bang-bang control.** The switching law resembles the
  <NoteLink to="pontryagin-maximum-principle">time-optimal</NoteLink> bang-bang solution: both switch on a curve in
  the state space. Time-optimal control switches once; sliding-mode control switches infinitely often to stay on the
  curve.
