## Architecture

**ResNet** stacks <NoteLink to="convolutional-layer">convolutional layers</NoteLink> in residual blocks, $\yvec = \xvec + F(\xvec)$, where $F$ is two or three
convolutions <Cite id="he2016" />. The identity shortcut lets <NoteLink to="gradient">gradients</NoteLink> reach early layers and made networks of more
than 100 layers trainable (see <NoteLink to="residual-network">residual network</NoteLink>). ResNet-50, 101 and 152 use
**bottleneck** blocks: a $1 \times 1$ convolution reduces the channels, a $3 \times 3$ convolution processes them and a
$1 \times 1$ convolution restores them. BiT keeps the ResNet design but replaces <NoteLink to="batch-normalisation">batch normalisation</NoteLink> with group
normalisation and standardised weights, which transfer better from large pre-training runs, and widens every layer
<Cite id="kolesnikov2020" />.

**ViT** cuts a $224 \times 224$ image into $16 \times 16$ patches, giving $N = HW/P^2 = 224^2/16^2 = 196$ patches.
Each flattened patch is mapped linearly to a vector, a <NoteLink to="learned-absolute-position-embeddings">learned position embedding</NoteLink> is added, and a learnable <NoteLink to="vision-transformer">class token</NoteLink>
is prepended. A standard <NoteLink to="transformer">transformer</NoteLink> encoder processes the 197 tokens, and a head
on the class token predicts the label <Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />. The sequence length grows as the inverse square of the patch size, so ViT-H/14, with $14 \times 14$ patches,
processes 256 tokens at $224 \times 224$. The Base and Large configurations are those of <Gloss name="bert" />;
Huge is new.

## Training

**ResNet** was trained from scratch on ImageNet-1k, 1.28 million labelled images in 1,000 classes
<Cite id="he2016" /> <Cite id="russakovsky2015" />.

**The ViT study** pre-trained both families on three datasets of increasing size and fine-tuned each model on ImageNet
<Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />: ImageNet-1k (1.3M images), ImageNet-21k (14M images, 21k classes) and JFT-300M (303M
images, 18k classes). The ResNet baselines were BiT models, trained with the same procedure where possible, so the
comparison isolates the architecture. Compute is reported in TPUv3-core-days: <Gloss name="tpu" /> cores used multiplied by days of
training.

## What is distinctive

**ResNet**'s convolutions build in two assumptions about images. **Locality**: each
output depends on a small neighbourhood of pixels. **Translation equivariance**: shifting the image shifts the feature
map. These hold in every layer, so the network does not have to learn them from data.

**ViT** keeps almost none of this. Only the patch cutting and the position embeddings refer to the two-dimensional
structure of the image; self-attention is global from the first layer, and spatial relations between patches must be
learned <Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />.

## Comparison

**Accuracy depends on the amount of pre-training data.** The ViT paper's central result is that the ranking of the two
families reverses as data grows <Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />. Fine-tuned ImageNet top-1 accuracy (%) at 384×384:

| Pre-trained on | ViT-B/16 | ViT-L/16 |
| -------------- | -------- | -------- |
| ImageNet-1k    | 77.91    | 76.53    |
| ImageNet-21k   | 83.97    | 85.15    |
| JFT-300M       | 84.15    | 87.12    |

Pre-trained on ImageNet-1k alone, the larger ViT-L/16 is worse than ViT-B/16, and both lie below the range of BiT
ResNets. With ImageNet-21k the two ViT sizes are similar. Only with JFT-300M does the larger model pay off. On random
subsets of JFT-300M, ResNets were better with 9M and 30M images, and ViT overtook them with 90M and more.

**Compute.** At equal pre-training compute on JFT-300M, ViT is more accurate. ViT-L/16 trained for 7 epochs reached
86.30% on ImageNet with 783 exaFLOPs; ResNet-152x2 trained for 14 epochs reached 85.56% with 1,126 exaFLOPs
<Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />. The headline models differ more: ViT-L/16 matched or beat BiT-L on all seven benchmarks
in the paper's main table, with 0.68k TPUv3-core-days against 9.9k.

**Inductive bias against data.** Locality and translation equivariance are correct for natural images, so ResNets need
fewer examples to learn good features. ViT must learn them. With enough data it learns them, and its fewer built-in
assumptions stop being a handicap. The ViT authors summarise this as large-scale training trumping inductive bias
<Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />.

**Cost at inference.** A convolution's cost grows linearly with the number of pixels. Self-attention over $N$ patches
costs $O(N^2)$, so halving the patch size multiplies the sequence length by 4 and the attention cost by 16.
