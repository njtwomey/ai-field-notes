## At a glance

All four models generate $256 \times 256$ ImageNet images conditioned on the class label. Lower FID is better; precision
measures how many samples look like real images, recall how much of the real distribution the samples cover.

## Architecture

**VQ-VAE-2** compresses an image into two grids of discrete codes, a coarse top level and a finer bottom level, using a
<NoteLink to="vq-vae">vector-quantised autoencoder</NoteLink>. Autoregressive PixelCNN-style priors, with self-attention,
model the codes; sampling draws codes one at a time and decodes them <Cite id="razavi2019" />.

**BigGAN-deep** is a <NoteLink to="generative-adversarial-network">GAN</NoteLink>: a generator maps noise and a class
embedding to an image in one forward pass, trained against a discriminator. It uses class-conditional batch
normalisation, self-attention and orthogonal regularisation. The **truncation trick** samples the noise from a truncated
normal to trade diversity for fidelity <Cite id="brock2019" />; the scores above use truncation 1.0.

**ADM** ("ablated diffusion model") is a <NoteLink to="denoising-diffusion-probabilistic-models">diffusion
model</NoteLink> whose U-Net predicts the noise in a noisy image. Dhariwal and Nichol improved the U-Net by ablation:
more attention heads, attention at several resolutions, BigGAN-style residual blocks for up- and down-sampling, and
adaptive group normalisation to inject the time step and class <Cite id="dhariwal2021" />. **ADM-G** adds **classifier
guidance**: the gradient of a classifier trained on noisy images, scaled by a factor, is added to the denoising step to
push samples toward the class (see <NoteLink to="diffusion-guidance">diffusion guidance</NoteLink>).

**LDM-4** is a <NoteLink to="latent-diffusion">latent diffusion model</NoteLink>. An autoencoder first compresses the
image by a factor of 4 in each spatial dimension, and the diffusion model runs on the $64 \times 64$ latent instead of
the $256 \times 256$ image <Cite id="rombach2022" />. **LDM-4-G** uses classifier-free guidance
<Cite id="ho2022" />, which needs no separate classifier.

## Training

The four models were trained on the same data, ImageNet at $256 \times 256$ with class labels, and are compared on the
same metrics. Their training costs differ by an order of magnitude. ADM-G's 962 V100-days are 916 for the diffusion model
and 46 for the classifier. LDM-4-G's 271 V100-days were spent on a single A100 for 178K steps with batch size 1,200
<Cite id="rombach2022" />.

## What is distinctive

**BigGAN-deep** samples in one network evaluation, hundreds of times fewer than the diffusion models, and has the highest
precision. Its recall of 0.28 is the lowest of the four: it covers little of the real distribution. This is the
signature of mode dropping, the GAN failure in which the generator ignores parts of the data.

**ADM** beat BigGAN-deep's FID on ImageNet, the benchmark on which GANs had led <Cite id="dhariwal2021" />. Without guidance its FID is 10.94, with recall 0.63. Classifier guidance lowers FID to 4.59
and raises precision from 0.69 to 0.82, at the cost of recall falling to 0.52. With only 25 DDIM steps, ADM-G still
scores FID 5.44, better than BigGAN-deep.

**LDM** moved diffusion into a compressed latent space. At $64 \times 64 \times$ channels instead of
$256 \times 256 \times 3$, each denoising step is cheaper, which cut training compute to 28% of ADM-G's and raised
throughput from 0.07 to 0.4 images per second. The latent-diffusion design, conditioned on text, became Stable Diffusion.

## Comparison

**Fidelity against diversity.** Precision and recall separate what FID combines. The GAN sits at high precision and low
recall. Unguided diffusion (ADM: 0.69, 0.63) sits at the opposite corner. Guidance moves diffusion toward the GAN's corner.
Its scale is a dial: for LDM-4-G, a classifier-free guidance scale of 1.25 gives FID 3.95, precision 0.81 and recall
0.55; a scale of 1.5 gives FID 3.60, precision 0.87 and recall 0.48 <Cite id="rombach2022" />. The best FID lies at an
intermediate trade-off.

**Likelihood-based models.** VQ-VAE-2 and diffusion models are trained by maximising a likelihood or a bound on it,
which penalises assigning zero probability to any real image. Both reach higher recall than BigGAN-deep. VQ-VAE-2's
precision of 0.36 shows that, in 2019, its samples were much less realistic than the GAN's.

**Cost.** At sampling time the GAN is by far the cheapest: one forward pass. Diffusion needs one network evaluation per
denoising step, 250 here. At training time the order reverses: BigGAN-deep's 128–256 V100-days are less than LDM-4-G's
271 and far less than ADM-G's 962, but GAN training is adversarial and can collapse <Cite id="brock2019" />, while the diffusion loss is a
regression target that trains stably.

**Metric caveats.** FID and Inception score use an Inception network trained on ImageNet, so they reward images that
the classifier finds recognisable. Guidance pushes samples toward images the classifier recognises, so part of its gain on these metrics reflects the
metric rather than realism.
