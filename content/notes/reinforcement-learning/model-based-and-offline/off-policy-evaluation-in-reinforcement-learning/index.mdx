<Definition>
Trajectories $\tau = (S_0, A_0, R_1, \dots, S_{T-1}, A_{T-1}, R_T)$ are collected by a **behaviour policy** $\pi_b$,
with the probability $\pi_b(A_t \mid S_t)$ of each action recorded. **Off-policy evaluation** (OPE) estimates the value
of a different **evaluation policy** $\pi_e$,

$$
v(\pi_e) = \expect_{\tau \sim \pi_e}\brack{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t R_{t+1}},
$$

from these trajectories alone <Cite id="precup2000" />. The one-step case $T = 1$ is
<NoteLink to="off-policy-evaluation">off-policy evaluation for contextual bandits</NoteLink>.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Deploying an untested policy in medicine, education or a live product can be costly or unsafe, and an
<NoteLink to="offline-reinforcement-learning">offline RL</NoteLink> method produces candidate policies that must be ranked before any is
tried. OPE answers "what would this policy have earned?" from logs of what another policy did. In the bandit case, one
<NoteLink to="importance-sampling">importance weight</NoteLink> per decision corrects the mismatch. In an <Gloss name="mdp" />, the
evaluation policy would also have visited different states, so the correction must account for every decision along
the way.

## Trajectory importance sampling

The trajectory's probability under a policy is $d_0(S_0)\prod_t\pi(A_t \mid S_t)p(S_{t+1}, R_{t+1} \mid S_t, A_t)$.
The start distribution and the dynamics are the same for both policies and cancel in the ratio, which leaves the
cumulative importance ratio

$$
\rho_{0:t} = \prod_{k=0}^{t}\frac{\pi_e(A_k \mid S_k)}{\pi_b(A_k \mid S_k)}.
$$

Reweighting the whole return, $\hat v_{\text{IS}} = \rho_{0:T-1}\sum_t\gamma^t R_{t+1}$, averaged over trajectories,
is unbiased when $\pi_b(a \mid s) > 0$ wherever $\pi_e(a \mid s) > 0$.

**The curse of horizon.** Each factor has <NoteLink to="expectation">expectation</NoteLink> 1 under $\pi_b$, but the product's variance grows geometrically.
If each factor has second moment $1 + c$ and the factors are independent, then $\expect[\rho_{0:T-1}^2] = (1 + c)^T$.
With $c = 0.1$, a modest mismatch, a 10-step horizon multiplies the second moment by 2.6 and a 50-step horizon by 117.
Long-horizon importance sampling therefore often needs <NoteLink to="variance-reduction">variance reduction</NoteLink>.

## Per-decision importance sampling

A reward cannot depend on actions taken after it. The reward $R_{t+1}$ needs only the ratio of the actions up to
$A_t$ <Cite id="precup2000" />:

$$
\hat v_{\text{PDIS}} = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t\rho_{0:t}R_{t+1}.
$$

**Unbiasedness.** Split the full weight on $R_{t+1}$ as $\rho_{0:T-1} = \rho_{0:t}\,\rho_{t+1:T-1}$. Condition on the
history up to $S_{t+1}$. Each later factor $\pi_e(A_k \mid S_k)/\pi_b(A_k \mid S_k)$ has expectation
$\sum_a\pi_b(a \mid S_k)\,\pi_e(a \mid S_k)/\pi_b(a \mid S_k) = 1$ given the history up to $S_k$, so by iterated
expectations $\expect[\rho_{t+1:T-1} \mid \text{history to } S_{t+1}] = 1$. Hence
$\expect[\rho_{0:T-1}R_{t+1}] = \expect[\rho_{0:t}R_{t+1}]$, and dropping the later factors changes no expectation. It
removes their variance. Early rewards now carry short products, and only late rewards carry the full one.

**Weighted variants.** Dividing by the average weight instead of $n$, as self-normalised importance sampling does for bandits, gives
weighted importance sampling. It is biased but consistent, and has lower variance than the unweighted estimator.

## Doubly robust estimation

Let $\hat Q(s, a)$ be an estimate of $q_{\pi_e}$, for example from fitted Q evaluation (below), and
$\hat V(s) = \sum_a\pi_e(a \mid s)\hat Q(s, a)$. The **doubly robust** estimator of Jiang and Li is defined backwards
along each trajectory, from $\hat v_{\text{DR}}^{(T)} = 0$ <Cite id="jiang2016" />:

$$
\hat v_{\text{DR}}^{(t)} = \hat V(S_t) + \rho_t\paren{R_{t+1} + \gamma\,\hat v_{\text{DR}}^{(t+1)} - \hat Q(S_t, A_t)},
\qquad \rho_t = \frac{\pi_e(A_t \mid S_t)}{\pi_b(A_t \mid S_t)},
$$

and the estimate is $\hat v_{\text{DR}}^{(0)}$. Each step starts from the model's prediction $\hat V(S_t)$ and adds an
importance-weighted correction: the observed reward plus the estimate of the rest, minus what the model predicted for
the action actually taken.

**Unbiased with correct ratios, whatever $\hat Q$.** Suppose, by induction from the end, that
$\hat v_{\text{DR}}^{(t+1)}$ has conditional mean $v_{\pi_e}(S_{t+1})$ given $S_{t+1}$. Given $S_t = s$, averaging the
correction over $A_t \sim \pi_b$ multiplies by $\pi_e/\pi_b$ and so turns it into an average over $A_t \sim \pi_e$:

$$
\expect\brack{\rho_t\paren{R_{t+1} + \gamma\hat v_{\text{DR}}^{(t+1)} - \hat Q(s, A_t)} \,\Big|\, S_t = s}
= \sum_a\pi_e(a \mid s)\paren{q_{\pi_e}(s, a) - \hat Q(s, a)}.
$$

Adding $\hat V(s) = \sum_a\pi_e(a \mid s)\hat Q(s, a)$ cancels the model and leaves
$\sum_a\pi_e(a \mid s)\,q_{\pi_e}(s, a) = v_{\pi_e}(s)$. The induction ends at $t = 0$ with an unbiased estimate.

**Low variance with a good $\hat Q$.** If $\hat Q = q_{\pi_e}$ exactly, each correction term has mean zero given the
past, and the variance that remains comes only from the randomness of rewards and transitions, not of the actions.
Unrolled, the estimator is per-decision importance sampling minus a control variate:

$$
\hat v_{\text{DR}} = \hat v_{\text{PDIS}} - \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t\paren{\rho_{0:t}\hat Q(S_t, A_t) - \rho_{0:t-1}\hat V(S_t)},
$$

with $\rho_{0:-1} = 1$. Each subtracted term has mean zero, by the same one-step calculation, so the subtraction removes
variance without adding bias. Jiang and Li also derive a Cramér–Rao lower bound on the variance of any unbiased OPE
estimator and show that the doubly robust estimator can match it <Cite id="jiang2016" />.

Thomas and Brunskill combine weighted importance sampling with the doubly robust estimator's control variate and
blend it with the model-based estimate to reduce error further <Cite id="thomas2016" />.

## Model-based evaluation

The direct method for sequences estimates $q_{\pi_e}$ without importance weights. **Fitted Q evaluation** regresses
$\hat Q(s, a)$ on targets $r + \gamma\sum_{a'}\pi_e(a' \mid s')\hat Q(s', a')$ over logged transitions, repeatedly, as
in <NoteLink to="temporal-difference-learning">temporal-difference learning</NoteLink> with the evaluation policy's
expectation in the target. It has low variance, but its bias under function approximation does not shrink with more
data, and it is least accurate for the actions $\pi_e$ takes and $\pi_b$ rarely does.

## Worked example

A two-step trajectory has $\gamma = 1$. The behaviour policy chose each action with probability 0.5. The evaluation
policy would have chosen the first logged action with probability 0.9 and the second with 0.2, so $\rho_0 = 1.8$ and
$\rho_1 = 0.4$. The rewards were $R_1 = 1$ and $R_2 = 2$.

- **Trajectory IS:** $\rho_0\rho_1(R_1 + R_2) = 1.8 \cdot 0.4 \cdot 3 = 2.16$.
- **Per-decision IS:** $\rho_0R_1 + \rho_0\rho_1R_2 = 1.8 + 1.44 = 3.24$. The first reward keeps its weight 1.8, instead
  of being scaled down by an action taken after it.
- **Doubly robust**, with $\hat Q(S_0, A_0) = 2.5$, $\hat V(S_0) = 2.6$, $\hat Q(S_1, A_1) = 1.5$ and
  $\hat V(S_1) = 1.8$. Backwards: $\hat v^{(1)} = 1.8 + 0.4\,(2 - 1.5) = 2.0$, then
  $\hat v^{(0)} = 2.6 + 1.8\,(1 + 2.0 - 2.5) = 3.5$.

With one trajectory the three numbers differ widely; averaged over many trajectories, all are unbiased, and their
variances differ.

## Assumptions

- **Coverage.** $\pi_b(a \mid s) > 0$ whenever $\pi_e(a \mid s) > 0$ in states $\pi_e$ can reach.
- **Known behaviour probabilities.** Logged at decision time, or estimated, in which case the estimators inherit the
  estimation error.
- **No unobserved confounding.** $\pi_b$ chose actions using only the recorded state.

## Pitfalls

- **Long horizons.** Importance-sampling estimators degrade exponentially with the horizon. Report the <Gloss name="ess">effective sample
  size</Gloss> of the weights alongside any estimate.
- **Deterministic behaviour policies.** A greedy logging policy gives zero probability to the evaluation policy's
  alternatives; no estimator can recover their value from its logs.
