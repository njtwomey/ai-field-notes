<Definition>
A **deterministic policy** maps each state to one action, $a = \mu_{\thetavec}(s) \in \reals^m$. The **deterministic
policy gradient theorem** states that the gradient of its expected return is

$$
\nabla_{\thetavec}J(\thetavec) = \expect_{s \sim \rho^\mu}\brack{\nabla_{\thetavec}\mu_{\thetavec}(s)\,\nabla_a q_\mu(s, a)\big|_{a = \mu_{\thetavec}(s)}},
$$

where $\rho^\mu$ is the policy's discounted state distribution, $q_\mu$ its action-value function, and
$\nabla_{\thetavec}\mu_{\thetavec}(s)$ the Jacobian of the action with respect to the parameters
<Cite id="silver2014" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

With continuous actions, a critic $Q(s, a)$ is a smooth surface over the action space. Its gradient $\nabla_a Q$ says
in which direction to move the action to increase value. The deterministic policy gradient moves the policy's output in
that direction, through the chain rule into $\thetavec$. No sampling over actions is needed, so the estimate has none of
the action noise that makes <NoteLink to="policy-gradient-theorem">stochastic policy gradients</NoteLink> high in
variance, especially in many action dimensions.

The alternative, maximising $Q(s, a)$ over $a$ at every step as in <NoteLink to="deep-q-network">DQN</NoteLink>, is
an inner optimisation problem per step. The actor $\mu_{\thetavec}$ learns to solve it approximately, once, for all
states.

## Derivation

The stochastic theorem in state form is $\nabla J = \sum_s\eta(s)\sum_a q_\pi(s, a)\nabla_{\thetavec}\pi_{\thetavec}(a \mid s)$,
with $\eta$ the discounted visitation. The gradient acts only on the policy inside the sum over actions, never on the
visitation or on $q_\pi$. The same holds for a deterministic policy, with the sum over actions replaced by evaluation
at $\mu_{\thetavec}(s)$: $\nabla J = \sum_s\eta(s)\,\nabla_{\thetavec}\brack{q_\mu(s, \mu_{\thetavec}(s))}$ with $q_\mu$
held fixed. The chain rule gives the theorem. Silver et al. prove it directly, and show it is the limit of the
stochastic policy gradient as the policy's variance shrinks to zero <Cite id="silver2014" />.

**Off-policy learning.** The theorem integrates over states only. Replacing $\rho^\mu$ by the state distribution of a
different, exploring behaviour policy gives an approximate gradient that needs no importance weights over actions. A
critic learned off-policy by Q-learning-style targets can therefore be combined with replayed data.

## DDPG

**Deep deterministic policy gradient** applies the theorem with neural networks and the stabilising devices of
<NoteLink to="deep-q-network">DQN</NoteLink> <Cite id="lillicrap2016" />:

- a **critic** $Q_{\phivec}(s, a)$ regressed on targets $y = r + \gamma Q_{\phivec^-}(s', \mu_{\thetavec^-}(s'))$, with
  target networks for both actor and critic;
- an **actor** updated by $\nabla_{\thetavec}\frac1B\sum_i Q_{\phivec}(s_i, \mu_{\thetavec}(s_i))$ over a minibatch
  from a replay buffer;
- **soft target updates** $\phivec^- \leftarrow \tau\phivec + (1 - \tau)\phivec^-$ with small $\tau$, such as 0.001;
- **exploration noise** added to the actor's output when acting, since a deterministic policy does not explore.

## TD3

The actor maximises the critic, so it seeks out and exploits the critic's overestimates, and these are fed back
through the targets. **Twin delayed DDPG** makes three changes <Cite id="fujimoto2018" />:

- **Clipped double Q-learning.** Learn two critics and use the smaller of their target estimates,
  $y = r + \gamma\min_{j = 1, 2}Q_{\phivec_j^-}(s', \tilde a)$. The minimum biases targets down rather than up, which is
  safer because underestimated actions are not preferred by the actor and so their errors do not propagate.
- **Target policy smoothing.** Use $\tilde a = \mu_{\thetavec^-}(s') + \epsilon$ with small clipped Gaussian noise
  $\epsilon$, so the target averages the critic over a neighbourhood of the action and narrow spurious peaks are
  smoothed away.
- **Delayed policy updates.** Update the actor and targets once per two critic updates, so the actor follows a critic
  that has had time to settle.

## Soft actor-critic and maximum-entropy RL

**Maximum-entropy RL** adds the policy's <NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink> to the reward, with temperature
$\alpha > 0$:

$$
J(\pi) = \expect_\pi\brack{\sum_t\gamma^t\paren{R_{t+1} + \alpha\,\entropy\brack{\pi(\cdot \mid S_t)}}}.
$$

The agent is rewarded for acting as randomly as possible while still collecting reward. This keeps exploration going
and makes learned behaviour robust to perturbations.

**The optimal policy is a softmax of $Q$.** At one state, choose the action distribution $\pi$ to maximise
$\sum_a\pi(a)Q(a) + \alpha\entropy[\pi]$ subject to $\sum_a\pi(a) = 1$. Setting the derivative of the Lagrangian to
zero, $Q(a) - \alpha(\log\pi(a) + 1) - \lambda = 0$, gives $\pi(a) \propto \exp(Q(a)/\alpha)$. The maximum value is
$\alpha\log\sum_a\exp(Q(a)/\alpha)$, a smooth maximum that tends to $\max_a Q(a)$ as $\alpha \to 0$; see
<NoteLink to="softmax-and-log-sum-exp">softmax and log-sum-exp</NoteLink>. The **soft Bellman equations** follow:

$$
q(s, a) = r(s, a) + \gamma\,\expect_{s'}\brack{v(s')}, \qquad v(s) = \expect_{a \sim \pi}\brack{q(s, a) - \alpha\log\pi(a \mid s)}.
$$

**Soft actor-critic** (SAC) learns two critics by these equations, with the clipped double-Q minimum of TD3, and trains
a stochastic Gaussian actor to minimise $\KL(\pi_{\thetavec}(\cdot \mid s) \,\|\, \exp(Q(s, \cdot)/\alpha)/Z(s))$. The
actor's sampled actions are written as $a = \tanh(\mu_{\thetavec}(s) + \sigma_{\thetavec}(s)\odot\epsilon)$ with
$\epsilon \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \eye)$, so the gradient flows through the sample by the
<NoteLink to="reparameterisation-trick">reparameterisation trick</NoteLink> <Cite id="haarnoja2018" />. SAC is
off-policy, uses a replay buffer, and is less sensitive to hyperparameters than DDPG.

## Worked example

**Deterministic gradient.** A one-dimensional problem has critic $Q(s, a) = 5 - (a - 2)^2$ at some state, and the
actor outputs $a = \mu_\theta(s) = \theta$ with $\theta = 0.5$. Then $\nabla_a Q = -2(a - 2) = 3$ at $a = 0.5$ and
$\nabla_\theta\mu = 1$, so the gradient is 3. A step of size 0.1 moves $\theta$ to 0.8, towards the critic's peak at 2.

**Soft policy.** Three actions have $Q = (1, 2, 3)$. With $\alpha = 1$ the optimal soft policy is
$\exp(Q)/\sum\exp(Q) = (0.090, 0.245, 0.665)$ and the soft value is $\log(e + e^2 + e^3) = 3.41$: the best action's
value 3 plus an entropy bonus. With $\alpha = 0.5$ the policy sharpens to $(0.016, 0.117, 0.867)$ and the soft value
falls to 3.07.

## Pitfalls

- **Critic exploitation.** The actor follows $\nabla_a Q$ wherever the critic is wrong, which is why TD3's changes
  matter more than they appear.
- **Exploration noise scale.** DDPG's performance depends heavily on the noise added to actions; SAC's learned variance
  avoids this choice.
- **Bounded actions.** A $\tanh$ squashing changes the density of the actions; SAC corrects the log-probability with
  the Jacobian of the squashing.
