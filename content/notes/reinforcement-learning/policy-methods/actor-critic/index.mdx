<Definition>
An **actor-critic** method maintains two learned functions. The **actor** is a policy $\pi_{\thetavec}(a \mid s)$,
updated along a policy-gradient estimate. The **critic** is a <Gloss name="value-function">value function</Gloss>
$V_{\wvec}(s)$, learned by <NoteLink to="temporal-difference-learning">temporal-difference learning</NoteLink> and used
to estimate the **advantage** $A_\pi(s, a) = q_\pi(s, a) - v_\pi(s)$ of each action taken. The actor update is

$$
\thetavec \leftarrow \thetavec + \alpha_{\thetavec}\,\hat A_t\,\nabla_{\thetavec}\log\pi_{\thetavec}(A_t \mid S_t),
$$

where $\hat A_t$ is the critic's advantage estimate <Cite id="sutton2018" />.
</Definition>

## Intuition

The <NoteLink to="policy-gradient-theorem">policy gradient theorem</NoteLink> weights each action's score by how good
the action was. <Gloss name="reinforce" /> measures that with the full return, which is unbiased and noisy, and must
wait for the episode to end. A critic answers the same question after one step: the action was good if the reward plus
the value of where it led beats the value of where it started. The actor proposes, the critic judges, and each improves
the other. The arrangement is generalised <NoteLink to="policy-iteration">policy iteration</NoteLink> with a parametric
policy.

## The TD error estimates the advantage

With a critic equal to the true value, $V = v_\pi$, the one-step TD error is
$\delta_t = R_{t+1} + \gamma v_\pi(S_{t+1}) - v_\pi(S_t)$. Its conditional mean given the state and action is

$$
\expect\brack{\delta_t \mid S_t = s, A_t = a} = \expect\brack{R_{t+1} + \gamma v_\pi(S_{t+1}) \mid s, a} - v_\pi(s) = q_\pi(s, a) - v_\pi(s),
$$

by the <NoteLink to="bellman-equations">Bellman equation</NoteLink> for $q_\pi$. So $\delta_t$ is an unbiased sample of
the advantage, and replacing $G_t - b(S_t)$ by $\delta_t$ in the policy gradient changes its variance but not its mean.
With an imperfect critic, $\delta_t$ is biased by the critic's errors.

**One-step actor-critic.** After each transition:

1. compute $\delta_t = R_{t+1} + \gamma V_{\wvec}(S_{t+1}) - V_{\wvec}(S_t)$, with $V = 0$ at terminal states;
2. update the critic, $\wvec \leftarrow \wvec + \alpha_{\wvec}\,\delta_t\nabla_{\wvec}V_{\wvec}(S_t)$;
3. update the actor,
   $\thetavec \leftarrow \thetavec + \alpha_{\thetavec}\,\delta_t\nabla_{\thetavec}\log\pi_{\thetavec}(A_t \mid S_t)$.

The critic usually learns faster than the actor, so that the actor sees a critic adapted to its current policy.

## Generalised advantage estimation

Between the one-step TD error and the Monte Carlo advantage lie the **$n$-step advantage estimates**

$$
\hat A_t^{(n)} = R_{t+1} + \gamma R_{t+2} + \dots + \gamma^{n-1}R_{t+n} + \gamma^n V(S_{t+n}) - V(S_t).
$$

Each is a sum of TD errors. Writing $\delta_{t+l} = R_{t+l+1} + \gamma V(S_{t+l+1}) - V(S_{t+l})$ and summing with
weights $\gamma^l$, the intermediate values cancel in pairs:

$$
\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\gamma^l\delta_{t+l} = \sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\gamma^l R_{t+l+1} + \gamma^n V(S_{t+n}) - V(S_t) = \hat A_t^{(n)}.
$$

Small $n$ relies on the critic and has low variance and high bias; large $n$ relies on sampled rewards and has the
opposite trade-off. **Generalised advantage estimation** (GAE) averages all of them with geometric weights, as the
$\lambda$-return does <Cite id="schulman2016" />:

$$
\hat A_t^{\text{GAE}(\gamma, \lambda)} = (1 - \lambda)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-1}\hat A_t^{(n)}
= (1 - \lambda)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-1}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\gamma^l\delta_{t+l}.
$$

Exchange the sums. The TD error $\delta_{t+l}$ appears in every $\hat A_t^{(n)}$ with $n \ge l + 1$, so its total weight
is
$(1 - \lambda)\gamma^l\sum_{n \ge l+1}\lambda^{n-1} = (1 - \lambda)\gamma^l\lambda^l/(1 - \lambda) = (\gamma\lambda)^l$.
Hence

$$
\hat A_t^{\text{GAE}(\gamma, \lambda)} = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\lambda)^l\,\delta_{t+l}.
$$

$\lambda = 0$ gives the one-step TD error $\delta_t$. $\lambda = 1$ gives $\sum_l\gamma^l\delta_{t+l} = G_t - V(S_t)$,
the Monte Carlo advantage with a value baseline. Values of $\lambda$ between about 0.92 and 0.99 worked best in the
experiments that introduced GAE <Cite id="schulman2016" />.

The estimates for a whole trajectory are computed backwards in one pass with the recursion
$\hat A_t = \delta_t + \gamma\lambda\hat A_{t+1}$, which is how
<NoteLink to="trust-region-and-proximal-policy-optimisation">proximal policy optimisation (PPO)</NoteLink>
implementations compute them.

## A2C and A3C

**A3C** (asynchronous advantage actor-critic) runs many actors in parallel threads, each on its own copy of the
environment. Each collects a few steps, computes $n$-step advantages with a shared critic, and applies its
<NoteLink to="gradient">gradients</NoteLink> asynchronously to shared parameters <Cite id="mnih2016" />. Parallel actors
decorrelate the data, which replaces the replay buffer of a <NoteLink to="deep-q-network">DQN</NoteLink> and keeps
learning on-policy. **A2C** is the synchronous version: wait for all actors, then take one batched gradient step. It
uses a <Gloss name="gpu" /> more efficiently.

Both share one network body between actor and critic, with a policy head and a value head, and minimise

$$
L = -\hat A_t\log\pi_{\thetavec}(A_t \mid S_t) + c_v\paren{\hat G_t - V(S_t)}^2 - c_e\,\entropy\brack{\pi_{\thetavec}(\cdot \mid S_t)}.
$$

The <NoteLink to="entropy">entropy</NoteLink> term, weighted by $c_e$, penalises premature collapse onto a single action
and keeps exploration going <Cite id="mnih2016" />.

## Worked example

A three-step episode has rewards $R_1 = 1$, $R_2 = 0$, $R_3 = 2$ and ends in a terminal state. The critic's values
along the way are $V(S_0) = 1.0$, $V(S_1) = 1.5$, $V(S_2) = 0.5$ and $V(S_3) = 0$. Take $\gamma = 0.9$ and
$\lambda = 0.8$, so $\gamma\lambda = 0.72$.

- TD errors: $\delta_0 = 1 + 0.9 \cdot 1.5 - 1 = 1.35$, $\delta_1 = 0 + 0.9 \cdot 0.5 - 1.5 = -1.05$,
  $\delta_2 = 2 + 0 - 0.5 = 1.5$.
- GAE backwards: $\hat A_2 = 1.5$, $\hat A_1 = -1.05 + 0.72 \cdot 1.5 = 0.03$,
  $\hat A_0 = 1.35 + 0.72 \cdot 0.03 = 1.372$.
- For comparison at $t = 0$: $\lambda = 0$ gives $1.35$, and $\lambda = 1$ gives
  $1.35 + 0.9 \cdot (-1.05) + 0.81 \cdot 1.5 = 1.62$, which equals $G_0 - V(S_0) = 1 + 0.81 \cdot 2 - 1$.

At $t = 1$ the critic expected 1.5 and the step itself disappointed ($\delta_1 < 0$), but the reward that followed made
up for it, and the GAE estimate is close to zero.

## Assumptions

- **A critic close to $v_\pi$.** The advantage estimate is biased by the critic's error, less so for $\lambda$ near 1.
- **On-policy data.** The advantage refers to the current policy. Off-policy actor-critic methods correct for the
  mismatch with importance weights or, as in <NoteLink to="deterministic-policy-gradients">DDPG and SAC</NoteLink>,
  use a critic of action values.

## Pitfalls

- **Unnormalised advantages.** Their scale changes as rewards change. Normalising them to zero mean and unit variance
  within a batch keeps the actor's step size stable.
- **Sharing a body without balancing the losses.** The value loss can dominate the policy loss in a shared network;
  $c_v$ must be tuned.
