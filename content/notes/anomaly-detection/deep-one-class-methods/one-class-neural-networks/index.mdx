<Definition>
A one-class neural network, introduced by Chalapathy, Menon and Chawla <Cite id="chalapathy2018" />, replaces the
kernel feature map of the <NoteLink to="one-class-support-vector-machine">one-class support vector machine</NoteLink> with a <NoteLink to="multilayer-perceptron">hidden layer</NoteLink>
$g(\Vmat\xvec)$, where $\Vmat$ is a weight matrix and $g$ a linear or sigmoid activation applied elementwise. It learns
$\Vmat$, an output weight vector $\wvec$ and an offset $r$ by minimising the one-class SVM objective directly. An input is
normal when $\hat y(\xvec) = \inner{\wvec}{g(\Vmat\xvec)} \ge r$ and anomalous otherwise.
</Definition>

## Intuition

The one-class SVM separates the data from the origin of a fixed feature space with the widest possible margin. Its
kernel decides which features exist. For images or text no fixed kernel describes the right features. OC-NN keeps the
objective and lets the features be learned, so the representation is shaped by the one-class task rather than by
reconstruction or by an unrelated pretraining task <Cite id="chalapathy2018" />.

The price is convexity. The kernel one-class SVM solves a quadratic programme with a unique solution. OC-NN is a neural
network trained by <NoteLink to="gradient-descent">gradient descent</NoteLink> and finds a local optimum.

## Objective

For training inputs $\xvec_1, \dots, \xvec_N$ and $\nu \in (0, 1]$, OC-NN solves <Cite id="chalapathy2018" />

$$
\min_{\wvec,\, \Vmat,\, r}\ \frac{1}{2}\norm{\wvec}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\norm{\Vmat}_F^2
+ \frac{1}{\nu N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} \max\paren{0,\ r - \hat y_n} - r,
\qquad \hat y_n = \inner{\wvec}{g(\Vmat\xvec_n)} .
$$

Compare the primal one-class SVM, with feature map $\phi$, offset $\rho$ and slacks $\xi_n$:
$\frac{1}{2}\norm{\wvec}^2 + \frac{1}{\nu N}\sum_n \xi_n - \rho$ subject to $\inner{\wvec}{\phi(\xvec_n)} \ge \rho - \xi_n$
and $\xi_n \ge 0$. At the optimum each slack equals $\max(0, \rho - \inner{\wvec}{\phi(\xvec_n)})$, so the two
objectives match term for term with $\phi(\xvec) = g(\Vmat\xvec)$, $\rho = r$, and one new term,
$\frac{1}{2}\norm{\Vmat}_F^2$, which is <Gloss name="weight-decay">weight decay</Gloss> on the feature map. The kernel SVM works in the dual because $\phi$
is implicit. OC-NN has an explicit $\phi$ and works in the primal.

In the experiments of Chalapathy and colleagues, the input to OC-NN is the code of a pretrained autoencoder, and a
single hidden layer with a linear activation worked best <Cite id="chalapathy2018" />.

## Alternating minimisation and the ν-quantile

OC-NN alternates two steps <Cite id="chalapathy2018" />:

1. With $r$ fixed, update $\wvec$ and $\Vmat$ by backpropagation on the objective.
2. With $\wvec$ and $\Vmat$ fixed, set $r$ to its optimal value.

The second step has a closed form. As a function of $r$ alone the objective is

$$
F(r) = \frac{1}{\nu N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} \max(0, r - \hat y_n) - r.
$$

$F$ is convex and piecewise linear. Raising $r$ by a small amount raises the sum at rate $1/(\nu N)$ for every $n$ with
$\hat y_n < r$ and lowers $-r$ at rate $1$, so the right <NoteLink to="derivative">derivative</NoteLink> is $n_{\le}(r)/(\nu N) - 1$ with
$n_{\le}(r) = \abs{\set{n : \hat y_n \le r}}$, and the left derivative is $n_{<}(r)/(\nu N) - 1$ with
$n_{<}(r) = \abs{\set{n : \hat y_n < r}}$. At the minimum the left derivative is at most zero and the right derivative at
least zero:

$$
\frac{n_{<}(r^*)}{N} \le \nu \le \frac{n_{\le}(r^*)}{N}.
$$

$r^*$ is the $\nu$-quantile of the outputs $\hat y_1, \dots, \hat y_N$. The same inequalities are the ν-property: at most
a fraction $\nu$ of training points is classed anomalous, $\hat y_n < r^*$, and at least a fraction $\nu$ lies on or
below the boundary.

## Worked example

Take nine outputs $\hat y = 1, 2, \dots, 9$ and $\nu = 0.33$, so $\nu N = 2.97$. Then
$F(r) = \frac{1}{2.97}\sum_n \max(0, r - \hat y_n) - r$:

| $r$    | 2        | 3        | 4        | 5        |
| ------ | -------- | -------- | -------- | -------- |
| $F(r)$ | $-1.663$ | $-1.990$ | $-1.980$ | $-1.633$ |

The minimum is at $r^* = 3$. Two outputs lie below it, $2 \le 2.97$, and three lie at or below it, $3 \ge 2.97$, as the
quantile condition requires. The points with outputs 1 and 2 are the training anomalies, and the decision score
$\hat y - r^*$ is negative for exactly those two. This example is the one Chalapathy and colleagues give
<Cite id="chalapathy2018" />.

## Collapse in the margin objective

<Gloss name="deep-svdd" /> can collapse to a constant map. OC-NN has the analogous weakness, because the objective rewards features
that are large on every input, not features that tell inputs apart.

Fix the features $\gvec_n = g(\Vmat\xvec_n)$ and consider the case with no slack, where every $\hat y_n \ge r$. Write
$\wvec = t\uvec$ with $\norm{\uvec} = 1$ and $t \ge 0$. The largest feasible offset is $r = t\,m(\uvec)$, with
$m(\uvec) = \min_n \inner{\uvec}{\gvec_n}$, the distance of the separating hyperplane from the origin. The objective in
$t$ is $\frac{1}{2}t^2 - t\,m(\uvec)$, minimised at $t = m(\uvec)$ with value $-\frac{1}{2}m(\uvec)^2$. The best feature
map is the one that maximises the margin $m(\uvec)$: every feature vector should lie far from the origin in a common
direction.

With a sigmoid activation, every feature vector lies in $(0, 1)^K$ for $K$ hidden units, so
$m(\uvec) \le \norm{\gvec_n} < \sqrt{K}$. The bound is approached by saturating every hidden unit on every input,
$\gvec_n \approx \ones$, which is a constant map. Only the weight decay $\frac{1}{2}\norm{\Vmat}_F^2$ stops the network
from reaching it, since saturation needs large weights. This is the bounded-activation route to collapse that Ruff and
colleagues describe for Deep SVDD <Cite id="ruff2018" />. A review by Ruff and colleagues notes that minimum-volume and
maximum-margin deep objectives alike admit such constant solutions without regularisation <Cite id="ruff2021" />.
Feeding OC-NN a frozen, pretrained code and training only a shallow head limits how far the features can drift.

## Assumptions

- **A fixed input representation that already carries the right information.** OC-NN refines features; it does not
  learn them from raw data on its own.
- **Mostly normal training data**, with $\nu$ at least the contamination, as for the one-class SVM.

## Pitfalls

- **ν is a training quantile.** As in the kernel case, it bounds the fraction of training points outside, not the
  false-alarm rate on new data.
- **Non-convex training.** Different initialisations give different boundaries. The alternation between <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> steps
  and the quantile update can oscillate if the gradient steps are large.
- **Collapse pressure.** Monitor the spread of $\hat y$ on held-out normal data. Outputs that all approach $r$ indicate
  features that no longer depend on the input.
