<Definition>
Censoring occurs when an observation exists in a dataset but its exact value is known only to lie beyond an upper bound
(right-censoring), below a lower bound (left-censoring), or within an interval. Truncation occurs when observations
falling outside a defined boundary are entirely excluded from the sampling frame. Coarsening represents the
deterministic loss of precision through rounding, digit heaping, or aggregation into categorical brackets.
</Definition>

Machine learning models frequently ingest duration, financial, and physical measurements that have suffered systematic
boundary distortions <Cite id="tobin1958, kaplan1958" />. Treating censored boundary limits as exact point values
severely underestimates event horizons and biases regression slopes, while discarding censored observations discards
long-surviving entities and injects survivorship bias.

## Detection limits and top-coding

Laboratory assays, physical sensors, and public census bureaus enforce hard mathematical thresholds:

1. **Limit of Detection (LOD):** In chemical, biological, and environmental measurements (e.g., blood viral loads,
   groundwater contaminants), physical sensors lose precision below a threshold $\tau_{\text{LOD}}$. Instruments output
   string flags such as `< 0.05 mg/L`. Naively replacing these entries with $0$, $\tau_{\text{LOD}}$, or
   $\tau_{\text{LOD}} / 2$ creates an artificial spike at the substitution value, distorting distributional variance.
2. **Top-Coding (Ceiling Effects):** In socioeconomic microdata, statistical agencies truncate high continuous values to
   preserve respondent anonymity. In the US Current Population Survey, annual incomes exceeding 150,000 USD are
   collapsed to a single constant. Standard OLS regression treating top-coded limits as literal numbers underestimates
   income inequality and attenuates education returns.

## Censoring

Censoring arises naturally whenever an observation window terminates before all events have materialized
<Cite id="kaplan1958" />:

- **Right-Censoring:** The genuine event time $T^*$ satisfies $T^* > C$, where $C$ is the censoring time (e.g., a
  subscription customer remains active when a study concludes on day 90). The observed record is the pair:

$$
Y = \min(T^*, C), \quad \delta = \mathbb{I}(T^* \le C)
$$

- **Left-Censoring:** The event occurred at some unrecorded point prior to observation initiation ($T^* \le C_0$, e.g.,
  an infection acquired before first clinic admission).
- **Independent vs Informative Censoring:** Standard estimators assume *independent (non-informative) censoring*: the
  censoring mechanism provides no information about the likelihood of event occurrence ($T^* \perp C \mid X$). If sick
  patients drop out of a clinical drug trial because their condition worsens, censoring is informative, and naive
  survival curves dramatically overstate drug efficacy.

In regression modeling, James Tobin formulated the **Tobit model** <Cite id="tobin1958" />, maximizing a piecewise
log-likelihood that models uncensored points with probability density $f(y_i)$ and censored points with cumulative
survival probability $1 - F(C_i)$:

$$
\log L = \sum_{i: \delta_i=1} \log f(y_i \mid x_i) + \sum_{i: \delta_i=0} \log \left(1 - F(C_i \mid x_i)\right)
$$

## Truncation

While censoring retains the entity record with a bounded label, **truncation** completely purges units from the dataset:

- **Left-Truncation (Delayed Entry):** Units enter the observation pool only if they have already survived up to a
  qualification time $L$. In retirement studies, individuals who die before age 65 never appear in the sampling frame.
- **Right-Truncation:** Only units whose events occurred prior to an observation cutoff are recorded. In epidemiology,
  observing patient hospital stays during an ongoing epidemic observes only short stays; long ongoing hospitalizations
  have not yet been discharged and are omitted.

Analyzing truncated data requires conditioning the likelihood on the probability of entering the sample:
$f(y \mid Y \ge L) = \frac{f(y)}{1 - F(L)}$. Ignoring truncation overestimates mortality or underestimates duration.

## Rounding and heaping

In empirical datasets, continuous quantities are coarsened into discrete steps:

- **Digit Preference and Heaping:** When survey respondents report age, income, or cigarette consumption, human memory
  defaults to multiples of 5, 10, or 20 (heaping). Examining empirical age histograms in developing nations reveals
  massive artificial spikes at ages 30, 35, 40, and 45.
- **Timestamp Coarsening:** High-frequency clickstream timestamps recorded in milliseconds on mobile devices might be
  aggregated to hourly or daily buckets in enterprise data warehouses. Discretization destroys temporal ordering between
  fast sequential actions (e.g., viewing an ad before clicking vs clicking before viewing).
- **Coarsening at Random (CAR):** Heitjan and Rubin established that coarsened data can be analyzed without explicitly
  modeling the coarsening mechanism if the coarsening probability is conditionally independent of the true latent value
  given the observed bracket <Cite id="heitjan1991" />.

## Interval-censored values

In periodic surveillance (e.g., annual dental exams, quarterly medical checkups, quarterly financial reporting), the
exact time of an event $T^*$ is never observed directly. Instead, analysts know only that an event occurred between two
discrete inspection timestamps $(L_i, R_i]$:

$$
P(L_i < T^* \le R_i) = F(R_i) - F(L_i)
$$

When intervals are wide, imputing the midpoint $t_i = (L_i + R_i) / 2$ severely understates timing uncertainty and can
induce false step-function artifacts in survival hazards. The non-parametric Turnbull estimator generalizes the
Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimator to arbitrary interval-censored data via Expectation-Maximization.

## Pitfalls

- **Discarding Censored Rows:** Filtering out right-censored observations (e.g., training a customer churn model only on
  users who have already churned) discards the most loyal, long-lived customers. The model learns that every customer
  eventually churns rapidly.
- **Imputing Censoring Limits as Targets:** Training an XGBoost regressor where right-censored subscriptions are
  assigned target value $y = C$. The model treats the artificial study duration $C$ as true tenure, creating severe
  under-prediction for high-value cohorts.
- **Confusing Truncation with Censoring:** Applying Kaplan-Meier survival curves to left-truncated data without
  delayed-entry risk-set adjustments falsely credits treatments with early survival benefits.
- **Heaping Artifacts in Anomaly Detection:** Anomaly detection algorithms that flag rare numerical values can mistake
  the natural absence of non-rounded numbers (e.g., reporting 37 instead of 35 or 40) as anomalous fraudulent activity.
