<Definition>
**Measurement and recording** is the step between a quantity in the world and the value stored in a dataset. An
instrument, a form or a piece of logging code produces the value. Each adds error, a bound, a rounding or a choice of
what to record. A model learns from the recorded value, not from the quantity, and inherits every such distortion.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A dataset column labelled "income", "blood pressure" or "clicks" is a record made by some process. The process can
add noise, as a scale does. It can shift values, as when two sites use different devices. It can cap values at a
detection limit, round them to the nearest five, or omit them past a threshold. It can record a convenient surrogate
instead of the quantity of interest, such as health-care cost in place of health need <Cite id="obermeyer2019" />. Each
of these has a known effect on what a model estimates, and most can be corrected once the mechanism is stated.

## Main ideas

**Random error attenuates; systematic error biases.** Classical measurement error adds zero-mean noise to a predictor.
A regression on the noisy predictor shrinks its coefficient towards zero by the reliability ratio
<Cite id="carroll2006" />. A systematic error, such as an instrument that reads high, shifts the estimate instead.
<NoteLink to="measurement-error-and-proxies" /> derives the attenuation and treats differences between instruments.

**A proxy target answers a different question.** When the target of interest cannot be observed, a related one is
used in its place. The model then predicts the proxy well and the target only as far as the two agree. Construct
validity asks whether a measurement captures the concept it is named after.

**Censoring, truncation and coarsening lose information in known patterns.** A censored value is known only to exceed or
fall below a bound. A truncated value never enters the data at all. A coarsened value is
rounded or grouped. <NoteLink to="censoring-and-coarsening" /> covers detection limits, top-coding, digit heaping and
interval-censored values, and the likelihoods that account for each <Cite id="heitjan1991" />.

**Logs record decisions made by code.** <NoteLink to="instrumentation-and-logging" /> treats logged data as the output
of a program. Only instrumented events appear. Event time and processing time differ. A new SDK release or a changed
event definition can look like a change in user behaviour <Cite id="kleppmann2017" />. Features are best logged as they
were served, so that training sees what the model saw.
