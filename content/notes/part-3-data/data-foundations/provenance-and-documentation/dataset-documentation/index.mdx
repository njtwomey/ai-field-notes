<Definition>
Dataset documentation is the structured metadata, provenance records, and legal declarations that accompany a machine
learning dataset throughout its lifecycle. Modeled after component datasheets in hardware engineering, dataset
documentation articulates why the data was created, who funded it, how instances were sampled and annotated, what
demographic and operational boundaries exist, and under which legal licenses it may be processed.
</Definition>

In the early decades of machine learning, datasets were frequently treated as interchangeable, neutral numeric
commodities downloaded from public FTP servers without attribution or provenance <Cite id="gebru2021, bender2018" />.
However, undocumented datasets introduce severe liabilities: silent distribution mismatch, copyright infringement,
privacy violations, and toxic feedback loops. Structured documentation transforms an informal data dump into an
auditable, legally defensible engineering asset.

## Datasheets for datasets

Timnit Gebru and colleagues proposed **Datasheets for Datasets** as a standard operating procedure to foster
transparency and accountability across machine learning <Cite id="gebru2021" />. A standard datasheet is organized
around seven lifecycle sections:

1. **Motivation:** For what purpose was the dataset created? Who created and funded the dataset?
2. **Composition:** What do the instances represent (e.g., documents, photos, audio clips)? How many instances are
   included? Are there subpopulation labels, confidential records, or protected demographic attributes?
3. **Collection Process:** What mechanisms were used to collect data (e.g., API scrapers, sensors, crowd-workers)? Were
   participants notified or consented? What time window does the data cover?
4. **Preprocessing, Cleaning and Labeling:** Was raw data filtered, de-identified, tokenized, or normalized? What
   guidelines were provided to annotators, and what was the measured inter-annotator agreement?
5. **Uses:** Has the dataset been used for previous tasks? What tasks should the dataset *not* be used for (e.g.,
   surveillance, high-stakes medical diagnosis without clinical trial validation)?
6. **Distribution:** How is the dataset distributed (public URL, academic gated repository)? Does an export control or
   digital rights management regime apply?
7. **Maintenance:** Who maintains the dataset? Is there an errata schedule, contact address, or mechanism for data
   subjects to request deletion under GDPR Article 17?

## Data cards and data statements

Complementary frameworks target specific subfields and operational interfaces <Cite id="bender2018, mitchell2019" />:

- **Data Statements for NLP:** Emily Bender and Batya Friedman formulated *Data Statements* specifically for language
  datasets <Cite id="bender2018" />. They mandate reporting linguistic variety (dialect, BCP-47 tag), speaker
  demographics (native status, age, gender, geographic origin), annotator demographics, speech situation (synchronous
  chat vs scripted reading), and text genre.
- **Data Cards:** Google's Data Cards framework operationalizes datasheets for enterprise production pipelines,
  incorporating automated lineage graphs, schema evolution tracking, and model card hooks <Cite id="mitchell2019" />.
- **Croissant Metadata Format:** The MLCommons Croissant specification embeds machine-readable JSON-LD schemas into
  dataset distributions, enabling automated dataset loaders (Hugging Face Datasets, TensorFlow Datasets) to verify
  licensing, splits, and record structures programmatically.

## Provenance and lineage

Data provenance records the immutable chronological audit trail tracking every transformation applied to raw source
bits:

$$
\text{Raw Sensor / Web Feed} \xrightarrow{\text{Extract}} \text{Bronze Lake} \xrightarrow{\text{Anonymize / Filter}} \text{Silver Delta} \xrightarrow{\text{Feature Store}} \text{Training Matrix} \xrightarrow{\text{Fit}} \text{Model Checkpoint}
$$

Modern MLOps architectures enforce provenance tracking via:
- **Content-Addressable Hashing:** Storing datasets identified by SHA-256 hashes of immutable artifacts (via Git LFS,
  DVC, or Content-Addressable Storage).
- **Data Lineage Graphs:** Utilizing systems like OpenLineage, Marquez, and Delta Lake commit logs to log parent-child
  DAG relations between input source partitions and trained model weights. If an upstream data provider issues a
  deletion request or discovers label poisoning, downstream impacted models can be deterministically identified and
  retrained.

## Consent and personal data

Datasets containing human behavior, voice, biometric imagery, or personal telemetry must navigate complex global privacy
regulations (GDPR in Europe, CCPA/CPRA in California, HIPAA in healthcare):

- **Informed Consent:** Terms of service agreements rarely constitute valid ethical consent for downstream machine
  learning training. Consent documentation must specify whether data subjects were explicitly informed that their
  contributions would be used to train commercial generative or predictive AI models.
- **De-identification and Re-identification Risk:** Stating that a dataset is "anonymized" is insufficient.
  Documentation must specify whether direct identifiers were purged, quasi-identifiers were perturbed ($k$-anonymity,
  $l$-diversity), or formal differential privacy mechanisms were applied.
- **Right to Erasure (Unlearning Protocols):** Clear pathways must be documented detailing how individuals can revoke
  consent and ensure their data points are expunged from subsequent training iterations.

## Licences

Machine learning datasets inherit legal restrictions from their constituent source materials:

| License Class | Examples | Commercial Use | Derivative Works | Key Obligations & Constraints |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Permissive Open Source** | MIT, Apache 2.0, BSD-3-Clause | Allowed | Allowed | Attribution required; Apache provides patent grant. |
| **Permissive Content** | CC-BY 4.0 | Allowed | Allowed | Strict attribution required; no commercial restriction. |
| **Public Domain** | CC0, Public Domain Dedication | Allowed | Allowed | Complete waiver of copyright claims worldwide. |
| **Non-Commercial** | CC-BY-NC 4.0 | Prohibited | Restricted | Training models for revenue-generating services breaches license. |
| **Share-Alike / Copyleft** | CC-BY-SA, GNU GPL | Conditional | Must Share-Alike | Trained model weights may be legally classified as derivative works. |
| **Proprietary / Terms of Service** | Scraped Web APIs (Reddit, X) | Conditional | Heavily Restricted | Commercial training frequently violates platform terms of service. |

Ingesting non-commercial (NC) or copyleft datasets into proprietary enterprise model training pipelines creates massive
legal vulnerability, exposing organizations to copyright infringement lawsuits and court-ordered model destruction
(algorithmic disgorgement).

## Versioning

Datasets are living assets. Correcting typos, purging poisoned samples, or appending newly arriving quarters of data
alters the training distribution.
- **Semantic Versioning for Data:** Incrementing major versions ($\text{v}2.0.0$) for breaking schema alterations or
  label ontology revisions; minor versions ($\text{v}1.1.0$) for added rows/partitions; patch versions ($\text{v}1.0.1$)
  for metadata or typo corrections.
- **Immutable Release Snapshots:** Never overwrite an existing dataset release under a mutable tag. Every model
  evaluation benchmark must link to an immutable content hash or frozen version tag (`imagenet-1k@v2012`).

## Deprecated and withdrawn datasets

Several prominent academic datasets have been formally withdrawn due to severe ethical and privacy violations:
- **DukeMTMC:** Withdrawn after revelations that students and pedestrians were filmed on campus without knowledge or
  consent for surveillance research.
- **TinyImages (80 Million Images):** Formally withdrawn by MIT in 2020 after discovery of racist, misogynistic, and
  derogatory automated labeling.
- **MS-Celeb-1M:** Deleted by Microsoft due to scraping of facial imagery without consent.

When a dataset is deprecated or retracted, dataset documentation registries must issue formal "tombstone" notices and
revocation digests, preventing automated pipelines from downloading cached local mirrors.

## Pitfalls

- **License Laundering:** Re-hosting scraped copyrighted text or images under a permissive Creative Commons license on
  public hubs (Hugging Face, Kaggle). Downstream developers who rely on the hub's stated license remain legally liable
  for underlying copyright infringement.
- **Documentation Rot:** Writing a comprehensive datasheet at dataset creation and never updating it as maintenance
  handoffs occur, data sources drift, or user takedown requests accumulate.
- **Ambiguous Derivative Status:** Assuming that because model weights are matrices of floating-point numbers, they are
  immune to copyright claims originating from the training data. Recent legal precedent and regulatory orders
  increasingly treat models trained on unlicensed data as infringing derivative works.
- **Ignoring Annotator Compensation:** Failing to document annotator hourly pay, geographic distribution, and working
  conditions. Datasets labeled by severely underpaid crowd-workers exhibit high label variance, subjective shortcuts,
  and unmonitored label noise.
