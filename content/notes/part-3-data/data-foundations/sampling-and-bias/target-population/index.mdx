<Definition>
The target population is the complete universe of real-world entities, environments, and operating conditions that an
artificial intelligence model is intended to serve when deployed in production. The accessible (or study) population is
the constrained subset of entities that could practically be identified, recruited, or recorded during dataset
construction.
</Definition>

Machine learning models routinely achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on internal cross-validation benchmarks while
failing catastrophically upon production deployment <Cite id="pearl2014transportability, sugiyama2007iwcv" />. This
divergence reflects a breakdown between *internal validity* (statistical soundness within the observed study sample) and
*external validity* (the degree to which causal and predictive relationships transfer across the gap between the
accessible population and the target population).

## Accessible and target populations

Every dataset is a convenience sample drawn from an accessible sub-population rather than the true target population:

1. **The Target Population ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{target}}$):** Defined by business requirements and operating scopes. For
   example:
   - *Clinical Diagnostic Tool:* All patients presenting with acute respiratory symptoms across all community hospitals
     in the national healthcare system over the next decade.
   - *Autonomous Driving Agent:* All weather conditions, lighting variations, geographic jurisdictions, and roadway
     geometries the vehicle will encounter in commercial fleet service.
2. **The Accessible Population ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{accessible}}$):** The entities reachable through existing
   instrumentation and administrative channels:
   - Academic medical centers with digital EHR infrastructures (excluding rural clinics and under-resourced hospitals).
   - High-contrast, sunny daytime test drives in Phoenix, Arizona (excluding blizzards in Chicago).
3. **The Target-Sample Gap:** When $\mathcal{P}_{\text{accessible}} \subset \mathcal{P}_{\text{target}}$, the training
   data suffers from *undercoverage*. If the probability distribution over covariates differs
   ($P_{\text{accessible}}(X) \neq P_{\text{target}}(X)$) or conditional relationships shift
   ($P_{\text{accessible}}(Y \mid X) \neq P_{\text{target}}(Y \mid X)$), empirical risk minimization on the accessible
   sample optimizes the wrong objective function.

## Inclusion and exclusion criteria

To ensure clean labels and experimental tractability, dataset curators establish explicit inclusion and exclusion rules:

- **Inclusion Criteria:** Baseline attributes mandatory for cohort eligibility (e.g., adult patients aged $\ge 18$,
  users with at least 5 completed transactions, images with minimum resolution $\ge 1080\text{p}$).
- **Exclusion Criteria:** Disqualifying attributes removed to eliminate confounding noise (e.g., patients with severe
  multi-morbidity, transactions flagged by heuristic risk rules, audio recordings with background street noise).

While filtering cleans training data, it silently shrinks the accessible population. If an exclusion criterion removes
the messy, complex tail cases that the deployed system will encounter in daily operations, the model learns a brittle
mapping optimized exclusively for sterile laboratory inputs.

## External validity

External validity evaluates whether the model's predictive guarantees *transport* to the target environment
<Cite id="pearl2014transportability" />:

1. **Covariate Shift (Sampling Selection):** The conditional predictive mechanism remains invariant
   ($P_{\text{target}}(Y \mid X) = P_{\text{source}}(Y \mid X)$), but the feature density shifts
   ($P_{\text{target}}(X) \neq P_{\text{source}}(X)$).
   - *Transport Solution:* Importance weighting using density ratios:

$$
w(x) = \frac{P_{\text{target}}(x)}{P_{\text{source}}(x)}
$$

Training risk is reweighted by $w(x_i)$, aligning the training gradient with target population expectations
<Cite id="sugiyama2007iwcv, kanamori2009ulsif" />. 2. **Positivity Violation (Lack of Common Support):** If there exist
regions in the target population where the source density is zero ($P_{\text{target}}(x) > 0$ while
$P_{\text{source}}(x) = 0$), importance weights diverge ($w(x) \to \infty$). In these unobserved regions, the model must
extrapolate blindly; external validity cannot be mathematically guaranteed. 3. **Concept Shift (Mechanism Violation):**
The underlying causal or statistical relationship differs between populations
($P_{\text{target}}(Y \mid X) \neq P_{\text{source}}(Y \mid X)$). For example, a credit risk model trained in an
economic boom does not transfer to an economic recession because the relationship between debt-to-income ratio and
default probability changes fundamentally.

## Stating the population before training

To prevent premature deployment and overconfident extrapolation, modern machine learning engineering mandates explicit
population specifications prior to dataset ingestion:

- **Datasheets and Model Cards:** Formally documenting target demographic boundaries, supported geographic
  jurisdictions, sensor hardware versions, and environmental operating ranges (e.g., "Validated exclusively on adult
  non-ICU patients aged 18–65 in tertiary urban facilities").
- **Target Distribution Assertions:** Embedding schema checks that compare real-time inference feature distributions
  against target population baselines using Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) or Population Stability Index (PSI).
- **Out-of-Scope Fallbacks:** Equipping models with conformal prediction sets or out-of-distribution detectors that
  trigger graceful rejection when an incoming query falls outside the validated target population boundary.

## Pitfalls

- **The WEIRD Demographic Trap:** A substantial portion of behavioral, NLP, and computer vision benchmark datasets are
  collected from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) undergraduate cohorts or US-centric web
  crawlers. Assuming these benchmarks represent global human linguistic or visual norms leads to severe degradation
  across international deployments.
- **Survivorship in Retrospective Cohorts:** In healthcare and churn datasets, defining cohorts based on criteria that
  require subjects to survive a 1-year follow-up window (immortal time bias) excludes the most vulnerable cases,
  creating false impressions of model efficacy.
- **Silent Temporal Drift:** Treating temporal validation splits as representative of the indefinite future. A target
  population defined in 2024 is non-stationary; consumer preferences, macro-economic conditions, and language
  conventions drift continuously.
- **Conflating High Accuracy with External Validity:** Reporting 99% Area Under the ROC Curve (AUROC) on an artificially
  balanced, heavily pre-filtered benchmark gives zero guarantee of viability when deployed on unfiltered real-world
  streams where positive class prevalence is 0.01% and input noise is high.
