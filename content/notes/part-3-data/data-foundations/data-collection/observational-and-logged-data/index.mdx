<Definition>
Experimental data is generated through active randomized assignment of treatments, ensuring that interventions are
independent of unobserved confounders. Observational (or found) data records empirical states and human choices as they
naturally transpired in the wild, subject to unmeasured confounding. Logged data records algorithmic actions selected by
an automated software policy along with user responses, providing bandit feedback where action propensities are known
but counterfactual outcomes remain unobserved.
</Definition>

Machine learning practitioners routinely train models indiscriminately across experimental AB test logs, passive web
scrapes, and historical recommendation records <Cite id="pearl2009, bottou2013" />. However, the statistical guarantees
that govern predictive inference diverge fundamentally from those governing causal intervention. While passive
prediction tolerates observational correlation, deciding how an autonomous agent should act requires understanding the
data-generating mechanism through randomized trials or counterfactual off-policy evaluation <Cite id="dudik2011" />.

## Randomised data

Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs) and online A/B experiments are the gold standard for causal discovery:

- **Random Assignment:** A treatment indicator $A \in \{0, 1\}$ is assigned via physical or algorithmic randomization
  (e.g., hash modulo on `user_id`):

$$
A \perp (Y(1), Y(0), X)
$$

Because treatment is independent of potential outcomes and baseline covariates $X$, confounding paths are severed.
- **Unbiased Average Treatment Effect (ATE):** The causal impact $\tau$ is directly estimated by the difference in
  sample means without requiring parametric adjustment:

$$
\tau = \mathbb{E}[Y(1) - Y(0)] = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid A=1] - \mathbb{E}[Y \mid A=0]
$$

Randomization guarantees internal validity, eliminating selection bias, collider stratification, and confounding.

## Found data

Found data consists of historical exhaust recorded without experimental intervention: medical health records, social
media feeds, criminal justice registries, and checkout scans.

In found data, actors choose their own treatments:
- Sicker patients take more intensive medications.
- Wealthier consumers buy premium subscriptions.
- Cautious drivers install vehicle telematics monitors.

Because treatment choice $A$ is confounded by latent variables $U$ ($A \leftarrow U \to Y$), naive predictive models
confound the treatment effect with baseline selection:

$$
\mathbb{E}[Y \mid A=1] - \mathbb{E}[Y \mid A=0] = \tau + \underbrace{\left(\mathbb{E}[Y(0) \mid A=1] - \mathbb{E}[Y(0) \mid A=0]\right)}_{\text{Selection Bias}}
$$

Estimating causal effects from found data requires strict, untestable structural assumptions:
1. **Conditional Ignorability (Unconfoundedness):** All common causes are observed: $(Y(1), Y(0)) \perp A \mid X$.
2. **Positivity (Overlap):** Every unit has a non-zero probability of receiving either treatment:
   $0 < P(A=1 \mid X=x) < 1$ for all $x$.

## Logged decisions and bandit feedback

Logged data represents a distinct hybrid regime central to modern search engines, ad platforms, and recommender systems
<Cite id="bottou2013, dudik2011" />:
- An automated logging policy $\pi_0$ observed context $x$, selected an action $a \sim \pi_0(a \mid x)$, and recorded an
  outcome $y$.
- Unlike completely found data, the probability distribution that produced the action—the **propensity score**
  $p_i = \pi_0(a_i \mid x_i)$—is explicitly known or can be retrieved from logging telemetry.
- Unlike full feedback in supervised learning, the system receives **partial (bandit) feedback**: it observes the reward
  for the action that was executed, but receives zero signal regarding what *would* have happened under alternative
  candidate actions.

To evaluate a new candidate policy $\pi$ without deploying it live, platforms use **Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE)**:
- **Inverse Propensity Scoring (IPS):**

$$
\hat{V}_{\text{IPS}}(\pi) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\pi(a_i \mid x_i)}{\pi_0(a_i \mid x_i)} y_i
$$

- **Doubly Robust (DR) Estimator:** Combines a parametric direct model $\hat{q}(x, a)$ with propensity weighting to
  achieve variance reduction while preserving asymptotic unbiasedness <Cite id="dudik2011" />:

$$
\hat{V}_{\text{DR}}(\pi) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left( \hat{q}(x_i, \pi(x_i)) + \frac{\mathbb{I}(a_i = \pi(x_i))}{\pi_0(a_i \mid x_i)} \left(y_i - \hat{q}(x_i, a_i)\right) \right)
$$

## Prediction versus intervention

A model trained on observational data learns conditional statistical expectations:

$$
f(x) \approx \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X=x]
$$

A model tasked with decision-making requires interventional distributions:

$$
f(x) \approx \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \operatorname{do}(X=x)]
$$

The difference between passive observation and active intervention is profound <Cite id="pearl2009" />:
1. **The Barometer Fallacy:** A regression model predicts storm arrival from falling barometer pressure with high
   accuracy ($P(\text{Storm} \mid \text{Low Barometer}) \approx 0.9$). But physically intervening on the barometer
   needle ($\operatorname{do}(\text{Barometer} = \text{High})$) does not alter atmospheric weather. A model deployed to
   control weather by adjusting the needle causes catastrophic failure.
2. **The ICU Mortality Paradox:** In observational medical records, patients receiving oxygen ventilators have higher
   mortality rates than patients not receiving ventilators
   ($P(\text{Death} \mid \text{Ventilator}=1) > P(\text{Death} \mid \text{Ventilator}=0)$). A pure predictive model
   concludes that ventilators kill patients, recommending that doctors withhold ventilation from acute respiratory
   patients.

Deploying predictive models in interventional settings without causal debiasing converts passive correlation into active
harm.

## Pitfalls

- **Deterministic Logging Policies:** If the production recommender policy $\pi_0$ is purely deterministic
  ($p \in \{0, 1\}$), overlap is completely violated: $\pi_0(a \mid x) = 0$ for all unselected items. Offline
  counterfactual evaluation becomes mathematically impossible. Production rankers must deliberately inject
  $\epsilon$-exploration or softmax randomization.
- **Extreme Propensity Variance:** When the logging policy rarely selects a specific action
  ($\pi_0(a \mid x) = 0.0001$), the IPS weight $1 / \pi_0 \to 10{,}000$, causing massive estimator variance that
  destabilizes policy gradient updates.
- **Feedback Loops from Self-Fulfilling Logs:** Training a fraud model on historical fraud flags: transactions that were
  blocked produce no downstream chargeback labels. The model is retrained solely on approved transactions, blinding it
  to emerging fraud attack vectors.
- **Conflating Association with Causation in Feature Selection:** Removing features because they have zero linear
  correlation with the label, ignoring that their true causal effect is masked by an opposing confounder.
