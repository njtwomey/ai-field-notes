<Definition>
A machine learning feedback loop occurs when predictions, ranking slates, or automated decisions executed by an active
production model alter human behavior or environmental state, thereby shaping the data distribution captured by
subsequent logging pipelines. When the model is iteratively retrained on this influenced telemetry, its initial
inductive biases and statistical errors compound, creating self-fulfilling prophecies.
</Definition>

Standard supervised learning operates under an implicit assumption of passive observation: the data distribution
$\mathcal{D}$ is an immutable ground truth, and the model's predictions exert zero causal influence on reality
<Cite id="bottou2013" />. In production systems—from social media recommenders to predictive policing and automated
underwriting—this assumption is false. Models actively alter the world they observe, inducing performative distribution
shift that entangles model parameters with training distributions <Cite id="perdomo2020, ensign2018" />.

## Models that shape their own data

Feedback mechanisms arise across three primary pathways:

1. **Exposure and Attention Gating:** In search engines, news feeds, and e-commerce catalogs, users can interact only
   with items the model decides to display in visible viewport slots. Items ranked at position 1 receive massive
   interaction volume, while items ranked at position 50 starve for exposure regardless of their intrinsic quality.
2. **Action-Contingent Observation (Selective Gatekeeping):** In credit lending, fraud prevention, and border security,
   outcomes materialize only for entities granted approval. The model never learns whether a rejected applicant would
   have repaid their loan or whether a blocked transaction was benign.
3. **Strategic User Adaptation (Goodhart's Law):** Human actors learn the model's scoring rubrics and alter their
   behavior or feature profiles to game the system (e.g., job candidates keyword-stuffing resumes, or content creators
   optimizing thumbnail clickbait to manipulate recommender algorithms).

## Performative prediction

Perdomo and colleagues formalized the mathematics of models that manipulate their own data as **Performative
Prediction** <Cite id="perdomo2020" />.

In classical empirical risk minimization, the distribution $\mathcal{D}$ is static:

$$
\theta^* = \arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{(X, Y) \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \ell(f_\theta(X), Y) \right]
$$

In performative prediction, deploying model parameters $\theta$ induces a new data distribution $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$:

$$
\mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{D}(\theta)
$$

This dynamic defines two distinct solution concepts:
- **Performative Stability:** A model $\theta_{\text{stable}}$ is performatively stable if it minimizes empirical risk
  over the exact distribution it itself induces:

$$
\theta_{\text{stable}} = \arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{(X, Y) \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_{\text{stable}})} \left[ \ell(f_\theta(X), Y) \right]
$$

Repeated retraining ($\theta_{t+1} = \arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}(\theta_t)} [\ell(\theta)]$) converges to
performative stability under Lipschitz smoothness conditions.
- **Performative Optimality:** A model $\theta_{\text{opt}}$ that minimizes true global risk across all possible
  deployable distributions:

$$
\theta_{\text{opt}} = \arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{(X, Y) \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta)} \left[ \ell(f_\theta(X), Y) \right]
$$

A performatively stable model is rarely performatively optimal: standard retraining can lock an organization into a
highly stable but severely suboptimal local equilibrium.

## Degenerate loops

When feedback loops are allowed to operate unconstrained without exploratory intervention, systems develop runaway
pathologies <Cite id="ensign2018" />:

1. **Predictive Policing Runaways:** Ensign et al. proved that predictive policing algorithms (such as PredPol) suffer
   from catastrophic runaway feedback loops <Cite id="ensign2018" />. If an algorithm initially predicts slightly higher
   crime in Neighborhood A, police dispatch sends more patrols to A. Because police discover crimes wherever they are
   physically stationed, Neighborhood A records an inflated count of arrests. Retraining the model causes it to send an
   even larger share of patrols to A, until $100\%$ of resources are concentrated in A while crime in Neighborhood B
   goes completely unmonitored.
2. **Recommender Monoculture and Filter Bubbles:** A music recommender slightly over-indexes on popular pop tracks.
   Users click these tracks because they are prominently displayed. The pipeline logs these clicks, retrains, and
   concludes that users possess zero interest in classical or jazz music, purging catalog diversity and homogenizing
   user taste.
3. **Model Collapse via Synthetic Web Text:** LLMs scraping web text ingest articles generated by earlier LLMs.
   Recursive self-training on model-generated data without grounding causes irreversible tail collapse and semantic
   degradation.

## Randomised exposure

Breaking feedback loops requires injecting controlled stochastic intervention into the data collection pipeline
<Cite id="bottou2013" />:

- **Epsilon-Greedy Exploration ($\epsilon$-Greedy):** Reserving a fixed proportion $\epsilon \in [0.02, 0.05]$ of
  production traffic for completely uniform random assignment across candidate items. This guarantees that unexposed
  catalog items accumulate observational feedback.
- **Contextual Bandit Policies (Thompson Sampling, LinUCB):** Maintaining Bayesian posterior uncertainty over item
  utilities and sampling actions according to their posterior probability of being optimal. Items with high predictive
  uncertainty receive exploratory exposure until their true quality is established.
- **Counterfactual Weighting (Inverse Propensity Scoring):** Weighting observed interactions by the inverse probability
  of display under the logging policy ($w_i = 1 / \pi_0(a_i \mid x_i)$). This down-weights clicks on items placed in
  position 1 while heavily boosting rare clicks achieved on items placed at lower ranks.

## Pitfalls

- **Mistaking Performative Stability for Predictive Accuracy:** Celebrating high accuracy metrics on retrospective logs
  when the model achieved that accuracy simply by forcing the world to conform to its predictions (e.g., an ad system
  predicting zero clicks on ads it refused to display).
- **Evaluating Retrained Models on Self-Generated Test Sets:** Validating a newly retrained model against a test set
  collected under the incumbent model's policy. The test set evaluates only what the old model chose to expose, blinding
  evaluators to out-of-policy failures.
- **Silent Feedback Loops in Credit Limits:** A credit scoring model reduces credit limits for borrowers predicted to
  struggle. Reducing credit limits elevates the borrower's credit utilization ratio, causing their credit score to
  plunge and triggering actual financial distress—creating the very default the model predicted.
- **Neglecting Systemic Monoculture:** When multiple competing firms in an industry (e.g., banks or hiring agencies) all
  purchase and deploy identical third-party foundation models, the models create industry-wide systemic exclusion:
  rejected individuals are locked out of the entire economy simultaneously.
