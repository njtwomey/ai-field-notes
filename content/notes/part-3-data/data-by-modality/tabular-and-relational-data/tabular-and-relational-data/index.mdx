<Definition>
**Tabular data** hold one entity or event per row and one typed attribute per column, with columns of mixed kinds:
numbers, categories, dates and text. **Relational data** spread an entity's information over several tables linked by
keys. Unlike pixels or samples of audio, table columns have no natural order or neighbourhood, so a model cannot exploit
spatial or temporal locality across them.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Most business, medical, scientific and administrative data are tables. Credit decisions, churn prediction, claims,
clinical risk scores and demand forecasts are built on them. The general preparation methods of Part III are written
for tables first. This chapter covers what is specific to the format: how a table is laid out, how several tables are
combined, what changes when rows repeat over time, and how tables are fed to models. On medium-sized tables, tree
ensembles still match or beat deep networks <Cite id="grinsztajn2022" />, which shapes much of the preparation advice.

## Main ideas

**Layout fixes the row unit.** In a tidy table each variable is a column and each observation a row
<Cite id="wickham2014tidy" />. Reshaping between wide and <NoteLink to="tidy-and-reshaped-tables">long
layouts</NoteLink> changes what a row is, and with it the right way to split. <NoteLink to="table-structure" /> covers
layout, nested <Gloss name="json" /> records and free-text columns.

**Relations are flattened or modelled.** <NoteLink to="multi-table-data" /> describes schemas of tables linked by keys
<Cite id="codd1970relational" />. One-to-many joins fan out rows and duplicate values. Learning either flattens the
schema with joins and aggregates, or reads the table graph directly, as relational deep learning does.

**Repeated measurements are correlated.** <NoteLink to="panel-data" /> observes the same units over time. It separates
variation within a unit from variation between units, handles dropout and unbalanced panels, and splits by unit or by
time. <NoteLink to="time-to-event-data" /> records durations with a censoring indicator and handles time-varying
covariates, landmarking and competing risks.

**Representation depends on the model.** <NoteLink to="tabular-data" /> explains why columns are heterogeneous and
unordered, and what that means for trees, which need little preprocessing, and for neural networks, which need scaled
numbers and an embedding per column. <NoteLink to="tabular-augmentation" /> explains why tables resist augmentation and
which methods help: scaled noise, mixup on embeddings, feature masking and swapping values between rows.
