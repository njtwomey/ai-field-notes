<Definition>
A **trajectory** is an ordered sequence of spatio-temporal coordinate observations
$T = \langle p_1, p_2, \dots, p_N \rangle$ representing the continuous physical movement of a discrete entity (vehicle,
pedestrian, animal, aircraft) over time, where each fix $p_i = (\mathbf{x}_i, t_i, \mathbf{a}_i)$ comprises planar or
geodetic coordinates $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$, a monotonically increasing timestamp $t_i \in \mathbb{R}$
($t_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_N$), and optional kinematic attributes $\mathbf{a}_i$ such as instantaneous velocity $v_i$,
bearing angle $\theta_i$, and altitude $z_i$ <Cite id="zheng2015trajectory" />.
</Definition>

Raw positioning telemetry from GPS, cellular tower handoffs, or automated vehicle tracking suffers from sensor drift,
multipath signal reflection in urban canyons, asynchronous sampling intervals, and lack of street topology. Transforming
raw trajectory streams into structured features requires Kalman filtering, map matching onto road networks, and
stay-point clustering <Cite id="newson2009" />.

## GPS Noise and Kinematic Filtering

Raw GNSS positioning fixes exhibit Gaussian measurement noise compounded by heavy-tailed non-Gaussian multipath
reflection errors caused by tall buildings, tunnels, and overpasses <Cite id="zheng2015trajectory" />.

### Kinematic Outlier Filtering

Outlier detection filters physically impossible telemetry points using kinematic thresholds:
1. **Speed Thresholding**: The average velocity between consecutive fixes $p_{i-1}$ and $p_i$ is computed:
   $$
   v_i = \frac{d_{\text{geodesic}}(\mathbf{x}_{i-1}, \mathbf{x}_i)}{t_i - t_{i-1}}
   $$
   Points where $v_i > v_{\max}$ (e.g. $v_{\max} = 150\text{ km/h}$ for urban ground vehicles) are flagged as multipath
   teleportation glitches and removed.
2. **Acceleration and Heading Acceleration**: High directional acceleration (sudden $180^\circ$ reversal within 1 second
   followed by immediate recovery) indicates receiver signal bounces.

### Trajectory Simplification (Douglas-Peucker Algorithm)

To compress dense GPS streams while preserving spatial topology, the **Douglas-Peucker algorithm** recursively
subsamples vertices within an error tolerance $\epsilon$ <Cite id="zheng2015trajectory" />:
1. Draw a straight line between trajectory endpoints $p_1$ and $p_N$.
2. Find the intermediate point $p_k$ with the maximum perpendicular distance to the chord:
   $$
   d_{\max} = \max_{1 < k < N} \operatorname{dist}_{\perp}(p_k, \overline{p_1 p_N})
   $$
3. If $d_{\max} > \epsilon$, keep $p_k$ and recursively simplify sub-trajectories $\langle p_1, \dots, p_k \rangle$ and
   $\langle p_k, \dots, p_N \rangle$. Otherwise, discard all intermediate vertices.

This guarantees that the simplified polyline deviates from the original trajectory by at most $\epsilon$ meters, often
achieving $80\text{--}90\%$ storage reduction.

## Map Matching (Hidden Markov Models)

Autonomous vehicles, delivery dispatchers, and traffic routing algorithms require mapping noisy, asynchronous GPS
coordinates onto a directed road graph $G = (V, E)$, where edges represent road segments.

### The Newson-Krumm HMM Formulation

Newson and Krumm <Cite id="newson2009" /> cast map matching as inference in a Hidden Markov Model (HMM):
- **Observations $\mathbf{z}_i$**: The noisy recorded GPS coordinates $p_i$.
- **Hidden States $s_{i, j}$**: Candidate road positions lying on road segment $r_j$ closest to $p_i$.

| HMM Formulation Component | Notation & Variable | Probabilistic Characterization | Physical & Routing Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Observation Sequence** | $\mathbf{z}_{1:T} = (z_1, \dots, z_T)$ | Raw recorded GPS coordinates $(\text{lat}, \text{lon}, t)$ | Observed sensor fixes corrupted by multipath and satellite receiver noise |
| **Latent State Sequence** | $\mathbf{s}_{1:T} = (s_1, \dots, s_T)$ | True candidate points snapped onto road edges $e \in E$ | Hidden vehicle positions constrained to lawful topology of road graph $G$ |
| **Emission Probability** | $p(z_i \mid s_{i, j})$ | Gaussian over orthogonal distance $\|\mathbf{x}_{z_i} - \mathbf{x}_{s_{i, j}}\|$ | Penalizes candidate road positions distant from recorded GPS coordinates |
| **Transition Probability** | $p(s_{i+1, k} \mid s_{i, j})$ | Exponential penalty on $\|d_{\text{net}} - d_{\text{geo}}\|$ | Penalizes topological road detours inconsistent with straight-line GPS elapsed time |

#### 1. Emission Probability

The probability that GPS measurement $z_i$ was generated by candidate road position $x_{i, j}$ follows a zero-mean
Gaussian distribution with sensor standard deviation $\sigma_z$:

$$
p(z_i \mid s_{i, j}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi \sigma_z^2}} \exp\left( -\frac{\|\mathbf{x}_{z_i} - \mathbf{x}_{s_{i, j}}\|_2^2}{2\sigma_z^2} \right)
$$

#### 2. Transition Probability

The probability of transitioning from candidate road position $s_{i, j}$ to $s_{i+1, k}$ penalizes discrepancies between
the shortest network path distance $d_{\text{net}}(s_{i, j}, s_{i+1, k})$ along the road graph and the great-circle
Euclidean distance $d_{\text{geo}}(z_i, z_{i+1})$ between measurements:

$$
p(s_{i+1, k} \mid s_{i, j}) = \frac{1}{\beta} \exp\left( -\frac{|d_{\text{net}}(s_{i, j}, s_{i+1, k}) - d_{\text{geo}}(z_i, z_{i+1})|}{\beta} \right)
$$

where $\beta$ is a calibrated route difference scale. If a vehicle moves between consecutive fixes separated by
$100\text{ m}$ in Euclidean distance, a road transition requiring an illegal U-turn or a $1{,}500\text{ m}$ detour
receives an exponentially suppressed probability <Cite id="newson2009" />.

#### 3. Viterbi Path Decoding

The globally optimal sequence of traversed road segments $\mathbf{s}^*$ is decoded in $O(N \cdot K^2)$ time using the
Viterbi dynamic programming algorithm:

$$
\mathbf{s}^* = \arg\max_{\mathbf{s}} \prod_{i=1}^N p(z_i \mid s_i) \prod_{i=1}^{N-1} p(s_{i+1} \mid s_i)
$$

## Stay-Point Detection

Raw trajectories contain long periods of spatial dwelling where the entity is stationary (e.g. parked at a warehouse,
sitting in an office, dining at a restaurant). Sensor noise causes GPS coordinates to drift within a local circle
<Cite id="zheng2015trajectory" />.

A **stay point** $S$ represents a contiguous sub-sequence $\langle p_m, p_{m+1}, \dots, p_k \rangle$ where:
1. The maximum spatial distance between the entry point $p_m$ and any subsequent point is bounded by a spatial threshold
   $D_{\max}$:
   $$
   \max_{m \le j \le k} d_{\text{geodesic}}(p_m, p_j) \le D_{\max}
   $$
2. The dwell duration exceeds a minimum time threshold $T_{\min}$:
   $$
   t_k - t_m \ge T_{\min}
   $$

The stay point is collapsed into a single semantic point of interest (POI) with spatial centroid
$\mathbf{x}_{\text{stay}} = \frac{1}{k - m + 1} \sum_{j=m}^k \mathbf{x}_j$, arrival time $t_m$, and departure time
$t_k$. Detecting stay points compresses millions of GPS fixes into discrete origin-destination (OD) trip matrices
<Cite id="zheng2015trajectory" />.

## Uneven Sampling Rates and Distance Metrics

Trajectories collected across fleets feature heterogeneous sampling intervals:
- **Linear vs Network Interpolation**: Naively interpolating missing coordinates linearly across 5-minute sampling gaps
  cuts through buildings, water bodies, and parks. Principled interpolation computes the shortest path along the
  underlying road graph constrained by speed limits.
- **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)**: Aligns two trajectories of different lengths and speeds by finding an alignment path
  $W$ that minimizes total point-to-point distances subject to monotonicity and boundary constraints.
- **Fréchet Distance**: Measures the minimum leash length required to connect a dog and its owner walking along their
  respective trajectories without backtracking.

## Pitfalls

1. **Ping-Ponging Between Parallel Lanes**: In dense dual-carriageway corridors or elevated double-decker expressways,
   high GPS noise causes map matching without transition penalties to oscillate rapidly between parallel northbound and
   southbound lanes.
2. **Re-identification and Privacy Vulnerabilities**: Trajectories are highly unique. Empirical studies show that just
   four coarse spatio-temporal points $(\mathbf{x}_i, t_i)$ are sufficient to uniquely re-identify over $95\%$ of
   individuals in anonymized mobility datasets, requiring differential privacy or spatial cloaking.
3. **Ghost Velocity Spikes from Sleep Modes**: When smartphones wake from low-power background sleep modes, GPS
   receivers report coarse cell-tower coordinates before acquiring satellite locks, creating artificial
   $500\text{ km/h}$ teleportation spikes that contaminate trip duration statistics.
