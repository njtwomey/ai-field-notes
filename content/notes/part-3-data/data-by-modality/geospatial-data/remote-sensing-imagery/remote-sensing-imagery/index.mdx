<Definition>
**Remote-sensing imagery** denotes digital raster observations capturing reflected solar irradiance or emitted thermal
radiation from the Earth's surface across discrete electromagnetic spectral bands, acquired by spaceborne or airborne
multispectral sensors <Cite id="drusch2012" />. Processing satellite imagery requires converting uncalibrated sensor
Digital Numbers (DN) through Top-of-Atmosphere (TOA) and Bottom-of-Atmosphere (BOA) surface reflectance, filtering
atmospheric aerosol scattering, computing biophysical band ratios (such as NDVI <Cite id="rouse1974" />), and
constructing cloud-free temporal composites.
</Definition>

Unlike standard 3-channel RGB computer vision, remote sensing operates on non-standard channel dimensions
($C \in [4, 13]$ for multispectral systems like Sentinel-2 and Landsat 8/9, up to $C > 200$ for hyperspectral sensors),
heterogeneous Ground Sampling Distances (GSD) across bands, 12-bit to 16-bit radiometric precision, and heavy
atmospheric path radiance <Cite id="drusch2012" />.

## Spectral Bands and Multi-Resolution Imagery

Multispectral satellites record surface interactions across diagnostic wavelength windows:
- **Visible Spectrum (B2 Blue $490\text{ nm}$, B3 Green $560\text{ nm}$, B4 Red $665\text{ nm}$)**: Soil, water
  turbidity, and chlorophyll absorption.
- **Red Edge (B5 $705\text{ nm}$, B6 $740\text{ nm}$, B7 $783\text{ nm}$)**: The sharp transition interval between
  photosynthetic absorption and cellular scattering, sensitive to leaf chlorophyll and nitrogen content.
- **Near-Infrared (NIR B8 $842\text{ nm}$, B8A $865\text{ nm}$)**: Highly reflected ($40\text{--}60\%$) by the spongy
  mesophyll internal structure of healthy leaves.
- **Shortwave Infrared (SWIR B11 $1{,}610\text{ nm}$, B12 $2{,}190\text{ nm}$)**: Sensitive to leaf moisture content,
  soil hydration, and geological mineral composition.
- **Atmospheric Bands (B1 Coastal Aerosol $443\text{ nm}$, B9 Water Vapour $945\text{ nm}$, B10 Cirrus
  $1{,}375\text{ nm}$)**: High atmospheric absorption bands used strictly for atmospheric calibration and thin cloud
  detection.

### Resolution Dimensions

Satellite imaging systems trade off four interdependent dimensions <Cite id="drusch2012" />:
1. **Spatial Resolution (Ground Sampling Distance, GSD)**: The physical dimension of a pixel projected on the ground.
   Sentinel-2 features multi-resolution bands: $10\text{ m}$ (RGB and NIR B8), $20\text{ m}$ (Red Edge, narrow NIR B8A,
   SWIR1, SWIR2), and $60\text{ m}$ (coastal, water vapour, cirrus).
2. **Spectral Resolution**: The number and spectral bandwidth $\Delta \lambda$ of channels. Narrow bands isolate subtle
   chemical absorption features that broadband RGB merges.
3. **Temporal Resolution (Revisit Time)**: The elapsed time between repeat passes over the exact same ground footprint
   (5 days for the twin Sentinel-2A/B constellation; 16 days for Landsat 8; sub-daily for commercial PlanetScope
   cubesats).
4. **Radiometric Resolution**: Bit depth quantization (e.g. 12-bit dynamic range $[0, 4095]$ or 16-bit storage
   $[0, 65535]$), resolving minute reflectance gradations in dark water bodies or shadowed mountain slopes.

## Radiometric and Atmospheric Correction

Raw raw satellite telemetry arrives as integer **Digital Numbers (DN)**. Producing physically consistent inputs across
seasons and sensors requires Level-1 and Level-2 processing:

| Processing Level | Physical Domain | Calibration & Normalization Step | Physical Units |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level-0 / Raw** | Raw Digital Numbers (DN) | Uncalibrated sensor voltage measurements | Quantized integer counts |
| **Level-1B** | Top-Of-Atmosphere Radiance | Sensor calibration (gain, bias, detector dark current) | Spectral radiance ($\text{W} \cdot \text{m}^{-2} \cdot \text{sr}^{-1} \cdot \mu\text{m}^{-1}$) |
| **Level-1C** | TOA Reflectance ($\rho_{\text{TOA}}$) | Solar zenith angle and Earth-Sun orbital distance correction | Dimensionless apparent planetary reflectance |
| **Level-2A** | Surface Reflectance ($\rho_{\text{BOA}}$) | Radiative transfer modeling (6S / Sen2Cor) correcting aerosols and water vapour | Bottom-Of-Atmosphere surface reflectance |

### 1. Top-Of-Atmosphere (TOA) Reflectance (Level-1C)

Raw DNs are converted to spectral radiance $L_\lambda = \text{scale} \times \text{DN} + \text{offset}$, then normalized
for solar zenith angle $\theta_s$ and Earth-Sun orbital distance $d$:

$$
\rho_{\text{TOA}}(\lambda) = \frac{\pi \cdot L_\lambda \cdot d^2}{\text{ESUN}_\lambda \cdot \cos \theta_s}
$$

where $\text{ESUN}_\lambda$ is the mean extraterrestrial solar spectral irradiance.

### 2. Bottom-Of-Atmosphere (BOA) Surface Reflectance (Level-2A)

Light traveling through the atmosphere suffers from Rayleigh molecular scattering ($\sigma_R \propto \lambda^{-4}$,
dominating Blue and Coastal bands) and Mie aerosol scattering:

$$
L_{\text{TOA}}(\lambda) = L_{\text{path}}(\lambda) + \frac{\rho_{\text{surface}}(\lambda) \cdot T_{\text{down}}(\lambda) \cdot T_{\text{up}}(\lambda) \cdot E_s}{\pi (1 - \rho_{\text{surface}}(\lambda) \cdot S(\lambda))}
$$

Atmospheric correction algorithms (e.g. Sen2Cor, LaSRC, 6S) estimate Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) and water vapor from
specialized absorption bands to invert this equation, isolating the true canopy surface reflectance
$\rho_{\text{surface}}(\lambda) \in [0, 1]$.

## Cloud Detection and Masking

Clouds and their projected ground shadows obscure optical imagery, creating extreme positive outliers in visible bands
and deep negative outliers in shadow regions.
- **Cirrus Detection**: The $1.38\ \mu\text{m}$ band (B10) captures solar radiation reflected by high-altitude
  ice-crystal cirrus clouds before reaching tropospheric water vapor.
- **Cloud Masking Algorithms**: Machine learning segmenters (such as `s2cloudless` or deep U-Net architectures) output
  per-pixel cloud probabilities.
- **Shadow Projection**: Cloud shadows are computed geometrically by projecting cloud mask contours along the solar
  azimuth and zenith vectors onto the digital elevation model (DEM). Cloud and shadow pixels are masked with `NaN` /
  `nodata` flags before feature extraction.

## Biophysical Band Indices

Combining bands into normalized difference ratios cancels out topography-induced solar illumination variation and
isolates specific chemical-biophysical properties <Cite id="rouse1974" />:

### Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI)

Exploits high chlorophyll absorption in Red and high mesophyll reflection in NIR <Cite id="rouse1974" />:

$$
\text{NDVI} = \frac{\rho_{\text{NIR}} - \rho_{\text{Red}}}{\rho_{\text{NIR}} + \rho_{\text{Red}}} \in [-1, +1]
$$

Dense healthy vegetation yields $\text{NDVI} \in [0.6, 0.9]$; bare soil yields $[0.1, 0.25]$; open water bodies yield
negative values ($[-0.5, 0.0]$).

### Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI)

Incorporates the Blue band to decouple atmospheric aerosol path radiance and resists saturation over dense tropical
rainforest canopies:

$$
\text{EVI} = G \cdot \frac{\rho_{\text{NIR}} - \rho_{\text{Red}}}{\rho_{\text{NIR}} + C_1 \rho_{\text{Red}} - C_2 \rho_{\text{Blue}} + L}
$$

(standard coefficients: $G = 2.5, C_1 = 6.0, C_2 = 7.5, L = 1.0$).

### Water and Burn Indices

- **Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI)**:
  $$
  \text{NDWI} = \frac{\rho_{\text{Green}} - \rho_{\text{NIR}}}{\rho_{\text{Green}} + \rho_{\text{NIR}}}
  $$
- **Normalized Burn Ratio (NBR)**:
  $$
  \text{NBR} = \frac{\rho_{\text{NIR}} - \rho_{\text{SWIR2}}}{\rho_{\text{NIR}} + \rho_{\text{SWIR2}}}
  $$
  Difference between pre-fire and post-fire NBR ($\Delta\text{NBR}$) maps burn severity and wildfire footprint
  boundaries.

## Temporal Compositing and Image Time Series

Because single satellite scenes are frequently contaminated by clouds, operational machine learning pipelines aggregate
image stacks across temporal windows (e.g. monthly or seasonal intervals):
- **Maximum NDVI / Greenest Pixel Composite**: Retains the pixel vector $(x, y)$ corresponding to the date with peak
  NDVI within the window, naturally filtering undetected clouds and shadows.
- **Median Compositing**: Computes the per-band median across valid (unmasked) cloud-free observations, producing
  seamless, cloud-free mosaics.
- **Crop Classification with Temporal Profiles**: Agricultural crop types (maize, soy, winter wheat) cannot be
  distinguished from a single snapshot, but exhibit distinct phenological green-up and harvest curves across the growing
  season.

## Pitfalls

1. **Mixing Level-1C (TOA) and Level-2A (BOA) Reflectance**: Training computer vision models on Level-1C
   top-of-atmosphere data overfits to atmospheric haze, sun elevation, and seasonal atmospheric turbidity rather than
   true ground reflectance.
2. **Ignoring Multi-Resolution Resampling Alignment**: Upsampling Sentinel-2 $20\text{ m}$ SWIR bands to match
   $10\text{ m}$ RGB/NIR bands using naive nearest-neighbor interpolation introduces blocky pixel grid artifacts.
   Bilinear or bicubic spline resampling matching the optical point spread function (PSF) is required.
3. **Seasonal Phenology Domain Shift**: Models trained exclusively on summer leaf-on imagery fail completely when
   evaluated on autumnal leaf-off or winter snow-covered test imagery, mistaking seasonal dormancy for land-use
   deforestation.
