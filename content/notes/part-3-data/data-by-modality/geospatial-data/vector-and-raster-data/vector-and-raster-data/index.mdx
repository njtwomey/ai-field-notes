<Definition>
**Vector and raster data** constitute the two dual data models of geographic information systems (GIS). **Vector data**
represents discrete geographic phenomena as coordinate-defined geometric primitives (points, lines, polygons) with
attached tabular attributes, whereas **raster data** represents continuous or categorical fields as regular tessellated
pixel grids $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ mapped to physical space via an affine geotransform
<Cite id="snyder1987" />.
</Definition>

Bridging the gap between continuous satellite imagery and discrete tabular administrative records requires
transformations: spatial joins via geometric predicates, scanline rasterisation of vector geometries, and zonal
statistics summarizing raster values within polygonal boundaries.

## Vector Data Model (OGC Simple Features)

The Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC) Simple Features Access standard formalizes vector geometry:
- **Point**: A single zero-dimensional coordinate tuple $P = (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ (e.g. sensor stations, GPS fixes,
  tree locations).
- **LineString**: A one-dimensional ordered sequence of two or more vertices connected by linear segments
  $L = (P_1, P_2, \dots, P_k)$ (e.g. road centerlines, river networks, flight trajectories).
- **Polygon**: A planar two-dimensional surface bounded by one exterior linear ring and zero or more interior rings
  (representing holes/islands). Every ring must be closed ($P_1 = P_k$) and non-self-intersecting.
- **Multi-Geometries**: Collections of like geometries treated as a single entity (e.g. MultiPolygon representing an
  archipelago state).

### Storage Formats

Vector geometries are serialized in structured exchange standards:
- **GeoJSON**: Text-based JSON serialization based on WGS84 coordinates. Human-readable but inefficient for large
  datasets ($> 100\text{ MB}$).
- **Shapefile (ESRI)**: Legacy multi-file format (`.shp`, `.shx`, `.dbf`, `.prj`) limited to $2\text{ GB}$ file sizes,
  10-character field names, and no native datetime types.
- **GeoPackage (`.gpkg`)**: Modern open SQLite-based container supporting spatial indexing, vector features, and raster
  tiles with full ACID compliance.
- **FlatGeobuf / GeoParquet**: Cloud-native columnar formats supporting HTTP range requests and spatial filtering
  without full file downloads.

## Raster Data Model

Rasters model continuous spatial fields (elevation, land surface temperature, satellite multispectral reflectance) as a
regular matrix of $H$ rows and $W$ columns with $C$ channels.

### The Affine Geotransform

A raster is anchored to geographic space by six affine transform parameters mapping pixel coordinates
$(c, r) \in [0, W) \times [0, H)$ to world coordinates $(x, y)$:

$$
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x_0 \\ y_0 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} c \\ r \end{pmatrix}
$$

where $(x_0, y_0)$ is the upper-left corner coordinate, $a_{11} = \Delta x$ is the pixel width (Ground Sampling
Distance, GSD), $a_{22} = -\Delta y$ is the negative pixel height, and $a_{12}, a_{21}$ specify rotational shear
(typically zero for north-up rasters).

### Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF (COG)

A standard TIFF file requires downloading the entire file to read a single sub-region. Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs (COGs)
organize data into internal regular tiles (e.g. $256 \times 256$ or $512 \times 512$ blocks) paired with downsampled
resolution overviews (pyramids with factors $2\times, 4\times, 8\times, 16\times$). Clients query spatial bounding boxes
and zoom levels via HTTP GET range requests, fetching only the required byte offsets.

## Spatial Joins and Indexing

A **spatial join** combines two spatial datasets based on a topological relationship between their geometries.

### Topological Predicates (DE-9IM)

The Dimensionally Extended 9-Intersection Model (DE-9IM) evaluates the intersections between the Interior ($I$),
Boundary ($B$), and Exterior ($E$) of two geometries $A$ and $B$:

$$
\operatorname{DE-9IM}(A, B) = \begin{pmatrix}
\dim(I(A) \cap I(B)) & \dim(I(A) \cap B(B)) & \dim(I(A) \cap E(B)) \\
\dim(B(A) \cap I(B)) & \dim(B(A) \cap B(B)) & \dim(B(A) \cap E(B)) \\
\dim(E(A) \cap I(B)) & \dim(E(A) \cap B(B)) & \dim(E(A) \cap E(B))
\end{pmatrix}
$$

Standard boolean predicates derive from this matrix: `intersects`, `contains`, `within`, `touches`, `crosses`, and
`overlaps`.

### Spatial Indexing with R-Trees

Naive pairwise intersection between $N$ points and $M$ polygons requires $O(N \cdot M)$ complex geometric computations.
An **R-tree** (or packed STRtree) groups geometric bounding boxes into a hierarchical balanced tree:
1. **Filter Step**: Query point $(x, y)$ is tested against minimum bounding boxes (MBRs) in the R-tree, pruning
   non-overlapping branches in $O(\log M)$ time.
2. **Refine Step**: Exact ray-casting point-in-polygon tests are executed only on the small candidate set of overlapping
   polygons.

## Rasterisation and Zonal Statistics

Transferring data across modalities is a cornerstone of spatial feature engineering:

| Inter-Modality Operation | Input Domain | Computational Mechanism | Generated Output |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rasterisation** | Vector Polygons | Ray-casting point-in-polygon tests on regular pixel centers $(c, r)$ | Discrete binary or categorical raster mask |
| **Vectorisation (Polygonize)** | Categorical Raster | Marching squares / connected component boundary tracing | Closed vector polygon geometries with world coordinates |
| **Zonal Statistics** | Vector Boundary + Continuous Raster | Aggregation ($\sum, \mu, \sigma, \operatorname{median}$) over masked cell values | Tabular feature rows summarizing areal distributions |

### Rasterisation

Rasterisation converts vector polygons into discrete binary or categorical grid masks:
- **Scanline Algorithm**: Computes intersections of horizontal scan lines with polygon edges, filling pixel spans
  between parity-matched edges.
- **Center-Point vs All-Touched Rule**: Center-point inclusion marks a pixel as active only if its coordinate center
  $(c + 0.5, r + 0.5)$ falls within the polygon; all-touched marks any pixel whose boundary intersects the polygon,
  over-estimating area for narrow features.

### Zonal Statistics

Zonal statistics aggregate continuous raster values $Z_{r, c}$ across the spatial footprint defined by vector zone
polygon $A$:
- **Areal Mean**:
  $$
  \bar{Z}_A = \frac{\sum_{(r, c) \in A} w_{r, c} Z_{r, c}}{\sum_{(r, c) \in A} w_{r, c}}
  $$
  where $w_{r, c} \in [0, 1]$ is the cell intersection fractional weight.
- **Zonal Sum / Extent**: $\sum_{(r, c) \in A} Z_{r, c}$, used to compute total regional population, biomass, or
  precipitation.

## Pitfalls

1. **Resolution Mismatch and Boundary Quantization**: Computing zonal statistics with coarse raster pixels (e.g.
   $1\text{ km}$ MODIS) on small urban vector parcels ($100\text{ m}$) causes perimeter mixed pixels to contaminate
   zonal estimates with adjacent industrial or water land cover.
2. **Topological Invalidity in Union Operations**: Geometric operations (dissolves, buffers, difference) on
   floating-point coordinate boundaries often produce microscopic sliver polygons and self-intersecting bowtie rings
   that cause spatial joins to crash or drop records.
3. **Unprojected Planar Area Calculations**: Executing polygon area calculations (`ST_Area`) on unprojected EPSG:4326
   geometries computes area in meaningless square degrees $(\Delta^\circ)^2$ rather than square meters, skewing density
   calculations by up to $100\%$ depending on latitude.
