<Definition>
**Geospatial data** attach observations to locations on the Earth. Locations are stored as coordinates in a reference
system, as geometries such as points, lines and polygons, or as grids of cells that cover an area. Nearby observations
tend to be similar, which helps prediction and undermines random splits.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Delivery times, property prices, crop yields, disease rates, land cover and traffic all vary over space. Models of them
take inputs from maps, satellites, GPS devices and administrative statistics, each in its own format and coordinate
system. Combining them requires a common reference frame, an explicit choice of spatial unit and features that capture
neighbourhood. Tobler's first law states the central fact: near things are more related than distant things
<Cite id="tobler1970" />. It makes location informative, and it makes a random
<NoteLink to="train-validation-and-test-splits">train-test split</NoteLink> leak information across nearby points.

## Main ideas

**Coordinates need a reference system.** <NoteLink to="coordinate-reference-systems" /> distinguishes geographic
coordinates on an ellipsoid from projected coordinates on a plane. Every projection distorts area, distance or angle
<Cite id="snyder1987" />. Mixing systems, or computing Euclidean distance on latitude and longitude, gives wrong
distances.

**Vector or raster.** <NoteLink to="vector-and-raster-data" /> represents the world as discrete geometries or as regular
grids, and connects them by spatial joins, rasterisation and zonal statistics. <NoteLink to="areal-aggregation" />
explains the modifiable areal unit problem <Cite id="openshaw1984" />: correlations change with the size and shape of
the aggregation units. It also explains the ecological fallacy, where area-level patterns differ from individual ones.

**Location becomes features.** <NoteLink to="spatial-features" /> indexes locations by hierarchical grids such as H3
and S2, computes distances to points of interest and spatial lags of neighbours, and measures spatial autocorrelation by
Moran's I. Spatial cross-validation holds out blocks with buffers so that test points are not next to training points.

**Satellites provide images with physics.** <NoteLink to="remote-sensing-imagery" /> covers multispectral bands,
atmospheric correction, cloud masking, indices such as NDVI and temporal composites.

**Movement is a sequence of points.** <NoteLink to="trajectory-data" /> filters noisy GPS fixes, matches them to a road
network with <Gloss name="hmm">hidden Markov models</Gloss>, detects stay points and handles uneven sampling.
