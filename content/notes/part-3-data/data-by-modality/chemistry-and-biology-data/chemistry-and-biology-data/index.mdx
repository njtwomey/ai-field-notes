<Definition>
**Chemistry and biology data** describe molecules, biological sequences and molecular measurements of cells and tissues.
A molecule can be written as a string, a fingerprint, a graph or a set of 3D coordinates. A gene or protein is a string
over a small alphabet. An omics experiment is a table of thousands of molecular features measured on few samples. Each
form fixes which models apply and which symmetries they must respect.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Property prediction for drug candidates, protein structure and function prediction, variant effect prediction and
single-cell analysis all learn from these data. Two features make them unusual. The representation is a choice with
large consequences, since the same molecule has many valid strings and many 3D shapes. And close analogues are
everywhere: molecules share scaffolds and proteins share ancestry. A random split then puts near-copies on both sides
and overstates how well a model predicts new chemistry or new protein families <Cite id="wu2018moleculenet" />.

## Main ideas

**A molecule has several representations.** <NoteLink to="molecular-representations" /> compares line notations
(SMILES and SELFIES), circular fingerprints such as ECFP <Cite id="rogers2010" />, attributed 2D graphs for message
passing, and 3D conformers, which need models invariant or equivariant to rotation and translation.

**Biological sequences are strings with ancestry.** <NoteLink to="biological-sequence-data" /> represents DNA, RNA and
proteins as strings, k-mer counts, alignments scored by substitution matrices, or embeddings from protein
<Gloss name="language-model">language models</Gloss>. Homologous sequences share function, so splits cluster sequences
by identity first.

**Omics tables are wide, noisy and batched.** <NoteLink to="omics-tables" /> covers count normalisation with dispersion
models <Cite id="love2014" />, batch effects corrected by empirical Bayes, and the regime of many more features than
samples, which needs false discovery rate control.

**Split by chemistry, not by row.** <NoteLink to="scaffold-and-similarity-splits" /> groups molecules by Bemis-Murcko
scaffold or by clusters of fingerprint similarity, and holds out whole groups, to measure generalisation to new
chemotypes.
