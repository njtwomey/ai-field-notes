<Definition>
An **omics table** is a high-dimensional tabular matrix $\mathbf{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{G \times S}$ (or
$\mathbb{N}^{G \times S}$) indexing $G$ molecular features (such as mRNA transcripts, protein abundances, metabolites,
or DNA methylation loci) measured across $S$ biological samples. These matrices operate under an extreme **$p \gg n$
regime** ($G \sim 10^4\text{--}10^6$ while $S \sim 10^1\text{--}10^3$), characterized by heteroskedastic count
dispersion, zero inflation, and systematic technical confounders across experimental batches
<Cite id="love2014, johnson2007" />.
</Definition>

Standard machine learning models (gradient boosted trees, neural networks, support vector machines) assume that input
features are homoskedastic, independent, and measured without systematic technical batch shifts. Direct application of
supervised learning to raw omics matrices invariably models technical artifacts (such as sequencing lane or reagent lot)
rather than true underlying biology <Cite id="johnson2007" />.

## Count Normalisation and Dispersion Modelling

In RNA sequencing (RNA-seq), raw values $Y_{gj} \in \mathbb{N}$ represent the number of cDNA fragments mapped to gene
$g$ in library $j$. Raw counts scale with both sequencing depth (library size $N_j = \sum_{g=1}^G Y_{gj}$) and
transcript length $l_g$.

### Library Size and Composition Metrics

- **Reads Per Kilobase Million (RPKM / FPKM)**:
  $$
  \text{RPKM}_{gj} = \frac{Y_{gj}}{\frac{l_g}{10^3} \times \frac{N_j}{10^6}}
  $$
- **Transcripts Per Million (TPM)**: Normalises for gene length prior to sequencing depth, ensuring that sum of TPMs
  across all genes is constant ($10^6$) in every sample:
  $$
  \text{TPM}_{gj} = \frac{Y_{gj} / l_g}{\sum_{k=1}^G (Y_{kj} / l_k)} \times 10^6
  $$

However, TPM and RPKM fail when a few highly expressed genes consume a disproportionate share of the sequencing library,
artificially depressing the relative proportions of all remaining genes.

### Median-of-Ratios Normalisation (DESeq2)

To account for composition bias, DESeq2 <Cite id="love2014" /> computes a sample-specific size factor $s_j$ relative to
a pseudo-reference sample constructed via the geometric mean across all $S$ libraries:

$$
s_j = \operatorname{median}_{g \in G^*} \frac{Y_{gj}}{\left(\prod_{k=1}^S Y_{gk}\right)^{1/S}}, \quad G^* = \left\{g \in \{1, \dots, G\} \;\middle|\; \prod_{k=1}^S Y_{gk} > 0\right\}
$$

The normalised count is $\tilde{Y}_{gj} = Y_{gj} / s_j$.

### Variance Stabilising Transformation (VST)

Gene expression counts follow a **Negative Binomial distribution** $\operatorname{NB}(\mu_{gj}, \alpha_g)$ with mean
$\mu_{gj} = s_j q_{gj}$ and quadratic variance:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(Y_{gj}) = \mu_{gj} + \alpha_g \mu_{gj}^2
$$

where $\alpha_g \ge 0$ is the gene-specific dispersion parameter. Because variance depends strongly on mean expression,
high-count genes dominate Euclidean distances in clustering and PCA. The **variance-stabilising transformation**
$g(\mu)$ satisfies $\left(\frac{dg}{d\mu}\right)^2 \operatorname{Var}(Y) \approx \text{const}$, yielding
<Cite id="love2014" />:

$$
g(\mu) = \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu + \alpha \mu^2}} \, d\mu = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\alpha}} \operatorname{arsinh}\left(\sqrt{\alpha \mu}\right)
$$

The transformed values $g(\tilde{Y}_{gj})$ exhibit approximately constant variance across dynamic ranges, allowing
standard Euclidean distance metrics and linear models to perform reliably.

## Batch Effects and Empirical Bayes Correction

A **batch effect** refers to systematic non-biological variation introduced by experimental handling: different
sequencing machines, reagent lots, extraction technicians, or collection dates <Cite id="johnson2007" />. In PCA
projections of raw transcriptomic data, the first principal component frequently separates samples by processing date
rather than disease status.

### The Location-Scale Batch Model (ComBat)

ComBat <Cite id="johnson2007" /> models gene expression $Y_{gij}$ for gene $g$ in batch $i$ for sample $j$ as:

$$
Y_{gij} = \alpha_g + \mathbf{x}_{ij}^\top \boldsymbol{\beta}_g + \gamma_{gi} + \delta_{gi} \epsilon_{gij}, \quad \epsilon_{gij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_g^2)
$$

where $\mathbf{x}_{ij}$ contains biological covariates of interest (e.g. disease phenotype, age), $\boldsymbol{\beta}_g$
represents the biological effect vector, $\gamma_{gi}$ is the additive location batch effect, and $\delta_{gi}$ is the
multiplicative scale batch effect.

Because sample sizes within individual batches are small ($n_i \approx 5\text{--}20$), estimating gene-specific
parameters $(\gamma_{gi}, \delta_{gi}^2)$ naively produces high variance. ComBat applies **Empirical Bayes shrinkage**
by placing conjugate hierarchical priors across all genes:

$$
\gamma_{gi} \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\gamma_i, \tau_i^2\right), \quad \delta_{gi}^2 \sim \operatorname{Inverse-Gamma}\left(\lambda_i, \theta_i\right)
$$

The adjusted expression value isolates the clean biological signal:

$$
Y_{gij}^* = \frac{Y_{gij} - \hat{\alpha}_g - \mathbf{x}_{ij}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_g - \hat{\gamma}_{gi}^*}{\hat{\delta}_{gi}^*} + \hat{\alpha}_g + \mathbf{x}_{ij}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_g
$$

where $\hat{\gamma}_{gi}^*$ and $\hat{\delta}_{gi}^*$ are empirical Bayes posterior mean estimates
<Cite id="johnson2007" />.

## The $p \gg n$ Regime and Feature Selection

When $G \gg S$, ordinary least squares regression has infinite non-unique interpolating solutions, and standard maximum
likelihood estimators overfit catastrophically.

### Multiple Hypothesis Testing and False Discovery Rate (FDR)

Testing whether each of $G = 20{,}000$ genes is differentially expressed under null hypothesis $H_{0, g}: \beta_g = 0$
at standard significance level $\alpha = 0.05$ produces $20{,}000 \times 0.05 = 1{,}000$ false positives purely by
chance.
- **Bonferroni Correction (FWER)**: Enforces $P \le \alpha / G = 2.5 \times 10^{-6}$. While controlling the family-wise
  error rate strictly, it causes massive false negatives, discarding genuine low-magnitude biological signals.
- **Benjamini-Hochberg Procedure (FDR)** <Cite id="benjamini1995" />: Sorts observed $p$-values
  $P_{(1)} \le P_{(2)} \le \dots \le P_{(G)}$ and identifies the largest rank $k^*$ satisfying:
  $$
  k^* = \max \left\{ k \;\middle|\; P_{(k)} \le \frac{k}{G} q \right\}
  $$
  Rejecting all null hypotheses for $k \le k^*$ guarantees that the expected proportion of false discoveries is bounded
  by target rate $q$ (typically $q = 0.05$ or $0.10$).

### Regularised Feature Selection

To train predictive classifiers (e.g., patient drug responders vs non-responders) in $p \gg n$, standard pipelines
employ the **Elastic Net**:

$$
\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta}} \frac{1}{2S} \|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2 + \lambda \left( \rho \|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_1 + \frac{1 - \rho}{2} \|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^2 \right), \quad \rho \in (0, 1)
$$

The $\ell_1$ penalty enforces sparsity, selecting a minimal predictive biomarker panel, while the $\ell_2$ penalty
preserves correlated gene clusters participating in shared biological pathways.

## Pitfalls

1. **Information Leakage via Global Batch Correction**: Running ComBat or VST across the entire dataset before
   cross-validation partitions leaks validation labels into the training distribution. All batch parameters and
   normalisation transformations must be fit strictly on training folds.
2. **Confounded Experimental Design**: If all disease cases are processed in Batch 1 and all healthy controls in Batch
   2, biological variation and batch variation are collinear
   ($\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{B}) < \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{X})$). No statistical algorithm can
   disentangle the true phenotype from technical artifacts.
3. **Zero Inflation and Dropout in scRNA-seq**: In single-cell RNA sequencing, over $80\%$ of entries in $\mathbf{Y}$
   are exact zeros due to limited mRNA capture efficiency (technical dropout) combined with true biological silencing.
   Applying bulk RNA-seq normalisation directly without zero-inflated negative binomial (ZINB) or hurdle modeling
   distorts expression statistics.
