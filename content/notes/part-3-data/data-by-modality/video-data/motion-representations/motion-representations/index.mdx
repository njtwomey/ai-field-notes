<Definition>
**Motion representations** are explicit mathematical and structural encodings of inter-frame temporal dynamics—such as
dense pixel displacement fields (optical flow), temporal pixel differences, or compressed-domain macroblock motion
vectors—provided to video architectures to isolate kinematics from static background scene semantics.
</Definition>

While 3D convolutional networks and video vision transformers can theoretically learn motion patterns directly from raw
RGB frame sequences, empirical practice demonstrates that models trained solely on RGB easily latch onto static
contextual shortcuts (e.g., identifying "tennis" exclusively from green grass textures rather than the swinging racket
stroke) <Cite id="simonyan2014twostream" />. Supplying explicit motion channels forces representations to encode
trajectory velocities, directions, and boundary deformations independently of photometric appearance.

## Optical flow

Optical flow estimates the apparent 2D motion velocity field $\mathbf{v}(x, y) = (u(x, y), v(x, y))$ of brightness
patterns between consecutive video frames $I_t$ and $I_{t+1}$ <Cite id="horn1981" />.

The classical formulation rests on the **brightness constancy assumption**, which posits that a surface point preserves
its luminance intensity as it translates over an infinitesimal interval $\Delta t$:

$$
I(x + u, \, y + v, \, t + \Delta t) \approx I(x, y, t)
$$

Taking the first-order Taylor series expansion yields the **optical flow constraint equation**:

$$
I_x u + I_y v + I_t = 0 \quad \iff \quad \nabla I \cdot \mathbf{v} + \frac{\partial I}{\partial t} = 0
$$

Where $I_x = \frac{\partial I}{\partial x}$ and $I_y = \frac{\partial I}{\partial y}$ represent spatial image gradients,
and $I_t = \frac{\partial I}{\partial t}$ is the temporal derivative. Because this single scalar equation contains two
unknowns $(u, v)$, the velocity vector cannot be uniquely determined locally along homogeneous edges—a fundamental
ambiguity known as the **aperture problem**.

To resolve this under-determination, algorithms impose global or local spatial regularization:
1. **Variational Formulations (Horn-Schunck and TV-L1)**: Formulate flow as an energy minimization problem combining the
   optical flow constraint with a spatial smoothness prior <Cite id="horn1981" />:
   $$
   \min_{u, v} \iint \left( (I_x u + I_y v + I_t)^2 + \alpha^2 \left( \|\nabla u\|_2^2 + \|\nabla v\|_2^2 \right) \right) dx \, dy
   $$
2. **Deep Recurrent Flow (RAFT)**: Modern architectures construct a 4D all-pairs correlation volume across multi-scale
   feature pyramids and iteratively refine flow fields using gated recurrent units (GRUs), achieving state-of-the-art
   sub-pixel precision across fast non-rigid deformations <Cite id="teed2020raft" />.

In **Two-Stream Networks**, optical flow is formatted as a multi-channel tensor of $L$ consecutive flow displacement
pairs $[2L, H, W]$ passed into a dedicated temporal convolutional or transformer stream
<Cite id="simonyan2014twostream" />.

## Frame differences

Temporal frame differencing is the simplest motion cue, computing absolute pixel-wise intensity variations between
adjacent frames:

$$
\Delta I_t(x, y) = |I_t(x, y) - I_{t-1}(x, y)|
$$

To capture acceleration or reduce background flicker, pipelines frequently employ symmetric second-order central
differences:

$$
\Delta^2 I_t(x, y) = |I_{t+1}(x, y) - 2I_t(x, y) + I_{t-1}(x, y)|
$$

- **Computational Advantage**: Differencing requires $O(1)$ operations per pixel without iterative numerical solvers or
  neural network forward passes, executing in under $0.2\,\text{ms}$ per frame on standard CPUs.
- **Fundamental Flaw**: Differencing does not compute a true velocity vector $\mathbf{v} = (u, v)$. Rather, by Taylor
  expansion:
  $$
  \Delta I_t(x, y) \approx |\nabla I(x, y) \cdot \mathbf{v}|
  $$
  The difference signal is heavily modulated by the underlying spatial image gradient $\nabla I$. A fast-moving object
  with uniform texture (e.g., a white sphere sliding across a white table) yields $\Delta I \approx 0$, whereas a
  slow-moving high-contrast edge generates massive difference responses. Differencing conflates textural contrast with
  physical velocity.

## Motion vectors from the codec

Video codecs (H.264, HEVC) compute block-based motion compensation during compression, partitioning predicted frames
(P-frames) into macroblocks (e.g., $16 \times 16$ or $8 \times 8$ pixels) and recording integer or sub-pixel
displacement vectors $\mathbf{v}_{\text{MV}}$ alongside DCT prediction residuals $R(x, y)$ <Cite id="wu2018coviar" />.

**Compressed-domain action recognition (CoViAR)** bypasses full RGB decompression entirely:
1. Parse the compressed container bitstream directly to extract motion vectors $\mathbf{v}_{\text{MV}}$ and DCT
   coefficients.
2. Accumulate motion vectors across P-frames relative to the preceding I-frame keyframe.
3. Train separate convolutional heads directly on the coarse motion vector fields and residual tensors.

- **Speed**: Extracting codec motion vectors runs at up to $500\text{--}1000\,\text{FPS}$ ($<1\,\text{ms}$ per frame),
  which is two orders of magnitude faster than dense optical flow algorithms.
- **Limitations**: Motion vectors are optimized for bitrate reduction rather than physical kinematics; encoders
  frequently choose zero-vector predictions or spatial intra-blocks if residual encoding is cheaper, introducing blocky
  discontinuities and noise in smooth visual areas.

## Pitfalls

1. **Confounding by Camera Ego-Motion**: When video is recorded by moving drones, handheld smartphones, or vehicular
   dashcams, camera pan and tilt induce massive global optical flow fields across the entire frame. If uncorrected,
   networks learn to classify camera operator movements rather than human subject actions. Robust pipelines estimate a
   global background homography matrix $\mathbf{H}_t$ via RANSAC and subtract background flow:
   $$
   \mathbf{v}_{\text{action}} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{measured}} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{homography}}
   $$
2. **Storage and Pre-extraction Bottlenecks**: Computing and saving 16-bit dense optical flow fields (forward and
   backward) for large benchmarks like Kinetics-400 generates hundreds of millions of files consuming over 20 terabytes
   of storage.
3. **Brightness Constancy Violations**: Flashing strobe lights, rapid shadows, and auto-exposure gain adjustments on
   mobile sensors violate $I_t + \nabla I \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$, causing optical flow algorithms to hallucinate extreme
   false-positive velocity spikes.
4. **Quantization Artifacts in 8-bit Flow Caching**: Clamping optical flow components into $[0, 255]$ PNG files via
   linear remapping $[u_{\min}, u_{\max}] \to [0, 255]$ quantizes small sub-pixel velocities, destroying fine motion
   signals in distant background objects.
