<Definition>
**Video augmentation** is the systematic application of label-preserving spatial, temporal, and spatio-temporal
transformations to video tensors $[B, C, T, H, W]$. It enforces temporal parameter consistency across spatial frames to
prevent artificial motion strobing while introducing stochastic temporal perturbations—including playback rate dilation,
temporal clipping jitter, and spatio-temporal tube masking—to build invariance to action pacing and viewpoint dynamics.
</Definition>

Directly applying 2D image augmentation libraries (e.g., standard torchvision or Albumentations) frame-by-frame on a
video clip produces severe visual degradation. Because 2D transforms draw random parameters independently for each
image, independent cropping, color jittering, or affine rotations introduce artificial, high-frequency inter-frame
displacement spikes. Video augmentation requires coordinated transformation pipelines where spatial parameters are tied
across time and dedicated temporal operators perturb the temporal manifold
<Cite id="tong2022videomae, yun2020videomix" />.

## Consistent spatial transforms

When applying spatial augmentations (such as random resized cropping, horizontal flips, rotations, or photometric
adjustments) to a video clip $\mathbf{I} = [I_1, I_2, \dots, I_T]$, all frames must share an identical transformation
parameter vector $\theta$ sampled once per clip from distribution $P(\Theta)$ <Cite id="feichtenhofer2019" />:

$$
\theta \sim P(\Theta), \quad \mathbf{I}' = \left[ T_\theta(I_1), \, T_\theta(I_2), \, \dots, \, T_\theta(I_T) \right]
$$

- **Parameter Tying**: If a random crop selects coordinates $(x_0, y_0, w, h)$, the identical bounding box must be
  extracted from every frame $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$. Similarly, if a horizontal flip is applied, all frames must flip
  concurrently.
- **Consequences of Independent Sampling**: If parameters $\theta_t \sim P(\Theta)$ are drawn independently per frame,
  adjacent frames undergo disjoint spatial translations:
  $$
  \|T_{\theta_t}(I_t) - T_{\theta_{t-1}}(I_{t-1})\|_2 \gg \|I_t - I_{t-1}\|_2
  $$
  This creates severe visual strobing, completely scrambling true physical velocity fields $\mathbf{v}(x, y)$ and
  injecting chaotic gradient noise into temporal attention and 3D convolutional kernels.

## Temporal jitter

Temporal jitter perturbs the temporal extraction window without altering physical playback mechanics:

1. **Random Temporal Offset**: Given an untrimmed video of $N$ frames, extracting a clip of duration $T \cdot \tau$
   frames draws start index $t_0$ uniformly from valid offsets:
   $$
   t_0 \sim \operatorname{Uniform}\left( 0, \, N - T \cdot \tau \right)
   $$
   This ensures the network encounters diverse action start phases across training epochs.
2. **Time Reversal**: Reversing the temporal frame sequence $I'_t = I_{T - t + 1}$ inverts the sign of underlying motion
   vectors:
   $$
   \mathbf{v}'(x, y, t) = -\mathbf{v}(x, y, T - t + 1)
   $$
   - *Symmetric Actions*: For cyclical, directionally symmetric activities (e.g., "waving hands", "jogging in place",
     "shuffling cards"), time reversal is strictly label-preserving and doubles effective dataset diversity.
   - *Asymmetric Actions and the Arrow of Time*: For causally directed thermodynamic or kinematic processes (e.g.,
     "dropping a plate", "pouring milk into coffee", "opening a box"), time reversal violates physical causality and
     corrupts semantic labels ("falling down" becomes "levitating up"). Asymmetric classes must be exempted from
     temporal reversal.

## Speed changes

Human activities exhibit wide natural tempo variations—a sprint differs kinematically from a slow jog, yet both share
core semantic structures. Playback speed perturbation resamples the temporal grid by a dilation factor
$\alpha \in [\alpha_{\min}, \alpha_{\max}]$ (typically $[0.5, 2.0]$) <Cite id="feichtenhofer2019" />:

$$
t' = \alpha \cdot t
$$

Frames at non-integer temporal coordinates $t'$ are synthesized via:
- **Nearest-Neighbor Frame Picking**: Snaps $t'$ to $\lfloor t' + 0.5 \rfloor$. Computationally lightweight, but can
  produce micro-stutter at low framerates.
- **Linear Frame Blending**: Blends adjacent frames via barycentric weights $\lambda = t' - \lfloor t' \rfloor$:
  $$
  I'(t') = (1 - \lambda) I_{\lfloor t' \rfloor} + \lambda I_{\lceil t' \rceil}
  $$
- **VideoMix / Spatio-Temporal CutMix**: Generalizing CutMix to video, VideoMix pastes a 3D spatio-temporal cuboid of
  dimensions $[T_{\text{box}}, H_{\text{box}}, W_{\text{box}}]$ from video $B$ into video $A$
  <Cite id="yun2020videomix" />:
  $$
  \mathbf{I}' = \mathbf{M} \odot \mathbf{I}_A + (\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{M}) \odot \mathbf{I}_B, \quad \mathbf{y}' = \lambda \mathbf{y}_A + (1 - \lambda) \mathbf{y}_B
  $$
  Where $\lambda = 1 - \frac{\text{Volume}(\mathbf{M})}{T \cdot H \cdot W}$, regularizing representations against
  over-fitting to localized spatio-temporal patches.

## Frame dropping

Temporal frame dropping simulates transmission packet loss, camera shutter dropouts, and temporal occlusions:

- **Random Frame Dropout**: Drops individual frames with probability $p_{\text{drop}} \in [0.1, 0.3]$, either zeroing
  pixel tensors or repeating the preceding frame to maintain fixed tensor dimensions.
- **VideoMAE Spatio-Temporal Tube Masking**: In masked autoencoding for video self-supervised pre-training, standard
  random 2D masking fails because high temporal correlation allows models to trivially reconstruct masked patches by
  copying unmasked pixels from adjacent frames <Cite id="tong2022videomae, feichtenhofer2022mae" />. VideoMAE employs
  **tube masking**, extending each 2D spatial mask patch across the entire temporal axis $T$:
  $$
  M(x, y, t) = M(x, y, 1) \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, T\}
  $$
  Because adjacent temporal information is simultaneously occluded, the encoder must learn high-level kinematic motion
  semantics rather than simple temporal interpolation. Tube masking enables extreme masking ratios
  $\rho \in [85\%, 90\%]$, slashing encoder FLOPs by up to $90\%$ while establishing state-of-the-art representations.

## Pitfalls

1. **Uncoordinated Multi-View Augmentation**: In multi-camera surveillance or robotics setups, applying spatial
   transforms with different random seeds across synchronized camera views breaks multi-view geometric consistency and
   epipolar constraints.
2. **Horizontal Flipping on Directional Labels**: Augmenting fine-grained actions such as "turning left", "writing from
   left to right", or "clockwise screw rotation" with random horizontal flips flips the ground-truth semantic target
   without updating the label, injecting label noise.
3. **Severe Temporal Deceleration Causing Static Stagnation**: Decelerating high-framerate clips by factors exceeding
   $3\times$ ($\alpha < 0.33$) results in multiple identical adjacent frames, effectively presenting the model with
   static imagery and degrading motion representation learning.
4. **Ignoring Label Smoothing in Spatio-Temporal MixUp**: Hard binary cutouts in 3D spatio-temporal VideoMix without
   linear label interpolation $(\lambda \mathbf{y}_A + (1-\lambda)\mathbf{y}_B)$ create extreme gradient spikes along
   cuboid boundaries.
