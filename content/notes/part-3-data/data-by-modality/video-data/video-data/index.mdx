<Definition>
**Video data** are sequences of images at a frame rate, usually with an audio track, stored by codecs that keep a few
full keyframes and encode the frames between them as differences. For learning, a model sees a short clip of sampled
frames, optionally with an explicit motion signal, and labels mark segments in time, object tracks or whole clips.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Action recognition, temporal localisation, tracking, video retrieval and video generation all learn from footage. A
minute of 30 frame-per-second video holds 1,800 images, most nearly identical to their neighbours. No model can take
them all, and decoding them dominates the cost of many pipelines. Preparation decides which frames a model sees, at
what spacing, with what motion information and with which labels. Each choice trades cost against the temporal detail
an action needs.

## Main ideas

**Decoding dominates.** <NoteLink to="digital-video" /> covers frame rates, codecs with keyframes and predicted frames,
containers and audio tracks. Random access to a frame requires decoding from the previous keyframe, so storage format
and sampling plan are chosen together.

**Sample a few frames per clip.** <NoteLink to="clip-sampling" /> chooses frames by dense sampling of consecutive
frames, uniform spacing over the video, or one frame from each of several segments as in temporal segment networks
<Cite id="wang2016tsn" />. The temporal stride is set by how fast the action unfolds. Two-pathway models sample a slow
and a fast stream at different rates <Cite id="feichtenhofer2019" />.

**Give motion explicitly.** <NoteLink to="motion-representations" /> computes optical flow, frame differences or the
motion vectors already stored by the codec, and feeds them alongside the frames, as two-stream networks do
<Cite id="simonyan2014twostream" />.

**Augment consistently across frames.** <NoteLink to="video-augmentation" /> applies the same spatial crop, flip and
colour change to every frame of a clip, and adds temporal changes such as jittered start times, speed changes and frame
dropping.

**Label in time.** <NoteLink to="video-annotation" /> marks temporal segments, object tracks and per-frame or per-clip
classes. Labelling keyframes and interpolating between them cuts cost.
