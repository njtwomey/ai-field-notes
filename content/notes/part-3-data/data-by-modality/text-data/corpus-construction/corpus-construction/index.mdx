<Definition>
**Corpus construction** is the end-to-end data engineering pipeline that converts massive, uncurated web dumps, domain
archives, books, and code into structured, high-quality text datasets for training <Gloss name="language-model">language
models</Gloss>. The pipeline executes five sequential stages: raw ingestion, heuristic text filtering, statistical
quality scoring, deduplication, and PII/safety redaction <Cite id="penedo2024" />.
</Definition>

Pretraining a frontier autoregressive language model demands trillions of tokens. Unfiltered web crawls (such as raw
Common Crawl WARC dumps) consist predominantly of navigational boilerplate, spam,
<Gloss name="machine-translation">machine translations</Gloss>, search engine optimisation gibberish, adult material,
and duplicated content. Direct pretraining on raw web dumps degrades sample efficiency and causes models to memorise
boilerplate. Curating massive corpora requires cascading filters that progressively discard up to 80% to 90% of raw
tokens while enriching factual, coherent prose <Cite id="soldaini2024" />.

## Sources

Pretraining corpora draw from diverse text distributions:

- **Web crawls:** Common Crawl provides petabytes of raw HTML pages updated monthly. Crawls deliver vast breadth but
  suffer from low median quality.
- **Curated encyclopedias and reference works:** Wikipedia, Wikibooks, and open-access scientific repositories (such as
  arXiv and PubMed Central) provide dense, high-credibility factual statements.
- **Books and literature:** Public domain libraries (Project Gutenberg) and digitised literature provide extended
  narrative coherence and long-range dependencies.
- **Source code:** Public code repositories (GitHub) contribute programming languages, markdown documentation, and
  commit messages, improving formal reasoning across modalities.
- **Conversational archives:** Forum discussions and dialogue logs provide colloquial phrasing and interactive
  <Gloss name="qa">question-answering</Gloss> dynamics.

## Quality filtering

Corpus filtering proceeds from cheap, rule-based heuristics to statistical language model scorers:

1. **Heuristic rules:** Reject documents with extreme line counts, abnormally short or long mean word lengths ($<3$ or
   $>10$ characters), excessive symbol-to-word ratios, or low stopword fractions <Cite id="dodge2021" />. If a page
   contains insufficient common grammatical particles (such as "the", "and", "in"), it is likely keyword-stuffed spam.
2. **Repetition filters:** Discard documents with excessive repeated characters, duplicated lines, or repeating
   $n$-grams. Web scraping bugs frequently capture infinite loops of navigation menus or calendar tables.
3. **Perplexity and statistical filtering:** Score extracted text against a compact language model trained on reference
   text (such as Wikipedia). CCNet <Cite id="wenzek2020" /> fits a 5-gram Kneser-Ney language model (via KenLM) or a
   fastText classifier, partitioning documents into perplexity tertiles and retaining the most fluent bracket.

## Near-duplicate removal

Web text contains extensive near-duplication from syndication, mirrored blog posts, license headers, and repeated
boilerplate. Removing near-duplicates reduces memorisation and accelerates downstream learning
<Cite id="lee2022dedup" />:

- **Exact line and paragraph deduplication:** Eliminates repeated boilerplate lines (such as "Terms and Conditions" or
  "Sign up for our newsletter") that appear millions of times across disparate URLs.
- **Document-level MinHash <Gloss name="lsh" />:** RefinedWeb <Cite id="penedo2023" /> and FineWeb
  <Cite id="penedo2024" /> extract $k=5$ word shingles from each document, generate MinHash signatures across $K=64$ or
  $128$ permutations, and group documents into candidate pairs using locality-sensitive hashing. If pair Jaccard
  similarity exceeds $\tau = 0.75$, only a single canonical copy is retained.
- **Cross-snapshot deduplication:** Removes duplicate web pages across successive Common Crawl dumps collected over
  multiple years, preventing older web content from receiving disproportionate training exposure.

## Personal data removal

Pretraining datasets undergo automated redaction of Personally Identifiable Information (PII) to protect individual
privacy and reduce model extraction vulnerabilities:

- **Regex pattern matching:** Detects structured PII, including email addresses, phone numbers, IPv4/IPv6 addresses,
  social security numbers, and credit card patterns.
- **<Gloss name="ner">Named Entity Recognition (NER)</Gloss>:** Detects names, residential addresses, and medical
  identifiers in unstructured text.
- **Anonymisation versus dropping:** Documents with isolated emails replace tokens with synthetic placeholders (such as
  `<EMAIL>`), whereas records with dense personal identifiers are dropped entirely.

## Safety filtering

Automated safety classifiers screen text for harmful, illegal, or abusive content:

- **Blocklist keyword filtering:** Fast matching against curated lists of offensive, adult, or hate-speech terms. If the
  ratio of blocklist terms exceeds a threshold, the document is excluded.
- **Toxicity classifiers:** Lightweight classifiers (such as fastText or distilled <Gloss name="roberta" /> models)
  predict probabilities of hate speech, harassment, and explicit sexual violence, dropping high-scoring passages.

## Data mixtures

Final pretraining corpora combine disparate sources into a calibrated mixture. Rather than sampling sources strictly
proportional to their raw crawled size, curricula apply deliberate multipliers:

- **Upweighting high-quality sources:** Text from Wikipedia, arXiv, and textbooks is commonly repeated 2 to 4 epochs to
  increase informational density.
- **Downweighting general web:** Raw web scrapes are downweighted to prevent colloquial forum noise from dominating
  pretraining representations.
- **Ablation studies:** Small proxy models evaluate domain mixtures across diverse downstream evaluation suites,
  identifying the optimal ratio of code, mathematics, literature, and general web prose prior to launching large-scale
  pretraining runs.

## Pitfalls

- **Over-filtering minority dialects:** Aggressive heuristic and perplexity filtering trained on standard English
  disproportionately discards African American Vernacular English (AAVE) and non-standard dialects
  <Cite id="dodge2021" />.
- **Synthetic <NoteLink to="feedback-loops">feedback loops</NoteLink>:** Scraping web dumps generated post-2023 risks
  ingesting text synthesized by previous language models, potentially leading to model collapse and degraded lexical
  diversity.
- **Benchmark leakage:** Without explicit cross-checking against evaluation benchmarks, corpus crawls ingest benchmark
  questions and answers, causing severe <NoteLink to="benchmark-contamination">benchmark contamination</NoteLink>.
