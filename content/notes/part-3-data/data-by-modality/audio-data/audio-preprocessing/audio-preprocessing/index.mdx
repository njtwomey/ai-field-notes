<Definition>
**Audio preprocessing** is the sequential digital signal processing pipeline applied to raw acoustic recordings to
standardize sampling rates, channel topologies, perceived loudness levels, spectral tilt, and temporal utterance
boundaries <Cite id="rabiner2007" />. Proper preprocessing ensures that downstream neural architectures learn
generalizable phonetic and acoustic representations rather than overfitting to recording hardware idiosyncrasies,
ambient microphone gain variations, or trailing background silence.
</Definition>

In production machine learning systems, raw audio arrives with extreme heterogeneity: studio podcasts recorded with
broadcast condensers, phone calls sampled at 8 kHz with heavy lossy compression, and smartphone voice memos with
variable speaker distances and background noise.

Feeding raw waveforms directly to neural models without preprocessing forces networks to waste representational capacity
compensating for volume discrepancies and spectral tilt. A standard preprocessing workflow encompasses loudness
normalization, pre-emphasis filtering, silence trimming, voice activity detection (VAD), segmentation, and
time-frequency transformation into log-mel spectrograms.

## Loudness normalisation

Variations in microphone sensitivity, speaker distance, and recording gain introduce severe amplitude imbalances across
datasets (often exceeding $30\text{ dB}$). Three strategies address amplitude normalisation:

### Peak normalisation

Scales the entire recording linearly so its maximum absolute sample reaches a target ceiling (e.g., $0\text{ dBFS}$ or
$-1.0\text{ dBFS}$):

$$
x_{\text{norm}}[n] = \frac{x[n]}{\max_k |x[k]|} \cdot 10^{\text{target\_dB} / 20}
$$

- **Limitation:** A single extraneous transient spike (such as an electrical click, microphone pop, or door slam) forces
  the normalization factor down, leaving the remainder of the conversational speech quiet and unnormalized.

### Root Mean Square (RMS) normalisation

Scales audio based on total average signal energy:

$$
\text{RMS} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N x[n]^2}
$$

- **Limitation:** RMS normalisation is heavily skewed by silence duration. A file with 10 seconds of speech and 20
  seconds of silence exhibits low RMS energy and gets boosted excessively, amplifying background noise.

### Integrated perceptual loudness (EBU R128 / ITU-R BS.1770)

The international broadcast standard calculates perceived loudness in Loudness Units relative to Full Scale (LUFS). It
applies:
1. **K-weighting filter:** Pre-filters audio to model the human ear's increased sensitivity to frequencies between
   $1\text{--}5\text{ kHz}$.
2. **Dual-gated RMS integration:** Excludes silent pauses below $-70\text{ LUFS}$ and quiet passages $10\text{ dB}$
   below un-gated loudness.

Standardizing audio datasets to a fixed integrated loudness (typically $-23\text{ LUFS}$ or $-16\text{ LUFS}$) achieves
consistent conversational volume across diverse acoustic environments without clipping.

## Pre-emphasis

In human speech production, the acoustic volume velocity source at the vibrating vocal cords naturally rolls off at
approximately $-6\text{ dB / octave}$ <Cite id="rabiner2007" />. Consequently, lower vowel formants
($F_1 \approx 300\text{--}800\text{ Hz}$) contain vastly higher spectral energy than higher formants
($F_2, F_3 \approx 2\text{--}4\text{ kHz}$) and unvoiced fricatives ($>5\text{ kHz}$).

To equalize dynamic range across the frequency spectrum, speech pipelines apply a first-order high-pass FIR
**pre-emphasis filter**:

$$
y[n] = x[n] - \alpha x[n-1]
$$

where $\alpha \in [0.95, 0.98]$ (typically $0.97$).

The frequency response of the pre-emphasis filter is:

$$
|H(e^{j\omega})|^2 = 1 + \alpha^2 - 2\alpha \cos(\omega)
$$

Pre-emphasis provides $+15\text{ to }+20\text{ dB}$ of high-frequency gain, boosting formant clarity and preventing
numerical underflow in high-frequency Short-Time Fourier Transform bins.

## Silence trimming

Audio recordings frequently contain seconds of dead air before speech begins and after speech concludes. Leading and
trailing silence inflates training compute and wastes attention spans in Transformer architectures.

Silence trimming algorithms:
1. Compute short-time energy $E[m] = \sum_{n} x[n]^2 w[n - m]$ across overlapping windows ($20\text{--}30\text{ ms}$).
2. Identify the first and last frames that exceed an energy threshold relative to the file peak (e.g.,
   $40\text{--}60\text{ dB}$ below peak).
3. **Safety padding:** Retain a buffer of $100\text{--}200\text{ ms}$ before the onset and after the offset to avoid
   clipping soft word-initial plosives ($/p/, /t/, /k/$) or unvoiced word-final consonants.

## Voice activity detection

While silence trimming removes outer borders, continuous audio contains internal pauses, breathing breaks, and ambient
noise. **Voice Activity Detection (VAD)** segments continuous audio into discrete speech chunks:

- **Energy and Zero-Crossing Rate (ZCR):** Voiced speech exhibits high energy and low zero-crossing rates; unvoiced
  speech exhibits moderate energy and high zero-crossing rates; background ambient noise exhibits low energy and low
  zero-crossing rates.
- **Statistical VAD (Sohn model):** Evaluates Gaussian likelihood ratios in the frequency domain between speech-present
  and speech-absent hypotheses.
- **Neural VAD (e.g., Silero VAD):** Compact recurrent or convolutional neural networks operating on short audio chunks,
  outputting frame-level speech probabilities $P(\text{speech} \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$.

Setting the VAD decision threshold requires balancing false alarms against speech clipping: an aggressive threshold
discards soft speech and word boundaries, directly elevating Word Error Rate (WER) in downstream ASR.

## Segmentation

Because deep neural networks require batched tensor inputs of uniform dimensions, variable-length audio files must be
segmented:

- **Fixed-length chunking:** Audio is sliced into non-overlapping or overlapping windows of fixed duration (e.g.,
  3-second or 10-second segments) for sound event detection (SED) and acoustic scene classification.
- **Utterance-level chunking:** In speech recognition, audio is split dynamically at natural acoustic pauses
  ($>400\text{ ms}$ of continuous silence detected by VAD), creating sentence-like segments of $5\text{--}30$ seconds.
- **Padding and masking:** Utterances within a mini-batch are right-padded with zeros to the maximum sequence length,
  accompanied by a boolean padding mask.

## From waveform to features

Once audio is normalized and segmented, time-domain waveforms are converted to spectral representations
<Cite id="davis1980" />:

1. **Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT):** Windowing the signal with Hann or Hamming windows ($25\text{ ms}$ window
   length, $10\text{ ms}$ hop length) to compute complex spectrograms $\mathbf{X}(t, f)$.
2. **Mel filterbank integration:** Passing power spectra $|\mathbf{X}(t, f)|^2$ through $M$ triangular overlapping
   bandpass filters spaced linearly below 1 kHz and logarithmically above 1 kHz:
   $$
   m = 2595 \log_{10}\left( 1 + \frac{f}{700} \right)
   $$
3. **Logarithmic compression:** Applying $\log(\mathbf{S}_{\text{mel}} + \epsilon)$ to mimic human perceptual loudness
   scaling according to the Weber-Fechner law.

## Pitfalls

- **Clipping weak consonants during silence trimming:** Aggressive thresholding (e.g., $-25\text{ dBFS}$) prematurely
  truncates soft unvoiced fricatives ($/s/, /f/, /\theta/$) and quiet word onsets, elevating ASR substitution errors.
- **Peak normalization on impulsive audio:** Normalizing files containing transient microphone pops or clicks leaves
  conversational speech quiet, defeating normalization objectives.
- **Phase corruption in audio generation pipelines:** Applying pre-emphasis filtering to training data for neural
  vocoders (HiFi-GAN, WaveGlow) alters acoustic phase; the inverse de-emphasis filter must be explicitly applied during
  waveform synthesis.
- **Resampling after STFT computation:** Frequency bins in an STFT depend strictly on the sample rate. Attempting to
  resample spectrograms via 2D image interpolation introduces severe harmonic aliasing. Resampling must always be
  performed in the raw time domain.
