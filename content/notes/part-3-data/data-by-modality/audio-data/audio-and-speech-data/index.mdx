<Definition>
**Audio data** are sequences of amplitude samples taken at a fixed rate and bit depth on one or more channels, usually
stored in a compressed format. **Speech data** pair such recordings with text: a transcript, word and phone timings,
or speaker turns. **Symbolic music** records notes and their timing rather than sound. The chapter covers how each is
stored, cleaned, augmented and labelled before a model sees it.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Speech recognition, speaker identification, keyword spotting, sound-event detection and music transcription all start
from recordings made on different devices, at different rates, in different rooms. A model trained on clean 16 kHz
read speech fails on 8 kHz telephone audio with background noise. Preparation removes differences that do not matter,
such as loudness and leading silence. Augmentation simulates the differences the model will meet, such as noise and
reverberation. For speech, the text side needs the same care as the audio: the normalisation of a transcript changes
the word error rate as much as many changes to the model.

## Main ideas

**Format fixes what can be heard.** <NoteLink to="digital-audio" /> covers sample rate, bit depth, channel layout and
codecs. The sample rate bounds the highest frequency a recording holds, at half the rate by the sampling theorem
<Cite id="oppenheim2010" />. Lossy codecs remove content a listener would not notice but a model might use.

**Bring recordings to one form.** <NoteLink to="audio-preprocessing" /> resamples to one rate and channel layout,
normalises loudness, trims silence, detects speech and segments long recordings into fixed lengths. The
<NoteLink to="spectral-features">spectral features</NoteLink> that follow, such as the mel spectrogram, belong to Part
II.

**Simulate the conditions of use.** <NoteLink to="audio-augmentation" /> mixes background noise at a chosen
<Gloss name="snr">signal-to-noise ratio</Gloss>, convolves with room impulse responses, perturbs speed and pitch,
simulates codecs and masks bands of the spectrogram, as in SpecAugment <Cite id="park2019" />.

**Speech needs aligned text.** <NoteLink to="speech-transcripts-and-alignment" /> sets transcription conventions and
uses forced alignment to find when each word or phone is spoken <Cite id="mcauliffe2017" />, which supports
segmentation, diarisation labels and training data for timing. <NoteLink to="transcript-normalisation" /> decides how
numerals, punctuation, casing and disfluencies are written before training and scoring.

**Music can be recorded as notes.** <NoteLink to="symbolic-music-data" /> covers MIDI events, piano rolls and score
formats, and their alignment to audio recordings.
