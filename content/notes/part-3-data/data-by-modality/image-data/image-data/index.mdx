<Definition>
**Image data** are grids of pixels, each holding one or more channel values at a fixed bit depth, in a colour space,
stored in a compressed format with metadata. For learning, images are brought to a fixed size and value range,
augmented to build in invariances, and paired with annotations that range from one class label to a mask per pixel.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Classification, detection, segmentation, pose estimation and retrieval all take images as input. The images arrive from
different cameras, at different sizes and orientations, with different compression, and sometimes with more than three
channels or more than two dimensions. A pipeline that ignores this fails quietly. An unrotated phone photograph, a
resize that changes the aspect ratio, or a normalisation that does not match the pretrained network each cost accuracy
without raising an error. Datasets also carry signatures of how they were collected. Models learn these as shortcuts
and fail where the shortcut breaks <Cite id="torralba2011" />.

## Main ideas

**Know the pixel format.** <NoteLink to="digital-images" /> covers channels, bit depth, colour spaces, compression and
orientation metadata <Cite id="gonzalez2018" />. Decoding an image correctly, in the right orientation and colour
space, is the first step.

**Fix size and value range.** <NoteLink to="image-preprocessing" /> resizes with a chosen interpolation, crops or pads
to an aspect ratio, normalises each channel with the statistics the model was trained with, and tiles images too large
to process whole.

**Augment to build invariances.** <NoteLink to="image-augmentation" /> trains on transformed copies: flips, crops,
rotations, colour jitter, erasing, mixup and CutMix, and policies learned or sampled at random. Boxes, masks and
keypoints are transformed with the image.

**Annotations have formats and conventions.** <NoteLink to="image-annotation-formats" /> covers class labels, bounding
boxes, polygons, masks and keypoints, and the <Gloss name="coco" /> <Cite id="lin2014" />, Pascal VOC and YOLO formats,
which differ in coordinate origin, box convention and scale.

**Screen quality and look for bias.** <NoteLink to="image-quality-screening" /> flags blurred, badly exposed,
over-compressed, corrupt and near-duplicate images. <NoteLink to="image-dataset-bias" /> covers capture signatures,
backgrounds, and site and scanner effects that correlate with labels.

**Some images are not photographs.** <NoteLink to="specialised-imaging-data" /> covers medical volumes, multispectral
and hyperspectral images, gigapixel whole-slide images and depth maps, each with its own format and value range.
