<Definition>
**Knowledge graph data** organizes multi-relational facts as directed labeled multigraphs structured into
subject-predicate-object triples $(h, r, t) \in \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{R} \times \mathcal{V}$. Because knowledge
graphs operate under the Open-World Assumption—where unobserved triples represent unknown rather than false
assertions—models require negative sampling during training and filtered ranking metrics during evaluation to prevent
benchmark leakage and false-negative penalties.
</Definition>

Real-world knowledge bases (such as Wikidata, Freebase, WordNet, and Gene Ontology) capture human knowledge across
millions of entities and thousands of predicate relation types. However, knowledge graphs present fundamental
statistical challenges that do not arise in standard homogeneous networks: relations exhibit severe cardinality
asymmetries (1-to-1, 1-to-N, N-to-1, N-to-M), reciprocal inverse relations easily induce test set data leakage, and
training objectives must manufacture synthetic negative triples from unannotated open-world universes
<Cite id="bordes2013transe, dettmers2018conve" />.

## Triples

The fundamental atomic unit of a knowledge graph is a fact triple:

$$
(h, \, r, \, t) \in \mathcal{E} \subset \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{R} \times \mathcal{V}
$$

Where $h \in \mathcal{V}$ is the head (subject) entity, $r \in \mathcal{R}$ is the directed relation type, and
$t \in \mathcal{V}$ is the tail (object) entity (e.g., `(Alan Turing, born_in, London)`).

Knowledge Graph Embedding (KGE) models map discrete entities and relations into continuous vector spaces
$\mathbf{h}, \mathbf{t} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and $\mathbf{r} \in \mathbb{R}^k$, defining a scoring function
$f(h, r, t) \in \mathbb{R}$ that assigns high scores to true facts:

1. **Translational Models (TransE)**: Models relations as spatial translation vectors in Euclidean space
   <Cite id="bordes2013transe" />:
   $$
   \mathbf{h} + \mathbf{r} \approx \mathbf{t} \implies f(h, r, t) = -\|\mathbf{h} + \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{t}\|_{1/2}
   $$
   TransE struggles with 1-to-N, N-to-1, and symmetric relations (if $(h, r, t)$ and $(t, r, h)$ hold, TransE forces
   $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{t}$).
2. **Rotational Complex Models (RotatE)**: Treats relations as element-wise rotations in complex vector space
   $\mathbb{C}^d$ <Cite id="sun2019rotate" />:
   $$
   \mathbf{t} = \mathbf{h} \circ \mathbf{r}, \quad |r_i| = 1 \implies f(h, r, t) = -\|\mathbf{h} \circ \mathbf{r} - \mathbf{t}\|
   $$
   Enables simultaneous modeling of symmetry, antisymmetry, inversion, and composition.
3. **Bilinear Models (ComplEx)**: Uses Hermitian inner products over complex embeddings to handle asymmetric relations
   <Cite id="trouillon2016complex" />:
   $$
   f(h, r, t) = \operatorname{Re}\left( \langle \mathbf{h}, \, \mathbf{r}, \, \bar{\mathbf{t}} \rangle \right) = \operatorname{Re}\left( \sum_{i=1}^d h_i r_i \bar{t}_i \right)
   $$

## Inverse-relation leakage

A pervasive failure mode in historical knowledge graph benchmarks (such as FB15k and WN18) was **inverse-relation test
set leakage** <Cite id="dettmers2018conve" />.

Many relations naturally possess semantic inverses:
- `hypernym` $\leftrightarrow$ `hyponym`
- `parent_of` $\leftrightarrow$ `child_of`
- `filmed_in` $\leftrightarrow$ `location_of_film`

Formally, a reciprocal relation pair satisfies:

$$
(h, \, r, \, t) \iff (t, \, r^{-1}, \, h)
$$

When researchers split triples randomly into train, validation, and test partitions, over $70\%$ of test triples in
FB15k and WN18 had their exact inverse relation present in the training set <Cite id="dettmers2018conve" />.
Consequently, models could achieve near-perfect test scores ($\text{MRR} > 0.90$) by simply memorizing a trivial 1-hop
lookup rule:

$$
P\left( (h, r, t) \mid (t, r^{-1}, h) \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}} \right) \approx 1.0
$$

The model learned no multi-hop reasoning, inductive paths, or semantic representations. This discovery necessitated the
creation of cleaned benchmarks:
- **FB15k-237**: Stripped all reciprocal relations and redundant paths, dropping trivial inverse test triples.
- **WN18RR**: Removed all reciprocal hypernym/hyponym and meronym/holonym pairs.

## Negative triples

Knowledge graphs only document observed positive facts ($\mathcal{D}^+$). Training discriminative or margin-based
ranking losses requires negative samples ($\mathcal{D}^-$) representing implausible or untrue statements:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{(h, r, t) \in \mathcal{D}^+} \sum_{(h', r, t') \in \mathcal{D}^-} \max\left( 0, \, \gamma - f(h, r, t) + f(h', r, t') \right)
$$

Negatives are generated by corrupting either the head or tail entity:

$$
\mathcal{D}^- = \left\{ (h', r, t) : h' \in \mathcal{V} \setminus \{h\} \right\} \cup \left\{ (h, r, t') : t' \in \mathcal{V} \setminus \{t\} \right\}
$$

Selecting corruption strategies depends heavily on relation cardinality:
- **Uniform Random Corruption**: Corrupts head or tail with equal $50\%$ probability.
- **Bernoulli Negative Sampling**: In 1-to-N relations (e.g., `(Christopher Nolan, directed, Inception)`), replacing the
  tail has a high probability of drawing another movie directed by Nolan, inadvertently generating a true fact as a
  negative (false-negative collision). Bernoulli sampling calculates the average tails per head ($\operatorname{tph}$)
  and heads per tail ($\operatorname{hpt}$), corrupting the head with probability:
  $$
  p_{\text{head}} = \frac{\operatorname{tph}}{\operatorname{tph} + \operatorname{hpt}}, \quad p_{\text{tail}} = 1 - p_{\text{head}}
  $$
  For 1-to-N relations ($\operatorname{tph} \gg \operatorname{hpt}$), the model preferentially corrupts the head entity,
  dramatically reducing false-negative collisions.

## The open-world assumption

Standard database systems operate under the **Closed-World Assumption (CWA)**: any fact not explicitly recorded in the
database is formally assumed to be false. In contrast, knowledge graphs adhere strictly to the **Open-World Assumption
(OWA)**:

$$
\text{An unobserved triple } (h, r, t) \notin \mathcal{D}^+ \text{ is unknown / unobserved, NOT false.}
$$

Because knowledge graphs are intrinsically incomplete (even massive knowledge bases like Wikidata miss $>50\%$ of
biographical or scientific relationships), link prediction evaluation must account for unobserved valid facts:

- **Raw vs. Filtered Evaluation Metrics**: When predicting the tail entity for $(h, r, ?)$, the model scores all
  candidate entities in $\mathcal{V}$, ranking them in descending score order.
  - *Raw MRR*: Directly measures the rank of the ground-truth tail $t$. If the model ranks valid alternative answers
    (e.g., other children of the subject) above $t$, raw evaluation severely penalizes the model.
  - *Filtered MRR*: Before calculating rank, all other known true triples
    $(h, r, t') \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}} \cup \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}} \cup \mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$ appearing
    above target $t$ are removed from the candidate pool:
    $$
    \operatorname{Rank}_{\text{filtered}}(t) = \operatorname{Rank}_{\text{raw}}(t) - \sum_{t' \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{true}} \setminus \{t\}} \mathbf{1}_{\{f(h, r, t') > f(h, r, t)\}}
    $$
  Filtered metrics provide the only fair, standardized evaluation under open-world conditions.

## Pitfalls

1. **Benchmarking on Unfiltered Datasets**: Evaluating models on legacy FB15k or WN18 gives completely misleading
   performance comparisons due to inverse relation leakage. Always benchmark on FB15k-237, WN18RR, or YAGO3-10
   <Cite id="dettmers2018conve" />.
2. **Ignoring Filtered Metric Protocols**: Reporting raw MRR or raw Hits@10 instead of filtered metrics severely
   underestimates performance on 1-to-N and N-to-M relations where models correctly rank un-annotated true facts at top
   positions.
3. **Uniform Negative Sampling on Skewed Multi-Relational Graphs**: Using uniform 50/50 corruption on extreme 1-to-N
   relations (e.g., `citizen_of`) generates constant false-negative collisions, corrupting gradient updates. Use
   Bernoulli or self-adversarial negative sampling <Cite id="sun2019rotate" />.
4. **Entity Cold-Start in Transductive KGEs**: Translational and bilinear embedding tables assign fixed vector
   parameters to specific entity IDs. When deployed on unseen entities at inference time, transductive KGEs fail
   completely; production systems require inductive text-augmented encoders (such as KG-BERT).
