<Definition>
**Graph data** consist of nodes joined by edges, with attributes on either. The examples to predict may be nodes,
edges or whole graphs. Because nodes share edges, examples are not independent: what a model may see of the rest of the
graph during training and testing has to be stated explicitly.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Social networks, citation networks, transaction networks, road networks, knowledge bases, molecules and recommender
interaction logs are all graphs. Fraud detection labels nodes, recommendation and knowledge-base completion predict
edges, and property prediction labels whole molecules. Some datasets are graphs from the start. Others become graphs
only when someone chooses which points to connect, and that choice fixes what a graph model can learn. Splitting is
harder than for rows: hiding a node does not hide its edges, and an edge in the test set may be implied by edges left in
training.

## Main ideas

**The graph is often constructed.** <NoteLink to="graph-construction" /> builds nearest-neighbour and similarity graphs
over points <Cite id="vonluxburg2007" />, and graphs of interactions or relations between entities. The choice of
neighbours and thresholds sets the graph's density and what message passing can propagate.

**Graphs come in kinds.** <NoteLink to="knowledge-graph-data" /> stores facts as subject-relation-object triples
<Cite id="bordes2013transe" />. Inverse relations leak test triples, negatives must be sampled, and a missing edge is
unknown rather than false. <NoteLink to="heterogeneous-and-temporal-graphs" /> covers typed nodes and edges, and graphs
that change as snapshots or event streams, whose splits must respect time.

**Structure becomes features.** <NoteLink to="graph-features" /> describes nodes, edges and graphs by degrees,
centralities, clustering coefficients and motifs, and gives positional
encodings from Laplacian eigenvectors or <NoteLink to="random-walk">random walks</NoteLink>.

**Splits must say what is hidden.** <NoteLink to="graph-splits-and-sampling" /> separates transductive splits, where
test nodes are present during training without labels, from inductive splits, where they are absent. Link prediction
needs held-out edges and sampled non-edges. Large graphs are trained on sampled neighbourhoods or subgraphs
<Cite id="hamilton2017graphsage" />.

**Augmentation perturbs topology and attributes.** <NoteLink to="graph-augmentation" /> drops edges, masks node features
and extracts subgraphs, to regularise deep networks and to make views for
<NoteLink to="contrastive-learning">contrastive learning</NoteLink>.
