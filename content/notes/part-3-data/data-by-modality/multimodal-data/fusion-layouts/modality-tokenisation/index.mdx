<Definition>
**Modality tokenisation** is the algorithmic transformation of continuous or non-symbolic signals (2D pixel grids,
continuous audio waveforms, multidimensional time-series streams) into standardized sequences of discrete integer tokens
$\mathbf{z} \in \{1, \dots, K\}^N$ or continuous vector embeddings $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$. This
enables transformer sequence backbones to process heterogeneous perception streams within a unified autoregressive or
bidirectional attention mechanism <Cite id="dosovitskiy2021, oord2017" />.
</Definition>

While natural language text naturally factorizes into discrete linguistic subwords via Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE),
continuous signals (vision, speech, physiological sensors) exhibit high temporal/spatial redundancy, dense signal rates,
and no natural whitespace boundaries <Cite id="defossez2022encodec" />. Tokenisation strategies trade off
spatial/temporal resolution against the quadratic computational scaling $O(N^2)$ of multi-head self-attention
<Cite id="nie2023" />.

## Image Patch Tokenisation (ViT)

The Vision Transformer (ViT) <Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" /> processes images without recurrent or convolutional inductive
biases by flattening images into a sequence of non-overlapping 2D patches:
1. An input image $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ is partitioned into a grid of non-overlapping
   patches of size $P \times P$:
   $$
   N = \frac{H \cdot W}{P^2} \text{ patches}
   $$
2. Each patch $\mathbf{x}_p^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{P^2 C}$ is linearly projected to hidden dimension $d$ using a
   learnable projection matrix $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{(P^2 C) \times d}$:
   $$
   \mathbf{z}_0 = \left[ \mathbf{x}_{\text{class}}; \, \mathbf{x}_p^{(1)} \mathbf{E}; \, \mathbf{x}_p^{(2)} \mathbf{E}; \dots; \, \mathbf{x}_p^{(N)} \mathbf{E} \right] + \mathbf{E}_{\text{pos}}
   $$
   where $\mathbf{E}_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(N + 1) \times d}$ provides 1D or 2D learnable positional embeddings,
   and $\mathbf{x}_{\text{class}}$ is a prepended classification token.

Patch size $P$ governs the resolution-compute Pareto frontier: reducing patch size from $16 \times 16$ to $8 \times 8$
on a $224 \times 224$ image increases token count four-fold from $N = 196$ to $N = 784$, inflating pairwise attention
FLOPs sixteen-fold ($O(N^2)$) <Cite id="dosovitskiy2021" />.

## Vector-Quantised Codebooks (VQ-VAE and VQ-GAN)

To treat images and continuous signals as discrete categorical sequences analogous to text tokens, Vector Quantised
Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAE) <Cite id="oord2017" /> and VQ-GAN <Cite id="esser2021" /> quantize continuous latent
feature maps against a learned discrete codebook:

$$
\mathcal{C} = \{\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \dots, \mathbf{e}_K\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d
$$

### Nearest-Neighbor Quantization

An encoder $E$ maps an input $\mathbf{x}$ to continuous latent representations
$\mathbf{z}_e(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times d}$. Each spatial feature vector $\mathbf{z}_e^{(i, j)}$ is
mapped to its nearest codebook vector:

$$
\mathbf{z}_q^{(i, j)} = \mathbf{e}_{k^*}, \quad k^* = \arg\min_{k \in \{1, \dots, K\}} \left\| \mathbf{z}_e^{(i, j)} - \mathbf{e}_k \right\|_2
$$

The discrete code indices $k^* \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ form a 2D integer token grid of size $h \times w$ (typically
downsampled by factor $8\times$ or $16\times$ relative to $H \times W$), ready for autoregressive generative modeling
with standard causal transformers <Cite id="esser2021" />.

### Straight-Through Gradient Estimator (STE)

Because the argmin quantization operator is non-differentiable ($\nabla_{\mathbf{z}_e} \mathbf{z}_q = \mathbf{0}$ almost
everywhere), backpropagation uses the **Straight-Through Estimator** <Cite id="oord2017" />:

$$
\mathbf{z}_q = \mathbf{z}_e + \operatorname{sg}\left[ \mathbf{z}_q - \mathbf{z}_e \right]
$$

where $\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]$ denotes the stop-gradient operator. The complete training objective optimizes
reconstruction loss, codebook vector alignment, and encoder commitment:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{VQ}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}}) + \left\| \operatorname{sg}[\mathbf{z}_e(\mathbf{x})] - \mathbf{z}_q \right\|_2^2 + \beta \left\| \mathbf{z}_e(\mathbf{x}) - \operatorname{sg}[\mathbf{z}_q] \right\|_2^2
$$

where $\beta \in [0.1, 0.5]$ penalizes the encoder from fluctuating faster than codebook embeddings update.

## Neural Audio Codecs (Residual Vector Quantization)

Raw audio sampled at $24\text{ kHz}$ or $44.1\text{ kHz}$ contains tens of thousands of samples per second. Single-stage
VQ codebooks cannot simultaneously capture acoustic pitch, vocal timbre, phonetic formants, and room reverberation
without massive codebook sizes ($K > 2^{30}$) that suffer from catastrophic codebook collapse
<Cite id="defossez2022encodec" />.

### Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ)

Neural audio codecs like EnCodec <Cite id="defossez2022encodec" /> and SoundStream structure quantization across $M$
hierarchical stages:
1. **Stage 1**: The continuous latent representation $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ is quantized by the first codebook
   $\mathcal{C}_1$, yielding residual $\mathbf{r}_1 = \mathbf{z} - \mathbf{e}_{q, 1}$.
2. **Subsequent Stages $m = 2, \dots, M$**: Each successive codebook quantizes the residual error of the preceding
   stage:
   $$
   \mathbf{e}_{q, m} = \mathcal{Q}_m(\mathbf{r}_{m-1}), \quad \mathbf{r}_m = \mathbf{r}_{m-1} - \mathbf{e}_{q, m}
   $$
3. **Quantized Reconstruction**: The aggregated latent vector is the sum of all $M$ codebook vectors:
   $$
   \hat{\mathbf{z}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \mathbf{e}_{q, m}
   $$

At $75\text{ frames/second}$ with $M = 8$ codebooks (each with $K = 1{,}024$ entries, requiring 10 bits), RVQ encodes
$24\text{ kHz}$ high-fidelity studio audio at only $6.0\text{ kbps}$, representing 1 second of audio as a compact matrix
of $75 \times 8 = 600$ discrete tokens <Cite id="defossez2022encodec" />.

## Time-Series Patches and Value Bins

In multivariate continuous time-series forecasting, point-wise tokenisation (treating each timestamp scalar as an
individual token) produces prohibitively long sequences that drown semantic trends in high-frequency sensor noise
<Cite id="nie2023" />.

### Subseries Patching (PatchTST)

PatchTST <Cite id="nie2023" /> groups consecutive time steps into overlapping subseries patches:
- Given a lookback horizon of length $L$, patches of length $P$ with stride $S$ are extracted:
  $$
  N = \left\lfloor \frac{L - P}{S} \right\rfloor + 1 \text{ tokens}
  $$
- Each continuous 1D patch $\mathbf{x}_p \in \mathbb{R}^P$ is mapped via a linear layer to embedding space
  $\mathbb{R}^d$.
- Patching aggregates local temporal patterns (daily seasonality, transient spikes) into single semantic tokens,
  reducing sequence length by $\approx S\times$ and allowing transformers to attend across long historical contexts.

### Quantile Value Binning and Numerical Tokenisation

For tabular sensors and autoregressive numerical modeling, continuous values $x \in \mathbb{R}$ are discretized into
categorical integer tokens using:
- **Quantile Binning**: Dividing training data into $B$ equal-frequency bins: $b(x) = k$ if $x \in [q_{k-1}, q_k)$,
  maximizing entropy across vocabulary tokens.
- **$\mu$-Law Companding**: Logarithmic non-linear companding
  $F(x) = \operatorname{sgn}(x) \frac{\ln(1 + \mu |x|)}{\ln(1 + \mu)}$, standardly used in speech and physical waveform
  digitisation.

## Pitfalls

1. **Token Rate Imbalance in Interleaved Multimodal Sequences**: When streaming synchronized video, audio, and text,
   video generates thousands of tokens per second while text generates fewer than five. Naive concatenation causes
   visual tokens to monopolize the context window and self-attention compute, starving text reasoning unless visual
   token pruning or cross-attention perceiver resamplers are used.
2. **Codebook Collapse in Vector Quantization**: During VQ training, a small subset of codebook vectors can become
   inactive (dead codes) while a handful of dominant vectors absorb all assignments, degrading effective vocabulary
   capacity. Codebook renewal mechanisms (reinitializing inactive vectors to randomly sampled encoder outputs) are
   necessary.
3. **Information Bottlenecks in Small Patch Sizes**: Setting patch size too large (e.g. $P = 32$) blurs high-frequency
   spatial edges and thin textual characters in document understanding, causing downstream OCR and vision-language
   models to hallucinate.
