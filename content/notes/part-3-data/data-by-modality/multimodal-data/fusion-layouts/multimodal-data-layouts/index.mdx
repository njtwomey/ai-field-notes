<Definition>
A data layout for fusion is the arrangement in which multimodal inputs reach a model. In early fusion the modalities'
features are concatenated into one input. In late fusion each modality goes to its own model and the predictions are
combined. In intermediate fusion each modality is encoded separately and the encodings interact inside the model. In an
interleaved layout every modality is turned into tokens placed in one sequence in document order. The layout fixes what
a training example is, which interactions between modalities a model can learn, and what happens when a modality is
missing.
</Definition>

## Intuition

The same recordings can be presented to a model in several ways. A video clip with its soundtrack can be one vector of
concatenated visual and audio features, two inputs to two classifiers whose votes are averaged, two token sequences that
attend to each other, or a single sequence of tokens. Each arrangement allows different models and demands different
preparation: how streams are aligned, how each modality is scaled, how variable-length inputs are padded and masked, and
how an absent modality is represented. The terms early and late fusion come from multimedia analysis
<Cite id="snoek2005" />; the survey of Baltrušaitis, Ahuja and Morency adds hybrid and model-based fusion to them
<Cite id="baltrusaitis2019" />.

## Early fusion

Early fusion concatenates the modalities' features into one vector per example, or per time step, and passes it to a
single model.

- **Per example.** Each modality is summarised as a fixed-length feature vector, such as an image embedding and the mean
  of a sensor window, and the vectors are joined.
- **Per time step.** The streams are put on a common time grid and their samples at each step are joined. This requires
  synchronised clocks and a shared rate (see <NoteLink to="temporal-synchronisation">temporal
  synchronisation</NoteLink>).

Early fusion lets the model combine modalities from its first layer. It also inherits every difference between them.
Features on different scales need scaling per modality block (see <NoteLink to="feature-scaling">feature
scaling</NoteLink>); a 2,048-dimensional image embedding beside a three-dimensional accelerometer reading dominates by
number alone; and an absent modality leaves a hole in the vector that must be filled with a placeholder and flagged
with an indicator (see <NoteLink to="missing-modalities">missing and unequal modalities</NoteLink>).

## Late fusion

Late fusion trains a model per modality and combines their outputs: by averaging probabilities, by a weighted vote, or
by a second-level model trained on the first-level outputs (see <NoteLink to="stacking">stacking</NoteLink>).

The layout has two data advantages. A prediction can be made from any subset of modalities, so missing inputs need no
imputation. Each per-modality model can be trained on its own data, including examples that have only that modality.
The cost is that no interaction between modalities is learned below the combination step: a sound that means one thing
with one image and another thing with another cannot be represented. Averaging also assumes the per-modality
probabilities are comparable, which holds only if each model is calibrated (see
<NoteLink to="classifier-calibration">classifier calibration</NoteLink>).

## Intermediate fusion

Intermediate fusion encodes each modality with its own encoder, usually into a sequence of tokens, and joins them inside
the network. The encoders can be pretrained separately, on the plentiful unpaired data of each modality, and the joint
layers learned on the scarcer <NoteLink to="cross-modal-pairing">paired data</NoteLink>.

- **Cross-attention.** Tokens of one modality attend to tokens of the other (see
  <NoteLink to="encoder-decoder-cross-attention">cross-attention</NoteLink>).
- **Attention bottlenecks.** Cross-modal information passes only through a few shared latent tokens, four in the
  reported experiments, and only in layers after a chosen fusion layer. Each modality must condense what it shares; the
  restriction improved audio-visual classification while reducing cost <Cite id="nagrani2021" />.
- **Projection into a <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss>.** LLaVA passes image features through a
  trained projection into the language model's word-embedding space and inserts the resulting image tokens into the text
  sequence <Cite id="liu2023visual" />.

As data, an intermediate-fusion example is a set of tensors, one per modality, each with its own length, padding and
attention mask. Batching pads each modality separately.

## Interleaved sequences

Web pages, books and slide decks mix text and images in an order that carries meaning: a sentence refers to the image
above it. An interleaved layout keeps that order. Each image becomes a block of tokens placed at its position in the
text, and a single model reads the whole sequence.

Flamingo trained on the MultiModal MassiveWeb dataset, extracted from about 43 million web pages. A resampler turns each
image into 64 visual tokens; text tokens cross-attend only to the image that most recently precedes them, and training
sequences hold up to five images <Cite id="alayrac2022" />. OBELICS provides an open interleaved dataset of 141 million
web documents with 353 million images and 115 billion text tokens <Cite id="laurencon2023" />. Chameleon fuses at the
token level: a tokenizer encodes a 512 × 512 image as 1,024 discrete codes from a codebook of 8,192, which share one
vocabulary with text, so images and text are modelled, and generated, by the same transformer
<Cite id="chameleon2024" />.

Preparing interleaved data adds steps of its own: inserting image placeholders or codes at the right positions, masking
which images each text token may see, counting image tokens against the context length, truncating documents without
cutting an image's tokens in half, and choosing which tokens carry the training loss.

## Choosing a layout

| Layout        | One example is              | Learns interactions | Missing modality at prediction | Needs paired data for |
| ------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------- |
| Early         | one joint vector            | from the first layer | needs a placeholder           | the whole model       |
| Late          | one input per model         | no                  | drop that model's vote          | the combination only  |
| Intermediate  | one tensor per modality     | in the joint layers | needs a placeholder or a mask   | the joint layers      |
| Interleaved   | one document                | throughout          | the sequence simply lacks it    | the whole model       |

## Assumptions

- Early fusion per time step assumes the streams are synchronised and resampled to a common rate.
- Late fusion assumes the modalities are informative enough on their own and that their outputs are comparable.
- Intermediate and interleaved layouts assume enough paired or interleaved data to train the joint layers.

## Pitfalls

- **Look-ahead from resampling.** Upsampling a slow stream by interpolation uses its next sample, which lies in the
  future of the time step being filled. For forecasting and online prediction, the slow stream is held at its last value
  instead (see <NoteLink to="forecasting-setups">forecasting setups</NoteLink>).
- **One modality by size.** In a concatenated vector the larger or larger-scaled block dominates the first layer;
  scaling per block or projecting each block to a common size first evens them.
- **Unequal comparisons.** A late-fusion model of two networks has about twice the parameters of either alone, so
  comparisons with single-modality models should account for capacity <Cite id="wang2020gradientblending" />.
- **Uncalibrated votes.** Averaging probabilities from an overconfident model and a calibrated one gives the first more
  say than it has earned.
- **Truncated documents.** Cutting an interleaved document at a fixed length can split an image's tokens or separate an
  image from the text that refers to it.
