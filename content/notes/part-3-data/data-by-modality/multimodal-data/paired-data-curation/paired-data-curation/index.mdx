<Definition>
Curating paired data selects a training set from a large pool of collected pairs, such as images with their alt-text or
videos with their subtitles. Curation removes pairs whose two halves do not describe each other, rebalances how often
each concept appears, removes duplicates and copies of evaluation data, and may rewrite weak captions. For contrastive
pretraining, a well-curated subset of a pool trains better models than the whole pool at the same compute.
</Definition>

## Intuition

Image–text pairs collected from the web are cheap and plentiful, and most of them are poor. An alt-text may be a file
name, a navigation label, a product code or a sentence about something else on the page. Some images appear thousands of
times; some concepts ("photo", "logo") dominate while most appear rarely; some images are unsafe; some are copies of the
test sets a model will be judged on.

A model trained on such a pool learns from every pair equally, so curation decides what the model learns. The DataComp
benchmark fixed the model, training recipe and compute and let only the training subset vary. Random subsets of its pool
always did better when larger. Subsets filtered by an image–text similarity score did best at an intermediate size,
about the 30% of the pool with the highest scores <Cite id="gadre2023" />. Throwing data away helped, provided the
right data were thrown away.

## A curation pipeline

Curation is a sequence of filters, from cheap rules to model-based scores. The DataComp pool of about 16.8 billion
downloaded pairs from Common Crawl shows the typical stages <Cite id="gadre2023" />:

1. **Safety.** Captions flagged by a toxicity classifier and images flagged by an explicit-content classifier were
   removed: about 19% of pairs, leaving about 13.6 billion.
2. **Evaluation-set deduplication.** Images that were near-duplicates of images in the evaluation sets were removed:
   about 3% of the 16.8 billion, leaving about 13.1 billion.
3. **Basic rules.** English captions, captions of more than two words and five characters, images whose shorter side
   exceeds 200 pixels and whose aspect ratio is below 3.
4. **Model-based scores.** Image–text similarity, or similarity of images to a target distribution.

Steps 1 and 2 were applied to the released pool; steps 3 and 4 were the filtering baselines that participants competed
to improve. Balancing, deduplication and caption rewriting, below, are further stages used by other pipelines.

## Filtering by similarity scores

A contrastive model such as <NoteLink to="clip">CLIP</NoteLink> embeds an image $x$ and a text $y$ as unit vectors
$f(x)$ and $g(y)$. Their cosine similarity $s(x, y) = f(x)^\top g(y)$, the quantity behind
<NoteLink to="clip-score">CLIPScore</NoteLink>, is high when the text describes the image. Filtering keeps the pairs
with $s$ above a threshold, or the top fraction of the pool by $s$.

- **LAION-5B** kept English pairs with similarity at least 0.28 under CLIP ViT-B/32, and other pairs with similarity at
  least 0.26 under a multilingual CLIP. The filter removed about 90% of more than 50 billion candidates, leaving just
  under 6 billion pairs <Cite id="schuhmann2022" />.
- **DataComp** found the top 30% by ViT-L/14 similarity to be the best single score filter. Intersecting it with an
  image-based filter, which clusters image embeddings into 100,000 groups and keeps the groups nearest to
  <Gloss name="imagenet" /> training images, gave DataComp-1B: 1.4 billion pairs on which a ViT-L/14 CLIP reaches 79.2%
  zero-shot ImageNet accuracy, 3.7 points above OpenAI's CLIP ViT-L/14 trained with the same compute
  <Cite id="gadre2023" />.
- **Data filtering networks** train the scoring model specifically to filter. A scorer's accuracy on downstream tasks
  does not predict how good its filtered data are: a model with lower ImageNet accuracy, trained on a small high-quality
  set, can produce better training data <Cite id="fang2023dfn" />.

A threshold is a property of the scoring model. The same pair has different similarities under different models, so a
threshold such as 0.28 does not transfer from one scorer to another.

## Balancing concepts

A pool's concepts follow a long-tailed distribution: a few appear in a large share of pairs and most appear rarely.
Score filtering does not change this. Balancing caps how many pairs any one concept contributes.

MetaCLIP reconstructs the curation described for the original CLIP data. It matches each caption against a list of
500,000 metadata entries (words and phrases) by substring matching. In a pool of 1.6 billion pairs with 5.6 billion
matches, 114,000 entries matched nothing, and the 16,000 entries with more than 20,000 matches, 3.2% of all entries,
accounted for 94.5% of matches. Balancing keeps every pair of an entry with fewer than $t = 20{,}000$ matches and
samples a pair of a more frequent entry with probability $t$ divided by that entry's count. Of 54 million captions
containing "photo", the balanced set keeps 20,000 <Cite id="xu2024metaclip" />.

Balancing matters as much as matching. Trained on 400 million pairs with a ViT-B/32 under a fixed budget, matching alone
reached 60.8% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy and matching with balancing 65.5%. At 400 million pairs, MetaCLIP reached
70.8% against CLIP's 68.3% with ViT-B and 76.2% against 75.5% with ViT-L <Cite id="xu2024metaclip" />. Balancing uses no
learned scorer, so it does not import a scorer's preferences.

## Deduplication

Duplicates waste compute and overweight what they repeat. Exact duplicates are found by hashing URLs, captions or image
bytes; near duplicates, such as resized or re-encoded copies, by perceptual hashes or nearest neighbours in an embedding
space (see <NoteLink to="deduplication-and-record-linkage">deduplication and record linkage</NoteLink> and
<NoteLink to="locality-sensitive-hashing">locality-sensitive hashing</NoteLink>).

Semantic deduplication goes further and removes pairs that differ in pixels but carry the same content. SemDeDup
clusters embeddings and removes near neighbours within each cluster; on a LAION subset it removed 50% of the data with
little loss in performance, halving training time <Cite id="abbas2023" />.

Deduplication against evaluation sets is a separate requirement: a model that has seen a test image during training is
not tested on it. The same problem for text corpora is treated in
<NoteLink to="benchmark-contamination">benchmark contamination</NoteLink>.

## Caption rewriting

Filtering discards a pair whose caption is weak even when its image is useful. Rewriting keeps the image and improves
the text. LaCLIP asks a <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss> to rewrite each caption while keeping its
key concepts and, during training, pairs each image at random with its original caption or a rewrite. Zero-shot ImageNet
accuracy rose by 8.2 points when training on CC12M and by 2.4 points on LAION-400M <Cite id="fan2023laclip" />.
Recap-DataComp-1B replaced the captions of 1.3 billion DataComp images with descriptions written by a vision-language
model built on LLaMA-3-8B <Cite id="li2024recap" />.

A captioning model writes what it sees in its own style and vocabulary. Its errors and hallucinations enter the training
data, and its uniform style removes the variety of human captions; mixing original and rewritten captions keeps both.

## Assumptions

- The scoring model's notion of a matching pair is the notion the trained model needs.
- The evaluation tasks used to compare filters represent the uses of the trained model.
- Concepts in the metadata or clusters used for balancing cover the concepts that matter.

## Pitfalls

- **Filters inherit their scorer's biases.** A study of CLIP filtering in DataComp found that data associated with
  LGBTQ+ people, older women and younger men are excluded at higher rates, that groups already under-represented lose a
  larger share, that the explicit-content filter missed sexually explicit material, and that copyrighted content was
  kept at high rates <Cite id="hong2024clipfilter" />. The figure shows the mechanism: pairs unlike the scorer's
  training data score lower and are the first correct pairs to go.
- **Scores reward text in images.** An image that contains its caption's words, such as a poster or a slide, scores high
  without showing anything; nearly 40% of LAION images contain text that overlaps significantly with the caption.
  T-MARS masks the text in each image before scoring, so that only visual agreement counts
  <Cite id="maini2024tmars" />.
- **Filters tuned to the benchmark.** The image-based filter keeps images that resemble ImageNet; ImageNet accuracy then
  rewards it. Gains on the target benchmark overstate gains elsewhere.
- **Circular evaluation.** A dataset filtered by a CLIP score and a generator evaluated by CLIPScore under the same
  model measure the same preference twice.
- **Transferring thresholds.** A similarity threshold chosen for one scoring model means something else under another.
- **Deleted is not gone.** A released index of URLs keeps pointing at whatever the URLs serve later, including content
  that a later audit flags. Curation is repeated when the pool is refreshed.
