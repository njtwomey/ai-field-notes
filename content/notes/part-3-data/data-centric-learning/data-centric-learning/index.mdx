<Definition>
**Data-centric learning** treats the training data as the main object of engineering. It
holds the model and training procedure fixed and changes the data: what the labels mean and how they are made, which
examples are added or created, which are kept and in what proportion, and which are fixed after a model reveals a
problem.
</Definition>

## What it is for

For many applied problems, standard architectures and training recipes are good enough, and the remaining error comes
from the data: ambiguous label definitions, disagreeing annotators, gaps in coverage, duplicates, and examples that
teach shortcuts. Changing the data then helps more than changing the model. The same holds at scale: the filtering,
mixture and ordering of a pretraining corpus decide much of a large model's ability, and a well-chosen subset can train
as well as the whole <Cite id="sorscher2022pruning" />. The subject turns this into methods with measurable effects.

## Main ideas

**Labels are designed, measured and aggregated.** <NoteLink to="labelling-and-annotation" /> opens the chapter on making
labels: schemas, guidelines, pilots and adjudication. <NoteLink to="label-definition" /> fixes the prediction time,
outcome window and cohort that define a target. <NoteLink to="annotator-agreement" /> measures consensus with
chance-corrected statistics, and <NoteLink to="annotator-aggregation" /> combines raters by majority vote, reliability
models such as Dawid-Skene, or soft labels. <NoteLink to="annotation-operations" />,
<NoteLink to="model-generated-labels" /> and <NoteLink to="delayed-and-partial-labels" /> cover running campaigns,
labels made by models, and labels that arrive late or incomplete.

**Examples can be created.** <NoteLink to="augmentation-and-synthetic-data" /> covers label-preserving transforms,
<NoteLink to="self-supervised-data-design">self-supervised data design</NoteLink>,
<NoteLink to="synthetic-data">synthetic data</NoteLink> and its evaluation, and simulation with domain randomisation.

**Examples can be valued and chosen.** <NoteLink to="data-selection-and-valuation" /> covers Shapley values and
<NoteLink to="data-valuation">influence functions</NoteLink> per example,
<NoteLink to="coresets-and-data-pruning">coresets, pruning and distillation</NoteLink>, and curricula, mixtures and
<NoteLink to="curricula-and-data-mixtures">scaling laws</NoteLink>.

**Errors point to the data to fix.** <NoteLink to="error-analysis-and-data-debugging" /> samples and categorises a
model's errors <Cite id="ng2018yearning" />, uses <NoteLink to="training-dynamics-diagnostics">training
dynamics</NoteLink> and hard examples to find problem data, and collects targeted examples for failing slices.

## How the notes fit together

The four chapters follow the life of a training set:

1. **Labelling and annotation** makes the labels, starting at <NoteLink to="labelling-and-annotation" />;
2. **Augmentation and synthetic data** adds examples, starting at <NoteLink to="augmentation-and-synthetic-data" />;
3. **Data selection and valuation** chooses among them, starting at <NoteLink to="data-selection-and-valuation" />;
4. **Error analysis and data debugging** feeds a model's failures back into the first three, starting at
   <NoteLink to="error-analysis-and-data-debugging" />.

Labelling comes first because the other chapters assume labels exist. The last three chapters can be read in any order.
