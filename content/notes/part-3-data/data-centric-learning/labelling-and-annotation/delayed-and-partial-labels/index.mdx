<Definition>
**Delayed and partial labels** describe training regimes where the ground-truth outcome is either temporally lagged or
incompletely observed. Under **delayed feedback**, positive outcomes materialize only after an elapsed duration
$D \sim f_D(t)$, causing immature recent records to appear negative due to right-censoring. Under **partial
supervision**, datasets contain either only verified positives alongside unlabelled mixtures (positive-unlabelled
learning) or a subset of candidate tags with unannotated classes (partial multi-label learning).
</Definition>

In production machine learning systems, assuming instantaneous, fully-specified ground truth is almost always wrong. In
online advertising, fraud detection, credit underwriting, medical diagnosis, and large-scale taxonomy tagging, labels
arrive asynchronously or provide only partial evidence. Treating delayed conversions as negative instances or imputing
zero for unannotated categories introduces structural sample bias, destroys probability calibration, and starves models
of legitimate positive signal <Cite id="chapelle2014, elkan2008" />.

## Label maturity

The temporal arrival of ground truth is governed by a **delay distribution** $D = t_{\text{label}} - t_{\text{event}}$.
For any event occurring at prediction time $t_{\text{pred}}$, the probability that a positive outcome ($Y=1$) is
observed within an elapsed observation window $t$ is defined by the cumulative distribution function:

$$
F_D(t) = P(D \le t \mid Y=1) = \int_0^t f_D(\tau) \, d\tau
$$

The corresponding survival function $S_D(t) = 1 - F_D(t) = P(D > t \mid Y=1)$ quantifies the probability that a positive
conversion remains unobserved after elapsed time $t$. The instantaneous conversion intensity is captured by the hazard
function:

$$
\lambda_D(t) = \frac{f_D(t)}{S_D(t)}
$$

| Application Domain | Typical Median Delay | Tail Horizon ($99^{\text{th}}$ Percentile) | Underlying Hazard Dynamics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Search Ad Clicks** | 15 minutes | 24 hours | Monotonically decaying exponential hazard |
| **E-Commerce Conversion** | 3 days | 30 days | Heavy-tailed log-normal or Weibull distribution |
| **Credit Card Chargeback** | 45 days | 120 days | Regulatory dispute window; multi-modal peaks |
| **Consumer Loan Default** | 6 months | 24 months | Discrete monthly billing cycle delinquency thresholds |

When an engineering pipeline trains on historical logs with a fixed observation window $\Delta t$, any positive event
whose delay exceeds $\Delta t$ ($D_i > \Delta t$) is irrevocably lost to right-censoring. If $\Delta t$ is shortened to
permit faster model iteration, the fraction of lost positives $S_D(\Delta t)$ escalates, systematically shifting the
observed target base rate downward <Cite id="chapelle2014" />.

## Recent examples that look negative

In continuous stream learning and daily model retraining, the delay distribution creates a pernicious failure mode:
**immature false-negative poisoning**.

| Record Entity | Event Timeline Status | Elapsed Duration ($\tau$) | Naive Streaming Status | True Underlying Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Record A** | Converted at $t_0 + 2\,\text{days}$ | Matured ($\tau > d$) | Observed Positive ($y = 1$) | Positive ($Y = 1$) |
| **Record B** | In-flight / Conversion pending | Immature ($\tau = 1\,\text{day}$) | **Falsely assigned $y = 0$** | Latent Positive ($Y = 1$) |
| **Record C** | True non-converter | Matured ($\tau \gg \tau_{\max}$) | Observed Negative ($y = 0$) | True Negative ($Y = 0$) |

If an example is ingested into training before its observation window matures ($t_{\text{elapsed}} < \tau_{\max}$), an
unobserved positive behaves identically to a true negative ($y = 0$). Training a standard binary cross-entropy loss
directly on recent logs forces the model to learn that recent features (e.g., newly launched items, recent marketing
campaigns, or current user sessions) have a near-zero conversion probability.

### Chapelle's delayed feedback model (DFM)

Olivier Chapelle formalized a survival-analytic likelihood that resolves this dilemma without discarding recent training
records <Cite id="chapelle2014" />. Let $Y \in \{0, 1\}$ denote the latent conversion indicator, and let $D \ge 0$
denote the conversion delay with conditional density $p(D \mid Y=1, \mathbf{x})$.

For an observed record with elapsed observation time $t$, the system observes a binary indicator
$Y_{\text{obs}} \in \{0, 1\}$ and elapsed time $t$:
1. If a conversion was observed ($Y_{\text{obs}} = 1$ at delay $d \le t$):
   $$
   P(Y_{\text{obs}}=1, D=d \mid \mathbf{x}) = P(Y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) \, p(D=d \mid Y=1, \mathbf{x})
   $$
2. If no conversion has been observed yet by elapsed time $t$ ($Y_{\text{obs}} = 0$):
   $$
   P(Y_{\text{obs}}=0 \mid \mathbf{x}, t) = P(Y=0 \mid \mathbf{x}) + P(Y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) \, P(D > t \mid Y=1, \mathbf{x})
   $$

The complete log-likelihood for a dataset of $N$ examples combines both regimes:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log \mathcal{L} &= \sum_{i:\, y_i = 1} \Big[ \log P(Y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i) + \log p(D=d_i \mid Y=1, \mathbf{x}_i) \Big] \\
&\quad + \sum_{i:\, y_i = 0} \log \Big[ 1 - P(Y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i) + P(Y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i) S_D(t_i \mid \mathbf{x}_i) \Big]
\end{aligned}
$$

By modeling conversion probability $P(Y=1 \mid \mathbf{x})$ and delay hazard $S_D(t \mid \mathbf{x})$ jointly (often
parameterized with exponential or Weibull survival heads), the gradient from unconverted examples is discounted by the
survival probability $S_D(t_i)$. An unconverted example that is only 2 hours old contributes minimal negative gradient,
whereas an unconverted example that is 45 days old contributes maximal negative gradient.

## Positive-unlabelled data

In many classification problems, explicit negative verification is impossible:
- In drug discovery, known molecule-disease interactions are documented ($s=1$), but unrecorded pairs ($s=0$) include
  both inactive compounds and unexamined active drugs.
- In fraud detection or cybersecurity, confirmed intrusions are flagged ($s=1$), while unflagged sessions ($s=0$)
  contain both legitimate users and undetected breaches.
- In recommender systems, user clicks indicate interest ($s=1$), but unclicked items ($s=0$) include both uninteresting
  items and relevant items never surfaced to the user.

This setting is **positive-unlabelled (PU) learning** <Cite id="elkan2008, bekker2020" />. Formally, let
$y \in \{0, 1\}$ be the true latent class, and let $s \in \{0, 1\}$ be the binary labelling indicator. The dataset
satisfies the one-sided verification condition:

$$
s = 1 \implies y = 1, \quad P(y=0 \mid s=1) = 0
$$

When $s = 0$, the example is drawn from an unlabelled mixture distribution:

$$
p(\mathbf{x} \mid s=0) = (1 - c) \, p(\mathbf{x} \mid y=1) + p(\mathbf{x} \mid y=0)
$$

### Selected completely at random (SCAR)

Under the Selected Completely At Random (SCAR) assumption, the probability of an example being labelled depends solely
on its positive status, independent of its feature values $\mathbf{x}$ <Cite id="elkan2008" />:

$$
P(s=1 \mid y=1, \mathbf{x}) = P(s=1 \mid y=1) = c
$$

where $c \in (0, 1]$ is the constant **label frequency** or labelling propensity. Charles Elkan and Keith Noto proved
that training a standard probabilistic classifier $g(\mathbf{x}) = P(s=1 \mid \mathbf{x})$ to separate labelled examples
($s=1$) from unlabelled examples ($s=0$) yields a monotonic surrogate of the true posterior:

$$
P(s=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = P(s=1 \mid y=1, \mathbf{x}) \, P(y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = c \cdot P(y=1 \mid \mathbf{x})
$$

Consequently, the true class posterior is recovered by simple rescaling:

$$
P(y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = \frac{P(s=1 \mid \mathbf{x})}{c}
$$

To estimate $c$ without external ground truth, Elkan and Noto leverage a validation set $V_+$ of verified positive
examples:

$$
\hat{c} = \frac{1}{|V_+|} \sum_{i \in V_+} g(\mathbf{x}_i)
$$

| Calibration Stage | Mathematical Quantity | Domain Range | Operational Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Apparent Classifier Output** | $g(\mathbf{x}) = P(s=1 \mid \mathbf{x})$ | $[0, c]$ | Predicted probability of being an observed label |
| **Calibration Scaling Factor** | $1 / c$ | $[1, \infty)$ | Inverse label propensity multiplier |
| **Calibrated Posterior Estimate** | $P(y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = g(\mathbf{x}) / c$ | $[0, 1]$ | Unbiased estimate of true positive probability |

### Selected at random (SAR) and unbiased risk estimators

When the probability of observation depends on observable covariates $\mathbf{x}$—such as search algorithms displaying
higher-priced goods more frequently—the SCAR assumption is violated. Under Selected At Random (SAR), the propensity
becomes covariate-dependent: $e_s(\mathbf{x}) = P(s=1 \mid y=1, \mathbf{x})$ <Cite id="bekker2020" />. Calibrating the
posterior requires propensity score reweighting:

$$
P(y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = \frac{P(s=1 \mid \mathbf{x})}{e_s(\mathbf{x})}
$$

Furthermore, du Plessis et al. established the **unbiased PU risk estimator**, which expresses the supervised risk
$\mathcal{R}(g) = \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}, y)}[\ell(g(\mathbf{x}), y)]$ purely in terms of positive marginals
$\mathcal{R}_+(g) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \mid y=1}[\ell(g(\mathbf{x}), 1)]$, positive loss under false negative
assignment $\mathcal{R}_-(g) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \mid y=1}[\ell(g(\mathbf{x}), -1)]$, and unlabelled marginals
$\mathcal{R}_U^-(g) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x} \sim U}[\ell(g(\mathbf{x}), -1)]$:

$$
\mathcal{R}_{\text{PU}}(g) = \pi \, \mathcal{R}_+(g) - \pi \, \mathcal{R}_-(g) + \mathcal{R}_U^-(g)
$$

where $\pi = P(y=1)$ is the class prior. This formulation guarantees that model optimization does not require imputing
synthetic negative labels to unlabelled records.

## Unannotated classes in multi-label sets

In extreme multi-label classification and large-scale vision or clinical datasets (e.g., ICD-10 medical coding, Open
Images), human annotators or automated pipelines only verify a small subset of candidate classes per document or image
<Cite id="cour2011" />. 

A standard multi-label target vector contains $K$ classes: $\mathbf{y}_i = (y_{i1}, \dots, y_{iK})$. When annotators are
tasked with identifying specific tags, unannotated classes are ambiguous:
- **Positive ($y_{ik} = +1$)**: Explicitly confirmed present.
- **Negative ($y_{ik} = -1$)**: Explicitly evaluated and confirmed absent.
- **Unannotated ($y_{ik} = \varnothing$)**: Never evaluated.

### The zero-imputation penalty

The naive engineering default is **negative sampling / zero-imputation**: assuming that any unannotated class is
negative ($y_{ik} = 0$). In datasets where positive class co-occurrences are common, this assumption causes severe
optimization pathology. As the label vocabulary scales into thousands of categories, zero-imputation floods gradient
updates with spurious false-negative signals, destroying model recall on high-frequency concepts <Cite id="cour2011" />.

### Tri-state masked loss formulation

To prevent negative gradient poisoning, multi-label pipelines adopt explicit tri-state supervision
$\mathbf{y}_i \in \{+1, -1, \varnothing\}^K$ with an observation mask matrix
$M_{ik} = \mathbb{I}(y_{ik} \ne \varnothing)$. The training objective is restricted strictly to evaluated categories:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{partial}}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i, \mathbf{y}_i, M_i) &= -\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^K M_{ik}} \sum_{k=1}^K M_{ik} \Big[ \mathbb{I}(y_{ik} = +1) \log \sigma(\hat{y}_{ik}) \\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad + \mathbb{I}(y_{ik} = -1) \log (1 - \sigma(\hat{y}_{ik})) \Big]
\end{aligned}
$$

Unannotated entries produce zero loss and zero gradient, preserving head-class representations while allowing candidate
labels to be resolved during semi-supervised refinement.

The contrast between these two supervision regimes is evident when evaluating partially annotated instances (e.g., an
image of a sheep on grass where only `"sheep"` is verified):

| Supervision Regime | Loss Formulation | Unannotated Class Treatment | Backpropagation Dynamics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zero-Imputation (Corrupted)** | $\sum_{k=1}^K \text{BCE}(\sigma(\hat{y}_{ik}), y_{ik}^{\text{imputed}})$ | Imputed as negative ($y=0$) | Actively penalizes valid visual detections (e.g., produces negative gradients for grass and field) |
| **Tri-State Masked Loss (Unbiased)** | $\frac{1}{\sum_k M_{ik}} \sum_{k: M_{ik}=1} \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\sigma(\hat{y}_{ik}), y_{ik})$ | Masked ($M_{ik} = 0$) | Zero gradient on unannotated classes; updates propagate strictly through verified supervisory targets |

## Pitfalls

1. **Uncalibrated continuous retraining**: Ingesting raw streaming events directly into model training without delay
   compensation causes severe prediction depression on newly deployed entities, creating an artificial negative bias
   against fresh inventory.
2. **Naive PU probability thresholding**: Evaluating unadjusted model predictions
   $g(\mathbf{x}) = P(s=1 \mid \mathbf{x})$ directly with standard decision thresholds (e.g., $\tau = 0.5$) when true
   positive prevalence is high but labelling frequency $c$ is low. If $c = 0.1$, the maximum attainable model score may
   be $0.10$, causing zero positive classifications under a naive $0.5$ threshold.
3. **Assuming exponential delay on non-exponential processes**: Using a single parameter exponential hazard
   $\lambda e^{-\lambda t}$ when conversion delay exhibits multi-modal weekly spikes (e.g., payroll cycles or weekend
   shopping delays) creates structural miscalibration at critical elapsed intervals.
4. **Negative feedback loops from delayed conversion suppression**: If a recommendation engine reduces traffic to new
   items because their immature conversion labels look negative, those items never accumulate sufficient impressions to
   convert, permanently suppressing viable catalog candidates.
5. **Masking without negative verification in partial sets**: If annotators only label positives and never explicitly
   verify true negatives, masking unannotated classes reduces the loss to positive-only cross-entropy, causing logits to
   diverge toward $+\infty$ unless bounded by background negative sampling or PU risk regularization.
