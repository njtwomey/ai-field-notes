<Definition>
**Augmentation** creates new training examples by transforming existing ones in ways that should not change their label.
**Synthetic data** are examples produced from scratch by a generator, a program, a <Gloss name="language-model">language
model</Gloss> or a simulator. Both add examples without new collection, and both are useful only as far as the created
examples resemble the data a model will meet.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Labelled data are scarce, expensive, private or missing the rare cases that matter. Augmentation multiplies the
examples a model sees and builds in invariances, such as to flips, crops or background noise. Synthetic data go further:
they fill gaps in coverage, replace records that cannot be shared, and supply exact labels for tasks where labelling is
hard, such as depth, pose or the answer to a generated maths problem. Both have limits. A transform that changes the
label teaches the wrong thing. A generator that misses the tails of the real distribution teaches the model to miss them
too, and models trained repeatedly on their own outputs degrade <Cite id="shumailov2024collapse" />.

## Main ideas

**Augmentation encodes invariance and regularises.** <NoteLink to="augmentation-principles" /> explains augmentation as
an <NoteLink to="inductive-bias">inductive bias</NoteLink> towards invariance or equivariance, as a data-dependent
regulariser, and as the engine of consistency training. Mixing methods such as mixup interpolate inputs and labels
<Cite id="zhang2018mixup" />.

**In self-supervision, the data design is the supervision.** <NoteLink to="self-supervised-data-design" /> covers
pretext tasks, augmentation pairs whose shared content the representation learns, and the composition of the
pretraining corpus.

**Synthesis has many generators.** <NoteLink to="synthetic-data" /> covers parametric recipes, datasets produced by
programs, generators for <NoteLink to="tabular-data">tabular data</NoteLink> such as CTGAN, data generated by language
models, privacy-preserving synthesis and model collapse. <NoteLink to="synthetic-data-evaluation" /> measures utility by
training on synthetic and testing on real data, separates fidelity from diversity with precision and recall in feature
space, and checks for memorised training records.

**Simulators give ground truth with a reality gap.** <NoteLink to="simulation-and-domain-randomisation" /> trains in
simulation, randomises textures, lighting and physics so that reality looks like one more variation
<Cite id="tobin2017domain" />, and adapts across the remaining gap.
