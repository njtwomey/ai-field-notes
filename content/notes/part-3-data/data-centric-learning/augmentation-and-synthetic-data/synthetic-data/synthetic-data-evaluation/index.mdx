<Definition>
**Evaluating synthetic data** is the rigorous multi-dimensional assessment of artificially manufactured datasets. It
measures downstream operational efficacy through **Train on Synthetic, Test on Real (TSTR)** benchmarks, mathematically
disentangles realism (**fidelity / precision**) from mode coverage (**diversity / recall**), and audits generators
against privacy leakage and verbatim training record **memorisation**.
</Definition>

Validating synthetic data is fundamentally harder than evaluating supervised models. A synthetic dataset cannot be
verified simply by computing an average log-likelihood or visually inspecting a curated gallery of cherry-picked
samples. Generative models can effortlessly fool surface inspections through severe **mode dropping** (repeatedly
outputting a handful of photorealistic samples while omitting the rest of the distribution) or **verbatim memorisation**
(achieving high realism by plagiarizing private training records). A complete evaluation protocol requires assessing
utility, fidelity, diversity, and privacy safety in parallel <Cite id="sajjadi2018, kynkaanniemi2019, heusel2017" />.

## Train on synthetic, test on real

The ultimate test of a synthetic dataset is whether an algorithm trained exclusively on synthetic samples can solve the
target task in the physical real world.

The standard empirical benchmark establishes a four-way comparative matrix:

| Training Split | Evaluation Split | Paradigm Name | Methodological Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}^{\text{train}}$ | $\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}^{\text{test}}$ | **TRTR (Train Real, Test Real)** | **Gold-standard empirical ceiling**: The highest expected performance achievable with genuine data. |
| $\mathcal{S}_{\text{synth}}$ | $\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}^{\text{test}}$ | **TSTR (Train Synth, Test Real)** | **Core utility benchmark**: Measures whether the synthetic dataset imparts generalizable decision boundaries. |
| $\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}^{\text{train}}$ | $\mathcal{S}_{\text{synth}}$ | **TRTS (Train Real, Test Synth)** | **Distribution coverage probe**: Tests whether a model trained on real data recognizes synthetic examples. |
| $\mathcal{S}_{\text{synth}}$ | $\mathcal{S}_{\text{synth}}$ | **TSTS (Train Synth, Test Synth)** | **Internal sanity check**: Often artificially high; measures internal generator manifold simplicity. |

### Relative utility metric

Downstream synthetic utility is quantified by the ratio of TSTR performance to the TRTR baseline:

$$
\operatorname{Utility}_{\text{relative}} = \frac{\operatorname{Metric}(\operatorname{TSTR})}{\operatorname{Metric}(\operatorname{TRTR})}
$$

A relative utility score $\ge 0.90$ indicates that the synthetic dataset successfully preserves the underlying causal
feature-target relationships necessary for downstream deployment.

| Evaluation Paradigm | Training Data | Evaluation Split | Metric Performance | Methodological Validity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **TSTS (Synthetic-on-Synthetic)** | Synthetic ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}}$) | Synthetic ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{test}}$) | $94.8\%$ | **Deceptive overconfidence**: exploits low-entropy generator artifacts |
| **TRTR (Real-on-Real)** | Real ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$) | Real ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$) | $88.5\%$ | **Gold standard reference**: natural real-world ceiling |
| **TSTR (Synthetic-on-Real)** | Synthetic ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{train}}$) | Real ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$) | $82.2\%$ | **True practical utility**: authentic test of production generalizability |

Relying exclusively on TSTS produces a dangerous illusion of success: models easily achieve $> 95\%$ accuracy on
synthetic test splits because the generator's manifold has lower entropy and fewer messy edge cases than the real world.

## Fidelity

**Fidelity** (or generative precision) measures whether individual synthetic samples are realistic: do generated points
lie within the true data manifold $\mathcal{M}_{\text{real}}$?

### Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)

Martin Heusel et al. established FID to measure the Wasserstein-2 distance between Gaussian approximations of real and
generated feature representations extracted from Inception-v3 <Cite id="heusel2017" />:

$$
\operatorname{FID} = \|\boldsymbol{\mu}_r - \boldsymbol{\mu}_g\|_2^2 + \operatorname{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r + \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g - 2\left(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g\right)^{1/2}\right)
$$

where $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_r, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r)$ and $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_g, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g)$ denote the
empirical mean and covariance matrices of real and synthetic feature embeddings. Lower FID values indicate higher
perceptual fidelity.

### Generative precision

Mehdi Sajjadi et al. and Tuomas Kynkäänniemi et al. formalized **Precision for Generative Models** to evaluate fidelity
geometrically without assuming Gaussianity <Cite id="sajjadi2018, kynkaanniemi2019" />.

By constructing a union of $k$-nearest neighbor hyperspheres $B(\mathbf{x}_i, r_i)$ around real points in embedding
space, the real data manifold is estimated as $\mathcal{M}_r = \bigcup_{i=1}^{N_r} B(\mathbf{x}_i, r_i)$. **Precision**
is defined as the fraction of synthetic samples $\mathbf{y} \in \mathcal{S}$ that fall inside the estimated real
manifold:

$$
\operatorname{Precision}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}, \mathcal{S}) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|} \sum_{\mathbf{y} \in \mathcal{S}} \mathbb{I}\left(\mathbf{y} \in \operatorname{Manifold}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}})\right)
$$

A synthetic sample falling outside $\mathcal{M}_r$ represents an unrealistic artifact or hallucination.

## Diversity

**Diversity** (or generative recall) measures distribution coverage: does the synthetic dataset span the full support
and variety of the real world, or has the generator suffered from mode dropping?

### Generative recall

Symmetrically to precision, Kynkäänniemi et al. define **Recall** by constructing $k$-NN hyperspheres around synthetic
samples to estimate the synthetic manifold $\mathcal{M}_s = \bigcup_{j=1}^{N_s} B(\mathbf{y}_j, r_j)$
<Cite id="kynkaanniemi2019" />. **Recall** is the fraction of real training instances that fall inside the synthetic
manifold:

$$
\operatorname{Recall}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}, \mathcal{S}) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}|} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{real}}} \mathbb{I}\left(\mathbf{x} \in \operatorname{Manifold}(\mathcal{S})\right)
$$

| Generative Regime | Real Distribution Support | Synthetic Generator Output | Precision | Recall | Primary Pathology |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Precision, Low Recall** | Modes $\mathcal{M}_r$: Dogs, Cats, Birds | Samples $\mathcal{S}$: Dogs exclusively | High ($\sim 98\%$) | Low ($\sim 33\%$) | **Mode dropping / diversity collapse**: Samples appear photorealistic, but whole distribution modes are omitted. |
| **Low Precision, High Recall** | Modes $\mathcal{M}_r$: Dogs, Cats, Birds | Samples $\mathcal{S}$: Dogs, Cats, Birds + noisy artifacts (e.g. 6-legged dogs) | Low ($\sim 45\%$) | High ($\sim 95\%$) | **Artifact proliferation / hallucination**: High distribution coverage, but off-manifold artifacts corrupt synthesis. |
| **Balanced Equilibrium** | Modes $\mathcal{M}_r$: Dogs, Cats, Birds | Samples $\mathcal{S}$: Dogs, Cats, Birds within genuine support | High ($\ge 90\%$) | High ($\ge 90\%$) | **Target synthesis**: High sample quality preserving complete real-world support and tail coverage. |


Decoupling precision from recall exposes the **truncation trick trap**: generators often sample with low temperature or
low truncation ($\psi \to 0$) to optimize FID or precision. While visual quality appears flawless, generative recall
plummets, erasing tail distribution modes and minority classes.

## Memorisation checks

A naive generative network can achieve near-perfect fidelity and recall simply by copying its training examples
verbatim. In privacy-sensitive settings, this constitutes a critical data leak.

### Nearest neighbor distance ratio (NNDR)

For every synthetic sample $\mathbf{y} \in \mathcal{S}$, compute the Euclidean distance to its closest real training
neighbor $\mathbf{x}_{(1)}$ and its second closest neighbor $\mathbf{x}_{(2)}$ in feature space:

$$
\operatorname{NNDR}(\mathbf{y}) = \frac{\|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{x}_{(1)}\|_2}{\|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{x}_{(2)}\|_2}
$$

- **NNDR $\approx 0$ ($< 0.2$)**: The synthetic sample is situated immediately on top of a specific training instance,
  indicating verbatim memorisation and replication.
- **NNDR $\in [0.4, 0.7]$**: The synthetic sample is situated between multiple real training points, indicating creative
  generalization along the data manifold.

### Membership inference audits

Audit teams train shadow classification models to predict whether a given individual record was present in the
generator's training set. If the shadow model achieves an Area Under the ROC Curve (AUROC) significantly higher than
$0.50$ (random guess), the synthetic dataset leaks private membership signal and violates privacy disclosure standards.

## Pitfalls

1. **Relying exclusively on TSTS**: Reporting downstream performance solely on synthetic validation sets; this hides the
   fact that the generator has eliminated physical complexity and real-world noise.
2. **FID feature space bias**: Using Inception-v3 features to evaluate non-natural image modalities (e.g. medical
   ultrasound, EEG sequences, or tabular customer profiles); Inception embeddings reflect ImageNet object textures and
   fail to capture domain-specific clinical invariants.
3. **Optimizing fidelity at the expense of diversity**: Restricting generative sampling to high-probability modes to
   artificially boost precision scores while completely discarding long-tail edge cases.
4. **Subtle memorisation masking**: Generating near-exact copies of private patient scans with slight random pixel noise
   or affine shifts; simple distance metrics miss the fact that identifiable anatomical structures were plagiarized
   verbatim.
5. **Evaluating synthetic text without perplexity bounds**: Assessing LLM-generated synthetic corpora solely through
   pairwise semantic embedding similarity; semantic similarity misses subtle repetitive token loops and grammatical
   deterioration characteristic of recursive model collapse <Cite id="shumailov2024collapse" />.
