<Definition>
A **procedurally generated environment** is defined as a probability distribution over Markov Decision Processes
$\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{M}}$ parameterized by a random generation seed $z \sim P(\mathcal{Z})$
<Cite id="cobbe2020procgen" />:

$$
\mathcal{M}_z = \langle \mathcal{S}_z, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}_z, \mathcal{R}_z, \gamma \rangle, \quad z \sim P(\mathcal{Z}).
$$

An agent trains on a finite training seed bank $\mathcal{Z}_{\text{train}} \subset \mathcal{Z}$
($|\mathcal{Z}_{\text{train}}| = K$), while zero-shot generalization is measured on disjoint unseen test seeds
$\mathcal{Z}_{\text{test}}$ ($\mathcal{Z}_{\text{train}} \cap \mathcal{Z}_{\text{test}} = \emptyset$).
</Definition>

## What these environments test

In classical reinforcement learning benchmarks (such as Atari 2600 or MuJoCo), the training and evaluation environments
are identical. This creates a critical experimental vulnerability:

1. **Disentangling optimization from generalization**: Deep neural policies with millions of parameters can achieve
   near-perfect training return by memorizing precise sequences of timing cues or pixel coordinates. Procedural
   generation prevents memorization by requiring policies to generalize to entirely unseen map layouts.
2. **Quantifying the generalization gap**: Generalization capability is formalized as the difference between empirical
   training return and unseen test return:

   $$
   \Delta_{\text{gen}}(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{z \in \mathcal{Z}_{\text{train}}} [J(\pi; \mathcal{M}_z)] - \mathbb{E}_{z \in \mathcal{Z}_{\text{test}}} [J(\pi; \mathcal{M}_z)].
   $$

3. **Invariance to visual distractors**: Procedural engines alter visual textures, background themes, enemy colors, and
   lighting across episodes, forcing convolutional vision encoders to learn robust semantic features (e.g.
   distinguishing a platform from a decorative background) rather than relying on brittle pixel shortcuts.
4. **Evaluating sample diversity**: Measuring test return as a function of training bank size $K \in [10, 500]$ reveals
   how many diverse environmental seeds are required for an algorithm to learn generalizable behavior.

## Catalogue

### Procgen Benchmark
Introduced by Cobbe et al. <Cite id="cobbe2020procgen" />, Procgen consists of 16 diverse 2D arcade games implemented
directly in C++:
- **Games**: Includes *CoinRun* (platform navigation), *StarPilot* (space shooting), *BigFish* (grow by eating smaller
  fish), *CaveFlyer*, *BossFight*, and *Chaser*.
- **High-throughput simulation**: Employs vectorised native C++ rendering, executing over $100{,}000$ environment steps
  per second on a single GPU.
- **Canonical evaluation protocol**: Agents are trained on a restricted training bank of $K=200$ or $K=500$ levels and
  evaluated on the full distribution $P(\mathcal{Z})$. Standard model-free RL algorithms (such as PPO with an Impala
  CNN) exhibit a substantial generalization gap unless regularized with data augmentations (Cutout, Random Crop) or L2
  weight decay.

### NetHack Learning Environment (NLE)
Built on the legendary 1987 rogue-like dungeon crawler *NetHack* <Cite id="kuttler2020" />:
- **Procedural dungeons and permadeath**: Every game run generates a new multi-level labyrinth featuring randomly placed
  rooms, corridors, traps, monsters, and items. Death is permanent; the player restarts from a completely new dungeon.
- **High-level symbolic complexity**: NetHack renders via ASCII / glyph terminal grids ($80 \times 24$ text tiles) with
  over $100$ discrete actions (movement, casting spells, quaffing potions, wielding tools). It tests long-horizon
  memory, common-sense reasoning, and systematic exploration.

### Crafter
A 2D open-world survival game benchmark developed by Hafner <Cite id="hafner2022" />:
- **Open-ended survival mechanics**: The agent must manage health, hunger, thirst, and energy meters in a procedurally
  generated top-down world containing grass, trees, water, stone, and dangerous nighttime creatures.
- **Hierarchical achievement tree**: Features 22 tiered milestones—from harvesting wood and placing a crafting table to
  mining iron and discovering diamonds.
- **Unbiased evaluation metric**: Crafter computes a single unified metric—the geometric mean of success rates across
  all 22 achievements—benchmarking exploration and skill synthesis without manual reward shaping.

## Interfaces and versions

In Gymnasium, Procgen environments permit explicit configuration of training and test seed sets:

```python
import gymnasium as gym

# Training environment restricted to K=200 levels:
train_env = gym.make(
    "procgen:procgen-coinrun-v0",
    num_levels=200,
    start_level=0,
    distribution_mode="hard",
)

# Test environment sampling from the infinite distribution of unseen levels:
test_env = gym.make(
    "procgen:procgen-coinrun-v0",
    num_levels=0,
    start_level=10000,
    distribution_mode="hard",
)
```

## Known problems and limitations

1. **Unsolvable level generation**: Due to random procedural seed generation, a small percentage of layouts may generate
   physically impossible topologies (e.g. a required key placed behind a locked door or an exit blocked by
   indestructible bedrock).
2. **Heavy-tailed difficulty distributions**: Certain generated seeds present vastly higher obstacle density or tighter
   timing windows than the median level, introducing high variance into evaluation metrics unless evaluated across
   thousands of independent episode rollouts.
3. **Exploration versus generalization conflation**: When an agent fails on an unseen level, it is often difficult to
   diagnose whether the failure stems from visual misinterpretation (representation failure) or inability to explore an
   unfamiliar maze branch (exploration failure).
