<Definition>
**Retrieval datasets** are structured benchmark collections consisting of a corpus of documents or passages
$\mathcal{D}$, a collection of natural language queries $\mathcal{Q}$, and relevance judgments $\mathcal{R}(q, d)$
indicating the degree of semantic or topical relevance between query $q \in \mathcal{Q}$ and document
$d \in \mathcal{D}$.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Information retrieval benchmarks evaluate lexical search, bi-encoder dense embeddings, and cross-encoder re-rankers
across distinct search regimes:

- **Passage & Document Retrieval:** Given an unconstrained natural language query, ranking the top-$K$ most relevant
  passages from collections ranging from millions to billions of web documents. Primary metrics include Mean Reciprocal
  Rank (MRR@10), Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG@10), and Recall@$K$ ($K \in \{100, 1000\}$).
- **Zero-Shot Domain Generalization:** Evaluating whether dense neural bi-encoders (e.g., DPR, Contriever, ColBERT)
  trained on general web text generalize without fine-tuning to specialized vertical domains—such as biomedical
  literature, financial statements, legal statutes, or factual scientific claims.
- **Exhaustive vs. Sparse Relevance:** Distinguishing between **pooled, multi-graded expert judgments** (where shallow
  top pools from diverse search engines are manually annotated on multi-point relevance scales) and **sparse
  click-derived single-positive labels** (where only one relevant passage is known per query, treating all other
  unannotated documents as negatives).
- **Open-Domain Question Answering (ODQA):** Retrieving evidence passages from Wikipedia to answer factoid queries
  without knowing the source document in advance.

### TREC Collections

Organized by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) since 1992:
- **Deep Pooling Methodology:** To avoid labeling million-document corpora exhaustively, NIST creates "depth-$K$ pools"
  by collecting the top $K$ documents returned by dozens of participating search systems. Human NIST assessors then read
  and grade each pooled document on ordinal relevance scales (e.g., 0 = Not Relevant, 1 = Relevant, 2 = Highly Relevant,
  3 = Navigational).
- **TREC Deep Learning Track (2019–2023):** Built upon MS MARCO query samples but evaluates participating models against
  deep NIST poolings (averaging $\sim 200$ judged documents per query), resolving the false-negative pitfalls of raw
  click logs.
- **TREC-COVID:** Designed during the pandemic, evaluating search precision across the rapidly growing CORD-19 medical
  research repository.

### MS MARCO

Introduced by Microsoft in 2016 from real Bing search logs (<Cite id="bajaj2016" />):
- **Scale:** Contains 8,841,823 passages extracted from 3.2 million web pages, paired with 1,010,916 real anonymized
  Bing user queries.
- **Labeling Mechanism:** Annotators were shown a search query and a set of candidate web snippets retrieved by Bing,
  marking the snippet that directly answered the user's information need and writing a natural language answer.
- **De Facto Training Standard:** MS MARCO is the ubiquitous training corpus for modern neural retrieval models:
  virtually all modern dense retrievers (DPR, ANCE, Contriever), learned sparse retrievers (SPLADE), and multi-vector
  late-interaction architectures (ColBERT) pre-train or fine-tune their representations on MS MARCO passage pairs.

### Natural Questions (NQ)

Released by Google in 2019:
- **Origin:** Real search engine queries entered into Google search, matched against entire Wikipedia pages.
- **Answer Spans:** Annotators identify a "long answer" (a paragraph, table, or list from Wikipedia completely answering
  the query) and an optional "short answer" (one or more exact entity spans).
- **Open-Domain QA Standard:** Popularized by Karpukhin et al. (Dense Passage Retrieval, DPR), the open-domain split
  splits Wikipedia into 21 million 100-word passages, testing whether a retriever can find the exact supporting
  paragraph from scratch.

### BEIR Benchmark

Introduced by Thakur et al. in 2021 to address the generalization crisis in neural information retrieval
(<Cite id="thakur2021" />):
- **Heterogeneous Diversity:** A unified benchmark evaluating 18 retrieval datasets across 9 distinct domains:
  - *Biomedical:* BioASQ, NFCorpus, TREC-COVID
  - *Financial & Legal:* FiQA-2018
  - *Fact Checking & Science:* SciFact, FEVER, Climate-FEVER
  - *Question Answering:* Quora Duplicate Questions, CQADupStack
  - *Argumentation & Web:* Touche-2020, DBPedia, Robust04
- **Zero-Shot Transfer Focus:** Models are strictly evaluated zero-shot without fine-tuning on the target datasets. BEIR
  established that traditional lexical search (BM25) frequently outperformed early dense neural models (DPR)
  out-of-domain, driving modern dense embedding advancements.

### MTEB (Massive Text Embedding Benchmark)

Introduced by Muennighoff et al. in 2023:
- **Unified Embedding Evaluation:** Evaluates text representation models across eight distinct downstream tasks:
  Retrieval, Re-ranking, Clustering, Pair Classification, Semantic Textual Similarity (STS), Classification,
  Summarization, and Reranking.
- **Global Leaderboard:** MTEB serves as the standard competitive benchmark for proprietary and open-source embedding
  models (OpenAI `text-embedding-3`, BAAI `bge-large`, Cohere `embed-v3`, Mistral `mistral-embed`).

## Choosing a dataset

- **Pre-training or fine-tuning neural search models?**:
  - Large-scale passage / document ranking → MS MARCO (8.8M passages)
  - Open-domain question answering evidence retrieval → Natural Questions (Wikipedia)
- **Evaluating zero-shot out-of-domain search performance?**:
  - BEIR Benchmark (18 datasets across 9 domains; Bio, Finance, Fact-checking)
- **Evaluating deep, multi-graded ranking without false-negative bias?**:
  - TREC Deep Learning Track (2019–2023 NIST deep-pooled assessments)
- **Evaluating a general-purpose text embedding model across multiple tasks?**:
  - MTEB (Massive Text Embedding Benchmark, 8 tasks including retrieval)

## Known problems

1. **False Negatives in Sparse Judgments:** In datasets like MS MARCO, only one passage per query was explicitly labeled
   as positive. If a dense retriever unearths an equally accurate, perfectly relevant passage from another web page,
   that passage is scored as a false negative ($y=0$), penalizing high-capacity neural models. Deep NIST-pooled
   evaluations (TREC DL) mitigate this issue.
2. **Lexical Matching Shortcuts vs. Semantic Reasoning:** Standard lexical baselines (BM25) excel on queries with rare
   proper nouns and keyword overlaps, while dense retrievers excel on vocabulary mismatches and paraphrasing. Benchmarks
   lacking complex lexical mismatch queries fail to demonstrate the true advantages of dense neural embeddings.
3. **Domain Shift and Vocabulary Fragility:** Dense neural models trained strictly on general search queries (MS MARCO)
   often collapse on specialized technical jargon, rare biomedical terms, or financial identifiers in zero-shot domains
   (BEIR), necessitating hybrid search architectures (combining BM25 with dense vectors).
4. **Corpus Size Discrepancies:** Small academic corpora ($10{,}000$ documents) allow linear brute-force cosine
   similarity searches, masking index scalability, quantization loss, and approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) retrieval
   degradation encountered on hundred-million-scale web indexes.
