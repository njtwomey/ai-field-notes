<Definition>
**Weather and climate datasets** are multi-decadal, gridded spatiotemporal archives of atmospheric, oceanic, and
land-surface variables—originating from numerical reanalysis, satellite observations, and coupled general circulation
models—used to train and evaluate data-driven weather forecasting, extreme event prediction, and climate downscaling
models.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Meteorological and climatological benchmarks evaluate models across distinct physical time scales and spatial domains:

- **Medium-Range Global Weather Forecasting:** Predicting 1-to-14-day global atmospheric states on spherical grids.
  Variables span geopotential height ($Z500$), temperature ($T850$), wind components ($U, V$), specific humidity ($Q$),
  mean sea-level pressure ($MSLP$), and 2-meter surface temperature ($T2M$). Performance is assessed via
  latitude-weighted Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Anomaly Correlation Coefficient (ACC) against numerical weather
  prediction (NWP) baselines.
- **Atmospheric Reanalysis:** Harmonizing historical observations (weather stations, weather balloons, satellite
  sounders) with physics-based atmospheric models via 4D variational data assimilation (4D-Var) to produce continuous,
  physically consistent historical records.
- **Centennial Climate Projections:** Multi-model general circulation simulations projecting atmospheric conditions,
  carbon cycles, and sea-surface temperatures across decades under diverse Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs) and
  emissions scenarios.
- **Hydrological Catchment Modeling:** Modeling terrestrial water balance, soil moisture dynamics, and river discharge
  from daily precipitation and temperature time series across geographical river basins.

### ERA5

Produced by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) as part of the Copernicus Climate Change
Service (<Cite id="hersbach2020era5" />):
- **Scale and Completeness:** The fifth-generation global atmospheric reanalysis, providing hourly estimates of
  atmospheric, land-surface, and oceanic variables from 1940 to the present.
- **Grid Geometry:** Native horizontal grid of $0.25^\circ \times 0.25^\circ$ ($\sim 31\text{ km}$ at the equator)
  resolved across 37 pressure levels (from $1000\text{ hPa}$ at surface to $1\text{ hPa}$ in the stratosphere).
- **Machine Learning Role:** Forms the universal training corpus for the modern wave of machine learning weather
  forecasting foundation models—including Google DeepMind's GraphCast (<Cite id="lam2023graphcast" />), Huawei's
  Pangu-Weather, and NVIDIA's FourCastNet.

### WeatherBench (WeatherBench 1 & 2)

Introduced by Rasp et al. to provide standardized, reproducible benchmarks for machine learning weather forecasting:
- **Standardized Splits and Regridding:** Provides preprocessed, horizontally regridded ERA5 datasets ($5.625^\circ$ for
  rapid training, $1.405^\circ$ for medium resolution, and full $0.25^\circ$ resolution), eliminating custom
  preprocessing discrepancies across research labs.
- **Official Evaluation Framework:** Evaluates deterministic and probabilistic forecasts over 1 to 14 days lead times
  using latitude-weighted metrics:
  $$
  \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_{\text{lat}} N_{\text{lon}}} \sum_{i,j} L(i) \cdot \left(y_{i,j} - \hat{y}_{i,j}\right)^2}
  $$
  where $L(i) = \frac{\cos(\text{lat}_i)}{\frac{1}{N_{\text{lat}}} \sum_k \cos(\text{lat}_k)}$ weights grid cells by
  surface area on the sphere.
- **WeatherBench 2:** Expanded in 2024 to evaluate ensembles, extreme weather events (tropical cyclones, atmospheric
  rivers), and direct head-to-head operational comparisons against the ECMWF Integrated Forecasting System (IFS).

### CMIP6 (Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6)

Coordinated by the World Climate Research Programme (WCRP):
- **Model Ensembles:** Integrates simulation outputs from dozens of climate research centers globally (including NOAA
  GFDL, NCAR CESM, Met Office HadGEM).
- **Experimental Scenarios:** Encompasses historical simulations (1850–2014) and future projections (2015–2100) under
  five Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSP1-1.9, SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, SSP3-7.0, and SSP5-8.5).
- **ML Opportunities:** Used to train deep climate downscaling models (super-resolution mapping from $100\text{ km}$
  global grids to $1\text{ km}$ local microclimates) and fast emulators of expensive physics parameterizations (cloud
  microphysics, radiative transfer).

### CAMELS (Catchment Attributes and MEteorology for Large-sample Studies)

Introduced by Addor et al. in 2017:
- **Catchment Diversity:** 671 minimally managed, gauge-observed river basins across the contiguous United States
  (expanded internationally to CAMELS-GB, CAMELS-BR, and CAMELS-CL).
- **Features:** Pairs daily meteorological forcing variables (precipitation, temperature, solar radiation, vapor
  pressure) with observed daily river runoff and static catchment descriptors (topography, soil permeability, vegetation
  indices, geology).
- **Hydrological Benchmark:** Demonstrates that deep recurrent models (Entity-Aware LSTM, EA-LSTM) significantly
  outperform traditional calibrated conceptual hydrological models in predicting un-gauged basin streamflow.

## Choosing a dataset

- **Building global data-driven weather forecasting models?**:
  - Need standardized evaluation and preprocessed regridded baselines?
  - Training full-scale high-resolution foundation models from scratch?
  - ERA5 (ECMWF, hourly 1940–present, $0.25^\circ$, 37 pressure levels)
- **Long-term multi-decadal climate modeling or downscaling?**:
  - CMIP6 (coupled global climate model ensembles, SSP emissions pathways)
- **River discharge, streamflow, and hydrology modeling?**:
  - CAMELS (671 catchments, daily meteorology and runoff time series)

## Known problems

1. **Latitude-Weighted Spatial Distortion on Regular Lat-Lon Grids:** Equirectangular lat-lon grids converge at the
   poles, concentrating an artificially high density of grid cells near the Arctic and Antarctic. Unweighted loss
   functions over-optimize polar performance at the expense of tropical and mid-latitude accuracy.
2. **Autoregressive Error Accumulation:** Weather prediction models are rolled out autoregressively step-by-step
   ($t \to t+6\text{h} \to t+12\text{h}$). Small spectral errors compound exponentially across long rollouts, leading to
   unphysical blurred states or numerical instability unless models are trained with multi-step rollout losses.
3. **Conservation Law Violations:** Pure data-driven deep networks are unconstrained by physical conservation laws
   (conservation of mass, energy, and moisture). Without physical regularization, models can unphysically create or
   destroy atmospheric water mass or atmospheric energy over multi-day rollouts.
4. **Terabyte-Scale Storage and I/O Bottlenecks:** Full-resolution ERA5 spans petabytes of compressed data. Training
   requires specialized cloud-optimized formats (Zarr, HDF5, NetCDF) and distributed sharding pipelines.
