<Definition>
**Mobility and trajectory datasets** are spatiotemporal sequences of chronologically ordered geographic
coordinates—tuples $p_i = (\text{lat}_i, \text{lon}_i, t_i)$ frequently augmented with altitude, instantaneous velocity,
heading, and sensor telemetry—capturing human, vehicular, or maritime movements through urban road networks or
geographic space.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Mobility benchmarks evaluate spatiotemporal machine learning algorithms across several key transport and location
problems:

- **Estimated Time of Arrival (ETA) / Travel-Time Prediction:** Predicting the total duration required for a vehicle to
  traverse a trajectory from origin to destination, conditioned on departure timestamp, route topology, and historical
  network congestion.
- **Destination & Next-Location Prediction:** Predicting the termination coordinates or next semantic point of interest
  (POI) given an incomplete prefix trajectory, testing sequence models on human routine and spatial Markovian dynamics.
- **Urban Demand & Origin-Destination (OD) Flow Forecasting:** Aggregating discrete trip events into spatial grid
  matrices or road network graphs to predict pick-up and drop-off traffic demand over consecutive temporal windows
  ($15\text{--}60\text{ min}$).
- **Map-Matching & Trajectory Simplification:** Projecting noisy, irregularly sampled raw GPS traces onto directed road
  network graph topologies (e.g., OpenStreetMap) to infer exact traversed road segments.
- **Transportation Mode Inference:** Classifying whether a movement segment was conducted via walking, bicycling, bus,
  subway, or private passenger car based on kinematic properties (speed profiles, acceleration, turn angles).

### GeoLife

Collected by Microsoft Research Asia under the supervision of Yu Zheng
(<Cite id="zheng2011geolife, zheng2015trajectory" />):
- **Cohort & Diversity:** Tracked 182 human users over more than five years (April 2007 to August 2012), capturing a
  wide range of daily outdoor movements, including commutes, weekend excursions, and long-distance travels.
- **High Temporal Fidelity:** Over $91\%$ of the trajectories were recorded in high-density mode (sampling every
  $1\text{ to }5\text{ seconds}$ or every $5\text{ to }10\text{ meters}$), providing micro-kinematic details absent from
  sparse fleet records.
- **Ground-Truth Transportation Modes:** A substantial portion of the dataset includes explicit ground-truth
  transportation mode labels (Walk, Bike, Bus, Car, Subway, Train, Airplane, Boat), establishing the benchmark for
  semantic mode segmentation using motion features.

### New York City Taxi Trip Records (TLC)

Maintained by the New York City Taxi and Limousine Commission (TLC):
- **Astronomical Scale:** Encompasses over 1.5 billion individual yellow taxi, green boro taxi, and For-Hire Vehicle
  (Uber, Lyft) trips recorded across Manhattan and outer boroughs since 2009.
- **Attribute Fields:** Records pick-up and drop-off timestamps, pick-up and drop-off locations (originally GPS
  coordinates, transitioned to 263 discrete NYC Taxi Zones in 2016 for privacy preservation), passenger counts, trip
  distances, itemized fares, tip amounts, and payment types.
- **Standard Demand Forecasting Testbed:** Widely used in spatiotemporal graph neural networks (STGCN, DCRNN, ASTGCN) to
  benchmark spatial grid and graph-based traffic demand forecasting.

### Porto Taxi Trajectories

Constructed for the ECML/PKDD 2015 Discovery Challenge:
- **Fleet Scope:** Monitored the complete fleet of 442 licensed taxis operating in the city of Porto, Portugal, across
  an entire continuous year (July 1, 2013 to June 30, 2014).
- **Trajectory Format:** Contains 1,710,670 complete taxi service trajectories. Each trajectory is represented as a
  variable-length list of GPS coordinate pairs sampled at uniform 15-second intervals from start to finish.
- **Challenge Tasks:** Formulated two benchmark competitions:
  - *Destination Prediction:* Predicting the final destination coordinates $(\text{lat}, \text{lon})$ of an ongoing trip
    given an initial prefix snippet.
  - *Travel Time Estimation:* Predicting total remaining travel duration from prefix observations. Winning solutions
    pioneered bidirectional recurrent neural networks and spatial embedding memories.

### T-Drive

Collected by Microsoft Research in Beijing:
- **Sparse Taxi Tracking:** 10,357 taxis tracked over one week in Beijing, generating 15 million GPS points covering 9
  million kilometers.
- **Sparse Sampling Challenge:** Demonstrates the algorithmic challenge of sparse trajectory data where sampling
  intervals average 3 minutes; algorithms must employ hidden Markov models (HMMs) or shortest-path routing over the road
  network graph to infer missing road segments.

## Choosing a dataset

- **Evaluating destination prediction or travel-time estimation (ETA)?**:
  - Porto Taxi Trajectories (1.7M trips, 15s uniform GPS breadcrumbs)
- **Evaluating human mobility, user routines, or travel-mode classification?**:
  - GeoLife (182 users, 5 years, high-frequency GPS + ground-truth transport modes)
- **Forecasting urban transportation demand & graph traffic flows?**:
  - NYC Taxi Trip Records (billions of trips, 263 discrete taxi zones, decade+ archive)
- **Testing sparse map-matching algorithms over road networks?**:
  - T-Drive (Beijing taxi fleet, 3-minute average sampling intervals)
- **Maritime navigation & vessel route anomaly detection?**:
  - AIS Maritime Trajectories (transponder pings, vessel MMSI identifiers)

## Known problems

1. **User Privacy and Location Re-Identification:** Trajectory traces are uniquely identifiable
   (<NoteLink to="trajectory-data">trajectory data</NoteLink>). Because individuals visit unique combinations of work
   and home locations, as few as four coarse spatiotemporal points can re-identify over $95\%$ of individuals in an
   anonymized dataset. Datasets increasingly obscure exact coordinates by aggregating into coarse administrative zones.
2. **GPS Sensor Noise and Urban Canyons:** Multipath satellite reflections between high-rise buildings ("urban canyon
   effect") and tunnel signal blackouts cause large localization errors (spikes of $50\text{--}100\text{ meters}$),
   introducing unphysical vehicle jumps and erratic velocity calculations.
3. **Unequal Spatial Sampling Density:** Fleet GPS pings are densely concentrated in congested commercial city centers,
   while suburban and rural zones suffer from extreme data sparsity, skewing models toward urban core routes.
4. **Missing Road Network Graph Topology:** Raw coordinate trajectories lack topological connectivity. Without explicit
   map-matching onto directed road network graphs, models fail to respect one-way street constraints, turn restrictions,
   and physical overpass/underpass separations.
