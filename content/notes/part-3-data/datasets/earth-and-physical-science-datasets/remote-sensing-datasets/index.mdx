<Definition>
**Remote-sensing and geospatial datasets** are georeferenced collections of satellite, aerial, and synthetic aperture
radar (SAR) imagery—paired with categorical land-cover classes, vector GIS polygons (building footprints, road
networks), or oriented bounding boxes—used to train computer vision models for planetary-scale Earth observation.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Earth observation benchmarks evaluate algorithms on distinctive physical and sensor properties rarely present in
conventional photographic computer vision:

- **Land-Use and Land-Cover (LULC) Classification:** Multi-class and multi-label patch classification mapping spectral
  reflectance signatures to ecological and anthropogenic terrain types (e.g., permanent crops, broadleaf forests, urban
  built environments, inland water bodies).
- **Multispectral & Hyperspectral Bands:** Images extending far beyond red-green-blue (RGB) to include Red Edge,
  Near-Infrared (NIR), and Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR) wavelengths at varying spatial resolutions ($10\text{ m}$,
  $20\text{ m}$, $60\text{ m}$ per pixel), enabling direct computation of biophysical indices such as the Normalized
  Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI).
- **Sub-Meter Structural Extraction:** Very high resolution (VHR) aerial and commercial satellite imagery
  ($0.3\text{--}0.5\text{ m}$ Ground Sample Distance [GSD]) evaluating polygon instance segmentation of complex building
  footprints and topological graph extraction of road centerlines.
- **Overhead Object Detection:** Detecting small, arbitrary-oriented target objects (vehicles, shipping containers,
  marine vessels, aircraft) viewed from nadir and off-nadir angles across vast spatial extents.
- **Temporal & Seasonal Dynamics:** Multi-temporal repeat passes over identical geospatial coordinates, testing
  robustness against seasonal illumination shifts, cloud cover, and rapid land development.

### EuroSAT

Introduced by Helber et al. in 2019 (<Cite id="helber2019eurosat" />):
- **Sensor Platform:** European Space Agency (ESA) Sentinel-2 satellite constellation.
- **Dataset Structure:** 27,000 georeferenced image patches ($64 \times 64$ pixels at $10\text{ m}$ spatial resolution)
  covering 10 distinct LULC categories: Annual Crop, Permanent Crop, Highway, River, Forest, Pasture, Herbaceous
  Vegetation, Industrial Buildings, Residential Buildings, and Sea/Lake.
- **Multispectral Benchmarking:** Provided in both standard RGB format and full 13-band multispectral GeoTIFF files,
  demonstrating that incorporating non-visible infrared and red-edge channels boosts classification accuracy by
  $2\text{--}4\%$ over pure RGB models.

### BigEarthNet

Developed by the Remote Sensing Image Analysis group at TU Berlin (<Cite id="sumbul2019bigearthnet" />):
- **Scale:** 590,326 Sentinel-2 image tiles ($120 \times 120$ pixels at $10\text{ m}$) collected across 10 European
  countries (Austria, Belgium, Finland, Ireland, Kosovo, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Portugal, Serbia, Switzerland).
- **Multi-Label Formulation:** Unlike EuroSAT's single-label patches, BigEarthNet tiles are annotated with multiple
  simultaneous land-use tags derived from the CORINE Land Cover (CLC) database, reflecting real-world spatial
  heterogeneity where forests, pastures, and settlements coexist within a single $1.2 \times 1.2\text{ km}$ footprint.
- **BigEarthNet-MM:** Later expanded to include co-registered synthetic aperture radar (SAR) dual-polarization imagery
  from Sentinel-1, enabling multimodal optical-radar fusion research.

### SpaceNet

A collaborative initiative launched by CosmiQ Works, Maxar Technologies, and AWS:
- **Challenge Series:** Eight distinct benchmark iterations addressing complex spatial analytics:
  - *SpaceNet 1 & 2:* Automated building footprint extraction from sub-meter imagery across Rio de Janeiro, Paris,
    Shanghai, Las Vegas, and Khartoum.
  - *SpaceNet 3 & 5:* Road network centerline graph extraction and automated topological routing (evaluated via the APLS
    [Average Path Length Similarity] metric).
  - *SpaceNet 8:* Flood disaster assessment, mapping building damage and road impassability following extreme weather
    events.
- **Label Precision:** Ground-truth annotations consist of high-fidelity vector GIS polygons rather than axis-aligned
  bounding boxes.

### xView

Created by the Defense Innovation Unit (DIU) and the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency:
- **Fine-Grained Overhead Detection:** 1 million bounding box annotations spanning 60 fine-grained object categories
  across complex scenes: cargo trucks, passenger vehicles, construction cranes, maritime vessels, railway cars, and
  shipping containers.
- **Scale and Rarity:** Features an extreme long-tail class distribution and small target sizes (often fewer than
  $20 \times 20$ pixels), testing multi-scale feature pyramid networks (FPNs) and vision transformers on small-object
  detection.

### Functional Map of the World (fMoW)

Released by IARPA in 2018:
- **Temporal Sequences:** Over 1 million satellite images capturing geospatial locations repeatedly over multi-year
  intervals.
- **Functional Ground Truth:** 63 functional categories categorizing not just physical structure (e.g., "building"), but
  societal function (e.g., hospital, water treatment plant, oil refinery, police station, primary school).
- **Metadata Richness:** Every image includes sun azimuth, sun elevation, satellite view angle, and precise UTC capture
  timestamps, fostering research into multi-temporal representation learning and seasonal domain adaptation.

## Choosing a dataset

- **Evaluating patch land-cover classification?**:
  - Quick single-label multispectral baseline → EuroSAT (27k patches, 13 bands)
  - Large-scale multi-label optical + SAR fusion → BigEarthNet (590k tiles, Sentinel-1/2)
- **Evaluating fine-grained overhead object detection?**:
  - xView (1M instances, 60 object classes, small bounding boxes)
- **Evaluating vector building segmentation or road graph routing?**:
  - SpaceNet (sub-meter GSD, GIS polygon masks, APLS metric)
- **Evaluating multi-temporal satellite sequences & functional land use?**:
  - fMoW (temporal repeats across 200+ countries, 63 facility classes)

## Known problems

1. **Spatial Autocorrelation and Geographic Data Leakage:** Satellite scenes exhibit strong spatial autocorrelation
   (<NoteLink to="remote-sensing-imagery">remote sensing</NoteLink>): tiles cut from neighboring coordinates share
   identical soil compositions, road patterns, and atmospheric weather. Splitting datasets using uniform random tile
   sampling causes extreme test-set leakage. Rigorous benchmarks require strict geographic tiling or spatial blocking
   (e.g., training on one city/country and testing on an entirely different continent).
2. **Atmospheric and Seasonal Domain Shift:** Atmospheric haze, cloud shadows, varying sun angles, and seasonal snow
   cover alter spectral pixel values drastically between satellite passes over the exact same physical coordinates.
3. **Resolution Mismatch Across Spectral Bands:** In multispectral platforms like Sentinel-2, RGB and NIR channels are
   captured at $10\text{ m}$ per pixel, red-edge and SWIR at $20\text{ m}$, and atmospheric bands at $60\text{ m}$.
   Models must employ channel-specific spatial upsampling or multi-resolution feature encoders.
4. **Extreme Small-Object Scale:** Unlike ImageNet where objects dominate the central image frame, overhead objects in
   xView and SpaceNet occupy tiny pixel fractions ($< 0.01\%$ of the scene), rendering standard computer vision anchors
   ineffective without dedicated small-object heads.
