<Definition>
**Earth and physical-science datasets** record or simulate the physical world: satellite images of the surface,
reanalyses and simulations of the atmosphere, computed properties of molecules and materials, solutions of
<Gloss name="pde">partial differential equations</Gloss>, collider events and sky surveys, and the movements of people
and vehicles. They come with units, coordinate systems and physical constraints that ordinary benchmarks lack.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Machine learning in the sciences learns from data whose ground truth is often a
simulation or a physical model. ERA5 reconstructs the global atmosphere hourly from 1940 by combining observations with
a weather model <Cite id="hersbach2020era5" />, and data-driven forecasters are trained on it. QM9 gives
quantum-chemical properties of about 134,000 small molecules computed by density functional theory
<Cite id="ramakrishnan2014qm9" />. EuroSAT turned Sentinel-2 satellite images into a land-use classification benchmark
<Cite id="helber2019eurosat" />. Each dataset inherits the approximations of its source, and each has dependence in
space or time that random splits ignore.

## Main ideas

**Images from orbit are spatially correlated.** <NoteLink to="remote-sensing-datasets" /> covers EuroSAT, BigEarthNet,
SpaceNet, xView and fMoW. Neighbouring scenes leak across random splits, seasons and atmosphere shift the inputs, and
overhead objects are small.

**Weather data are gridded and huge.** <NoteLink to="weather-and-climate-datasets" /> covers ERA5, WeatherBench, CMIP6
and the CAMELS hydrology set. Latitude-longitude grids over-represent the poles unless errors are weighted by latitude,
and autoregressive forecasts accumulate error.

**Chemistry labels are computed.** <NoteLink to="chemistry-and-materials-datasets" /> covers QM9, ZINC, MoleculeNet, the
Materials Project and Open Catalyst. Properties computed by density functional theory differ from experiment, and
random splits put analogues on both sides.

**Physics data test surrogates and discovery.** <NoteLink to="physics-and-astronomy-datasets" /> covers PDEBench and
The Well for neural PDE solvers, CERN Open Data for collider events, and Galaxy Zoo and SDSS for sky surveys.
Surrogates trained on simulations miss fine scales and may not transfer to real detectors.

**Trajectories are personal.** <NoteLink to="mobility-and-trajectory-datasets" /> covers GeoLife, NYC taxi records,
Porto taxis, T-Drive and AIS ship tracks. Traces identify individuals, and GPS noise is worst in dense cities.
