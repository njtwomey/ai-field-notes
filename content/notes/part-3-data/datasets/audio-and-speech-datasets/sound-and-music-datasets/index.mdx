<Definition>
**Sound and music datasets** are acoustic benchmark collections capturing non-speech environmental soundscapes, everyday
acoustic events (e.g. footsteps, sirens, breaking glass), and multi-instrumental musical performances. They formalize
environmental sound classification (ESC), polyphonic sound event detection (SED), music information retrieval (MIR),
automatic music transcription (AMT), and multi-stem audio source separation
<Cite id="gemmeke2017, hawthorne2018, rafii2017" />.
</Definition>

Unlike human speech which follows discrete linguistic phonotactics, environmental and musical sounds exhibit continuous
spectral textures, overlapping polyphony, broad frequency bandwidths ($20\text{ Hz}\text{--}20\text{ kHz}$), and
variable harmonic decay. Benchmarks evaluate whether neural audio backbones (such as PANNs, AST, and Audio Spectrogram
Transformers) can parse complex acoustic scenes.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Sound and music benchmarks formalize several distinct signal processing and audio machine learning challenges:
1. **Environmental Sound Classification (ESC)**: Assigning categorical sound event labels to short audio clips,
   evaluated via top-1 accuracy (ESC-50, UrbanSound8K) <Cite id="piczak2015, salamon2014" />.
2. **Weakly Supervised Sound Event Detection (SED)**: Multi-label classification where clips have overall tags but lack
   frame-level onset/offset timestamps, evaluated by mean Average Precision (mAP) on hierarchical ontologies (AudioSet)
   <Cite id="gemmeke2017" />.
3. **Automatic Music Transcription (AMT)**: Converting continuous polyphonic music waveforms into discrete symbolic MIDI
   events with precise onset timestamps, pitch values, and note velocities (MAESTRO) <Cite id="hawthorne2018" />.
4. **Music Source Separation**: Demixing a complex stereophonic master track into isolated instrument stems (*vocals*,
   *drums*, *bass*, *other*), evaluated via Signal-to-Distortion Ratio (SDR in dB) (MUSDB18) <Cite id="rafii2017" />.

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### AudioSet: The ImageNet of General Audio (2017)

Constructed by Google Research (Gemmeke et al. <Cite id="gemmeke2017" />):
- **Scale and Ontology**: 2.1 million 10-second YouTube video soundtracks mapped onto a structured hierarchical ontology
  of 527 sound classes organized into mutually non-exclusive categories (*human sounds*, *animal sounds*, *musical
  instruments*, *natural sounds*, *sounds of things*).
- **Weak Labeling**: Each 10-second clip is annotated with tags describing whether an event occurs anywhere within the
  window, driving weakly supervised multiple-instance learning and large-scale audio foundation models.

### ESC-50: Environmental Sound Benchmark (2015)

Introduced by Karol Piczak <Cite id="piczak2015" />:
- **Standardized Folds**: Exactly 2,000 environmental recordings balanced across 50 categories (40 clips per category)
  organized into 5 major groups: animals, natural water/wind sounds, human non-speech sounds, interior domestic sounds,
  and exterior urban noises.
- **Strict Benchmark Hygiene**: Pre-arranged into 5 balanced folds where recordings from the same source clip are never
  split across train and test partitions.

### MAESTRO: Virtuosic Piano Transcription (2018)

Created by Google Magenta (Hawthorne et al. <Cite id="hawthorne2018" />):
- **Acoustic Precision**: Captured from Yamaha Disklavier reproducing pianos during ten years of the International
  Piano-e-Competition.
- **Sub-Millisecond Alignment**: The Disklavier records key strikes and pedal movements with optical sensors, producing
  exact MIDI velocity and timestamp files aligned to high-fidelity acoustic audio with sub-millisecond precision.
- **Benchmark Standard**: The gold standard benchmark for automatic piano transcription (Onsets and Frames).

### MUSDB18: Benchmark for Music Demixing (2017)

Created by Rafii et al. <Cite id="rafii2017" /> for the Signal Separation Evaluation Campaign (SiSEC):
- **Isolated Multi-Track Stems**: 150 professional multi-track studio recordings (100 train, 50 test) with discrete
  tracks for *vocals*, *drums*, *bass*, and *other*, summing linearly to the full master mix:
  $$
  x_{\text{mix}}(t) = x_{\text{vocals}}(t) + x_{\text{drums}}(t) + x_{\text{bass}}(t) + x_{\text{other}}(t)
  $$
- **Metric Formulation**: Standard benchmark evaluating Signal-to-Distortion Ratio (SDR), Signal-to-Interference Ratio
  (SIR), and Signal-to-Artifacts Ratio (SAR).

## Choosing a Sound or Music Dataset

- **General Environmental Audio Pretraining**:
  - AudioSet (2M+ clips, 527 classes, foundation pretraining)
- **Environmental Sound Classification Benchmarking**:
  - *Clean 50-class environmental standard*: ESC-50
  - *Urban city noise*: UrbanSound8K
- **Automatic Music Transcription (Piano)**:
  - MAESTRO (Sub-millisecond aligned MIDI + virtuosic audio)
- **Audio Source Separation / Stem Demixing**:
  - MUSDB18 (Isolated vocals, drums, bass, and accompaniment)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Weak Label Temporal Ambiguity**: In AudioSet, a clip labeled *"dog bark"* may contain 9.5 seconds of highway noise
   and a single 0.5-second bark. Models trained under global pooling can assign high attention to spurious background
   cues.
2. **GTZAN Repetition Faults**: GTZAN suffered from documented dataset flaws: identical song fragments appearing in both
   training and test sets, and distorted audio encodings. Modern MIR benchmarks prefer FSD50K or MAESTRO.
3. **Phase Incoherence in Source Separation**: Time-frequency masking on spectrograms ignores phase information,
   requiring complex neural vocoders or complex-valued STFT networks to avoid phase cancellation artifacts upon inverse
   STFT reconstruction.
