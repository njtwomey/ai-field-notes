<Definition>
**Speech datasets** are audio benchmark collections pairing continuous pressure waveforms $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ with
phonetic, lexical, speaker, or intent annotations. They formalize automatic speech recognition (ASR), phonetic
alignment, speaker identification and diarization, keyword spotting, and neural text-to-speech (TTS) synthesis across
acoustic recording environments, microphone channels, and speaker demographics <Cite id="panayotov2015, ardila2020" />.
</Definition>

Acoustic speech varies across speaker vocal tract physiology, regional accents, prosodic cadence, background noise, and
reverberant room impulse responses (RIR). Speech benchmarks provide standardized audio splits and normalized transcripts
to evaluate acoustic models and speech foundation models (e.g. Whisper, Wav2Vec 2.0).

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Speech benchmarks formalize several distinct linguistic and acoustic evaluation tasks:
1. **Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)**: Mapping acoustic frames to orthographic text, evaluated by **Word Error Rate
   (WER)** and **Character Error Rate (CER)**:
   $$
   \text{WER} = \frac{S + D + I}{N} = \frac{\text{Substitutions} + \text{Deletions} + \text{Insertions}}{\text{Reference Word Count}}
   $$
2. **Phonetic Recognition & Alignment**: Transcribing raw phones evaluated via Phone Error Rate (PER) on time-aligned
   acoustic frames (TIMIT).
3. **Speaker Verification & Identification**: Determining whether two spoken utterances belong to the same individual
   ($1:1$ verification), evaluated via Equal Error Rate (EER) under cosine similarity of speaker embeddings (VoxCeleb)
   <Cite id="nagrani2021" />.
4. **Embedded Keyword Spotting (KWS)**: On-device wake-word detection under milliwatt power budgets and low latency
   (Speech Commands).
5. **Text-to-Speech (TTS) Synthesis**: Generating natural human speech waveforms from text, evaluated via Mean Opinion
   Score (MOS) and mel-cepstral distortion (MCD).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### LibriSpeech: The Modern ASR Benchmark (2015)

Introduced by Panayotov et al. <Cite id="panayotov2015" /> from Johns Hopkins and Kaldi contributors:
- **Audio Source**: Read public-domain audiobooks from the LibriVox project, aligned with Project Gutenberg texts.
- **Partitions**:
  - `train-clean-100` and `train-clean-360`: Clean acoustic speech with high signal-to-noise ratios.
  - `train-other-500`: Audio with higher background noise or accented cadence.
  - `test-clean` vs `test-other`: Standard benchmark evaluation sets to measure clean versus acoustically challenging
    degradation.
- **Metric**: Standard testbed where conformer, transformer, and transducer ASR architectures benchmark WER progress.

### Common Voice: Crowdsourced Global Multilingualism (2020)

Initiated by Mozilla (Ardila et al. <Cite id="ardila2020" />):
- **Open Crowdsourcing**: Volunteers read prompted sentences on web browsers and validate others' recordings via up/down
  votes.
- **Linguistic Diversity**: Covers over 100 languages—including under-resourced languages (Basque, Welsh, Kinyarwanda)
  that lack commercial speech corpora.
- **Demographic Metadata**: Annotates self-reported speaker age, sex, and regional accent, enabling rigorous algorithmic
  demographic equity and accent bias audits.

### VoxCeleb: Speaker Verification in the Wild (2017–2021)

Developed by the Visual Geometry Group at Oxford <Cite id="nagrani2021" />:
- **In-the-Wild Challenges**: Extracted from YouTube celebrity interviews containing real ambient babble, background
  laughter, room reverberation, overlapping speech, and varied recording equipment.
- **Multimodal Ground Truth**: Paired with face video frames, enabling audio-visual active speaker detection, visual
  speech recognition (lip reading), and audio-visual source separation.

## Choosing a Speech Dataset

- **Standard English ASR Benchmarking**:
  - LibriSpeech (1k hours, clean/other splits, standard WER metric)
- **Multilingual & Accent Robustness**:
  - Mozilla Common Voice (> 100 languages, demographic metadata)
- **Acoustic-Phonetic Modeling & Frame Alignment**:
  - TIMIT (Hand-aligned phonetic boundaries)
- **Speaker Verification & Biometrics**:
  - VoxCeleb 1 & 2 (1M+ in-the-wild conversational utterances)
- **Embedded On-Device Wake-Word Detection**:
  - Google Speech Commands (1-second keyword clips)
- **Neural Text-to-Speech Synthesis**:
  - LJSpeech (Pristine single-speaker studio recordings)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Audiobook Pronunciation Bias**: Reading aloud from classic 19th-century literature (LibriSpeech) features formal
   syntax, deliberate cadence, and archaic vocabulary that fails to prepare models for spontaneous conversational speech
   with disfluencies (*"um"*, *"uh"*, false starts).
2. **Text Normalization Inconsistencies**: Discrepancies in handling punctuation, numbers (e.g. `"$50"` vs
   `"fifty dollars"`), contractions, and acronyms across transcripts can arbitrarily swing measured WER by
   $2\text{--}4\%$ independent of acoustic accuracy (see <NoteLink to="transcript-normalisation">transcript
   normalisation</NoteLink>).
3. **Overlapping Speech in Conversational Diarization**: In natural human meetings, participants frequently speak
   simultaneously. Single-channel datasets recorded with close-talk microphones fail to represent real cocktail-party
   interference.
