<Definition>
**Medical imaging datasets** are curated collections of clinical diagnostic imaging studies—including projection
radiography (X-ray), computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), digital whole-slide histopathology
(WSI), and dermoscopy—paired with radiologist reports, disease classifications, or voxel- and pixel-level anatomical and
lesion segmentations.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Medical imaging benchmarks span diverse modalities and physiological tissue scales:

- **Thoracic Radiography (Chest X-ray):** 2D projection imaging capturing radiographic shadows of the lungs, pleura,
  mediastinum, and cardiac silhouette. Tasks evaluate multi-label thorax pathology classification (atelectasis,
  cardiomegaly, consolidation, edema, effusion, pneumonia, pneumothorax), weak disease localization via bounding boxes
  or attention heatmaps, and multimodal radiology report generation.
- **Volumetric Neuroimaging (Brain MRI):** Multi-parametric 3D structural imaging (T1, T1-contrast enhanced, T2, and T2
  Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery [FLAIR]) mapping intracranial anatomy. Benchmarks measure sub-compartment
  volumetric tumor segmentation (enhancing tumor core, non-enhancing solid core, peritumoral edema, necrotic regions).
- **Thoracic Volumetric CT:** 3D helical X-ray attenuation volumes measured in Hounsfield Units (HU). Tasks evaluate
  lung nodule candidate detection, 3D volumetric contour segmentation, and pulmonary malignancy risk stratification.
- **Gigapixel Whole-Slide Histopathology (WSI):** High-resolution microscopic digitized glass slides (often
  $50{,}000 \times 50{,}000$ to $100{,}000 \times 100{,}000$ pixels) stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E). Tasks
  evaluate weak-label slide classification, patch-level metastasis identification, and lymph node micro- and
  macro-metastasis segmentation.
- **Dermoscopy:** Epiluminescence microscopy photographing pigmented skin lesions under cross-polarized light. Tasks
  evaluate binary and multi-class classification separating malignant melanoma and basal cell carcinoma from benign nevi
  and seborrheic keratoses.

### ChestX-ray14

Released in 2017 by the National Institutes of Health Clinical Center (<Cite id="wang2017chestxray" />):
- **Source and Scope:** 112,120 frontal chest radiographs extracted from the PACS system of 30,805 unique patients
  between 1992 and 2015.
- **Label Extraction:** Disease labels were automatically mined from radiology reports using an automated rule-based NLP
  tool (NegBio / MetaMap) mapping to 14 thoracic disease categories: Atelectasis, Cardiomegaly, Effusion, Infiltration,
  Mass, Nodule, Pneumonia, Pneumothorax, Consolidation, Edema, Emphysema, Fibrosis, Pleural Thickening, and Hernia.
- **Validation:** A subset of 880 images includes bounding-box annotations provided by a radiologist to benchmark weakly
  supervised localization.
- **Standard Baseline:** DenseNet-121 and ResNet-50 models achieve mean AUROC scores of $0.80\text{--}0.85$ across the
  14 conditions.

### CheXpert

Introduced in 2019 by Stanford Medicine to establish radiologist-level diagnostic benchmarking
(<Cite id="irvin2019chexpert" />):
- **Scale:** 224,316 chest radiographs from 65,240 patients collected at Stanford Hospital, including both frontal and
  lateral views.
- **Explicit Uncertainty Modeling:** CheXpert captures clinical ambiguity directly. For each of 14 observations, labels
  are marked as positive ($1$), negative ($0$), uncertain ($u$), or unmentioned (blank). Evaluation frameworks test
  distinct policies for treating uncertain labels ($u\text{-zeros}$, $u\text{-ones}$, $u\text{-multiclass}$, and
  $u\text{-ignore}$).
- **Expert Benchmark Test Set:** The official evaluation benchmark evaluates five primary thoracic conditions
  (Cardiomegaly, Edema, Consolidation, Atelectasis, Pleural Effusion) on a sequestered test set evaluated against the
  consensus ground truth of five board-certified radiologists.

### MIMIC-CXR

Maintained by PhysioNet and Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (<Cite id="johnson2016mimic" />):
- **Multimodal Linkage:** 377,110 DICOM chest radiographs spanning 227,835 imaging studies from 65,379 unique emergency
  and inpatient visits. Uniquely, each study includes the complete de-identified free-text radiology report (Findings
  and Impression sections).
- **Core ML Tasks:** Forms the preeminent benchmark for clinical vision-language models: automated radiology report
  generation (e.g., Clinical-BERT, multimodal LLMs), image-text contrastive pre-training (e.g., ConVIRT, BioViL,
  MedCLIP), and cross-modal retrieval.
- **Access Protocol:** Protected health information requires completion of human-subject ethics training (CITI) and a
  formal PhysioNet Data Use Agreement.

### BraTS (Brain Tumor Segmentation Challenge)

Organized annually as part of MICCAI:
- **Imaging Modalities:** Standardized co-registered, skull-stripped, and isotropically resampled ($1\text{ mm}^3$) 3D
  brain MRI volumes across four sequences: native T1, post-contrast T1-weighted (T1ce), T2-weighted, and T2
  Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery (FLAIR).
- **Segmentation Targets:** Multi-class voxel masks distinguishing the necrotic core (NCR), peritumoral edematous tissue
  (ED), and active enhancing tumor (ET). Evaluated using Dice Similarity Coefficients and 95% Hausdorff Distances
  ($HD_{95}$) over Whole Tumor (WT), Tumor Core (TC), and Enhancing Tumor (ET).
- **Dominant Architectures:** 3D U-Net variants, nnU-Net, and hierarchical Swin-UNETR transformers.

### LIDC-IDRI

The **Lung Image Database Consortium and Image Database Resource Initiative**:
- **Dataset Structure:** 1,018 clinical diagnostic thoracic CT scans collected across seven academic medical centers.
- **Multi-Reader Consensus:** Four experienced thoracic radiologists independently reviewed each scan in a two-phase
  process (blinded read followed by unblinded consensus), contouring every detected pulmonary nodule between
  $3\text{ mm}$ and $30\text{ mm}$ and providing subjective lesion malignancy scores ($1\text{--}5$).
- **Significance:** Remains the benchmark for 3D pulmonary nodule detection algorithms and radiomics feature extraction.

### CAMELYON

The **CAMELYON16 and CAMELYON17 Challenges**:
- **Data Modality:** Digital whole-slide histopathology images (WSIs) scanned at $20\times$ and $40\times$ magnification
  ($0.25\text{--}0.50\,\mu\text{m/pixel}$) of sentinel lymph node sections from breast cancer patients.
- **Tasks:**
  - *CAMELYON16:* Automated detection of individual lymph node metastases, evaluated by Free-Response ROC (FROC) at
    false positive rates of $0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4,$ and $8$ per slide.
  - *CAMELYON17:* Patient-level pathological nodal staging (pN-stage: pN0, pN0(i+), pN1mi, pN1, pN2) across five
    sentinel nodes per patient.
- **Architectural Approaches:** Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) methods, attention-based slide pooling (CLAM), and
  foundation feature extractors trained via self-supervised learning (DINO, HIPT).

### ISIC Archive

Curated by the **International Skin Imaging Collaboration**:
- **Scope:** More than 70,000 dermoscopic photographs covering diverse lesion categories: Melanoma, Melanocytic Nevus,
  Basal Cell Carcinoma, Actinic Keratosis, Benign Keratosis, Dermatofibroma, and Vascular Lesions.
- **Clinical Importance:** Provides the primary standardized benchmark for computer-aided triage of pigmented skin
  lesions and early melanoma detection.

## Choosing a dataset

- **Analyzing 2D projection thoracic radiographs (X-ray)?**:
  - Pure vision pathology classification & localization?
  - Multimodal vision-language (report generation / CLIP)?
  - MIMIC-CXR (paired DICOM images & free-text radiology reports)
- **Segmenting 3D volumetric clinical scans?**:
  - Multi-sequence brain MRI tumor segmentation? → BraTS (MICCAI)
  - Volumetric chest CT lung nodule detection? → LIDC-IDRI (4-radiologist consensus)
- **Processing gigapixel whole-slide pathology (WSI)?**:
  - CAMELYON16 / CAMELYON17 (lymph node metastasis & pN staging)
- **Dermatology & skin cancer screening?**:
  - ISIC Archive (dermoscopic photographs, biopsy-confirmed labels)

## Known problems

1. **Label Noise from Rule-Based NLP Extraction:** Both ChestX-ray14 and CheXpert derive image labels from unstructured
   radiology text using automated extractors. Natural language ambiguity, negated clauses ("cannot rule out pneumonia"),
   and differential diagnoses introduce label noise ($10\text{--}15\%$ false positive/negative rates).
2. **Confounding Hospital Signatures and Shortcut Learning:** Medical deep learning models notoriously exploit
   non-clinical artifacts (<NoteLink to="data-leakage">shortcut learning</NoteLink>): hospital-specific lead marker
   fonts, patient support hardware, portables vs. upright imaging tags, or tube positioning markers. Models can predict
   pneumonia not by identifying lung consolidation, but by detecting that the patient is supine in an ICU bed.
3. **Severe Class and Demographic Imbalance:** Dangerous pathologies are heavily underrepresented relative to normal
   scans. Furthermore, skin lesion datasets (such as ISIC) have historically suffered from severe under-representation
   of darker Fitzpatrick skin phototypes (Types V and VI), leading to degraded sensitivity on diverse populations.
4. **Gigapixel Memory Constraints in Pathology:** Histopathology WSIs frequently exceed $100{,}000 \times 100{,}000$
   pixels, preventing end-to-end backpropagation. Practitioners must rely on patch cropping, tissue foreground masking,
   and Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) aggregators, which can lose macro-architectural tissue context.
