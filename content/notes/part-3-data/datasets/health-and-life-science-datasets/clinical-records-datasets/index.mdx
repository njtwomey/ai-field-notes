<Definition>
**Clinical records datasets** are structured and semi-structured collections of electronic health records (EHR), claims
registries, and longitudinal patient cohorts comprising demographic profiles, diagnostic billing codes (ICD-9/10),
laboratory panels, clinical vital signs, medication orders, and de-identified physician progress notes.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Clinical record benchmarks evaluate machine learning models on high-dimensional, irregular, and multimodal healthcare
trajectories:

- **Acuity & In-Hospital Decompensation:** Early prediction of acute physiological deterioration, such as in-hospital
  mortality, length of stay (LOS), unplanned readmission within 30 days, and rapid onset of sepsis or acute kidney
  injury (AKI) from hourly bedside vital signs and lab tests.
- **Phenotyping & Disease Trajectories:** Multi-label classification mapping sparse longitudinal event sequences (ICD
  codes, CPT procedure codes, prescription fills) to clinical phenotypes, diagnostic groupings, and comorbid progression
  pathways.
- **Treatment Recommendation & Counterfactual Outcomes:** Evaluating observational off-policy reinforcement learning and
  causal inference for individualized dosing regimens (e.g., intravenous fluids and vasopressors in septic shock) and
  mechanical ventilation weaning.
- **Population Genetics & Epidemiology:** Genome-wide association studies (GWAS), polygenic risk scoring, and
  multi-modal risk prediction combining electronic records with genetic arrays, imaging biomarkers, and lifestyle
  questionnaires.

### MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV

Developed by the MIT Laboratory for Computational Physiology in partnership with Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center
(<Cite id="johnson2016mimic" />):
- **MIMIC-III Architecture:** Released in 2016, MIMIC-III covers 38,597 distinct adult patients and 7,870 neonates
  admitted to critical care units between 2001 and 2012. It contains timestamped charted physiological events, nursing
  notes, physician progress reports, discharge summaries, laboratory chemistry panels, fluid input/output balances, and
  billing codes.
- **MIMIC-IV Modernization:** Released in 2020, MIMIC-IV expands beyond the ICU to encompass hospital-wide admissions
  and emergency department stays (MIMIC-IV-ED) between 2008 and 2019. It introduces a modular relational database
  structure (`core`, `hosp`, `icu`, `ed`), standardized mapping to OMOP Common Data Model (CDM), and updated ICD-10
  diagnostic coding.
- **Standardized Benchmarks:** Open-source benchmark suites (such as the MIMIC-Extract and MIMIC-IV Benchmark pipelines)
  define standardized train/validation/test splits and cohort inclusion criteria for 4 core tasks: in-hospital
  mortality, physiological decompensation within 24 hours, length-of-stay estimation, and 25-group phenotypic multilabel
  classification.

### eICU Collaborative Research Database

Curated by Philips Healthcare and MIT (<Cite id="pollard2018eicu" />):
- **Multi-Center Representation:** Unlike MIMIC's single academic medical center footprint, the eICU-CRD compiles
  telemetry from 208 community and teaching hospitals across the United States between 2014 and 2015.
- **Data Attributes:** Contains over 200,000 ICU admissions comprising time-stamped physiological monitoring data, nurse
  charting, acute physiology scores (APACHE IVa risk predictions), diagnostic admission tags, and medication infusion
  records.
- **External Validation Benchmark:** Serves as the primary public testbed for cross-site generalizability: testing
  whether clinical decision support models trained on one healthcare system degrade when evaluated against different
  hospital workflows, demographic mixes, and telemetry vendor setups.

### UK Biobank

Established by the Wellcome Trust, Medical Research Council, and the UK Department of Health:
- **Depth and Scale:** Follows 502,000 volunteer participants recruited between 2006 and 2010 across the United Kingdom.
- **Multimodal Health Profiles:** Combines extensive baseline questionnaire phenotypes (diet, physical activity,
  socio-economic factors) with genome-wide SNP genotyping arrays, whole-exome and whole-genome sequencing (WGS),
  multi-organ MRI imaging (brain, cardiac, abdominal, and liver fat scans on over 100,000 participants), accelerated
  physical activity monitoring (wrist-worn accelerometers), and longitudinal electronic health linkage to National
  Health Service (NHS) hospital episode statistics, cancer registries, and death records.
- **Scientific Role:** Drives modern machine learning for genomics, complex polygenic risk profiling, and biomarker
  discovery.

### Synthea

Developed by the MITRE Corporation:
- **Synthetic Disease Modeling:** An open-source, agent-based microsimulation engine that generates realistic
  longitudinal synthetic patient medical histories from statistical demographic and epidemiological disease models.
- **Standardized Interoperability:** Emits complete synthetic health records formatted in modern healthcare
  interoperability standards: HL7 FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources), OMOP CDM, and CSV.
- **Use Cases:** Enables end-to-end development, continuous integration testing, and machine learning algorithm
  prototyping without navigating Institutional Review Board (IRB) reviews, HIPAA constraints, or data use agreements.

## Choosing a dataset

- **Building acute intensive care (ICU) / inpatient models?**:
  - Need single-center ICU + rich free-text clinical notes?
  - Need modern large-scale ED + hospital + ICU cohort?
  - Evaluating multi-center cross-hospital generalizability?
  - eICU-CRD (208 hospitals, 200k admissions, varied ICU types)
- **Long-term epidemiological cohorts & multi-modal genetics?**:
  - UK Biobank (500k participants, genomics, multi-organ MRI, NHS linkage)
- **Prototyping software & pipelines without PHI / ethics clearance?**:
  - Synthea (agent-based synthetic patient generator, HL7 FHIR export)

## Known problems

1. **Informative Observation and Missingness Biases:** In clinical records, observations are not missing at random
   ($MNAR$). A troponin or lactate lab test is measured specifically because a physician suspects acute coronary
   ischemia or sepsis (<NoteLink to="clinical-and-health-data">clinical sampling bias</NoteLink>). The mere measurement
   frequency and time interval of an observation often convey stronger predictive signals than the test's numerical
   value.
2. **Practice Variation and Distribution Shift:** Treatment protocols, laboratory reference ranges, and coding
   incentives differ drastically across hospitals and regions. A model trained to predict mortality from vasopressor
   administration will fail in facilities where clinicians initiate vasopressors earlier or at different blood pressure
   thresholds.
3. **Confounding by Indication in Treatment Evaluation:** When evaluating causal treatment effects or reinforcement
   learning policies from observational EHR data, treatments are assigned based on unobserved clinical judgment.
   High-risk patients receive more aggressive interventions, introducing severe confounding where interventions can
   appear positively correlated with death.
4. **Data De-Identification Artifacts:** To comply with HIPAA Safe Harbor regulations, real clinical dates are randomly
   shifted into arbitrary future years (preserving relative intervals within a patient record), and patient ages above
   89 are aggregated or obscured to prevent identity re-identification.
