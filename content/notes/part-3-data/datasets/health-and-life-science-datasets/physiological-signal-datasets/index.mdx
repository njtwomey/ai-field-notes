<Definition>
**Physiological signal datasets** are curated collections of continuous or episodic bio-potential and physiological
time-series recordings—such as electrocardiograms (ECG), electroencephalograms (EEG), electromyograms (EMG),
photoplethysmograms (PPG), and polysomnography (PSG)—annotated with clinical diagnoses, physiological states, or
transient electrophysiological events.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Physiological recordings capture time-varying voltage gradients and physiological state transitions generated by
biological organs:

- **Cardiac Electrophysiology (ECG):** Surface potential differences induced by myocardial depolarization and
  repolarization (P-wave, QRS-complex, T-wave). Measurements focus on beat-level arrhythmia classification (premature
  ventricular contractions, atrial fibrillation), rhythm-level disturbances, conduction blocks, and myocardial
  infarction localization across single-lead wearable strips to standard 12-lead diagnostic montages.
- **Cerebral Electrophysiology (EEG):** Microvolt-level scalp potential fluctuations reflecting synchronous pyramidal
  neuron activity. Tasks evaluate epileptic spike-wave discharge detection, seizure onset and termination boundary
  segmentation, sleep stage scoring, and encephalopathy screening across 10–20 international electrode montages.
- **Polysomnography (PSG):** Multi-modal sleep monitoring combining multi-channel EEG, electrooculography (EOG),
  submental EMG, respiratory airflow, and pulse oximetry ($SpO_2$) to score sleep architecture (Wake, N1, N2, N3, REM)
  and detect obstructive sleep apnea events.
- **Hemodynamics & Perfusion (PPG, Arterial Lines):** Optical and invasive blood pressure pulse waves measuring cardiac
  output, pulse transit time, beat-to-beat blood pressure estimation, and peripheral oxygen saturation.

### MIT-BIH Arrhythmia

Collected between 1975 and 1979 by Boston's Beth Israel Hospital and MIT, the **MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database** is the
foundational benchmark for computerized ECG analysis (<Cite id="goldberger2000" />):
- **Data Structure:** Consists of 48 half-hour two-lead ECG recordings digitised at $360\text{ Hz}$ with 11-bit
  resolution over a $10\text{ mV}$ range. Twenty-three recordings were chosen at random from a set of 4,000 24-hour
  ambulatory ECG tapes; twenty-five were selected to include less common but clinically significant arrhythmias.
- **Annotations:** Contains over 109,000 manually verified heartbeat annotations. Under the standard Association for the
  Advancement of Medical Instrumentation (AAMI) EC57 standard, these annotations are grouped into five superclasses:
  Normal (N), Supraventricular Ectopic (S), Ventricular Ectopic (V), Fusion (F), and Unknown (Q).
- **Benchmark Baselines:** Modern 1D CNNs, Wavelet ResNets, and BiLSTM architectures reliably exceed $98\%$ overall
  accuracy on inter-patient beat classification benchmarks, though minority class sensitivity (such as supraventricular
  ectopic beats) remains challenging.

### PTB-XL

Released in 2020 by the Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt, **PTB-XL** represents the modern gold standard for
large-scale, 12-lead clinical ECG machine learning (<Cite id="wagner2020ptbxl" />):
- **Scale and Metadata:** 21,837 10-second standard 12-lead ECG records from 18,885 distinct patients ($52\%$ male,
  median age 62). All records are sampled at both $500\text{ Hz}$ and $100\text{ Hz}$, paired with rich demographic
  metadata, diagnostic statements, and signal quality metrics.
- **Diagnostic Hierarchy:** Annotations follow the international SCP-ECG standard, grouped into 71 diagnostic statements
  structured into a hierarchical ontology: 5 superclasses (Normal ECG, Myocardial Infarction, ST/T-Change, Conduction
  Disturbance, Hypertrophy), 24 subclasses, and 44 form/rhythm statements.
- **Standardised Splits:** PTB-XL provides a fixed 10-fold patient-stratified split to eliminate inter-patient test
  leakage. Standard multi-label AUROC benchmarks hover around $0.92\text{--}0.94$ for ResNet-1D and Inception1D
  architectures.

### PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenges

The annual **PhysioNet/CinC Challenges** have steered open physiological benchmarking since 2000
(<Cite id="goldberger2000" />):
- **Benchmark Tasks:** Landmark editions include automated detection of atrial fibrillation from single-lead short ECG
  recordings (2017), early prediction of sepsis from bedside clinical vitals (2019), automated multi-label 12-lead ECG
  diagnosis (2020/2021), and automated sleep apnea / sleep stage detection (2018).
- **Hidden Test Evaluation:** Submissions are containerized and evaluated against hidden, sequestered clinical datasets,
  preventing test set overfitting and measuring true cross-hospital generalization.

### Sleep-EDF (Expanded)

The **Sleep-EDF Database** comprises polysomnographic recordings designed for automated sleep staging:
- **Signals:** Includes two scalp EEG channels ($Fpz\text{-}Cz$ and $Pz\text{-}Oz$), horizontal EOG, submental chin EMG,
  and event markers.
- **Annotations:** Each 30-second epoch is assigned a sleep stage by trained sleep technicians following the
  Rechtschaffen & Kales (R&K) standard (Wake, S1, S2, S3, S4, REM, Movement), often mapped onto the modern 5-stage
  American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) framework (Wake, N1, N2, N3, REM).
- **Key Challenges:** Severe class imbalance (N2 and Wake dominate; transition stage N1 constitutes less than
  $5\text{--}10\%$ of recorded epochs).

### CHB-MIT Scalp EEG

The **CHB-MIT Scalp EEG Database** is a public benchmark for automated epileptic seizure detection:
- **Cohort:** Collected at Boston Children's Hospital, covering 22 pediatric subjects (plus one duplicate recording
  session, totaling 23 cases) with intractable seizures who were monitored over several days following withdrawal of
  anti-seizure medications.
- **Content:** 844 continuous hours of 23-channel surface EEG sampled at $256\text{ Hz}$, containing 198 confirmed
  clinical and subclinical seizure events with millisecond-resolution onset and termination stamps.
- **Evaluation Criteria:** Patient-specific versus cross-patient seizure detection; metrics emphasize high true-positive
  seizure sensitivity with less than 1 false alarm per 24 hours of continuous recording.

### TUH EEG Corpus (TUEG)

Maintained by the Neural Engineering Data Consortium at Temple University, the **Temple University Hospital (TUH) EEG
Corpus** is the world's largest public clinical EEG resource:
- **Scale:** Spans more than 25,000 EEG sessions across 10,000+ individual patients, capturing real-world clinical
  heterogeneity (inpatient, outpatient, emergency, ICU recordings) with variable lead montages and significant movement
  artifacts.
- **Curated Subsets:**
  - **TUSZ (TUH Seizure):** Detailed start/stop time annotations for focal, generalized, and absence seizures across
    hundreds of hours.
  - **TUAB (TUH Abnormal):** Binary normal versus abnormal clinical screening on standard adult EEG studies.
  - **TUEV (TUH Events):** Epoch-level annotations of transient epileptiform events (spikes, sharp waves) and slowing
    patterns.

## Choosing a dataset

- **Evaluating cardiac electrophysiology (ECG)?**:
  - Need classic single-beat arrhythmia classification?
  - Need clinical 12-lead multi-label diagnostic screening?
  - PTB-XL (21k records, SCP-ECG standard, fixed splits)
- **Evaluating neurophysiology / brain signals (EEG)?**:
  - Detecting pediatric epileptic seizures & onset times?
  - Large-scale clinical abnormality screening or adult seizures?
  - Automated sleep staging & polysomnography (PSG)?
  - Sleep-EDF Expanded (197 nights, 30s epochs, R&K / AASM stages)
- **Testing generalized multi-modal clinical monitoring?**:
  - PhysioNet/CinC Challenges (AF detection, early sepsis, bedside ICU)

## Known problems

1. **Patient Leakage Across Train and Test Splits:** Because physiological signals are uniquely shaped by each patient's
   individual anatomy (heart axis orientation, skull thickness, electrode contact impedance), random sample-level or
   beat-level splitting leads to severe test-set data leakage (<NoteLink to="data-leakage">data leakage</NoteLink>).
   Beats or segments from the same patient must never appear in both training and test sets.
2. **Extreme Class Imbalance:** Life-threatening arrhythmias (such as ventricular fibrillation or asystole) and
   epileptiform seizures represent rare transient anomalies within hours or days of normal baseline background activity.
   Models trained on raw loss functions frequently achieve $99\%$ accuracy while failing to detect a single clinical
   event.
3. **Electrode Motion and High-Frequency Artifacts:** Real-world recordings contain severe motion artifacts, baseline
   wander ($< 0.5\text{ Hz}$ respiratory drift), muscle tremor EMG interference ($20\text{--}100\text{ Hz}$), and
   powerline interference ($50/60\text{ Hz}$). Benchmarks recorded in quiet research labs understate the preprocessing
   and domain adaptation necessary for wearable or intensive care deployment.
4. **Inter-Annotator Diagnostic Disagreement:** Sleep staging and EEG seizure boundary delineation suffer from
   substantial human inter-rater variability; consensus Cohen's kappa scores between board-certified
   electroencephalographers frequently range between $0.65$ and $0.80$.
