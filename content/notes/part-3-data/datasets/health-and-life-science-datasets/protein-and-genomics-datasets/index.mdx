<Definition>
**Protein and genomics datasets** are curated biological repositories and benchmarking suites containing macromolecular
sequences (DNA, RNA, proteins), experimentally determined 3D atomic structures, molecular variation catalogs, and
cellular transcriptomic expressions, used to train models for structural biology, variant pathogenicity, and cellular
state discovery.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Biological sequence and structural data span fundamental layers of molecular biology:

- **Macromolecular 3D Conformation:** Atomic Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ of protein backbones
  ($N, C_\alpha, C, O$) and side-chain rotamers, nucleic acids, and small-molecule complexes determined via X-ray
  crystallography, cryogenic electron microscopy (cryo-EM), and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR). Tasks evaluate de novo
  structure prediction, complex docking, and binding affinity estimation.
- **Protein Sequence & Function:** Primary amino acid strings ($20$ standard letters) annotated with evolutionary
  families, catalytic active sites, post-translational modifications, and Gene Ontology (GO) functional hierarchies.
- **Population Genomic Variation:** Variant call formats (VCF) mapping single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), short
  insertions/deletions (indels), and structural copy number variants (CNVs) across diverse human populations and
  ancestral lineages.
- **Multi-Omics Cancer Profiling:** Matched tumor-normal tissue sequencing capturing somatic mutations, copy number
  alterations, DNA methylation, and bulk RNA-seq transcript expression profiles linked to oncological survival.
- **Single-Cell Transcriptomics:** High-dimensional sparse counts measuring gene expression across individual cells
  (scRNA-seq), evaluating cell-type classification, batch effect correction, RNA velocity, and gene regulatory network
  inference.

### Protein Data Bank (PDB)

Established in 1971 and maintained by the Worldwide Protein Data Bank (<Cite id="berman2000pdb" />):
- **Scope:** The singular global archive of experimentally resolved macromolecular 3D structures. Holds over 215,000
  structures resolved by X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, and solution NMR.
- **Data Details:** Standardized in the modern macromolecular Crystallographic Information Framework (mmCIF) format,
  recording Cartesian coordinates of all resolved atoms, temperature factors (B-factors), stereochemical constraints,
  and experimental reflection data.
- **Role in Machine Learning:** PDB forms the foundational training set for revolutionary structural deep learning
  frameworks—including AlphaFold (<Cite id="jumper2021" />), RoseTTAFold, and ESMFold—which learn physical folding
  geometry from geometric invariant point clouds and invariant coordinate frames.

### CASP (Critical Assessment of Structure Prediction)

Founded in 1994 by John Moult, CASP is the biennial blind challenge in computational structural biology:
- **Challenge Design:** Research teams submit structural predictions for protein sequences whose experimental structures
  have been recently solved by crystallographers but sequestered from public release.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Global Distance Test Total Score (GDT-TS), measuring the percentage of predicted $C_\alpha$
  atoms falling within $1, 2, 4,$ and $8\,\text{Å}$ of the experimental ground truth.
- **The AlphaFold Breakthrough:** At CASP14 (2020), DeepMind's AlphaFold 2 achieved an overall median GDT-TS of 92.4,
  demonstrating structural prediction accuracy competitive with experimental X-ray crystallography
  (<Cite id="jumper2021" />). Subsequent editions (CASP15/16) have pushed evaluation toward protein-protein multimers,
  RNA 3D structures, and small-molecule ligand-protein docking complexes.

### UniProt

Maintained by EMBL-EBI, the Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics (SIB), and the Protein Information Resource (PIR):
- **UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot:** High-quality, manually curated, and non-redundant database of over 570,000 protein sequences
  with literature-verified functional annotations, catalytic enzyme mechanisms, and subcellular localization tags.
- **UniProtKB/TrEMBL:** Automatically translated from EMBL/GenBank nucleotide sequences, encompassing hundreds of
  millions of uncurated primary protein sequences.
- **Foundation Models:** UniProt serves as the pre-training corpus for biological foundation models (ESM-2, ProtTrans),
  which model evolutionary co-conservation and masked residue prediction analogous to large language models.

### 1000 Genomes Project

The definitive reference catalog of human genetic variation:
- **Cohort:** Deeply sequenced 2,504 individuals from 26 ancestral populations across Africa, East Asia, Europe, South
  Asia, and the Americas.
- **Catalogued Variants:** Discovered over 88 million single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), 3.6 million short indels,
  and 60,000 structural variants.
- **Use Cases:** Serves as the primary reference panel for genotype imputation, linkage disequilibrium modeling, and
  training deep learning tools for variant effect prediction (such as DeepVariant and PrimateAI).

### The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)

Joint initiative by the National Cancer Institute (NCI) and the National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI):
- **Cohort:** Encompasses matched primary tumor and normal blood/tissue samples from over 11,000 patients spanning 33
  cancer types (including breast, glioblastoma, lung, and melanoma).
- **Multi-Omics Profiling:** Provides whole-exome sequencing (WES), mRNA expression arrays (RNA-seq), microRNA
  expression, DNA methylation arrays, copy number alteration profiles (Affymetrix SNP 6.0), and linked clinical
  progression-free and overall survival data.
- **Machine Learning Applications:** Drives cancer subtyping, biomarker discovery, and deep survival modeling
  (<NoteLink to="survival-datasets">survival datasets</NoteLink>).

### Single-Cell Atlases (Human Cell Atlas / Tabula Sapiens)

Consortium initiatives mapping cellular resolution across organs:
- **Scale:** Hundreds of millions of sequenced single cells across developmental stages and organs, packaged in
  standardized H5AD (`AnnData`) formats.
- **Benchmark Tasks:** Unsupervised cell type annotation, batch effect normalization (e.g., scVI, Harmony), RNA velocity
  vector field estimation, and perturbation response prediction (e.g., scGen, GEARS).

## Choosing a dataset

- **Working on protein 3D structures and folding?**:
  - Pre-training or training geometric structure models?
  - Evaluating blind de novo structure prediction performance?
  - CASP (biennial blind evaluation targets, GDT-TS metric)
- **Working on biological sequence language models?**:
  - UniProt (Swiss-Prot for curated ground truth, TrEMBL for scale)
- **Modeling human genetic variation and SNP effects?**:
  - 1000 Genomes Project (2,504 individuals, 88M SNPs, VCF reference panel)
- **Multi-omics cancer progression & survival analysis?**:
  - TCGA (11k patients, 33 tumor types, RNA-seq + mutations + survival)
- **Single-cell transcriptomics & cellular dynamics?**:
  - Single-Cell Atlases / Tabula Sapiens (scRNA-seq count matrices)

## Known problems

1. **Homology Leakage and Evolutionary Redundancy:** Biological sequences share deep evolutionary ancestry. Random
   splitting of protein sequences or structures produces extreme performance inflation because near-identical homologs
   ($> 90\%$ sequence identity) appear in both train and test sets. Rigorous benchmarks require sequence-identity
   clustering (e.g., MMseqs2 or CD-HIT) to enforce strict maximum homology thresholds ($< 30\%$ sequence identity)
   across splits.
2. **Experimental Resolution and Conformation Dynamics:** Structures in the PDB represent static crystal snapshots,
   frequently influenced by crystal packing artifacts, cryo-conditions, or missing flexible loops. In reality, proteins
   are dynamic ensembles with multiple functional conformers.
3. **Severe Technical Batch Effects in Single-Cell Data:** Single-cell RNA sequencing datasets suffer from substantial
   non-biological batch variation introduced by differing sequencing depths, dissociation protocols, reagent lots, and
   machine platforms.
4. **Extreme Class Imbalance in Pathogenic Variants:** Of the billions of possible human genetic mutations, only an
   infinitesimal fraction are confirmed pathogenic variants. Models trained without careful calibrated loss functions
   suffer from severe false positive rates.
