<Definition>
**Graph benchmark datasets** are standardized collections of relational graph topologies—comprising vertex sets $V$,
edge relation sets $E$, and associated attribute tensors $X_V$ and $X_E$—used to systematically evaluate graph neural
networks (GNNs) across node-level, edge-level, and graph-level prediction tasks.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Graph benchmarks test model capacity across three fundamental operational levels:

- **Node-Level Classification & Regression:** Predicting attributes or discrete classes of individual vertices $v \in V$
  given their local features and neighborhood connectivity. Tasks differentiate between **transductive learning** (all
  nodes observed during training, but only a subset are labeled) and **inductive learning** (evaluating generalization
  to entirely unseen disconnected test graphs).
- **Edge-Level Link Prediction:** Predicting the existence, likelihood, or category of unobserved edges $(u, v) \in E$.
  Common settings include citation recommendation, drug-target interaction screening, and social collaboration
  forecasting.
- **Graph-Level Property Prediction:** Classifying or predicting scalar properties for entire discrete graph instances
  $G_i = (V_i, E_i)$ (e.g., molecular bioactivity, toxicity, chemical solubility, algorithm runtime).
- **Relational Homophily vs. Heterophily:** Testing whether model performance holds when connected nodes share identical
  labels (high homophily, e.g., citation networks) versus when connected nodes systematically belong to different
  classes (heterophily, e.g., fraud rings, protein interactions).

### Zachary's Karate Club

Collected by Wayne W. Zachary in 1977:
- **Topology:** A 34-member university karate club whose interpersonal friendships were observed over three years.
  During the study, a political dispute between the administrator ("John A") and the instructor ("Mr. Hi") split the
  club into two distinct factions.
- **Role:** Serves as the quintessential microscopic toy benchmark for testing graph clustering algorithms, spectral
  graph partitioners, and unsupervised node representation embeddings.

### Cora, CiteSeer, and PubMed

The foundational citation benchmarks popularized by Kipf and Welling (<Cite id="kipf2017" />):
- **Graph Structure:** Nodes represent scientific publications; directed edges represent bibliographic citations
  (treated as undirected edges in standard GNN benchmarks).
  - *Cora:* 2,708 machine learning papers across 7 subject classes; each paper has a 1,433-dimensional binary
    bag-of-words feature vector.
  - *CiteSeer:* 3,327 computer science papers across 6 classes; 3,703-dimensional sparse features.
  - *PubMed:* 19,717 diabetes-related medical papers across 3 classes; 500-dimensional TF-IDF features.
- **Benchmark Legacy:** The standard "Planetoid" semi-supervised split uses only 20 labeled nodes per class for
  training. While historical benchmarks demonstrated huge gains for Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) and Graph
  Attention Networks (GAT), modern evaluations find that these datasets are overly simplistic, saturated ($> 84\%$ on
  Cora), and sensitive to random seed variance.

### Open Graph Benchmark (OGB)

Introduced by Hu et al. in 2020 to overcome the limitations of toy graph benchmarks (<Cite id="hu2020ogb" />):
- **Standardized Task Taxonomy:**
  - **Node Property Prediction (`ogbn-*`):** `ogbn-arxiv` (169k computer science papers with temporal splits based on
    publication year), `ogbn-products` (2.4M Amazon co-purchasing nodes with community-aware splits), and
    `ogbn-proteins` (large multi-task protein network).
  - **Link Property Prediction (`ogbl-*`):** `ogbl-collab` (author collaboration forecasting), `ogbl-citation2` (paper
    citation recommendation evaluated with MRR), and `ogbl-ppa` (protein-protein association).
  - **Graph Property Prediction (`ogbg-*`):** `ogbg-molhiv` and `ogbg-molpcba` (molecular scaffold splits from
    PubChem/ChEMBL testing out-of-distribution molecular property generalization), and `ogbg-code2` (abstract syntax
    tree code comprehension).
- **Realistic Data Splits:** Completely replaces unrealistic uniform random node splitting with domain-meaningful
  splits—such as publication timestamps (**temporal splits**) or chemical Murcko scaffolds (**scaffold
  splits**)—accurately exposing out-of-distribution generalization failure.

### TUDataset

The comprehensive collection of graph-level classification benchmarks maintained by Morris et al.:
- **Categorization:** Includes chemoinformatics benchmarks (MUTAG for mutagenic nitroaromatic compounds, PTC, NCI1),
  bioinformatics graphs (PROTEINS, ENZYMES), and social interaction ego-networks (IMDB-BINARY, COLLAB, REDDIT-MULTI-5K).
- **Graph Isomorphism Testing:** TUDataset benchmarks served as the testbed that established the equivalence between
  message-passing GNNs and the 1-dimensional Weisfeiler-Lehman (1-WL) graph isomorphism test, motivating the Graph
  Isomorphism Network (GIN).

### Protein-Protein Interaction (PPI)

Introduced by Zitnik & Leskovec and utilized by Hamilton et al. in GraphSAGE:
- **Inductive Architecture:** Consists of 24 distinct graphs corresponding to different human tissues (20 for training,
  2 for validation, 2 for testing).
- **Task Formulation:** Inductive multi-label node classification where models must assign 121 biological Gene Ontology
  functional tags to nodes in completely unseen test graphs, demanding structural feature generalizability without
  transductive memorization.

## Choosing a dataset

- **Evaluating node classification?**:
  - Want modern large-scale benchmarks with realistic splits?
  - Need pure inductive generalization to unseen test graphs?
  - Quick sanity check or prototyping?
  - Cora / PubMed (small citation graphs, fast to train)
- **Evaluating link prediction?**:
  - ogbl-citation2 / ogbl-collab (large-scale bibliographic graphs)
- **Evaluating whole-graph classification or molecular regression?**:
  - Small-scale graph isomorphism & WL-kernel tests → TUDataset (MUTAG, ENZYMES)
  - Real-world molecular property prediction → ogbg-molhiv / ogbg-molpcba

## Known problems

1. **Information Leakage in Random Graph Splits:** Uniform random node splits
   (<NoteLink to="graph-splits-and-sampling">graph splits</NoteLink>) allow test nodes to share direct edges with
   training nodes, inflating accuracy through direct neighbor label propagation rather than learned representations.
2. **Benchmark Saturation on Toy Citation Graphs:** Cora, CiteSeer, and PubMed contain severe topological artifacts;
   high degrees of class homophily and small graph diameters allow simple multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) with label
   propagation to outperform complex deep architectures.
3. **Graph Size and Oversmoothing:** As graph neural networks grow deeper ($> 4$ layers) on small graphs, repeated
   neighborhood averaging causes node feature vectors across the entire graph to converge to indistinguishable uniform
   vectors (**oversmoothing**), while on large graphs (OGB), high-degree hub nodes cause exponential neighborhood
   expansion (**neighborhood explosion**).
4. **Scaffold Divergence in Molecular Graphs:** On molecular graph benchmarks (`ogbg-molhiv`), structurally dissimilar
   chemical scaffolds in the test set often result in massive performance drops compared to random validation splits,
   reflecting real-world drug screening hardness.
