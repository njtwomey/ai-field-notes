<Definition>
**Knowledge graph datasets** are collections of structured relational facts represented as directed multi-relational
graphs of subject-predicate-object triples $(h, r, t) \in \mathcal{E} \times \mathcal{R} \times \mathcal{E}$, where
entities $\mathcal{E}$ correspond to real-world concepts or objects, and relations $\mathcal{R}$ denote typed semantic
associations.
</Definition>

## What these datasets measure

Knowledge graph benchmarks evaluate multi-relational reasoning and knowledge completion algorithms:

- **Link Prediction / Entity Ranking:** Given an incomplete query $(h, r, ?)$ or $(?, r, t)$, ranking all candidate
  entities $e \in \mathcal{E}$ according to their predicted likelihood. Evaluated using Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) and
  Hits@$K$ (typically Hits@1, Hits@3, and Hits@10) under the **filtered setting** (removing other known valid true
  triples from the candidate pool during evaluation).
- **Relation Prediction:** Given an entity pair $(h, ?, t)$, predicting the semantic relation type connecting them.
- **Relational Pattern Modeling:** Benchmarking an embedding model's algebraic ability to express core logical patterns:
  - *Symmetry / Antisymmetry:* $r(h, t) \implies r(t, h)$ (e.g., `spouse_of`) vs. $r(h, t) \implies \neg r(t, h)$ (e.g.,
    `parent_of`).
  - *Inversion:* $r_1(h, t) \iff r_2(t, h)$ (e.g., `hypernym` vs. `hyponym`).
  - *Composition:* $r_1(x, y) \land r_2(y, z) \implies r_3(x, z)$ (e.g., `father_of` $\land$ `brother_of` $\implies$
    `uncle_of`).
  - *1-to-$N$, $N$-to-1, and $N$-to-$N$ mappings.*
- **Inductive vs. Transductive Knowledge Completion:** Evaluating whether embeddings can infer facts over completely
  unseen entities introduced at test time via textual entity descriptions or graph neighborhood topologies.

### FB15k-237

Introduced by Toutanova and Chen in 2015 to fix the severe structural flaws of the original FB15k benchmark
(<Cite id="toutanova2015wn18rr" />):
- **Freebase Provenance:** Extracted from Freebase (<Cite id="bollacker2008freebase" />), capturing general world
  knowledge spanning actors, films, locations, and sports.
- **Leakage Elimination:** In the original FB15k dataset, Bordes et al. included inverse relation pairs (e.g.,
  `/film/film/starring` and `/film/actor/film`). A simple memorization rule $r_{\text{inv}}(t, h)$ trivially predicted
  over $80\%$ of test triples. FB15k-237 removed all redundant inverse relation pairs, leaving 237 distinct relation
  types.
- **Relational Diversity:** Requires models to capture complex multi-hop compositional reasoning and dense $N$-to-$N$
  associations.

### WN18RR

Constructed by Dettmers et al. in 2018 as a leak-free derivative of WordNet WN18:
- **Lexical Taxonomy:** Built upon the WordNet lexical semantic network of English synsets.
- **Inverse Pair Removal:** Like FB15k, the original WN18 was trivialized by reciprocal pairs such as `_hypernym` and
  `_hyponym`. WN18RR eliminated inverse relations, leaving 11 relations (including `_hypernym`,
  `_derivationally_related_form`, `_instance_hypernym`, `_member_meronym`, and `_synset_domain_topic_of`).
- **Geometric Hierarchy:** Because WordNet forms a directed acyclic tree hierarchy, hyperbolic geometry embeddings (such
  as Poincaré and Lorentz space embeddings like MurP) demonstrate superior representational efficiency compared to
  Euclidean translational models.

### YAGO3-10

Derived from the YAGO (Yet Another Great Ontology) project:
- **Knowledge Fusion:** Integrates structured infoboxes and category taxonomies from multilingual Wikipedias, WordNet
  synsets, and geographic metadata from GeoNames.
- **Density Filter:** YAGO3-10 filters the graph such that every included entity participates in at least 10 distinct
  relational facts, resulting in 123,182 entities and 37 relations.
- **Characteristics:** Contains rich biographical, geographical, and institutional relationships (e.g., `graduatedFrom`,
  `hasWonPrize`, `isLocatedIn`).

### Wikidata5M

Introduced to scale knowledge graph representation learning from closed entity IDs to open web scale:
- **Web-Scale Magnitude:** Encompasses 4.59 million entities, 822 relation types, and over 20 million relational triples
  extracted from live Wikidata dumps.
- **Inductive Text Grounding:** Pairs every entity with its corresponding Wikipedia introductory text paragraph,
  allowing multimodal text-graph models (like SimKGC) to embed newly added entities zero-shot via bi-encoder language
  models.

## Choosing a dataset

- **Evaluating multi-relational link prediction (MRR / Hits@10)?**:
  - General world knowledge with dense relations?
  - Hierarchical, tree-like, or lexical structures?
  - Dense encyclopedic biographies and facts?
  - YAGO3-10 (123k entities with $\ge 10$ relations each)
- **Evaluating web-scale or inductive text-grounded completion?**:
  - Wikidata5M (4.6M entities paired with textual descriptions)
- **Evaluating common-sense reasoning for LLMs?**:
  - ConceptNet (common-sense assertions: `Causes`, `UsedFor`, `CapableOf`)

## Known problems

1. **Test-Set Leakage via Inverse Relations:** In uncurated datasets (such as raw FB15k and WN18), inverse relation
   pairs ($r$ and $r^{-1}$) allow trivial rule lookups to achieve near-perfect Hits@10 scores without learning any
   underlying relational structure. Always verify that derivative benchmarks have applied inverse pruning filters
   (<NoteLink to="graph-splits-and-sampling">graph splits</NoteLink>).
2. **Open-World Negative Sampling:** Knowledge graphs operate under the **Open-World Assumption (OWA)**: unobserved
   triples $(h, r, t) \notin \mathcal{G}$ are unknown, not necessarily false. Training requires corrupting heads or
   tails to generate pseudo-negative triples; inevitably, some randomly generated "negatives" are valid facts that were
   merely missing from the database.
3. **Severe Hub Entity Skew:** Real-world knowledge graphs follow power-law degree distributions. High-degree hub
   entities (e.g., United States, Male, English) appear across hundreds of thousands of triples, biasing prediction
   algorithms toward recommending popular hub entities in ambiguous queries.
4. **Static Approximations of Dynamic Truth:** Real-world factual knowledge evolves continuously (e.g., heads of state,
   corporate affiliations, sports rosters). Static benchmarks lack temporal validity timestamps, confounding models when
   facts change over time.
