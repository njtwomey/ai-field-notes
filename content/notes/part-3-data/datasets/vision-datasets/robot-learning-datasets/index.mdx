<Definition>
**Robot learning datasets** are empirical collections of physical robotic trajectories recording synchronized sequences
of observations $\mathbf{o}_t$ (multi-view RGB cameras, wrist-mounted depth sensors, joint encoders) paired with
continuous action commands $\mathbf{a}_t$ (end-effector $\Delta\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, orientation
$\Delta\mathbf{r} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and gripper open/close binary states) alongside natural language instructions. They
serve as the training foundation for imitation learning, behavior cloning, and cross-embodiment generalist robot
policies <Cite id="brohan2022rt1, padalkar2023" />.
</Definition>

Historically, robot learning was bottlenecked by the "single robot in a clean lab" paradigm: models overfitted to
specific lighting, table heights, and gripper dynamics. Modern robot datasets aggregate teleoperated demonstrations
across dozens of academic labs, diverse robot arms, and real household and industrial environments
<Cite id="walke2023bridgedata, padalkar2023" />.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Robot learning benchmarks formalize several core physical intelligence challenges:
1. **Behavior Cloning & Multi-Task Policy Learning**: Mapping image histories and task instructions $l$ to action
   distributions $P(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \mathbf{o}_{\le t}, l)$ via diffusion policies or autoregressive transformers
   <Cite id="brohan2022rt1" />.
2. **Cross-Embodiment Transfer**: Training unified policies across diverse kinematic morphologies (e.g. 7-DoF Franka
   Emika Panda, 6-DoF UR5, WidowX, mobile manipulators) where joint spaces and control frequencies differ widely
   <Cite id="padalkar2023" />.
3. **Out-of-Domain Generalization**: Evaluating physical task completion rates when objects, distractors, lighting, and
   table backgrounds are unseen during demonstration collection.
4. **Primary Evaluation Metrics**: **Physical Success Rate ($\%$)**, trajectory completion percentage, and safety
   constraint compliance during closed-loop real-world policy rollouts.

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### Open X-Embodiment: The ImageNet Moment for Robotics (2023)

Coordinated by the Open X-Embodiment Collaboration across 34 global institutions <Cite id="padalkar2023" />:
- **Morphological Diversity**: Unifies 60 existing datasets spanning 22 different robot hardware platforms (Franka, UR5,
  Kuka, WidowX, Google Everyday Robots, Boston Dynamics Spot).
- **Standardized Schema**: Translates heterogeneous sensor configurations and robot kinematics into the standardized
  RLDS (Reinforcement Learning Datasets) format, normalizing actions into a common 7-dimensional end-effector coordinate
  frame.
- **RT-X Generalist Models**: Demonstrated that co-training policies (RT-1-X, RT-2-X) on this multi-embodiment mixture
  produced up to $50\%$ higher success rates on individual downstream robots than training solely on single-robot data.

### RT-1: Large-Scale Fleet Collection (2022)

Constructed by Google DeepMind (Brohan et al. <Cite id="brohan2022rt1" />):
- **Fleet Operation**: Collected over 130,000 demonstrations across a fleet of 13 mobile manipulator robots operating in
  real office kitchens over 17 months.
- **Skills and Instructions**: Covers over 700 diverse natural language instructions (e.g. *"pick up green can from top
  drawer and place on counter"*).
- **Tokenized Transformers**: Converted continuous actions into discrete tokens (256 bins per dimension), enabling
  autoregressive Transformer policies that generalize compositionally to novel objects and background distractors.

### BridgeData V2: Low-Cost Generalist Manipulation (2023)

Introduced by Walke et al. <Cite id="walke2023bridgedata" /> at UC Berkeley:
- **Affordable Hardware Focus**: Demonstrations collected on low-cost WidowX 250 robot arms
  ($\approx 4{,}000\text{ USD}$), democratizing robot learning research beyond multi-million dollar industrial arms.
- **Cross-Environment Generalization**: Spans 24 distinct physical kitchen, toy-sink, and office setups with varying
  lighting, surface materials, and clutter.

## Choosing a Robot Learning Dataset

- **Foundation Model Pretraining Across Diverse Hardware**:
  - Open X-Embodiment (22 robot morphologies, 1M+ trajectories)
- **Mobile Manipulation & Long-Horizon Kitchen Tasks**:
  - RT-1 (Everyday Robots mobile bases, 700+ tasks)
- **High-Precision Industrial & Laboratory Franka Arms**:
  - DROID (76k Franka trajectories across 564 scenes)
- **Low-Cost Desktop Arm Prototyping**:
  - BridgeData V2 (WidowX arm, 24 diverse physical environments)
- **High-Frequency Bimanual Dexterous Assembly**:
  - ALOHA / ACT (Dual-arm teleoperated 50 Hz joint positions)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Hardware Morphology Mismatch**: A policy trained on a 7-DoF Franka cannot directly command a 4-DoF SCARA robot or a
   dexterous five-fingered hand without policy re-projection or embodiment-specific adapter heads
   <Cite id="padalkar2023" />.
2. **Teleoperation Demonstrator Suboptimality**: Human operators controlling robots via VR controllers or
   leader-follower linkages exhibit tremors, hesitations, inconsistent paths, and variable execution speeds, introducing
   multimodal action noise.
3. **The Sim-to-Real and Real-to-Real Distribution Shift**: Minor physical changes—such as a slightly displaced camera
   mount, differing cable tensions, or worn gripper pads—can collapse the closed-loop success rate of a behavior-cloned
   policy.
