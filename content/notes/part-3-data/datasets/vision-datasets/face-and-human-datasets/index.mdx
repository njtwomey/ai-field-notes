<Definition>
**Face and human datasets** are specialized computer vision benchmark collections capturing human biometrics, anatomical
facial landmarks, facial attribute annotations, generative portrait distributions, and 2D/3D articulated skeletal
kinematic chains. They evaluate facial identification, attribute classification, generative synthesis, and human pose
estimation, while serving as focal points for algorithmic privacy, unconsented scraping, and demographic intersectional
bias audits <Cite id="huang2008lfw, liu2015celeba, buolamwini2018" />.
</Definition>

Modeling human subjects presents unique geometric challenges (articulated kinematics, self-occlusion, deformable soft
tissue, variable expression) and profound ethical and legal responsibilities (biometric privacy, right of publicity, and
demographic representation equity).

## What These Datasets Measure

Human-centric visual benchmarks formalize several distinct evaluation settings:
1. **Face Verification and Identification (1:1 and 1:N)**: Determining whether two disparate unconstrained images depict
   the same person ($P(\text{same}) = 1$), evaluated via Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) at low False Accept
   Rates ($\text{FAR} \le 10^{-4}$) <Cite id="huang2008lfw" />.
2. **Facial Attribute Classification**: Multi-label binary classification across 40 phenotypic attributes (e.g.
   *smiling*, *eyeglasses*, *young*, *heavy makeup*) <Cite id="liu2015celeba" />.
3. **High-Fidelity Generative Modeling**: Synthesizing diverse, photorealistic human faces evaluated via Fréchet
   Inception Distance (FID) and Precision/Recall metrics on $1024 \times 1024$ natural portraits (FFHQ)
   <Cite id="karras2019stylegan" />.
4. **2D and 3D Human Pose Estimation**: Localizing anatomical skeletal keypoints (shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips,
   knees, ankles) measured by **PCK** (Percentage of Correct Keypoints) and **MPJPE** (Mean Per Joint Position Error in
   millimeters).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### Labeled Faces in the Wild (LFW, 2008)

Introduced by Huang et al. <Cite id="huang2008lfw" /> at UMASS Amherst:
- **Historical Significance**: The definitive benchmark that moved biometric face recognition from controlled, frontal
  passport-style laboratory lighting (FERET) to unconstrained in-the-wild poses, lighting, and expressions.
- **Evaluation Protocol**: 10-fold cross-validation on 6,000 pairwise face comparisons (3,000 matched pairs, 3,000
  mismatched pairs). Deep learning methods (DeepFace, FaceNet) surpassed human baseline accuracy ($97.5\%$) on LFW in
  2014–2015, effectively saturating the benchmark.

### CelebA: Multi-Attribute Facial Benchmark (2015)

Developed by Liu et al. <Cite id="liu2015celeba" /> from the Chinese University of Hong Kong:
- **Rich Annotations**: 202,599 face images annotated with 40 binary facial attributes (*Arched Eyebrows*, *Bags Under
  Eyes*, *Bald*, *Bangs*, *Goatee*, *Pale Skin*, *Pointy Nose*, etc.) alongside 5 landmark locations.
- **Role in Generative Modeling**: The standard training ground for conditional GANs, variational autoencoders (VAEs),
  and latent space editing (e.g. manipulating age, gender expression, and hair color via latent vector arithmetic).

### FFHQ: Flickr-Faces-HQ (2019)

Curated by NVIDIA researchers alongside StyleGAN <Cite id="karras2019stylegan" />:
- **High Visual Fidelity**: 70,000 images crawled from Flickr under permissive licenses, automatically cropped and
  aligned at $1024 \times 1024$ resolution.
- **Representation Diversity**: Designed to offer wider diversity in age (infants to elderly), ethnicity, image
  background, and accessories (eyeglasses, hats, scarves) compared to celebrity-centric datasets like CelebA.

### Human3.6M: Ground-Truth 3D Human Pose (2014)

Created by Ionescu et al. (2014):
- **Laboratory Acquisition**: Recorded using high-speed optical motion capture (Vicon) systems paired with calibrated
  multi-view RGB cameras in a studio setting.
- **Activities**: 11 professional actors performing 15 everyday physical activities (walking, sitting, discussing,
  smoking, greeting).
- **Metric**: Standard testbed for predicting 3D metric joint coordinates from single monocular RGB frames, reporting
  Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) after rigid Procrustes alignment.

## Choosing a Human-Centric Dataset

- **Facial Identity & Metric Verification**:
  - LFW (Pairwise verification protocol)
- **Multi-Attribute Classification & Latent Editing**:
  - CelebA (40 attributes, identity labels)
- **High-Resolution Generative Synthesis**:
  - FFHQ (1024x1024 aligned photorealistic portraits)
- **2D Articulated In-the-Wild Pose**:
  - MPII Human Pose / COCO Keypoints
- **3D Metric Skeletal Kinematics**:
  - Human3.6M (Calibrated multi-view optical MoCap)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Intersectional Demographic Biases**: Audits by Buolamwini and Gebru <Cite id="buolamwini2018" /> (Gender Shades)
   demonstrated that commercial face recognition systems trained on datasets like LFW and CelebA had error rates up to
   $34.7\%$ for dark-skinned females, compared to $< 0.8\%$ for light-skinned males, directly stemming from severe
   Caucasian and male demographic over-representation in public web scrapes.
2. **Lack of Informed Consent**: Many legacy face datasets scraped individuals from web searches, Flickr, and YouTube
   without subjects' knowledge or consent, sparking privacy controversies and resulting in major dataset withdrawals
   (e.g. MS-Celeb-1M, MegaFace, DukeMTMC).
3. **Subjective and Stereotypical Attribute Labels**: Human annotation of subjective attributes (*attractive*, *chubby*,
   *young*) in CelebA embeds cultural stereotypes and annotator subjectivities into supervised classifiers.
