<Definition>
**Detection and segmentation datasets** are visual benchmark collections providing fine-grained spatial ground
truth—including axis-aligned bounding boxes $b = (x_{\min}, y_{\min}, x_{\max}, y_{\max})$, polygonal instance
boundaries, and dense per-pixel semantic category maps $M \in \{1, \dots, K\}^{H \times W}$. They formalize object
localization, instance separation, and full panoptic scene decomposition
<Cite id="everingham2010, lin2014, cordts2016" />.
</Definition>

While classification assigns a single categorical label to an entire image, detection and segmentation require
simultaneous recognition ("what is there") and spatial delineation ("where is it located"). These benchmarks drive
downstream applications in autonomous driving, medical imaging, robotic manipulation, and visual editing.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Detection and segmentation benchmarks formalize several distinct spatial reasoning tasks:
1. **Object Detection**: Predicting bounding boxes and category labels for distinct foreground objects ("things").
   Evaluated via **Mean Average Precision (mAP)** across Intersection-over-Union (IoU) thresholds, notably
   $\text{mAP}@[.50:.05:.95]$ <Cite id="lin2014" />.
2. **Semantic Segmentation**: Assigning a category label to every individual pixel without distinguishing between
   separate object instances. Evaluated via **mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU)** across classes:
   $$
   \text{mIoU} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{|P_k \cap G_k|}{|P_k \cup G_k|}
   $$
3. **Instance Segmentation**: Segmenting individual object masks for every detectable instance of countable objects
   ("things").
4. **Panoptic Segmentation**: Unifying stuff (uncountable amorphous regions like *sky*, *road*, *grass*) and things
   (countable objects like *pedestrian*, *car*) into a non-overlapping pixel partition, evaluated via Panoptic Quality
   (PQ).
5. **Promptable Segmentation (Foundation Models)**: Zero-shot mask generation given points, boxes, or text prompts
   (SA-1B).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### Pascal VOC: The Pioneer of Object Benchmarking (2007–2012)

Organized by Everingham et al. <Cite id="everingham2010" />, the PASCAL Visual Object Classes challenge established
modern spatial evaluation:
- **Scope**: 20 object classes (*aeroplane*, *bicycle*, *bird*, *boat*, *bottle*, *bus*, *car*, *cat*, *chair*, *cow*,
  *diningtable*, *dog*, *horse*, *motorbike*, *person*, *pottedplant*, *sheep*, *sofa*, *train*, *tvmonitor*).
- **Metric Formulation**: Defined the standard $0.5$ IoU threshold for detection true positives, establishing the VOC
  Average Precision metric calculated as the area under the interpolated precision-recall curve.

### MS COCO: Common Objects in Context (2014)

Created by Lin et al. <Cite id="lin2014" /> to shift the visual recognition community from isolated, canonical object
portraits to complex everyday scenes:
- **Dense Context**: Average of $7.3$ object instances per image with severe occlusion, clutter, scale variation, and
  natural background interaction.
- **Strict Metric Standard**: Introduced $\text{mAP}@[.50:.05:.95]$, averaging mAP across 10 IoU thresholds from $0.50$
  to $0.95$ in steps of $0.05$. This penalized loose bounding boxes and forced algorithms (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN,
  DETR) to predict precise object boundaries.
- **Extensions**: Extended to panoptic segmentation (COCO-Panoptic), dense human keypoints (COCO-Keypoints), and dense
  captions.

### Cityscapes: Urban Street Scene Understanding (2016)

Constructed by Cordts et al. <Cite id="cordts2016" /> to advance automotive perception for autonomous driving:
- **Acquisition**: High-resolution video sequences ($2048 \times 1024$) recorded across 50 European cities under varied
  seasons and lighting conditions.
- **High-Quality Ground Truth**: 5,000 images with manual polygon annotations requiring an average of 1.5 hours per
  image, plus 20,000 weakly annotated frames.
- **Benchmark Role**: Standard testbed for real-time semantic segmentation architectures (BiSeNet, PSPNet, SegFormer)
  operating under automotive inference latency constraints.

### SA-1B: Web-Scale Segment Anything (2023)

Developed by Meta AI for the Segment Anything Model (SAM):
- **Scale**: Over 11 million high-resolution images licensed with privacy blur, containing 1.1 billion high-quality
  segmentation masks (400x more masks than any prior dataset).
- **Data Engine Loop**: Trained using a model-in-the-loop pipeline: human annotators corrected model proposals, which
  trained better models, culminating in fully automatic mask generation.

## Choosing a Detection or Segmentation Dataset

- **Standard Bounding Box & Instance Benchmarking**:
  - MS COCO (80 classes, standard mAP metric, ubiquitous baseline)
- **Urban Driving & Dense Street Scene Parsing**:
  - Cityscapes (High-resolution 2048x1024, automotive street views)
- **Long-Tailed Visual Recognition (> 1,000 categories)**:
  - LVIS (Extreme category imbalance, dense small-object masks)
- **Complex Interior / Outdoor Scene Parsing**:
  - ADE20K (150 semantic stuff & things classes)
- **Promptable Mask Foundation Model Pretraining**:
  - SA-1B (Class-agnostic 1.1B masks, zero-shot segmentation)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Polygon Boundary Approximation Artifacts**: Hand-drawn polygon annotations in COCO and Cityscapes often cut off
   fine structures (such as bicycle spokes, hair, and thin tree branches), unfairly penalizing sharp modern
   edge-preserving neural segmenters.
2. **Missing Foreground Object Annotations**: In large-vocabulary benchmarks like Open Images and LVIS, images contain
   thousands of valid visual objects. Human annotators frequently miss background instances, causing false positive
   penalties when models correctly detect unlabelled objects.
3. **IoU Sensitivity to Small Objects**: In small objects ($< 32 \times 32$ pixels), a 1-pixel boundary displacement
   drastically drops the Intersection-over-Union, producing artificially low AP scores for distant objects regardless of
   semantic detection accuracy.
