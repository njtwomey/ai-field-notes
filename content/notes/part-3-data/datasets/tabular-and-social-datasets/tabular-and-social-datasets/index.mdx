<Definition>
**Tabular and social datasets** are the standard tables used to compare methods on structured data: general
classification and regression suites, and datasets about people and economies used for fairness, causal inference,
finance and <NoteLink to="survival-analysis">survival analysis</NoteLink>. Each note lists the datasets of one use, what
they measure, and the known problems that limit what results on them mean.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A tabular method is judged by its performance across many tables, not one. Suites such as OpenML-CC18 and the benchmark
of Grinsztajn and colleagues fix a collection and a protocol, and the latter showed tree ensembles ahead of deep
networks on medium-sized tables <Cite id="grinsztajn2022" />. Social datasets carry more weight. Their labels are
administrative records, their populations are specific places and decades, and their conclusions are often generalised
beyond both. Fairness results drawn from the 1994 Adult census extract, for example, do not all hold on recent census
data <Cite id="ding2021retiring" />.

## Main ideas

**General suites fix a protocol.** <NoteLink to="tabular-benchmark-datasets" /> covers UCI classics, OpenML-CC18 and
modern suites, and their pitfalls: years of tuning on a few UCI tables, missing values coded as zeros, and
<NoteLink to="data-leakage">target leakage</NoteLink> in crowd-contributed data.

**Fairness data encode their history.** <NoteLink to="fairness-datasets" /> covers COMPAS, Adult, German Credit, Law
School Admissions and Folktables. In COMPAS the target is re-arrest, which reflects policing as well as behaviour.

**Causal benchmarks need counterfactuals.** <NoteLink to="causal-inference-datasets" /> covers LaLonde's job-training
data <Cite id="lalonde1986" />, the semi-synthetic IHDP and ACIC benchmarks, the Twins data and Criteo's uplift data.
Semi-synthetic outcomes can favour particular estimators, and weak overlap forces extrapolation.

**Financial data are non-stationary.** <NoteLink to="finance-and-economics-datasets" /> covers FRED-MD macroeconomic
panels, Fama-French factor portfolios, limit order books such as FI-2010 and World Bank indicators. Revised vintages,
survivorship and regime changes inflate backtests.

**<NoteLink to="time-to-event-data">Survival data</NoteLink> are censored.** <NoteLink to="survival-datasets" /> covers
METABRIC, SUPPORT, Rotterdam and GBSG2, and FLCHAIN. Informative censoring and competing risks break the standard
assumptions.
