<Definition>
**Survival datasets** (or time-to-event datasets) are benchmark collections recording the elapsed time $T$ until an
event of interest occurs (such as disease recurrence, patient mortality, customer churn, or mechanical component
failure), paired with an event indicator $\delta \in \{0, 1\}$. They formalize evaluation under **right-censoring**,
where the study concludes or units are lost to follow-up before the event is observed ($T_i = \min(T_i^*, C_i)$)
<Cite id="cox1972, kalbfleisch2002" />.
</Definition>

Standard regression losses (MSE, MAE) fail on survival data because treating censored observation times as exact failure
events introduces systematic downward bias, while dropping censored observations discards informative lower bounds
($T_i^* > C_i$). Survival benchmarks evaluate semiparametric Cox models, random survival forests, and deep neural
survival architectures under partial likelihood and ranking losses <Cite id="katzman2018" />.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Survival benchmarks evaluate models along three core axes:
1. **Concordance Index (C-Index / Harrell's C)**: Measures ranking discrimination—the proportion of evaluable subject
   pairs where the model correctly predicts that the unit with higher predicted risk experiences the event first:
   $$
   C = \frac{\sum_{i < j} \mathbf{1}_{T_i < T_j} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\delta_i = 1} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\hat{r}_i > \hat{r}_j}}{\sum_{i < j} \mathbf{1}_{T_i < T_j} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\delta_i = 1}}
   $$
2. **Time-Dependent Calibration & Brier Score**: Evaluates whether predicted survival curves
   $\hat{S}(t \mid x) = P(T > t \mid x)$ accurately estimate true survival probabilities across time horizons $t$,
   re-weighted via Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighting (IPCW).
3. **High-Dimensional Omics Integration**: Combining clinical baseline covariates (age, grade, stage) with
   high-dimensional microarrays or RNA-seq expression profiles ($P \gg N$) without overfitting <Cite id="curtis2012" />.

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### METABRIC: Molecular Taxonomy of Breast Cancer (2012)

Introduced by Curtis et al. <Cite id="curtis2012" /> and adopted as the canonical genomics benchmark in DeepSurv
<Cite id="katzman2018" />:
- **Data Scale**: 1,981 breast cancer specimens profiling patient clinical indicators (age, tumor size, histological
  grade, hormone receptor status) coupled with extensive microarray gene expression profiles and copy-number
  alterations.
- **Censoring**: $42.1\%$ censoring rate over extended follow-up exceeding 250 months.
- **Role in Machine Learning**: Tests whether deep non-linear feature extractors can extract prognostic hazard
  representations from genomic expression manifolds that outperform standard linear Cox models.

### SUPPORT: Study to Understand Prognoses and Preferences for Outcomes (1995)

Conducted across five major US teaching hospitals to predict survival in severely ill hospitalized patients:
- **Cohort**: 9,105 patients with acute illness across nine disease categories (e.g. acute respiratory failure,
  multi-organ failure, coma).
- **Physiological Measurements**: 14 physiological variables including mean blood pressure, white blood cell count,
  serum creatinine, and APACHE III score.
- **Missing Data Challenge**: Features informative clinical missingness, serving as a dual benchmark for time-to-event
  estimation and missing-data imputation.

### Rotterdam and GBSG2: Generalization Across Clinical Trials

Frequently paired to evaluate survival domain generalization:
- **Rotterdam Study**: 2,293 primary breast cancer patients treated in the Netherlands.
- **German Breast Cancer Study Group (GBSG2)**: 686 node-positive breast cancer patients enrolled in a comprehensive
  multi-center randomized clinical trial.
- **Transfer Evaluation**: Models are traditionally trained on Rotterdam and evaluated on the independent GBSG2 cohort
  to verify that predicted hazard ratios do not overfit to single-hospital treatment protocols.

## Choosing a Survival Dataset

- **High-Dimensional Genomics & Deep Learning**:
  - METABRIC (Gene expression profiles + clinical baselines)
- **Large Clinical Cohort & ICU Physiology**:
  - SUPPORT (9k patients, continuous laboratory vitals)
- **Multi-Center Trial Cross-Validation**:
  - *Rotterdam (Train)*: GBSG2 (Independent OOD Test)
- **High-Censoring Population Epidemiology**:
  - FLCHAIN (> 70% right-censoring rate)
- **Fast Algorithmic Prototyping**:
  - WHAS500 / Veteran's Lung Cancer (N < 1,000, low dimension)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Informative Censoring (Violation of Non-Informative Censoring)**: Standard survival models assume the censoring
   distribution $C$ is independent of the failure time $T^*$ given covariates ($T^* \perp C \mid X$). If sickest
   patients drop out of a clinical study early, censoring is informative, biasing the Kaplan-Meier and Cox estimators
   <Cite id="kalbfleisch2002" />.
2. **Competing Risks**: In elderly or multi-morbidity cohorts (such as FLCHAIN), patients may die of cardiovascular
   events before developing the target hematologic outcome. Treating competing causes of death as simple right-censoring
   violates survival assumptions and inflates cumulative incidence.
3. **Small Sample Overfitting in Genomic Profiling**: Datasets with tens of thousands of gene expression features but
   fewer than 2,000 patients suffer severe risk of discovering spurious genomic correlations unless strong
   regularization or pathway priors are applied.
