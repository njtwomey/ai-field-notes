<Definition>
**Finance and economics datasets** are empirical benchmark collections documenting asset pricing dynamics, order-level
market microstructure, corporate fundamentals, and national macroeconomic aggregates. They formalize machine learning
evaluation under extreme market noise, non-stationary structural breaks, endogenous feedback loops, and strict temporal
point-in-time constraints <Cite id="mccracken2016, ntakaris2018, lopezdeprado2018" />.
</Definition>

Unlike physical sciences where data conforms to invariant natural laws, financial data is generated by strategic,
profit-seeking agents. Pure statistical arbitrage patterns quickly decay, leaving behind heavy-tailed returns,
volatility clustering, and low signal-to-noise ratios ($\text{SNR} \ll 1$) <Cite id="cont2001" />.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Financial and macroeconomic benchmarks span several distinct temporal and structural resolutions:
1. **Microstructure and High-Frequency Execution ($10^{-3}\text{--}10^0$ seconds)**: Multi-level Limit Order Book (LOB)
   dynamics, bid-ask spread crossing, order queue replenishment, and micro-price forecasting <Cite id="ntakaris2018" />.
2. **Cross-Sectional Asset Pricing (Daily to Monthly)**: Factor risk premiums (size, value, momentum, profitability,
   investment) across thousands of equities, evaluated by Sharpe ratio, alpha, and portfolio turnover.
3. **Macroeconomic Forecasting (Monthly to Quarterly)**: High-dimensional panel forecasting of GDP growth, inflation
   (CPI), unemployment, and interest rates subject to vintage revisions <Cite id="mccracken2016" />.
4. **Primary Evaluation Metrics**: Directional accuracy (hit ratio), **Information Ratio**, maximum drawdown, mean
   squared forecast error (MSFE), and economic utility under realistic execution slippage.

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### FRED-MD: Macroeconomic Big Data Panel (2016)

Maintained by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis and introduced by McCracken and Ng <Cite id="mccracken2016" />:
- **Panel Structure**: Over 120 monthly macroeconomic series continuously tracked from January 1959 to present.
- **Eight Economic Groups**: Output and income, labor market, housing, consumption and orders, money and credit,
  interest rates and exchange rates, prices, and stock market indices.
- **Stationarity Transformation Code**: Each series includes a standard transformation code (first difference, log first
  difference, second difference) to achieve covariance stationarity for factor modeling, dynamic factor models (DFM),
  and recurrent forecasting networks.

### Kenneth French Data Library: Cross-Sectional Factors

Maintained by Kenneth French, this library provides standard academic benchmarks for quantitative finance:
- **Factor Portfolios**: Daily, weekly, and monthly returns of the Fama-French 3-Factor (Market, SMB, HML) and 5-Factor
  models (adding RMW profitability and CMA investment).
- **Sorted Portfolios**: 25 portfolios sorted on Size and Book-to-Market, 30 Industry portfolios, and Momentum
  portfolios from 1926 to present.
- **Role**: Serves as the baseline risk adjustment model; machine learning models predicting equity returns must
  demonstrate alpha orthogonal to these benchmark factors.

### FI-2010: Standardized Limit Order Book Benchmark (2018)

Introduced by Ntakaris et al. <Cite id="ntakaris2018" /> to address the lack of public standardized high-frequency
benchmarks:
- **Market Scope**: Collected over 10 trading days on NASDAQ OMX Nordic for five representative Finnish companies
  (Nokia, Wärtsilä, Sampo, Rautaruukki, Metso).
- **Feature Representation**: 4.5 million snapshots of the top 10 price and volume levels on both bid and ask sides
  ($40$-dimensional feature vectors).
- **Benchmark Tasks**: Predict whether the mid-price will rise, fall, or stay within a threshold margin over forecast
  horizons $k \in \{10, 20, 30, 50, 100\}$ events.

## Choosing a Finance or Economics Dataset

- **Microsecond to Millisecond Market Microstructure**:
  - FI-2010 (Normalized 10-level LOB features, standardized splits)
- **Monthly Asset Pricing & Systematic Factor Investing**:
  - Kenneth French Data Library (Size, Value, Momentum factors)
- **Macroeconomic Policy, Inflation, & Business Cycles**:
  - FRED-MD (Monthly 120+ stationarity-transformed series)
- **Multi-Decade Cross-Country Socioeconomic Development**:
  - World Bank Open Data (Global national indicators)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Look-Ahead and Vintage Revision Bias**: Macroeconomic indicators (GDP, non-farm payrolls) are routinely revised
   months or years after initial publication <Cite id="mccracken2016" />. Training models on finalized revisions rather
   than real-time first-release vintages introduces severe look-ahead leakage.
2. **Survivorship Bias**: Filtering equity universes using current index constituents (e.g. current S&P 500 members)
   retroactively deletes bankrupt and acquired companies, radically inflating backtested model returns
   <Cite id="lopezdeprado2018" />.
3. **Non-Stationary Regime Shifts**: Market structural breaks (such as the 2008 financial crisis, zero-interest-rate
   regimes, and algorithmic high-frequency trading market structure shifts) render older historical statistical
   relationships non-stationary.
