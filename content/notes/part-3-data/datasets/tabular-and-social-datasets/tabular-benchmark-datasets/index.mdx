<Definition>
**Tabular benchmark datasets** are standardized collections of structured data matrices where rows represent independent
observations and columns represent heterogeneous features (continuous measurements, discrete counts, categorical
indicators, and missing values). They serve as the definitive evaluation ground for comparing tree-based ensembles (such
as XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost) against deep learning architectures for tabular data <Cite id="grinsztajn2022" />.
</Definition>

Unlike vision or audio where data lies on smooth low-dimensional manifolds with spatial or temporal translation
equivariance, tabular datasets feature coordinate-dependent features with arbitrary ordering, extreme marginal skews,
multimodal distributions, and categorical encodings with variable cardinality.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Tabular benchmarks evaluate algorithmic performance across several fundamental challenges:
1. **Heterogeneous Feature Interactions**: Combining dense continuous variables with uncalibrated categorical factors
   without destroying relational structure.
2. **Inductive Biases (Trees vs Neural Networks)**: Evaluating whether neural networks can overcome rotational
   invariance to model axis-aligned decision boundaries, which tree-based recursive splits exploit natively
   <Cite id="grinsztajn2022" />.
3. **Missingness and High Cardinality**: Robustness to informative missing values (Missing Not At Random) and
   categorical levels with long-tailed frequency distributions.
4. **Primary Evaluation Metrics**: Classification tasks report **ROC-AUC**, **log-loss**, and **balanced accuracy**;
   regression benchmarks evaluate **RMSE**, **MAE**, and coefficient of determination ($R^2$).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### Grinsztajn Benchmark Suite (2022)

Grinsztajn, Oyallon, and Varoquaux <Cite id="grinsztajn2022" /> constructed a rigorous tabular benchmark specifically
designed to test the comparative advantages of tree-based ensembles (XGBoost, CatBoost, Random Forests) versus modern
tabular deep architectures (ResNet, FT-Transformer, SAINT):
- **Curated Partitions**: 45 distinct datasets divided into numerical-only datasets and mixed categorical/numerical
  datasets across classification and regression.
- **Strict Protocol**: Medium-sized regimes ($N \le 50{,}000$) where tree models historically dominate; tuning protocols
  with extensive hyperparameter search under fixed computational budgets.
- **Empirical Takeaways**: Tree-based models remain consistently superior to deep models on tabular data, largely
  because neural networks struggle with uninformative features, non-smooth target functions, and lack rotation
  invariance on coordinate axes.

### OpenML-CC18 Curated Benchmark

The OpenML Curated Classification benchmark 2018 (OpenML-CC18) addresses historical benchmark leakage and small-sample
instability:
- **Stringent Inclusion Criteria**: Excludes datasets with fewer than 500 instances or more than 100,000 instances;
  excludes datasets with target leaks, near-deterministic rules, or extreme class imbalance ($> 99\%$).
- **Standardized Splits**: 10-fold cross-validation with fixed seed splits directly downloadable via programmatic APIs,
  preventing split fishing.

### Adult Census Income (1996)

Extracted from the 1994 US Census Bureau database by Ron Kohavi <Cite id="kohavi1996" />:
- **Target**: Predict whether annual income exceeds $50{,}000\text{ USD}$.
- **Attributes**: 14 attributes spanning demographic, educational, occupational, and marital factors.
- **Role**: Longest-running benchmark for both classification algorithms and algorithmic fairness research, though now
  widely recognized to have severe documentation defects and outdated socioeconomic demographics.

### HIGGS: Large-Scale Extreme Scale Physics

Produced by Baldi et al. (2014) from high-energy physics simulations:
- **Scale**: 11 million instances, 28 continuous physical features (kinematic properties of exotic particles).
- **Benchmark Significance**: One of the earliest tabular problems demonstrating that deep multi-layer perceptrons could
  match and slightly outperform gradient boosted decision trees when sample size ($N > 10^7$) is massive and features
  are homogeneous physical measurements.

## Choosing a Tabular Benchmark

- **Rigorous Algorithm Comparison (Trees vs Deep Learning)**:
  - *Mixed categorical & numerical*: Grinsztajn Mixed Suite
  - *Broad multi-task evaluation*: OpenML-CC18
- **Large-Scale Deep Learning Scaling**:
  - *Homogeneous continuous features ($N > 10^7$)*: HIGGS
- **Realistic Mixed Feature Engineering**:
  - *Complex real estate attributes ($P \approx 80$)*: Ames Housing
  - *High-dimensional anonymized vectors*: Kaggle Santander
- **Sociodemographic & Baseline Sanity Check**:
  - *Historical reference (with demographic caveats)*: Adult Census

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Benchmark Overfitting to UCI**: Decades of machine learning research tuned hyperparameters on a tiny subset of UCI
   datasets (Adult, Iris, Wine, Breast Cancer), creating artificial optimizer bias toward specific small tabular
   regimes.
2. **Missing Value Artifacts**: Many classical tabular datasets impute missing categories as `"Unknown"` or numerical
   zeros, obscuring real-world missingness patterns (e.g. missing at random vs missing not at random).
3. **Data Leakage in OpenML/Kaggle**: Many crowd-contributed tabular datasets contain target leakage (features computed
   using the label or point-in-time future information).
