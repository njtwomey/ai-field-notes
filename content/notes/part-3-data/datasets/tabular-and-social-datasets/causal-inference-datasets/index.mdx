<Definition>
**Causal inference and uplift datasets** are empirical and semi-synthetic benchmark corpora structured to evaluate
models estimating treatment effects—including the Average Treatment Effect ($\text{ATE}$) and Conditional Average
Treatment Effect ($\text{CATE}$ or uplift). Because the fundamental problem of causal inference dictates that only one
potential outcome ($Y(1)$ or $Y(0)$) is observed for any given unit, these benchmarks employ randomized controlled
trials (RCTs), synthetic counterfactual simulations, or natural twin twin-birth studies to provide ground truth
<Cite id="rubin1974, lalonde1986" />.
</Definition>

Standard machine learning models optimize correlation rather than intervention. Causal benchmarks evaluate whether
models can adjust for observational confounders $X$, estimate unbiased propensity scores $e(X) = P(T = 1 \mid X)$, and
predict incremental treatment uplift $\tau(X) = \mathbb{E}[Y(1) - Y(0) \mid X]$ <Cite id="imbens2015" />.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Causal benchmarks formalize distinct evaluation regimes:
1. **Average Treatment Effect (ATE)**:
   $$
   \tau = \mathbb{E}[Y(1) - Y(0)]
   $$
   evaluated against known experimental benchmarks (e.g. comparing observational estimates to gold-standard RCT effects
   in LaLonde).
2. **Heterogeneous Treatment Effects (CATE / Uplift)**:
   $$
   \tau(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y(1) - Y(0) \mid X = x]
   $$
   evaluated using **PEHE** (Precision in Estimating Heterogeneous Effects):
   $$
   \text{PEHE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left( (\hat{Y}_i(1) - \hat{Y}_i(0)) - (Y_i(1) - Y_i(0)) \right)^2
   $$
3. **Uplift Curves and Qini Curves**: In marketing and digital interventions, models rank individuals by predicted
   uplift $\hat{\tau}(x)$; performance is measured via the **Qini coefficient** or Area Under the Uplift Curve (AUUC).
4. **Confounder Overlap and Positivity**: Testing estimator stability when treatment assignment propensities $e(X)$
   approach $0$ or $1$ (positivity violations).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### LaLonde: National Supported Work Demonstration (1986)

The foundational econometric benchmark introduced by Robert LaLonde <Cite id="lalonde1986" />:
- **Design**: Analyzed the National Supported Work (NSW) demonstration, an experimental program providing subsidized
  employment to disadvantaged workers.
- **The Finding**: LaLonde replaced the randomized control group with observational control groups drawn from general
  population surveys (CPS and PSID). Standard econometric regression adjustments failed to replicate the experimental
  benchmark, producing wild estimates ranging from positive gains to severe negative penalties.
- **Legacy**: Catalyzed modern observational causal inference, propensity score matching <Cite id="rosenbaum1983" />,
  and doubly robust estimation <Cite id="imbens2015" />.

### IHDP: Infant Health and Development Program (2011)

Constructed by Jennifer Hill (2011) using data from an intensive randomized early-intervention study on low-birth-weight
infants:
- **Design**: Takes 25 real pre-treatment maternal and child covariates from 747 infants, but introduces selection bias
  by deliberately removing a non-random subset of treated children (e.g. children with non-white mothers).
- **Outcomes**: Ground-truth response surfaces $Y(0)$ and $Y(1)$ are simulated using non-linear functions (typically
  setting B from Hill 2011).
- **Benchmarking**: Standard benchmark for comparing Causal Forests, Targeted Maximum Likelihood Estimation (TMLE), and
  causal neural networks (TARNet, CFRNet).

### Twins: Natural Counterfactual Pairing (2017)

Extracted from US birth records (1989–1991) by Louizos et al. (2017):
- **Cohort**: 11,400 twin births where both twins weighed under 2,000g.
- **Setup**: Treatment $T=1$ is assigned to the heavier twin, $T=0$ to the lighter twin. Outcome $Y$ is mortality within
  the first year of life.
- **Quasi-Counterfactual Ground Truth**: Because twins share genetic makeup and gestational environment, the outcome of
  the sibling serves as the counterfactual outcome for the other, allowing empirical evaluation of individual-level
  treatment effect predictions.

### Criteo AI Lab Uplift Benchmark (2018)

The largest publicly available industrial randomized uplift dataset:
- **Scale**: Nearly 14 million impressions with 12 anonymized continuous marketing features.
- **Experiment**: Approximately $84.6\%$ of traffic received advertising impressions ($T=1$), while $15.4\%$ were
  assigned to a randomized control holdout ($T=0$).
- **Business Uplift**: Benchmarks uplift algorithms aiming to identify "persuadable" users (who convert only if shown an
  ad) versus "sure things" (who convert regardless) and "lost causes" (who never convert).

## Choosing a Causal Inference Dataset

- **Methodology & Confounder Adjustment Theory**:
  - LaLonde (Reconciling observational controls with RCT ground truth)
- **Individualized Treatment Effects (CATE) & PEHE**:
  - *Semi-synthetic with simulated response surfaces*: IHDP
  - *Real genetic twin counterfactual pairs*: Twins
- **Large-Scale Machine Learning & High Dimensionality**:
  - ACIC Competitions (Complex non-linear DAGs, sparsity sweeps)
- **Digital Marketing & Incremental Uplift**:
  - *Web advertising clicks & conversions*: Criteo Uplift
  - *Retail email marketing campaigns*: Hillstrom Email

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Semi-Synthetic Artifacts**: In semi-synthetic benchmarks (like IHDP and ACIC), simulated functional forms often
   favor specific estimators (e.g. Gaussian process priors or tree splits) that may not reflect real physical or social
   causal mechanisms.
2. **Positivity Violations**: Many observational benchmarks lack adequate covariate overlap between treated and control
   units. In regions of covariate space where $P(T = 1 \mid X) \approx 0$, causal estimates rely on model extrapolation
   rather than empirical evidence <Cite id="imbens2015" />.
3. **SUTVA Violations**: Most benchmarks assume the Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (no interference between
   units). In digital marketing (e.g. Criteo) or economic network interventions, treatment of one unit frequently
   affects peers.
