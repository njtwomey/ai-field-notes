<Definition>
**Anomaly detection datasets** are benchmark corpora specifically curated to evaluate algorithms in identifying patterns
that do not conform to expected nominal behavior, $P(X \mid Y = \text{nominal})$. Because anomalous events are
inherently rare, polymorphic, and often unobserved during training, these benchmarks formalize unsupervised,
semi-supervised (one-class classification), and weakly supervised out-of-distribution detection across tabular records,
industrial imaging, and time series <Cite id="chandola2009, bergmann2019" />.
</Definition>

Unlike standard balanced classification benchmarks where classes share comparable support, anomaly detection benchmarks
evaluate algorithms under severe class imbalance (often $< 1\%$ anomaly contamination) and open-world novelty, where
test anomalies exhibit structural faults never encountered during training.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Anomaly detection benchmarks formalize distinct operational regimes and evaluation settings
<Cite id="han2022adbench" />:

1. **Unsupervised Anomaly Detection (UAD)**: The training partition contains unlabelled data with an unknown
   contamination rate $\gamma = P(Y = \text{anomaly}) \in [0, 0.1]$. Models must estimate density or reconstruct
   patterns without positive labels.
2. **Semi-Supervised / One-Class Classification (OCC)**: The training partition consists strictly of nominal (clean)
   samples ($Y = 0$). Test sets introduce unseen physical defects, structural anomalies, or out-of-distribution
   instances ($Y = 1$).
3. **Pixel-Level vs Sample-Level Detection**: In vision benchmarks (e.g. MVTec AD, VisA), models must not only output an
   image-level anomaly score $s(X) \in \mathbb{R}$, but also produce pixel-accurate segmentation masks localizing
   scratches, contaminations, or missing subcomponents <Cite id="bergmann2019, zou2022visa" />.
4. **Primary Evaluation Metrics**: Because threshold selection depends on operational false-positive tolerance,
   benchmarks evaluate threshold-free rankings using **ROC-AUC** (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic),
   **PR-AUC** (Precision-Recall AUC), and **PRO** (Per-Region Overlap for pixel localization).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### MVTec AD: Industrial Vision Benchmark (2019)

Introduced by Bergmann et al. <Cite id="bergmann2019" />, MVTec AD is the standard benchmark for unsupervised visual
anomaly detection and defect localization:
- **Structure**: Covers 5 texture categories (*carpet*, *grid*, *leather*, *tile*, *wood*) and 10 object categories
  (*bottle*, *cable*, *capsule*, *hazelnut*, *metal nut*, *pill*, *screw*, *toothbrush*, *transistor*, *zipper*).
- **Protocol**: Models train purely on defect-free nominal images ($3{,}629$ training images). The test set ($1{,}725$
  images) includes both defect-free samples and defective samples across 73 real physical fault classes.
- **Pixel Ground Truth**: Comprehensive pixel-level binary masks accompany every anomalous test sample, enabling exact
  evaluation of localization metrics:
  $$
  \text{PRO} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{|R_k \cap M|}{|R_k|}
  $$
  where $R_k$ represents individual connected ground-truth defect regions and $M$ is the thresholded prediction mask.

### VisA: Visual Anomaly in Multi-Part Objects (2022)

Developed by Zou et al. <Cite id="zou2022visa" />, VisA expands industrial visual anomaly detection to complex objects
with multiple components:
- **Scale**: 10,821 high-resolution images across 12 industrial objects divided into three structural categories:
  complex structures (*printed circuit boards*), multiple object clusters (*capsules*, *macaroni*, *screws*), and single
  objects (*cashews*, *chewing gum*).
- **Challenge**: In multi-object or multi-part settings, anomaly detectors cannot simply model global feature averages;
  they must reason about relative spatial layouts, component counts, and assembly integrity.

### ADBench: Comprehensive Tabular Benchmark (2022)

Curated by Han et al. <Cite id="han2022adbench" />, ADBench establishes a standardized evaluation across 57 tabular
datasets:
- **Data Collections**: Aggregates classical tabular datasets (from medicine, finance, and physics), NLP tabular
  embeddings, and vision tabular embeddings.
- **Controlled Noise Sweeps**: Systematically injects varying anomaly contamination levels
  $\gamma \in \{1\%, 5\%, 10\%, 20\%\}$, irrelevant noise features, and semantic concept shifts.
- **Benchmarked Families**: Evaluates 14 algorithmic paradigms, revealing that ensemble tree methods (Isolation Forest,
  XGBOD) and deep reconstruction models exhibit starkly different trade-offs depending on feature dimensionality and
  contamination rate.

### Credit Card Fraud Detection: Real-World Transaction Imbalance

The ULB Machine Learning Group benchmark provides 284,807 real European credit card transactions from September 2013:
- **Severe Imbalance**: Contains exactly 492 fraud cases ($0.172\%$).
- **Privacy Transformation**: 28 numerical features resulting from Principal Component Analysis (PCA) due to
  confidentiality constraints, along with raw *Time* and transaction *Amount*.
- **Evaluation Discipline**: Standard ROC-AUC can yield deceptively optimistic scores ($> 0.98$) due to the overwhelming
  nominal majority; PR-AUC and cost-sensitive business metrics are mandatory for realistic evaluation.

## Choosing an Anomaly Detection Dataset

- **High-Resolution Visual Inspection & Localization**:
  - *Single objects and continuous textures*: MVTec AD
  - *Multi-part assemblies, components, & counts*: VisA
- **Tabular Outlier Discovery & Benchmarking**:
  - *Systematic benchmark across 57 datasets*: ADBench
  - *Classical medical / sensor point anomalies*: ODDS (Arrhythmia, Thyroid)
- **Financial & Transactional Fraud**:
  - *High-imbalance transaction streams*: Credit Card Fraud

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Synthetic Tabular Inversion**: Many historical tabular outlier benchmarks (including partitions in ODDS) were
   synthesized by downsampling a minority class from a standard classification task (e.g. downsampling class 9 in
   MNIST). Downsampled semantic classes do not exhibit the localized density support or feature distributions typical of
   real operational anomalies <Cite id="han2022adbench" />.
2. **Deceptive ROC-AUC Scores**: In extreme imbalance ($< 0.1\%$ anomalies), a high false-positive rate can still yield
   an ROC-AUC above $0.95$ because the false positive rate denominator includes millions of true negatives. PR-AUC and
   precision at top-$k$ recall must be reported.
3. **Alignment and Camera Rigidity in Visual Benchmarks**: MVTec AD images are captured in controlled, perfectly
   registered industrial settings with fixed camera angles and lighting. Algorithms relying on rigid spatial coordinates
   often fail in real production lines where parts undergo rotation and translation.
