<Definition>
**Known dataset defects** are documented structural, semantic, or legal flaws in foundational machine learning benchmark
corpora. These encompass pervasive label errors within test partitions, near-duplicate sample contamination across train
and evaluation splits, demographic representation biases, and ethical or privacy violations that have precipitated
official dataset retractions <Cite id="northcutt2021pervasive, beyer2020, birhane2021" />.
</Definition>

Machine learning benchmarks are frequently treated as immutable gold standards. However, systematic audits have revealed
that canonical datasets contain non-trivial rates of mislabelling, train-test leakage, and severe copyright or ethical
infringements, leading to artificial score inflation and unstable leaderboard rankings <Cite id="recht2019" />.

## Label Errors in Canonical Test Sets

Standard machine learning evaluation assumes that the test partition provides pristine ground truth. In an audit across
10 benchmark datasets, Northcutt, Athalye, and Mueller <Cite id="northcutt2021pervasive" /> demonstrated that test set
label errors are pervasive, with an average error rate of $3.4\%$:

| Benchmark Dataset | Test Set Size | Estimated Test Label Error Rate | Erroneous Test Samples | Canonical Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **QuickDraw** | 50,000,000 | $\approx 10.12\%$ | $\approx 5{,}060{,}000$ | Google Creative Lab |
| **ImageNet (ILSVRC2012)** | 50,000 | $\approx 5.83\%$ | $\approx 2{,}915$ | Russakovsky et al. (2015) |
| **Amazon Reviews** | 750,000 | $\approx 3.96\%$ | $\approx 29{,}700$ | McAuley & Leskovec |
| **CIFAR-100** | 10,000 | $\approx 4.02\%$ | $\approx 402$ | Krizhevsky (2009) |
| **Caltech-256** | 6,160 | $\approx 3.75\%$ | $\approx 231$ | Griffin et al. (2007) |
| **CIFAR-10** | 10,000 | $\approx 0.54\%$ | $\approx 54$ | Krizhevsky (2009) |
| **MNIST** | 10,000 | $\approx 0.15\%$ | $\approx 15$ | LeCun et al. (1998) |

### The Benchmark Destabilization Effect

When test error rates approach or exceed algorithmic error rates ($5\text{--}6\%$), benchmark rankings become unstable
<Cite id="northcutt2021pervasive" />:
- **Penalizing Superior Models**: Modern high-capacity models (e.g. Vision Transformers) often predict the correct
  visual object (e.g. a digital camera) when the crowdsourced ground-truth label was erroneous (e.g. labeled as *lens
  cap*), causing the superior model to be penalized.
- **ImageNet ReaL**: Beyer et al. <Cite id="beyer2020" /> addressed this in ImageNet by collecting multi-label
  reassessments (**ReaL** labels), revealing that single-label evaluation underestimated modern model accuracy by
  several percentage points and obscured real performance distinctions.

## Train-Test Leakage and Near-Duplicates

Data scraping pipelines frequently ingest duplicated, slightly resized, cropped, or re-compressed instances across
splits:
1. **Exact Duplicates**: Identical byte streams or identical feature vectors appearing in both training and test
   partitions.
2. **Perceptual Near-Duplicates**: In web corpora (e.g. C4, LAION-5B) and vision benchmarks (CIFAR, ImageNet), images or
   text passages with minor formatting variations appear in both splits <Cite id="dodge2021" />.
3. **Generalization Inflation**: Models that memorize training samples achieve artificially high validation metrics
   without learning underlying conceptual rules. Deduplication using MinHash LSH or perceptual hashing (pHash) often
   drops measured validation performance by $1\text{--}5\%$.

## Retracted and Deprecated Datasets

Multiple foundational corpora have been formally retracted, restricted, or taken offline due to severe ethical, legal,
or privacy violations:

| Dataset | Modality / Origin | Reason for Withdrawal / Deprecation | Outcome & Lesson |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **80 Million Tiny Images** | Vision ($32 \times 32$ images, MIT) | Contained thousands of racially derogatory labels, slurs, and non-consensual pornographic imagery <Cite id="birhane2021" /> | **Permanently retracted** in 2020 by MIT; researchers urged to delete all local copies. |
| **MS-Celeb-1M** | Vision (10M face images, Microsoft) | Scraped public figures, journalists, and civilians without explicit consent or right-of-publicity agreements | **Taken down** in 2019 following privacy and surveillance ethics scrutiny. |
| **DukeMTMC** | Surveillance Video (Duke University) | Recorded students on campus without consent for multi-target multi-camera pedestrian tracking | **Retracted** in 2019 due to IRB and human surveillance ethics violations. |
| **Stanford Brainwash** | Surveillance Video (Stanford University) | Secretly recorded patrons inside a college cafe without knowledge or consent | **Taken down** in 2019 by Stanford researchers. |
| **LAION-5B** | 5.85B multimodal image-text pairs | Audits identified non-trivial volumes of illegal CSAM URLs within Common Crawl scrape indices | **Temporarily taken down** in late 2023 for rigorous filtering and safety sanitization. |

## Remediation and Benchmark Hygiene

Addressing dataset defects requires strict procedural hygiene throughout the data lifecycle:
- **Clean Test Sets**: Re-annotating test splits with multi-annotator consensus or multi-label targets (such as ImageNet
  ReaL <Cite id="beyer2020" /> and CIFAR-10N <Cite id="northcutt2021pervasive" />).
- **Rigorous Cross-Split Deduplication**: Applying semantic deduplication (e.g. MinHash LSH, embedding cosine similarity
  $> 0.95$, pHash) prior to partitioning splits.
- **Dataset Auditing and Documentation**: Releasing standardized documentation (Datasheets for Datasets, Model Cards)
  documenting data provenance, annotator demographics, filtering heuristics, and copyright licensing.
