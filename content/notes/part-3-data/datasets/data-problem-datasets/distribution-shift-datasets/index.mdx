<Definition>
**Distribution-shift datasets** are curated benchmark collections specifically structured to evaluate machine learning
models when the test distribution diverges from the training distribution,
$P_{\text{train}}(X, Y) \ne P_{\text{test}}(X, Y)$. Rather than uniform random splits, they partition data across
physical hospital sites, geographical regions, sensor deployments, acquisition years, synthetic image corruptions, or
demographic subpopulations to measure out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization and spurious shortcut reliance
<Cite id="koh2021, hendrycks2019" />.
</Definition>

Standard empirical risk minimization (ERM) on independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) test sets conceals deep
model vulnerabilities: neural networks exploit spurious background correlations and brittle high-frequency textures that
collapse under minor operational domain shifts <Cite id="recht2019, sagawa2020" />.

## What These Datasets Measure

Distribution-shift benchmarks formalize distinct mathematical failure modes:
1. **Covariate Shift**: $P_{\text{train}}(X) \ne P_{\text{test}}(X)$ while labeling rule $P(Y \mid X)$ remains invariant
   (e.g. ImageNet-C corruptions or camera trap angle shifts).
2. **Concept Shift**: $P_{\text{train}}(Y \mid X) \ne P_{\text{test}}(Y \mid X)$ (e.g. medical diagnosis criteria
   varying across hospital protocols).
3. **Subpopulation and Group Shift**: The dataset partitions into discrete groups $g \in \mathcal{G}$ defined by
   attributes (e.g. race, background landscape), where group proportions change between train and test distributions
   <Cite id="sagawa2020" />.
4. **Temporal Shift**: Data collected chronologically, where test data reflects future time horizons (e.g. FMoW
   satellite imagery across 2002–2018).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### WILDS: In-the-Wild Distribution Shifts (2021)

Developed by Koh et al. <Cite id="koh2021" />, WILDS unifies 10 real-world datasets spanning diverse domains where
shifts occur naturally:
- **Camelyon17 (Biomedical Vision)**: Lymph node histopathology slides collected across 5 hospitals. Models train on 3
  hospitals and evaluate on an unseen 4th hospital, testing robustness to tissue staining and slide scanner artifacts.
- **iWildCam (Ecology Vision)**: 200,000 animal photos from camera traps. Models train on images from a set of camera
  traps and evaluate on entirely unseen camera traps in new forest locations.
- **PovertyMap (Remote Sensing)**: Multispectral satellite imagery predicting rural wealth indices across 23 African
  countries, split geographically by country to test spatial out-of-domain transfer.
- **CivilComments-WILDS (NLP)**: 450,000 online comments evaluated for toxicity, evaluated across 8 demographic identity
  groups (e.g. mentions of LGBTQ+, Black, Christian) using worst-group accuracy.
- **Amazon-WILDS (NLP)**: Product sentiment classification where training and test partitions contain disjoint sets of
  user reviewers, testing robustness to individual writing styles.

### ImageNet-C and Corruption Robustness (2019)

Hendrycks and Dietterich <Cite id="hendrycks2019" /> constructed ImageNet-C by systematically applying 15 algorithmic
corruptions to the ImageNet validation set across 5 discrete severity levels $s \in \{1, \dots, 5\}$:
- **Noise**: Gaussian, Shot, and Impulse noise (sensor ISO grain).
- **Blur**: Defocus, Glass, Motion, and Zoom blur (camera lens and vehicle motion).
- **Weather**: Snow, Frost, Fog, and Brightness (atmospheric visibility).
- **Digital**: Contrast, Elastic transform, Pixelation, and JPEG compression.

Robustness is evaluated via **Mean Corruption Error (mCE)**, normalizing the model's classification error $E_{s, c}^f$
against standard AlexNet:

$$
\text{mCE} = \frac{1}{15} \sum_{c=1}^{15} \left( \frac{\sum_{s=1}^5 E_{s, c}^f}{\sum_{s=1}^5 E_{s, c}^{\text{AlexNet}}} \right)
$$

### ImageNetV2: Test Set Reproduction (2019)

Recht et al. <Cite id="recht2019" /> repeated the original 2012 ImageNet data collection protocol from Mechanical Turk
ten years later to test whether models had overfitted to the static ImageNet validation set:
- Followed identical search queries, candidate image retrieval, and human annotation filtering to construct 10,000 new
  test images.
- **Universal Generalization Drop**: Across every neural network architecture tested (AlexNet, VGG, ResNet,
  EfficientNet), top-1 accuracy dropped by $11\text{--}14\%$ absolute percentage points on ImageNetV2.
- Revealed that models learn dataset-specific distribution quirks rather than invariant physical visual concepts.

### Waterbirds: Spurious Correlation and Group DRO (2020)

Sagawa et al. <Cite id="sagawa2020" /> synthesized Waterbirds by placing foreground bird images from Caltech-UCSD Birds
(CUB-200) onto land and water backgrounds from the Places dataset:
- Four subpopulations
  $g \in \{(\text{landbird}, \text{land}), (\text{landbird}, \text{water}), (\text{waterbird}, \text{land}), (\text{waterbird}, \text{water})\}$.
- **Spurious Correlation in Training Set**: $95\%$ of waterbirds appear on water backgrounds; $95\%$ of landbirds appear
  on land backgrounds.
- Standard ERM models learn to predict the background landscape rather than bird anatomy, achieving $> 97\%$ overall
  accuracy on the majority groups while dropping to $< 60\%$ on minority groups (waterbirds on land).
- Benchmarks **Group Distributionally Robust Optimization (Group DRO)**:
  $$
  \min_\theta \max_{g \in \mathcal{G}} \mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim P_g} \left[ \ell(f_\theta(x), y) \right]
  $$
  which directly minimizes the worst-case loss across all demographic subpopulations.

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Synthetic vs Natural Shift Gap**: While ImageNet-C corruptions (Gaussian noise, pixelation) are standardized, high
   performance on synthetic corruptions does not reliably guarantee robustness to natural physical domain shifts (such
   as changing hospital scanners in Camelyon17).
2. **Trade-off Between Average and Worst-Group Accuracy**: Methods optimizing worst-case group robustness (such as Group
   DRO or reweighting) often suffer an inductive penalty on average overall test accuracy.
3. **Availability of Group Annotations**: Group DRO and subgroup benchmarks assume that domain labels
   $g \in \mathcal{G}$ are perfectly known at training time, which is rarely true in unstructured production
   deployments.
