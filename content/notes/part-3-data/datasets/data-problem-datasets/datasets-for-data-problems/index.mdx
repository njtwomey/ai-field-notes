<Definition>
**Datasets for data problems** are benchmarks whose purpose is to reproduce a defect of real data in a controlled,
measurable way: a shift between training and test distributions, corrupted labels, or rare anomalous examples. A
fourth group of notes records defects found after the fact in datasets built for other purposes.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Methods for robustness, noisy labels and anomaly detection cannot be judged on clean, identically distributed data. They
need a benchmark where the problem is present and the ground truth is known. Shift benchmarks hold out domains,
hospitals, regions or years <Cite id="koh2021" />. Noisy-label benchmarks pair noisy labels with a clean subset. Anomaly
benchmarks mark the rare cases. The same scrutiny applies to the standard benchmarks themselves: about 6% of the
<Gloss name="imagenet" /> validation labels are wrong <Cite id="northcutt2021pervasive" />, which changes the ranking of
models near the top.

## Main ideas

**Shift benchmarks hold out an axis.** <NoteLink to="distribution-shift-datasets" /> covers WILDS for natural shifts
across domains, DomainBed for <NoteLink to="domain-generalisation">domain generalisation</NoteLink>, ImageNet-C for
synthetic corruptions, ImageNetV2 for a fresh replication of a test set, and Waterbirds for
<NoteLink to="subpopulation-shift-and-shortcuts">spurious correlations</NoteLink>. Each says which axis is held out and
how a gap should be read.

**Noisy-label benchmarks need clean references.** <NoteLink to="noisy-label-datasets" /> covers synthetic noise
injected through a transition matrix, human noise from crowdsourcing such as CIFAR-10N, and web noise such as
Clothing1M and WebVision, with the clean subsets used to score methods.

**Anomaly benchmarks fix what counts as anomalous.** <NoteLink to="anomaly-detection-datasets" /> covers tabular suites
such as ADBench <Cite id="han2022adbench" /> and ODDS, industrial inspection images such as MVTec AD and VisA, and fraud
data. Many older tabular suites make anomalies by downsampling one class of a classification task, and such anomalies
look unlike operational ones.

**Standard datasets have defects.** <NoteLink to="known-dataset-defects" /> catalogues label errors in canonical test
sets, near-duplicates across train and test splits, and datasets withdrawn for ethical or legal reasons.
