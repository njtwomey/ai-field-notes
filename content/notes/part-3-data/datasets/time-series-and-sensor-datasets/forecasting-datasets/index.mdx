<Definition>
**Forecasting datasets** are time-series benchmark collections structured to evaluate algorithms that predict future
continuous trajectories $\mathbf{y}_{t+1:t+H} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times H}$ given historical lookback observations
$\mathbf{x}_{1:t} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times L}$ over fixed forecast horizons $H$. Spanning global macroeconomic series,
retail supply chains, electrical power grids, and multi-billion-point pretraining repositories, they evaluate
autoregressive statistical models, deep temporal architectures, and time-series foundation models
<Cite id="makridakis2020, zhou2021" />.
</Definition>

Forecasting benchmarks differ fundamentally from standard regression: observations exhibit strict temporal causality
(future observations cannot be shuffled or seen during training), multi-scale seasonality (hourly, daily, weekly, annual
cycles), non-stationary trends, and intermittent zero-demand spikes.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Forecasting benchmarks evaluate models across several structural regimes:
1. **Univariate Multi-Horizon Forecasting**: Predicting future scalar values across fixed forecast horizons
   ($H \in [6, 48]$), evaluated by scale-independent metrics such as **Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE)** and **sMAPE**
   <Cite id="makridakis2020" />:
   $$
   \text{MASE} = \frac{\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^H |y_{t+h} - \hat{y}_{t+h}|}{\frac{1}{t-1}\sum_{i=2}^t |y_i - y_{i-1}|}
   $$
2. **Long-Sequence Time-Series Forecasting (LSTF)**: Evaluating transformer and linear architectures (Informer, DLinear,
   PatchTST) on extended forecast horizons ($H \in [96, 720]$ time steps) under multivariate sensor feeds (ETT, Weather,
   Traffic) <Cite id="zhou2021" />.
3. **Hierarchical & Intermittent Supply Chain Demand**: Aggregating SKU sales across geographical store hierarchies
   (Walmart M5), where items exhibit sparse, intermittent zero-inflated demand distributions.
4. **Zero-Shot Pretraining (Foundation Models)**: Pretraining large time-series transformers (e.g. Chronos, TimesFM,
   MOIRAI) on multi-billion-token corpora (LOTSA) for zero-shot inference on held-out datasets (GIFT-Eval).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### The M4 Competition: 100,000 Time Series (2018–2020)

The fourth Makridakis Competition <Cite id="makridakis2020" />:
- **Scale and Rigor**: 100,000 diverse time series spanning demographic, finance, macro, micro, and industry categories
  across yearly, quarterly, monthly, weekly, daily, and hourly frequencies.
- **The Breakthrough**: The winning method (Smyl's ES-RNN) combined classical Holt-Winters exponential smoothing with
  deep recurrent neural networks, demonstrating that hybrid statistical-neural models outperformed pure deep learning or
  pure statistical methods.
- **Evaluation Discipline**: Enforced strict out-of-sample holdout partitions and established the Overall Weighted
  Average (OWA) combining sMAPE and MASE.

### ETT: Electricity Transformer Temperature Benchmark (2021)

Introduced by Zhou et al. <Cite id="zhou2021" /> alongside the Informer architecture:
- **Two Sub-Stations**: Two separate electricity transformer stations in China (ETTh1/ETTh2 hourly; ETTm1/ETTm2
  15-minute resolution) recorded across 24 months.
- **LSTF Standard**: Established the standard Long-Sequence Time-Series Forecasting benchmark, testing model performance
  when forecast horizons scale up to $H \in \{96, 192, 336, 720\}$ steps.
- **Architecture Proving Ground**: The battleground where PatchTST, DLinear, and modern linear forecasting networks
  proved that simple channel-independent linear mappings could consistently outperform complex quadratic-attention
  temporal transformers.

### M5 Competition: Hierarchical Retail Demand (2020)

Hosted on Kaggle by Makridakis et al.:
- **Hierarchical Coherence**: Real point-of-sale data from Walmart covering 30,490 unique products across 10 stores in
  California, Texas, and Wisconsin.
- **Exogenous Variables**: Paired with calendar events (Super Bowl, Easter), promotions, SNAP food-stamp benefits, and
  item prices.
- **Tree Ensembles Domination**: LightGBM models with recursive lag features and target encoding dominated the
  leaderboard, proving the continued power of gradient boosting on tabular-time series data.

## Choosing a Forecasting Dataset

- **Long-Horizon Deep Learning & Transformer Architectures**:
  - ETT / Electricity / Traffic / Weather (Horizons H in [96, 720])
- **Multi-Scale Statistical & Hybrid Model Benchmarking**:
  - M4 Competition (100k series, yearly to hourly frequencies)
- **Retail Supply Chain & Hierarchical Intermittent Demand**:
  - M5 Competition (Walmart sales, hierarchical aggregations)
- **Foundation Model Zero-Shot Pretraining & Evaluation**:
  - *Pretraining corpus (27B observations)*: LOTSA
  - *Zero-shot foundation model evaluation*: GIFT-Eval / Monash Archive

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Information Leakage via Point-in-Time Forward Filtering**: Normalizing entire time series using global dataset mean
   and variance $\mu_{\text{global}}, \sigma_{\text{global}}$ before splitting leaks future scale into training windows.
   Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN) is necessary to ensure causality.
2. **Short-Term Repetitive Baselines**: In smooth datasets (like ETT oil temperature), repeating the last observed value
   (naive persistence) or copying the prior seasonal cycle often achieves competitive MSE against billion-parameter
   models <Cite id="zhou2021" />.
3. **Point-Prediction Limitations**: Minimizing MSE only yields conditional mean forecasts
   $\mathbb{E}[y_{t+h} \mid x_{1:t}]$. Operational decisions in energy grids and supply chains require probabilistic
   quantile forecasts (Continuous Ranked Probability Score, CRPS).
