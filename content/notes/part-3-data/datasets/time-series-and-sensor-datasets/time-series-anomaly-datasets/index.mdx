<Definition>
**Time-series anomaly datasets** are sequential benchmark collections capturing continuous temporal telemetry from web
servers, industrial cyber-physical control systems, spacecraft sensors, and synthetic oscillators where rare anomalous
segments diverge from nominal temporal dynamics. They benchmark unsupervised, semi-supervised, and self-supervised
anomaly detectors on point spikes, structural regime changes, contextual frequency anomalies, and subsequence motif
breakdowns <Cite id="wu2021, dau2019" />.
</Definition>

Detecting anomalies in time series requires reasoning over temporal context: a scalar measurement of $80^\circ\text{C}$
may be perfectly normal during peak afternoon load but catastrophic at midnight. Benchmarks evaluate whether models can
learn normal temporal dynamics and identify unexpected deviations under extreme class imbalance <Cite id="wu2021" />.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Time-series anomaly benchmarks evaluate distinct failure modes:
1. **Point Anomalies**: Isolated single-timestep spikes or dropouts that exceed local noise thresholds.
2. **Contextual & Seasonal Anomalies**: Values that fall within global min-max ranges but violate expected periodic
   cycles (e.g. high CPU load during scheduled maintenance vs off-peak hours).
3. **Subsequence Pattern Anomalies**: Extended segments exhibiting abnormal frequencies, phase shifts, or shapelet motif
   breakdowns (e.g. flatlines or arrhythmia).
4. **Evaluation Metrics & Point-Adjusted Pitfalls**: Traditional point-adjusted metrics (where predicting a single true
   positive timestep marks the entire anomaly window as correctly detected) artificially inflate F1-scores to $> 0.95$
   on trivial baselines, driving adoption of Range-AUC, Affiliation-F1, and VUS (Volume Under the Surface)
   <Cite id="wu2021" />.

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### The UCR Anomaly Archive: Exposing Benchmark Flaws (2021)

Constructed by Renjie Wu and Eamonn Keogh <Cite id="wu2021" /> at UC Riverside:
- **Critique of Existing Suites**: Wu and Keogh demonstrated that popular benchmarks (NAB, Yahoo S5, SMD) suffered from
  severe fatal flaws: trivial anomalies solvable by 1-line thresholding rules, mislabeled ground truth, and unrealistic
  synthetic artifacts that created an "illusion of progress" in the literature.
- **The UCR Fix**: Curated 250 diverse datasets derived from human electrocardiograms, NASA space telemetry, insect
  flight logs, and robotics where each dataset contains exactly one genuine anomaly window carefully verified by domain
  experts.
- **Protocol Rule**: Prohibited manual parameter tuning on the test split, requiring fully unsupervised or
  self-supervised score generation.

### Server Machine Dataset (SMD, 2019)

Curated by Su et al. from a major commercial cloud provider:
- **Telemetry Scale**: Collected over 5 weeks from 28 server machines across 38 distinct metric dimensions (e.g. CPU
  load, network ingress/egress, memory usage, disk I/O).
- **Industrial Role**: Standard testbed for evaluating multivariate deep reconstruction models (such as OmniAnomaly,
  USAD, and TranAD) that model correlations across hundreds of server telemetry metrics.

### Secure Water Treatment (SWaT, 2016)

Constructed at the Singapore University of Technology and Design:
- **Physical Testbed**: A scaled-down operational water treatment facility running a 6-stage industrial process
  (chemical dosing, ultrafiltration, dechlorination, reverse osmosis).
- **Cyber-Physical Attacks**: Recorded continuous telemetry from 51 sensors and actuators (flow meters, motorized
  valves, pumps) while subjecting the physical plant to 36 distinct cyber-attacks designed to cause physical overflows,
  pipe bursts, and water contamination.

## Choosing a Time-Series Anomaly Dataset

- **Rigorous Algorithm Comparison (Flaw-Free Benchmark)**:
  - UCR Anomaly Archive (250 curated single-anomaly series)
- **Large-Scale Multi-Domain Evaluation**:
  - TSB-UAD (1,980 series, standardized Affiliation/VUS metrics)
- **Multivariate Cloud Server Telemetry ($D \approx 40$)**:
  - Server Machine Dataset (SMD)
- **Cyber-Physical & Industrial Control Systems**:
  - SWaT (51 channels, real physical water treatment plant attacks)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Flawed Point-Adjusted Evaluation**: In standard benchmarks, if an algorithm triggers an alert at any single
   timestep within an anomalous window $[t_1, t_2]$, standard point-adjustment protocols retroactively credit the
   algorithm with correctly detecting the entire window. Wu and Keogh <Cite id="wu2021" /> showed that random noise
   guessing achieves state-of-the-art F1-scores under this metric.
2. **Trivial Anomaly Signatures**: Many legacy datasets in Yahoo S5 and NAB can be solved with $100\%$ precision by
   finding the global maximum value ($\arg\max |x_t|$) or computing rolling z-scores, rendering complex deep models
   unnecessary <Cite id="wu2021" />.
3. **Run-to-Failure Scarcity**: Real industrial machinery operates for months before experiencing true mechanical
   failure. Datasets without true physical degradation often rely on synthetic perturbations that fail to reflect real
   physics.
