<Definition>
**Time-series and sensor datasets** are the archives on which methods for series classification, forecasting,
<NoteLink to="anomaly-detection">anomaly detection</NoteLink> and activity recognition are compared. Each fixes a set of
series, a split and a metric. The notes list the standard archives, what they measure and the flaws that affect results
on them.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Series methods are compared across many datasets because no single series is representative. The UCR archive gave
classification a shared set of 128 univariate problems with fixed splits <Cite id="dau2019" />. The M-competitions gave
forecasting large, public, out-of-sample comparisons <Cite id="makridakis2020" />. Both changed which methods were
believed to work. Several of the most cited archives also have serious flaws. Some anomaly benchmarks can be solved by a
one-line rule <Cite id="wu2021" />, and the point-adjusted metric often used with them credits a detector with a
whole anomalous window after a single hit. Knowing the
flaws is part of using the data.

## Main ideas

**Classification archives fix the splits.** <NoteLink to="time-series-classification-datasets" /> covers the UCR
univariate and UEA multivariate archives. Many methods were tuned on the published splits, and many UCR series are
outlines of images rather than measurements over time.

**Forecasting suites span frequencies and domains.** <NoteLink to="forecasting-datasets" /> covers the M-competitions,
the Monash archive, the ETT, electricity and traffic sets used for long-horizon forecasting, and LOTSA for foundation
models. On smooth series, repeating the last value is a strong baseline that many papers omit.

**Anomaly benchmarks need scrutiny.** <NoteLink to="time-series-anomaly-datasets" /> covers NAB, Yahoo S5, SMD, SWaT,
the UCR anomaly archive and TSB-UAD, with their trivial anomalies, mislabelled points and point-adjusted scoring.

**Activity data test generalisation to people.** <NoteLink to="activity-recognition-datasets" /> covers UCI HAR,
WISDM, PAMAP2, Opportunity and the free-living Capture-24. Scores from scripted laboratory sessions overstate accuracy
in daily life, and random splits of <NoteLink to="time-series-windowing">overlapping windows</NoteLink> leak subjects.
