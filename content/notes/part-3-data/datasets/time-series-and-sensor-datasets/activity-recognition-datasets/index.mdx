<Definition>
**Activity recognition and wearable datasets** are multi-channel sensor benchmark collections capturing continuous
body-worn inertial measurements—including triaxial accelerometers ($a_x, a_y, a_z$), gyroscopes
($\omega_x, \omega_y, \omega_z$), magnetometers, and photoplethysmography (PPG) vitals—paired with annotated human
physical activity classes. They benchmark ambulatory gait classification, posture transitions, sports analytics, and
free-living daily activity tracking across varying body placements and sensor sampling rates
<Cite id="anguita2013, reiss2012pamap2, bulling2014" />.
</Definition>

Human Activity Recognition (HAR) models must separate dynamic user motion from static gravitational acceleration vectors
($1g \approx 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$). Because human biomechanics vary across body builds, ages, and gait styles, evaluation
mandates strict leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) cross-validation to test cross-person generalization rather than random
sample shuffling <Cite id="chan2024capture24" />.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

HAR benchmarks formalize several distinct signal processing and recognition challenges:
1. **Locomotion vs Postural Static States**: Distinguishing periodic repetitive movements (*walking*, *jogging*,
   *climbing stairs*) from stationary postures (*sitting*, *standing*, *lying down*) from sliding inertial windows
   <Cite id="anguita2013" />.
2. **Body Sensor Placement & Multi-Node Fusion**: Evaluating how recognition accuracy degrades when sensors shift
   between dominant wrist, waist, chest, ankle, or pocket locations (PAMAP2, Opportunity).
3. **Laboratory Scripting vs Unconstrained Free Living**: Comparing clean laboratory protocols (where subjects perform
   activities on command in fixed order) against unscripted natural 24-hour free-living environments where activity
   transitions are continuous and rare (Capture-24, SPHERE) <Cite id="chan2024capture24, twomey2016sphere" />.
4. **Primary Evaluation Metrics**: **Macro F1-score**, per-class sensitivity, and Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO)
   cross-validation accuracy.

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### UCI HAR: The Benchmark Prototyping Standard (2013)

Introduced by Anguita et al. <Cite id="anguita2013" /> at the University of Genoa:
- **Smartphone Sensing**: Mounted a Samsung Galaxy S2 smartphone on the waist of 30 volunteers aged 19–48.
- **Protocol**: Volunteers performed six basic activities: *walking*, *walking upstairs*, *walking downstairs*,
  *sitting*, *standing*, and *laying down*.
- **Feature Extraction Pipeline**: Provided both raw triaxial accelerometer/gyroscope signals ($128$-sample sliding
  windows with $50\%$ overlap) and a pre-computed 561-dimensional feature vector capturing time-domain statistics and
  FFT spectral energies.

### PAMAP2: Multi-Sensor Physical Activity Monitoring (2012)

Created by Reiss and Stricker <Cite id="reiss2012pamap2" />:
- **Multi-Node Body Sensor Network**: Positioned three high-frequency Colibri wireless IMUs on the subject's dominant
  wrist, chest, and dominant ankle, recording 3D acceleration, gyroscopic rotation, and magnetometer fields at 100 Hz
  alongside wireless heart rate monitoring.
- **Protocol Richness**: Evaluated 18 distinct activities spanning low-intensity domestic tasks (ironing, vacuum
  cleaning), locomotion (Nordic walking, running), and vigorous athletic training (cycling, rope jumping).

### Capture-24: Large-Scale Unconstrained Free Living (2024)

Developed by Chan et al. <Cite id="chan2024capture24" /> from the University of Oxford:
- **Free-Living Paradigm**: Over 151 participants wore an Axivity AX3 triaxial accelerometer on their dominant wrist for
  continuous 24-hour periods during their normal daily lives.
- **Wearable Camera Validation**: Subjects wore a chest-mounted automated time-lapse camera (SenseCam) that took photos
  every 20–30 seconds, providing over 4,000 hours of visually verified ground-truth labels.
- **Real-World Reality Check**: Exposes the massive performance drop when models trained on tidy laboratory datasets are
  deployed in real-world messy home environments where activities are fluid, unscripted, and interspersed with long
  sedentary intervals.

## Choosing an Activity Recognition Dataset

- **Fast Baseline & Algorithmic Benchmarking**:
  - UCI HAR (Clean waist smartphone signals, standard benchmark)
- **Multi-Node Inertial Sensor Network Fusion**:
  - PAMAP2 (Wrist + Chest + Ankle IMUs + Heart Rate vitals)
- **Fine-Grained Object & Kitchen Manipulations**:
  - Opportunity (72 wearable & ambient sensors, fine gestures)
- **True In-the-Wild 24-Hour Free-Living Monitoring**:
  - Capture-24 (151 participants, 24h unscripted, camera ground truth)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **The In-the-Lab vs Free-Living Generalization Gap**: Models achieving $> 98\%$ accuracy on scripted laboratory
   benchmarks (like UCI HAR) regularly drop to $< 60\%$ on free-living cohorts (like Capture-24) due to natural movement
   variation and unstructured transitions <Cite id="chan2024capture24" />.
2. **Subject Leakage Across Folds**: Randomly splitting overlapping sliding windows across train and test sets causes
   extreme data leakage (adjacent windows share $50\%$ identical samples), giving the false impression of near-perfect
   generalization. Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) partitioning is mandatory <Cite id="bulling2014" />.
3. **Sensor Orientation and Frame Misalignment**: Wearable devices rotate freely around wrists or slip inside pockets,
   altering the relative projection of the 1g gravity vector across coordinate axes unless orientation-invariant
   magnitude transforms ($r = \sqrt{a_x^2 + a_y^2 + a_z^2}$) are applied.
