<Definition>
**Multilingual datasets** are parallel and cross-lingual benchmark corpora spanning dozens to hundreds of diverse human
languages across multiple typographical scripts and language families. They formalize machine learning evaluation for
many-to-many machine translation, cross-lingual representation transfer, and zero-shot cross-lingual language
understanding (where models fine-tune on English supervision and evaluate directly on non-English targets)
<Cite id="hu2020xtreme, goyal2022flores, conneau2020xlmr" />.
</Definition>

While high-resource languages (English, Chinese, Spanish) have petabytes of digitized web text, the vast majority of the
world's 7,000+ living languages suffer from severe digital data poverty. Multilingual benchmarks provide verified,
human-translated evaluation suites to assess equitable linguistic performance and prevent digital language extinction
<Cite id="goyal2022flores" />.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Multilingual benchmarks evaluate models along three primary methodological axes:
1. **Zero-Shot Cross-Lingual Transfer**: Fine-tuning a multilingual encoder (such as mBERT or XLM-RoBERTa) on labeled
   English data for tasks like sentiment, NER, or QA, and evaluating transfer accuracy on target language $L_i$ without
   any target-language training examples <Cite id="conneau2020xlmr" />.
2. **Massively Multilingual Machine Translation**: Translating between any language pair $(L_i \to L_j)$ in an
   $N \times N$ matrix, evaluated by sentence-level spBLEU and chrF++ scores (FLORES) <Cite id="goyal2022flores" />.
3. **Typological & Script Diversity**: Measuring performance across disparate morphological structures (analytic,
   agglutinative, fusional) and non-Latin writing systems (Cyrillic, Devanagari, Arabic, Ge'ez, CJK).
4. **Primary Evaluation Metrics**: **spBLEU** (SentencePiece BLEU), **chrF++** (character n-gram F-score), cross-lingual
   macro F1, and cross-lingual transfer gap ($\text{Acc}_{\text{English}} - \text{Acc}_{\text{Target}}$).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### FLORES-101 and FLORES-200: Many-to-Many Translation (2022)

Developed by Meta AI for the No Language Left Behind (NLLB) project <Cite id="goyal2022flores" />:
- **N-Way Parallel Design**: 3,001 sentences sampled from English Wikipedia, covering news, travel, and books,
  translated by professional human translators into 204 distinct languages.
- **True Cross-Lingual Evaluation**: Because every single sentence is translated into all 204 languages, researchers can
  evaluate translation between any arbitrary language pair ($204 \times 203 = 41{,}412$ directions) without English as
  an intermediate pivot language.
- **Low-Resource Focus**: Includes dozens of African, South Asian, and indigenous languages that historically had zero
  standardized evaluation sets.

### XTREME: Multi-Task Cross-Lingual Benchmark (2020)

Constructed by Google Research and DeepMind (Hu et al. <Cite id="hu2020xtreme" />):
- **40 Diverse Languages**: Spanning 14 language families and 12 writing scripts.
- **Nine Canonical Tasks**:
  - *Sentence Classification*: XNLI and PAWS-X (paraphrase identification).
  - *Structured Tagging*: Universal Dependencies (POS tagging) and WikiANN (NER).
  - *Question Answering*: XQuAD, MLQA, and TyDi QA.
  - *Sentence Retrieval*: Tatoeba bitext alignment.
- **Standard Protocol**: Evaluates the *cross-lingual transfer gap*—how much accuracy drops when an English-trained
  model is deployed on low-resource target languages without target-language adaptation.

### XNLI: Cross-Lingual Natural Language Inference (2018)

Introduced by Conneau et al.:
- **High-Fidelity Translations**: 7,500 human-translated test and dev premise-hypothesis pairs derived from MultiNLI
  across 15 languages: English, French, Spanish, German, Greek, Bulgarian, Russian, Turkish, Arabic, Vietnamese, Thai,
  Chinese, Hindi, Swahili, and Urdu.
- **Foundation Benchmark**: Serves as the primary validation metric for cross-lingual pretraining objectives (such as
  Translation Language Modeling in XLM and contrastive multilingual alignment).

## Choosing a Multilingual Dataset

- **Machine Translation Quality (N-to-N Evaluation)**:
  - FLORES-200 (3,001 N-way parallel professionally translated sentences)
- **Broad Cross-Lingual NLU & Zero-Shot Transfer**:
  - XTREME / XTREME-R (40+ languages, 9 diverse NLP tasks)
- **Reading Comprehension & Question Answering**:
  - TyDi QA (11 typologically diverse languages written by native speakers)
- **Large-Scale Parallel Pretraining Corpora**:
  - OPUS (Open-source parallel web, subtitle, and parliamentary text)
- **Massive Multilingual Pretraining Web Text**:
  - mC4 / CC100 (Unsupervised web crawls across 100+ languages)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Translationese Artifacts**: In parallel benchmarks translated from English source texts (like FLORES or XNLI), the
   target sentences often reflect English syntax, idioms, and sentence length distributions rather than authentic native
   linguistic expressions.
2. **Tokenizer Fertility Disparity**: Subword tokenizers (Byte-Pair Encoding, WordPiece) trained primarily on Latin
   corpora segment low-resource or non-Latin words into dozens of individual character or byte tokens, dramatically
   increasing inference latency and degrading context window capacity (see <NoteLink to="multilingual-text">multilingual
   text</NoteLink>).
3. **Script and Formatting Inconsistencies**: Low-resource web scrapes frequently mix incompatible Unicode character
   encodings, font glyphs, and non-standard transliterations, confounding language identification filters.
