<Definition>
**Language-model pretraining corpora** are massive, multi-billion to multi-trillion token text collections compiled to
train autoregressive next-token prediction and masked language models. Spanning open web crawl archives, digitized book
repositories, encyclopedias, open-source code repositories, and academic preprints, they supply the foundational
broad-domain linguistic exposure from which foundation models acquire linguistic fluency, world knowledge, and
in-context reasoning <Cite id="raffel2020, gao2020pile, penedo2023" />.
</Definition>

Modern large language models require trillions of tokens to satisfy Chinchilla-optimal compute scaling laws. Because raw
web scrapes contain vast quantities of spam, boilerplate navigation menus, machine translation garbage, and toxic hate
speech, pretraining corpus curation centers on rigorous filtering, heuristic quality scoring, deduplication, and safety
sanitization <Cite id="dodge2021, penedo2023" />.

---

## What These Corpora Measure and Provide

Pretraining corpora determine the fundamental capability frontiers of language models:
1. **Per-Token Pretraining Efficiency**: Measuring downstream zero-shot accuracy per compute FLOP as a function of data
   filtering stringency and deduplication <Cite id="penedo2023" />.
2. **Domain Mixture Proportions**: Balancing high-quality web text (Common Crawl) with specialized high-density domains:
   mathematical reasoning (arXiv, Proof-Pile), coding logic (GitHub), structured knowledge (Wikipedia), and long-form
   narrative coherence (Books).
3. **Deduplication Regimes**: Removing exact matches, URL duplicates, and fuzzy near-duplicates via MinHash LSH and
   suffix arrays, preventing memorization and test set contamination.
4. **Primary Quality Indicators**: Perplexity on held-out reference domains, downstream benchmark win rates, and
   repetition/memorization rates.

## Detailed Corpus Profiles

### C4: Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus (2020)

Engineered by Raffel et al. <Cite id="raffel2020" /> for T5:
- **Heuristic Quality Filtering**: Stripped lines ending without punctuation, discarded pages containing boilerplate
  words (*"lorem ipsum"*, *"terms of service"*), removed code snippets (lines containing curly brackets), and filtered
  lines containing terms from a "dirty words" blocklist.
- **Audit Findings**: Dodge et al. <Cite id="dodge2021" /> revealed that C4's blocklist disproportionately eliminated
  content from marginalized and LGBTQ+ communities, while search engine optimizations and Wikipedia mirrors were
  over-represented.

### The Pile: Diverse Curated 825GB Corpus (2020)

Constructed by EleutherAI (Gao et al. <Cite id="gao2020pile" />):
- **22 High-Quality Domains**: Academic publications (PubMed Central, arXiv), books (Books3, BookCorpus2), technical
  discourse (StackExchange, GitHub), legal documents (FreeLaw), and conversational text (OpenSubtitles, Hacker News).
- **Domain Specialization**: Demonstrated that language models trained on diverse academic and technical subsets
  acquired superior mathematical and scientific reasoning compared to models trained on homogeneous web text.

### RefinedWeb: Macro Data-Centric Web Filtering (2023)

Introduced by Penedo et al. <Cite id="penedo2023" /> for the Falcon models:
- **Web-Only Hypothesis**: Challenged the consensus that curated multi-domain data was strictly necessary, proving that
  rigorously filtered web data alone could match or exceed models trained on specialized corpora.
- **Aggressive Deduplication Pipeline**: Applied URL deduplication, document-level MinHash deduplication, and line-level
  suffix array deduplication, removing over $90\%$ of raw crawled text to leave an exceptionally clean 5-trillion-token
  stream.

### FineWeb and FineWeb-Edu (2024)

Released by Hugging Face:
- **Data Scale**: 15 trillion tokens processed from 96 historical Common Crawl snapshots.
- **FineWeb-Edu**: Applied a lightweight annotation model trained on synthetic educational scores from Llama-3-70B,
  isolating 1.3 trillion tokens of exceptionally rich pedagogical explanations, tutorials, and academic prose that
  dramatically accelerated model learning speed.

## Choosing a Pretraining Corpus

- **State-of-the-Art Open Pretraining (> 1T tokens)**:
  - *General web text with high educational density*: FineWeb / FineWeb-Edu
  - *Highly deduplicated web-scale text*: RefinedWeb
- **Scientific, Mathematical, & Multi-Domain Curation**:
  - The Pile / Dolma (Explicit academic, code, & legal mixtures)
- **Reproducible Transparent Research & Open Pipelines**:
  - Dolma (Fully documented, inspectable open-source pipeline)
- **Classical Baseline Reproduction**:
  - C4 (Standard reference for T5 and early encoder-decoder models)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Benchmark Contamination and Test Leakage**: Crawling web dumps invariably ingests evaluation questions, benchmark
   answers, and solution keys from GitHub and Wikipedia, causing models to achieve high evaluation scores via verbatim
   memorization <Cite id="dodge2021" />.
2. **Copyright, Piracy, and Legal Challenges**: Massive corpora frequently include unlicensed copyrighted books (e.g.
   Books3 extracted from Bibliotik), leading to copyright lawsuits and dataset takedowns.
3. **Machine-Generated Synthetic Sludge**: Modern web crawls increasingly ingest lower-quality text generated by earlier
   language models, threatening model collapse if recursive training loops lack rigorous filtering.
