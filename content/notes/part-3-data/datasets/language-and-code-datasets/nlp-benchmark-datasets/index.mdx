<Definition>
**Natural-language processing benchmarks** are standardized collections of linguistic evaluation suites measuring
algorithmic competence across canonical NLP tasks—including sequence classification, token-level named entity
recognition (NER), syntactic parsing, extractive reading comprehension, cross-sentence natural language inference (NLI),
and bilingual machine translation <Cite id="wang2018glue, rajpurkar2016" />.
</Definition>

Before multi-task benchmark suites like GLUE, models were evaluated on isolated datasets (e.g. IMDB for sentiment, CoNLL
for tagging), leading to architectures overfitted to narrow task conventions. Benchmark suites established standardized
splits, hidden evaluation servers, and unified aggregate score metrics that drove the transformer revolution from BERT
and RoBERTa to T5 <Cite id="wang2018glue, wang2019superglue" />.

---

## What These Datasets Measure

Classical NLP benchmarks formalize several distinct linguistic reasoning capabilities:
1. **Sequence Classification & Sentiment Analysis**: Predicting coarse or fine-grained polarities and topic categories
   from word sequences (IMDB, SST-2, AG News).
2. **Token Tagging & Named Entity Recognition (NER)**: Assigning BIO labels (e.g. `B-PER`, `I-ORG`, `O`) to each token
   under sequence-level evaluation metrics (Span F1) (CoNLL-2003).
3. **Extractive Question Answering**: Given a reference context paragraph $C$ and question $Q$, extracting exact answer
   token spans $[i_{\text{start}}, i_{\text{end}}]$, evaluated via exact match (EM) and token-level F1 (SQuAD)
   <Cite id="rajpurkar2016" />.
4. **Natural Language Inference (NLI)**: Determining whether a premise sentence entails, contradicts, or is neutral
   toward a hypothesis sentence (SNLI, MultiNLI).
5. **Machine Translation**: Generating target-language sentences from source-language inputs, evaluated by corpus-level
   BLEU, METEOR, and neural chrF/COMET metrics (WMT).

## Detailed Benchmark Profiles

### GLUE: General Language Understanding Evaluation (2018)

Introduced by Wang et al. <Cite id="wang2018glue" /> to benchmark general-purpose linguistic representations:
- **Nine Sub-Tasks**:
  - *Single-Sentence Tasks*: CoLA (linguistic acceptability / grammar) and SST-2 (Stanford Sentiment Treebank).
  - *Similarity and Paraphrase*: MRPC (Microsoft Research Paraphrase), QQP (Quora Question Pairs), and STS-B (Semantic
    Textual Similarity).
  - *Inference Tasks*: MNLI (Multi-Genre NLI), QNLI (Question NLI), RTE (Recognizing Textual Entailment), and WNLI
    (Winograd NLI).
- **Hidden Test Server**: Ground-truth test labels were held private on a leaderboard server to prevent test set tuning
  and p-hacking.
- **Saturation**: Pretrained transformers (RoBERTa, ALBERT, T5) surpassed human baseline performance ($87.1$) within 18
  months of GLUE's release.

### SuperGLUE: A Stickier Benchmark (2019)

Created by Wang et al. <Cite id="wang2019superglue" /> when GLUE saturated:
- **Challenging Tasks**: Retained RTE and WNLI from GLUE while introducing eight harder reasoning benchmarks: BoolQ
  (boolean QA), CB (CommitmentBank), COPA (Choice of Plausible Alternatives), MultiRC (Multi-Sentence Reading
  Comprehension), ReCoRD (Reading Comprehension with Commonsense Reasoning), and WSC (Winograd Schema Challenge).
- **Complex Formats**: Required multi-sentence reasoning, coreference resolution, and causal commonsense inference
  beyond surface syntactic matching.

### SQuAD: Stanford Question Answering Dataset (2016–2018)

Developed by Rajpurkar et al. <Cite id="rajpurkar2016" /> at Stanford:
- **SQuAD 1.1**: Over 100,000 question-answer pairs curated by crowd workers on 536 Wikipedia articles. The target
  answer is guaranteed to be an exact substring span in the provided context passage.
- **SQuAD 2.0**: Added 50,000 unanswerable questions written adversarially by crowd workers to look plausible. Models
  must not only extract valid answers but explicitly predict whether the passage contains sufficient evidence to answer
  the question at all.

## Choosing an NLP Benchmark

- **General Sentence Representation & Encoder Quality**:
  - GLUE / SuperGLUE (Standardized multi-task aggregate suite)
- **Extractive Reading Comprehension**:
  - SQuAD 2.0 (Span extraction + unanswerable question detection)
- **Sequence Tagging & Information Extraction**:
  - CoNLL-2003 (Token-level NER span F1)
- **Cross-Sentence Semantic Inference**:
  - MultiNLI (Genre-diverse entailment, contradiction, neutrality)
- **Bilingual Translation Quality**:
  - WMT Shared Tasks (Standard parallel sentence corpora, BLEU/COMET)

## Known Problems and Benchmark Pitfalls

1. **Statistical Shortcut Learning**: In NLI benchmarks (SNLI), models frequently learned spurious surface heuristics
   (e.g. sentences containing *"not"*, *"never"*, or *"nobody"* were almost always contradictions), performing well
   without modeling logical semantics.
2. **Benchmark Saturation**: Large pretrained language models rapidy saturated accuracy metrics on GLUE, masking
   lingering brittle failure modes on out-of-domain edge cases and adversarial perturbations.
3. **Overlapping Substrings in Extractive QA**: SQuAD's exact-match span metric penalizes generated synonyms or
   paraphrased answers that are semantically valid but differ in surface wording from annotator spans
   <Cite id="rajpurkar2016" />.
