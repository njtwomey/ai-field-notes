<Definition>
**Driving and robotics data** comprises the multimodal perception streams, state telemetry, and actuation trajectories
collected by physical embodied agents operating in open-world environments. This data combines high-bandwidth
exteroceptive sensors (surround cameras, LiDAR point clouds, radar, ultrasonics) with proprioceptive measurements (IMUs,
wheel encoders, joint torques). It is characterized by strict spatiotemporal calibration requirements, long-tailed
safety-critical corner cases, and heavy reliance on offline auto-labeling and teleoperated demonstrations
<Cite id="caesar2020nuscenes, sun2020waymo" />.
</Definition>

Unlike web text or static image datasets, robotics and autonomous driving data captures dynamic physical interactions
subject to real-time physical constraints. A perception error or control delay does not simply degrade a benchmark
score—it risks physical collision and catastrophic failure. Consequently, data pipelines must preserve sub-degree
spatial alignment across heterogeneous sensors and curate the rare, safety-critical edge cases that dictate real-world
reliability.

## Sensor suites and calibration

Autonomous vehicles and advanced robotic manipulators deploy complementary sensor suites to achieve robust 3D scene
understanding:

- **Surround RGB cameras:** Provide high-resolution visual texture, color, and optical semantics necessary for reading
  traffic signals, road signs, and pedestrian body language. Multi-camera arrays typically span $360^\circ$ panoramic
  coverage (e.g. 6 to 8 cameras in modern autonomous driving suites <Cite id="caesar2020nuscenes" />).
- **LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging):** Fires pulsed laser beams (e.g. 905 nm or 1550 nm) to produce dense 3D point
  clouds $(x, y, z, \text{intensity}, \text{elongation})$. LiDAR delivers millimeter-level metric depth independent of
  ambient illumination or shadow glare.
- **Radar and ultrasonics:** Radio-frequency sensors measuring direct Doppler velocity and distance. Radars penetrate
  adverse weather (dense fog, snow, torrential rain) that blinds optical cameras and scatters LiDAR photons.
- **Proprioceptive telemetry:** High-frequency (100 Hz to 1 kHz) Inertial Measurement Units (IMU), wheel odometry, and
  CAN bus feeds recording ego-velocity, steering angle, acceleration, and suspension pitch.

### Spatiotemporal calibration

Cross-modal sensor fusion models—such as Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) perception transformers—require exact spatial and
temporal alignment:

1. **Camera intrinsics ($K$):** Models lens focal lengths $(f_x, f_y)$, principal optical centers $(c_x, c_y)$, and
   radial/tangential distortion parameters, rectifying wide-angle optical warp.
2. **Sensor extrinsics ($[R \mid t] \in \text{SE}(3)$):** The 6-DoF rotation matrix $R$ and translation vector $t$
   mapping coordinates from each sensor's local coordinate frame into the vehicle's body center. Even a minor
   $0.5^\circ$ angular calibration drift between a roof-mounted LiDAR and a windshield camera produces a 20+ pixel
   projection misalignment at 50 meters, corrupting multimodal bounding box fusion.
3. **Temporal synchronization:** Precision Time Protocol (PTP) and hardware triggers ensure all cameras, lasers, and IMU
   samples fire within microsecond offsets. Unsynchronized sensors cause moving vehicles to appear in contradictory 3D
   positions across modalities.

## Rare scenarios

In real-world deployment, physical environments are governed by extreme long-tail distributions:

- **The nominal majority:** Over $99.9\%$ of recorded driving and manipulation hours consist of uninformative, nominal
  events—driving down straight highways in clear daylight or repetitive pick-and-place cycles.
- **The safety-critical long tail:** Autonomous safety is determined by the $0.01\%$ long tail of **corner cases**:
  pedestrians stepping into traffic from behind parked delivery vans, emergency vehicles violating red lights,
  construction workers communicating via hand gestures, mattress debris on highways, or unusual reflections at dusk
  <Cite id="sun2020waymo" />.
- **Active mining and trigger logging:** Commercial fleets implement edge-triggered recording: rather than streaming
  terabytes of routine footage back to cloud servers, onboard compute continuously analyzes prediction entropy, tracking
  residuals, emergency braking interventions, and driver takeover disengagements, transmitting only anomalous event
  windows to engineering data lakes.
- **Closed-loop synthetic generation:** High-fidelity simulation platforms (such as CARLA or NVIDIA Isaac Sim)
  reconstruct mined real-world incidents, synthetically varying lighting, weather, pedestrian trajectories, and braking
  latencies to expand the density of rare scenarios in training sets.

## Automatic labelling

Manual human annotation of 3D bounding boxes ($x, y, z, l, w, h, \theta$) and semantic point-cloud masks across billions
of video frames is economically and temporally impossible. Modern autonomous systems rely on large-scale **offline
auto-labeling pipelines**:

- **Bidirectional temporal processing:** Onboard models must predict causally in real time ($t \le t_{\text{current}}$).
  Offline cloud auto-labeling engines have access to both past and future video frames (e.g.
  $[t - 5\text{s}, t + 5\text{s}]$), allowing multi-pass forward-backward Kalman smoothing and global optimization to
  track occluded objects through time.
- **3D scene decomposition:** High-capacity neural radiance fields (NeRF) or 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruct the
  static background geometry, cleanly isolating dynamic moving obstacles from static street architecture.
- **Heavy ensemble supervision:** Distilling multi-gigabyte foundation models, dense vision-language models, and vision
  transformers running on high-memory cloud clusters into compact, real-time onboard models.

## Teleoperated demonstrations

In embodied robotic manipulation (such as bimanual assembly or household chores), collecting training data relies on
**human teleoperation**:

- **Demonstration collection:** Human operators control robotic arms and grippers using VR controllers, 3D space-mice,
  or bilateral master-slave leader arms (such as ALOHA).
- **Trajectory logging:** Pipelines log continuous state-action trajectories
  $\tau = (s_0, a_0, s_1, a_1, \dots, s_T, a_T)$ at 20 Hz to 50 Hz, capturing multi-view camera feeds, joint angles,
  end-effector poses, and continuous gripper opening widths.
- **Imitation learning & error compounding:** Behavior cloning policies trained via supervised regression on human
  demonstrations suffer from compounding execution errors: slight physical slips push the robot into states never seen
  during clean expert demonstrations. Robust data pipelines deliberately record corrective recovery demonstrations—human
  operators recovering the robot from perturbed, off-target configurations.

## Pitfalls

- **Neglecting online calibration drift:** Failing to monitor mechanical sensor vibration, thermal expansion, or vehicle
  tire wear, allowing extrinsic calibration matrices to drift out of alignment during deployment.
- **Training on uncurated nominal miles:** Burning millions of GPU training hours on repetitive highway data while
  starving perception models of long-tail edge-case diversity.
- **Temporal frame desynchronization:** Fusing camera frames and LiDAR sweeps without compensating for ego-motion
  rolling-shutter delays, creating "ghost" spatial artifacts.
- **Teleoperator latency contamination:** Human teleoperators compensating for visual streaming latency by making
  hesitant or jerky movements, embedding noisy control artifacts into training trajectories.
