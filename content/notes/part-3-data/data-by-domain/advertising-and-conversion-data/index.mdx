<Definition>
**Advertising and conversion data** comprises the interaction logs, impression feeds, user contexts, and conversion
signals generated in computational advertising and real-time bidding (RTB) auctions. This data is characterized by
extreme class imbalance ($P(\text{conversion}) \ll 1\%$), heavy-tailed **delayed feedback** where conversion events
arrive days or weeks after ad clicks, and **attribution window** rules that assign commercial credit across multi-touch
customer journeys <Cite id="mcmahan2013adclick, chapelle2014" />.
</Definition>

In digital advertising auctions, machine learning models estimate Click-Through Rate (CTR, $P(\text{click} \mid x)$) and
Conversion Rate (CVR, $P(\text{conversion} \mid \text{click}, x)$) to compute expected revenue bids:

$$
\text{eCPM} = \text{pCTR} \times \text{pCVR} \times \text{TargetValue} \times 1{,}000
$$

Because bids scale linearly with predicted probabilities, advertising models require precise numerical probability
calibration rather than simple binary classification rank-order accuracy. Managing delayed feedback and negative
sampling ratios is essential to avoid over-bidding or under-valuing campaigns.

## Delayed conversions

Unlike click events (which register within milliseconds of an ad impression), conversion actions—such as booking a
hotel, purchasing an appliance, or opening a bank account—exhibit substantial temporal delays:

$$
\text{Click Event } (t = 0) \longrightarrow \text{Browsing \& Research} \longrightarrow \text{Cart Add} \longrightarrow \text{Purchase Conversion } (t = +8\text{ days})
$$

| Snapshot Timing | Ingestion Treatment | Recorded Label | Ground Truth Status | Structural Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Premature ($t = 2\text{d}$)** | Naive Binary Pipeline | $y = 0$ (Negative) | Right-censored positive | **False negative bias**: under-values fresh campaigns |
| **Premature ($t = 2\text{d}$)** | Delayed Feedback Model | Survival hazard ($D > 2\text{d}$) | Censored positive | **Unbiased calibration**: supports real-time bidding |

### The censorship dilemma

If a machine learning pipeline constructs training examples 48 hours after an ad click, an unconverted record falls into
one of two unobservable states <Cite id="chapelle2014" />:
1. **True Negative:** The user examined the product and decided never to convert.
2. **Right-Censored Positive:** The user will convert on day 7, but the label has not yet landed.

Labeling all un-converted clicks as negatives introduces systematic **false negative bias**. Because recently launched
ad campaigns have had less elapsed time to accumulate delayed conversions, naive models underestimate the CVR of fresh
ads, starving new campaigns of auction exposure.

### Delayed Feedback Modeling (DFM)

To resolve censorship without waiting months for conversion windows to close, Olivier Chapelle
<Cite id="chapelle2014" /> introduced Delayed Feedback Models grounded in survival analysis. The model decomposes the
joint probability of conversion indicator $C \in \{0, 1\}$ and elapsed delay $D \in \mathbb{R}^+$:

$$
P(C = 1, D = d \mid x) = P(C = 1 \mid x) \cdot P(D = d \mid C = 1, x)
$$

The probability of conversion is parameterized via logistic regression or deep neural nets, while delay duration $D$ is
parameterized via an exponential hazard rate $\lambda(x)$. For clicks that have not yet converted after elapsed
observation time $E$, the likelihood integrates over all unobserved future times:

$$
P(C = 0 \text{ or } D > E \mid x) = 1 - P(C = 1 \mid x) + P(C = 1 \mid x) \int_E^\infty P(D = t \mid C = 1, x) \, dt
$$

Maximizing this censored log-likelihood enables continuous online learning on fresh traffic without waiting for full
attribution closure.

## Attribution windows

An **attribution window** defines the maximum elapsed duration between an ad interaction (click or impression) and a
downstream conversion for that ad to claim credit:

- **Window truncation:** Typical industry standards specify 1-day, 7-day, or 30-day post-click windows. Conversions
  occurring at day 31 are discarded and assigned zero attribution credit. While truncation bounds state retention for ad
  networks, setting windows too short truncates legitimate long-cycle purchases.
- **Attribution credit assignment:**
  - **Last-Touch Attribution (LTA):** Assigns 100% of commercial credit to the final ad clicked prior to conversion. LTA
    artificially over-values bottom-of-funnel branded search and retargeting ads while starving top-of-funnel brand
    discovery campaigns of revenue credit.
  - **First-Touch Attribution (FTA):** Assigns 100% credit to the initial touchpoint.
  - **Multi-Touch Attribution (MTA):** Distributes fractional credit across all intermediate touchpoints via time-decay
    weighting, position-based rules (40% first, 40% last, 20% middle), or cooperative game-theoretic Shapley value
    formulations.

## Downsampled negatives

Internet ad exchanges log tens of billions of impressions daily. Because typical click-through rates range from $0.1\%$
to $2\%$, negative (unclicked or unconverted) records outnumber positives by orders of magnitude ($10^3:1$ to $10^5:1$).
Storing, transferring, and training deep architectures on all negative impressions is cost-prohibitive.

### Negative subsampling

Production pipelines <Cite id="he2014facebook, mcmahan2013adclick" /> retain 100% of positive conversion events while
downsampling negative impressions with sampling probability $w \in (0, 1]$ (e.g., $w = 0.05$):

$$
\text{Dataset Size Reduction} \approx 1 - w \quad (95\%\text{ storage savings})
$$

### Mathematical calibration correction

Training a binary classifier on negatively downsampled data shifts the empirical label distribution, forcing the model
to output an artificially inflated predicted probability $p'$.

To recover the true, unbiased posterior probability $p = P(Y = 1 \mid x)$ required for auction pricing, practitioners
apply the exact calibration adjustment formula <Cite id="he2014facebook" />:

$$
p = \frac{p'}{p' + \frac{1 - p'}{w}}
$$

```
Raw Model Output:    p' = 0.10  (Trained with w = 0.01 negative sampling)
Calibration Formula: p  = 0.10 / (0.10 + (1 - 0.10) / 0.01)
                     p  = 0.10 / (0.10 + 90.0)
Corrected Posterior: p  ≈ 0.00111  (True real-world probability)
```

Failing to apply this adjustment produces a 90x over-estimation of conversion probability, causing automated bidding
systems to submit ruinously high bids in second-price and first-price ad auctions.

## Pitfalls

- **Bidding with uncalibrated probabilities:** Omitting the downsampling correction formula, resulting in inflated eCPM
  bids that exhaust advertising budgets within minutes.
- **Treating delayed conversions as hard negatives:** Training batch models on recent traffic without survival analysis
  adjustments, causing models to suppress newly released high-value ad creatives.
- **Attribution gaming by bottom-of-funnel retargeters:** Employing naive last-touch attribution, allowing retargeting
  networks to bid exclusively on high-intent users about to convert organically, claiming undeserved credit without
  generating incremental lift.
- **Unchecked click fraud and bot traffic:** Training conversion models on bot-generated impressions and click-farm
  traffic, corrupting feature representations with non-human behavioral patterns.
