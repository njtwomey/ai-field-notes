<Definition>
**Data by domain** describes how data from one field of application are generated, recorded and labelled, and the
biases that follow. A domain is defined by the process that produces the data, such as clinical care, trading, a search
engine or a factory, rather than by the data's form. Two domains with the same modality can need opposite treatment.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The same tabular or sequential methods meet very different data in practice. A clinical record is shaped by billing
codes and by when patients choose to visit. A price series is revised after publication and loses the firms that went
bankrupt. A click log records only what the system chose to show, in the position it chose. Each domain has a few
well-known traps that make models look better offline than they are in use. Experts in the field know them, and
newcomers repeat them. Each note names the traps of one domain and points to the general method that handles them.

## Main ideas

**Records reflect the process that made them.** <NoteLink to="clinical-and-health-data" /> covers coded diagnoses and
procedures, irregular visits, cohort definitions, labels taken from records, and biases such as immortal time
<Cite id="johnson2016mimic" />. <NoteLink to="survey-and-official-statistics" /> covers design weights, non-response,
mode effects and <NoteLink to="k-anonymity-and-disclosure-control">disclosure control</NoteLink>.
<NoteLink to="scientific-data" /> covers units, measurement uncertainty, simulation outputs and physical constraints.

**Time leaks in markets.** <NoteLink to="financial-data" /> covers returns, corporate-action adjustments, order books
and reporting dates. Look-ahead and survivorship bias make backtests optimistic <Cite id="lopezdeprado2018" />.
<NoteLink to="retail-and-demand-data" /> covers sales that understate demand during stock-outs, promotions, product
hierarchies and new items without history.

**Labels arrive late or are contested.** <NoteLink to="fraud-and-security-data" /> covers adaptive adversaries, labels
that arrive weeks later or are disputed, and extreme rarity. <NoteLink to="advertising-and-conversion-data" /> covers
conversions that arrive days after clicks, attribution windows and downsampled negatives.
<NoteLink to="industrial-sensor-and-maintenance-data" /> covers run-to-failure records, labels from maintenance logs and
sensors replaced during the record.

**Logged behaviour depends on what was shown.** <NoteLink to="interaction-logs" /> covers impressions, implicit and
explicit feedback, exposure and <Gloss name="position-bias">position bias</Gloss>, and logging propensities
<Cite id="joachims2017ultr" />. <NoteLink to="search-and-relevance-data" /> covers pooled relevance judgements, graded
relevance and <NoteLink to="click-models">click models</NoteLink>. <NoteLink to="user-generated-content" /> covers bots,
spam, moderation labels and language that drifts by community.

**Physical systems need rare events.** <NoteLink to="driving-and-robotics-data" /> covers calibrated sensor suites, the
rare scenarios that matter most, automatic labelling and teleoperated demonstrations.

## How the notes fit together

The twelve notes are independent and can be read in any order. The reading order starts with the domains whose traps
recur elsewhere: clinical data (cohorts and immortal time), financial data (look-ahead and survivorship) and interaction
logs (exposure bias). Fraud, advertising and retail data reuse the delayed-label and censoring ideas of the earlier
notes. Search data reuse the exposure ideas of interaction logs.
