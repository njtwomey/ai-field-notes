<Definition>
**Learned imputation** fits a supervised, low-rank, or generative model $\mathcal{M}_\theta$ to approximate the joint
probability distribution $p(\Ymat)$ or the set of conditional expectations $\mathbb{E}[Y_j \mid \Ymat_{-j}]$ across
observed entries $\Ymat_{\text{obs}}$. 

Unlike heuristic single imputers (such as unconditional mean or median fills), learned imputers exploit multivariate
dependencies and non-linear manifolds to predict or sample unobserved entries:

$$
\hat{Y}_{i, \text{mis}} \sim p_{\mathcal{M}_\theta}\left(Y_{i, \text{mis}} \mid \yvec_{i, \text{obs}}\right).
$$
</Definition>

## Iterative forest imputation (MissForest)

MissForest (Stekhoven & Bühlmann 2012) is a non-parametric, iterative imputation algorithm built on ensembles of
decision trees <Cite id="stekhoven2012" />. It handles mixed data types (continuous, ordinal, and nominal variables),
captures complex non-linear feature interactions, and requires no hyperparameter tuning or distribution assumptions.

### Algorithmic procedure
1. **Initialisation:** Sort the features $\Ymat = [\yvec_1, \dots, \yvec_D]$ in ascending order of missingness
   proportion. Missing values are initially filled using unconditional mean (continuous) or mode (categorical)
   imputation to produce $\tilde{\Ymat}^{(0)}$.
2. **Iterative chained estimation:** For iterations $t = 1, 2, \dots, T_{\max}$:
   - For each feature $j \in \{1, \dots, D\}$:
     - Partition feature $\yvec_j$ into observed indices $\mathcal{S}_{\text{obs}}^{(j)}$ and missing indices
       $\mathcal{S}_{\text{mis}}^{(j)}$.
     - Train a Random Forest $\mathcal{M}_j$ using the remaining features $\tilde{\Ymat}_{-j}^{(t)}$ as predictors,
       fitted exclusively on observed rows $\mathcal{S}_{\text{obs}}^{(j)}$.
     - Predict the missing entries:
       
       $$
       \tilde{\Ymat}_{\text{mis}, j}^{(t)} = \mathcal{M}_j\left(\tilde{\Ymat}_{\text{mis}, -j}^{(t)}\right).
       $$
3. **Stopping criterion:** Convergence is assessed by measuring the change in imputed values between consecutive
   iterations. For continuous variables, normalized root mean square error (NRMSE) is computed:
   
   $$
   \gamma_{\text{cont}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}_{\text{mis}}} \left(\tilde{Y}_{ij}^{(t)} - \tilde{Y}_{ij}^{(t-1)}\right)^2}{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}_{\text{mis}}} \left(\tilde{Y}_{ij}^{(t)}\right)^2}}.
   $$

   Iteration halts when $\gamma_{\text{cont}}$ (and the proportion of falsely classified categorical entries
   $\gamma_{\text{cat}}$) increases for the first time, signaling that subsequent passes risk overfitting to residual
   noise. Typically, convergence is achieved in 4 to 8 iterations.

While highly accurate on complex tabular manifolds, MissForest incurs substantial computational cost: each iteration
trains $D$ independent random forest ensembles, scaling as $\mathcal{O}(T \cdot D \cdot B \cdot N \log N)$ where $B$ is
the number of trees per forest.

## Low-rank matrix completion

When data features are linear combinations of a small number of latent factors (such as collaborative filtering ratings,
multi-sensor telemetry, or genomics arrays), incomplete data matrices can be reconstructed using low-rank matrix
completion <Cite id="candes2009matrix" />.

Let $\Omega = \{(i, j) : R_{ij} = 1\}$ denote the set of observed matrix coordinates, and let
$\mathcal{P}_\Omega(\Xmat)$ denote the projection operator that preserves entries in $\Omega$ and zeroes out entries
outside $\Omega$. Matrix completion seeks the lowest-rank matrix that agrees with the observed data:

$$
\min_{\Xmat \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}} \operatorname{rank}(\Xmat) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathcal{P}_\Omega(\Xmat) = \mathcal{P}_\Omega(\Ymat).
$$

Because matrix rank minimization is NP-hard, Candès & Recht (2009) established that under mild incoherence conditions,
rank minimization can be solved exactly via its convex relaxation: the **nuclear norm**
$\|\Xmat\|_* = \sum_k \sigma_k(\Xmat)$ (the sum of singular values) <Cite id="candes2009matrix" />:

$$
\min_{\Xmat \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}} \|\Xmat\|_* + \frac{1}{2\lambda} \left\|\mathcal{P}_\Omega(\Xmat - \Ymat)\right\|_F^2.
$$

Standard solvers utilize Singular Value Thresholding (SVT) or Alternating Least Squares (ALS) factorizing
$\Xmat = \Umat \Vmat^\top$ with rank $r \ll \min(N, D)$. Low-rank completion is computationally efficient and scales to
massive sparse matrices, but it fails to capture non-linear manifold geometry or complex tree-structured interactions.

## Deep generative imputers: autoencoders, GAIN, and diffusion

Modern deep learning architectures formulate imputation as generative modeling conditioned on incomplete observations.

### Denoising autoencoders (DAEs)
Denoising autoencoders mask a random subset of observed inputs during training and optimize reconstruction loss over the
artificially corrupted coordinates. At inference time, the observed entries $\yvec_{\text{obs}}$ are fed into the
network, and the reconstructed output $\hat{\yvec} = g(f(\yvec))$ provides imputed values for missing positions.

### Generative Adversarial Imputation Networks (GAIN)
GAIN (Yoon et al. 2018) adapts the GAN framework directly to arbitrary missingness patterns <Cite id="yoon2018gain" />:
- **Generator ($G$):** Takes the observed data $\tilde{\Ymat}$, the binary mask $\Mmat = \mathbf{1} - \Rmat$, and random
  noise $\Zmat$, outputting completed matrix
  $\hat{\Ymat} = \Mmat \odot \tilde{\Ymat} + (\mathbf{1} - \Mmat) \odot G(\tilde{\Ymat}, \Mmat, \Zmat)$.
- **Discriminator ($D$):** Predicts which components of $\hat{\Ymat}$ were genuine observations versus synthetic
  imputations.
- **Hint mechanism:** To prevent trivial local minima, a hint vector
  $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{B} \odot \Mmat + 0.5(\mathbf{1} - \mathbf{B})$ reveals partial ground truth to the
  discriminator, ensuring the generator correctly models the true conditional distribution
  $p(\Ymat_{\text{mis}} \mid \Ymat_{\text{obs}})$.

### Score-based diffusion imputers
Diffusion models (such as TabDDPM or CSDI) train a forward stochastic differential equation that progressively diffuses
data toward Gaussian noise, paired with a learned reverse drift network. During sampling, the observed coordinates
$\mathcal{P}_\Omega(\Ymat)$ are clamped to their known noisy states at each timestep, forcing reverse generation to
sample directly from the conditional posterior $p(\Ymat_{\text{mis}} \mid \Ymat_{\text{obs}})$.

## Evaluating against simple baseline fills

A crucial operational question is whether complex learned imputers justify their computational overhead compared to
simple baselines (such as median imputation paired with missing indicator flags).

| Scenario | Recommended Imputer | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Downstream Gradient Boosted Trees (GBDT)** | Simple median / native NaN routing | Tree algorithms naturally split on missingness indicators; deep imputation adds latency without meaningful AUC/RMSE gain. |
| **Downstream Linear / Neural Models** | Stochastic regression or MissForest | Neural networks and linear models lack native branching and benefit substantially from faithful non-linear manifold recovery. |
| **Unsupervised Analysis (PCA, Clustering)** | MissForest or Matrix Completion | Preserving multivariate covariance and metric distances is essential; simple mean fills distort cluster boundaries. |
| **High-Throughput Online Inference (&lt;10ms)** | Mean / median lookup tables | Iterative forests and diffusion models cannot satisfy strict online latency SLAs. |

In extensive empirical benchmarks on tabular datasets, sophisticated neural imputers frequently underperform MissForest
or tuned single imputation pipelines due to the inductive bias of decision trees on heterogeneous tabular features.

## Operational latency and deployment architectures

Deploying learned imputers in production environments requires decoupling training from scoring:

1. **Offline batch pipelines:** Imputers such as MissForest or matrix completion are fully viable in offline batch ETL
   pipelines where hourly or daily latency is acceptable.
2. **Real-time online serving:** In low-latency scoring services, learned imputers are typically pre-compiled:
   - Quantized regression trees or compact multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) predict missing features using fixed input
     sub-graphs.
   - Fallback cascading: If a high-latency learned imputer times out, the serving pipeline defaults to pre-computed
     population medians.

## Pitfalls

- **Data leakage in cross-validation:** Fitting an iterative imputer like MissForest across the full dataset prior to
  cross-validation splits causes target and feature leakage across validation folds. The imputer must be wrapped inside
  a formal pipeline (e.g. `scikit-learn` `Pipeline`) that fits strictly on training folds.
- **Inference latency bottlenecks:** Attempting to invoke an iterative random forest imputer at prediction time requires
  evaluating hundreds of decision trees sequentially, introducing unacceptable tail latency.
- **Overfitting on small samples:** Complex deep generative models (such as autoencoders or GAIN) require substantial
  training sample sizes to learn stable representations. On small datasets ($N < 5{,}000$), they readily overfit to
  spurious patterns, underperforming simple $k$-NN or MissForest.
