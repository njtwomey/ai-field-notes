<Definition>
**Splitting** divides a dataset into parts with different roles: one to fit a model, one to tune it and one to assess
it. **Leakage** is any route by which information that would not be available at prediction time reaches training or
evaluation <Cite id="kaufman2012" />. A split is sound when its evaluation rows are new in the same way that deployment
data will be new, and nothing about them leaks back.
</Definition>

## What it is for

A validation or test score is useful only as a forecast of performance on future data. The forecast fails in two ways.
The split can be easier than deployment, for example by putting rows of the same patient, the same product or the same
week on both sides. Or information can leak: a feature computed from the target, a scaler fitted on all rows, a
duplicate copied across the split, a value recorded after the prediction time. Both inflate the score. A review of
machine-learning-based science found leakage in 294 papers across 17 fields <Cite id="kapoor2023" />.
The chapter gives the rules for splitting and the checks for leakage.

## Main ideas

**Match the split to deployment.** <NoteLink to="train-validation-and-test-splits" /> sets out random, stratified,
grouped, temporal and spatial splits. The rule behind the choice is in
<NoteLink to="matching-the-split-to-deployment" />: hold out whatever will be new in production, whether a period, an
entity or a site. <NoteLink to="temporal-splits" /> trains on the past and evaluates on the future with gaps, purging
and embargoes. <NoteLink to="stable-split-assignment" /> hashes persistent identifiers so that each entity stays on the
same side as the data grow.

**Leakage has a few recurring forms.** <NoteLink to="data-leakage" /> names them: features derived from the target,
train-test contamination through fitted preprocessing or duplicates, and temporal leakage.
<NoteLink to="point-in-time-correctness" /> builds each feature only from facts known at the prediction time, using
as-of joins. <NoteLink to="leakage-case-studies" /> collects real failures: patient overlap, scanner artefacts,
duplicated code, inverse relations in knowledge graphs and shuffled time series.

**Leakage can be audited.** <NoteLink to="detecting-leakage" /> checks when each feature becomes known, searches for
duplicates across the split, refits every pipeline step inside the training fold and runs controls whose outcome is
known if nothing leaks, such as shuffled labels. <NoteLink to="adversarial-validation" /> trains a classifier to tell
training rows from test rows. If it succeeds, the two differ, and its <NoteLink to="feature-importance-measures">feature
importances</NoteLink> say where.

**The test set is a measuring instrument.** <NoteLink to="test-sets" /> covers sizing a test set for a confidence
interval, covering slices, <NoteLink to="test-set-reuse">adaptive overfitting</NoteLink> from repeated use, and
<NoteLink to="out-of-distribution-test-sets">out-of-distribution test sets</NoteLink>.
