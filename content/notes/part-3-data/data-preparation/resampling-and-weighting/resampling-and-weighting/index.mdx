<Definition>
**Resampling and weighting** change the contribution of each training example to the loss. A sample weight $w_i \ge 0$
multiplies example $i$'s loss term. Resampling draws examples with unequal probabilities, which has the same effect in
expectation. Both make the training objective estimate the risk under a distribution other than the one the
data were drawn from.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The rows in a dataset rarely arrive in the proportions that matter at deployment. A survey oversamples small regions. A
training sample over-represents inputs that will be rare in use. A treatment was given more often to some patients than
to others. A few classes or target ranges dominate while the rare ones carry most of the cost. Weighting re-aligns the
average training loss with the risk of interest. The price is variance: a few heavily weighted rows dominate the
estimate, and the <Gloss name="ess">effective sample size</Gloss> falls well below the row count <Cite id="kish1965" />.

## Main ideas

**Weights retarget the empirical risk.** If the deployment density $q(\xvec)$ differs from the training density
$p(\xvec)$, weighting each example by $w(\xvec) = q(\xvec)/p(\xvec)$ makes the weighted training loss an unbiased
estimate of the deployment risk <Cite id="shimodaira2000" />. <NoteLink to="sample-weights" /> derives this and covers
the four common sources of weights: survey design weights, importance weights for
<NoteLink to="shift-and-drift-in-data">covariate shift</NoteLink>, inverse propensity scores and recency decay.

**Weights cost effective sample size.** Kish's effective sample size
$n_{\text{eff}} = (\sum_i w_i)^2 / \sum_i w_i^2$ equals $n$ for equal weights and shrinks as the weights spread out.
Clipping and normalising the weights trade a little bias for a large cut in variance.

**Rare target ranges need their own treatment.** In <NoteLink to="imbalanced-regression" />, extreme values of a
continuous target are rare and badly fitted under squared error. Density-based weights, resampling methods such as
SMOTER and SMOGN, splits stratified on a binned target and error reported by target range address it.

**Long tails of classes.** <NoteLink to="long-tailed-recognition" /> trains classifiers where a few head classes have
most of the examples. Class-balanced sampling, losses weighted by the effective number of samples
<Cite id="cui2019" />, logit adjustment and decoupled training of features and classifier raise accuracy on the tail.
