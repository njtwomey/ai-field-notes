<Definition>
**Scaling and transforms** are fixed maps applied to numeric values before a model sees them. Scaling changes the
location and spread of each feature. A non-linear transform changes the shape of a distribution. Whitening changes the
joint distribution of several features, removing their correlations. Each map has constants estimated on training
data and is applied unchanged to every later row.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Raw numeric columns differ in units, in skew and in how they vary together. Many learning methods are sensitive to all
three. <NoteLink to="gradient-descent">Gradient descent</NoteLink> converges slowly when features have very different
scales. Distances and penalties are dominated by the widest or most skewed feature. Least squares assumes a noise
variance that does not grow with the mean. Strongly correlated inputs make a covariance matrix nearly singular. A
transform chosen for the model removes the problem at its source, before training. Tree ensembles split on order alone
and need almost none of this.

## Main ideas

**Scale without changing shape.** <NoteLink to="scaling-and-normalisation" /> covers the affine scalers
(standardisation, min-max and robust scaling), which keep each distribution's shape, the rank-based quantile transform,
which removes skew and outliers at the cost of distances, and <NoteLink to="row-normalisation">row
normalisation</NoteLink>, which scales each example to unit norm.

**Change shape to fix skew and unequal variance.** A logarithm or a fitted power $x^\lambda$ shortens a long right tail.
Box-Cox needs positive values; Yeo-Johnson accepts any sign <Cite id="box1964" />. A variance-stabilising transform,
such as the square root of counts, is chosen so that the variance no longer depends on the mean. Proportions and shares
that sum to one live on a bounded space and need logit or log-ratio transforms instead.

**Transforming the target changes what a prediction means.** A model fitted to $\log y$ predicts the conditional mean
of $\log y$. Exponentiating that prediction gives an estimate of the median of $y$, not its mean.
<NoteLink to="retransformation-bias" /> derives the gap and the smearing correction that closes it.

**Whiten to remove correlation.** <NoteLink to="whitening-and-decorrelation" /> maps a feature vector to one with
identity covariance. PCA whitening rotates onto the principal axes and rescales. ZCA whitening rotates back, giving the
whitened vector closest to the original <Cite id="kessy2018" />. Small eigenvalues are regularised, because dividing by
them amplifies noise.
