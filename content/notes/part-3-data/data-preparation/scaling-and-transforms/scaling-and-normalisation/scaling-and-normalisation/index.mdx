<Definition>
**Scaling and normalisation** are maps that change the numeric range of data without
changing which example is which. Column scaling applies one affine or monotone map to each feature, with constants
estimated on the training rows. Row normalisation divides each example by its own norm. Both are fitted preprocessing
steps and travel with the model.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Raw features arrive in arbitrary units. Income varies over tens of thousands of dollars, age over tens of years and a
ratio over fractions of one. Any method that treats the coordinates of $\xvec$ alike is affected by a change of units
that carries no information. Gradient descent converges slowly on a badly conditioned loss <Cite id="lecun1998" />.
Distance-based methods such as $k$-nearest neighbours and $k$-means let the widest feature dominate. Ridge and lasso
penalties shrink a coefficient by an amount that depends on its feature's units <Cite id="hastie2009" />.
<NoteLink to="decision-tree">Decision trees</NoteLink> split on order alone and need no scaling.

## Main ideas

**Affine column scaling.** <NoteLink to="feature-scaling" /> maps each feature $x_j$ to $(x_j - a_j)/b_j$.
Standardisation uses the training mean and standard deviation, min-max scaling the minimum and range, and robust scaling
the median and interquartile range. The map keeps the shape of each distribution. A skewed or outlier-ridden column
stays skewed after it.

**Rank-based transforms.** <NoteLink to="quantile-and-rank-transforms" /> replace each value by its quantile under the
training distribution, then optionally map it to a uniform or Gaussian scale. Outliers and skew vanish. Distances
between values are lost, and values outside the training range are clipped to its ends.

**Row normalisation.** <NoteLink to="row-normalisation" /> scales each example, not each feature, to unit norm. After
$L_2$ normalisation, Euclidean distance ranks pairs exactly as cosine similarity does. It suits <Gloss name="tf-idf" />
vectors and embeddings, where direction carries the meaning and length reflects document size or encoder quirks. It fits
no parameters, so it cannot leak across a split.

**Fit on training rows only.** Every column scaler estimates constants from data. Those constants are fitted on the
training fold and applied unchanged to validation, test and production rows. Fitting them on all rows leaks test
statistics into training. <NoteLink to="preprocessing-pipelines" /> shows how a pipeline enforces this.
