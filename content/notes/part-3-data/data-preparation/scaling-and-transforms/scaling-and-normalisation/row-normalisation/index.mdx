<Definition>
**Row normalisation** (or instance normalisation) is an individual sample transformation that scales each example vector
$x_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$ independently to have unit norm under a chosen metric norm $\|\cdot\|_p$, discarding the
magnitude of the instance while preserving its directional orientation.
</Definition>

Unlike column scaling (such as `StandardScaler` or `MinMaxScaler`), which operates column-wise across the entire dataset
to normalize feature distributions across samples, row normalisation operates horizontally along axis 1 within each
individual instance:

$$
x_i' = \frac{x_i}{\|x_i\|_p}
$$

Because the transformation depends exclusively on the elements of that single observation vector, row normalisation is
**completely stateless**: it estimates no parameters from training distributions, avoids cross-validation leakage by
construction, and can be applied seamlessly to streaming inference requests without storing reference statistics.

## Unit-norm rows and norm choices

Depending on the domain semantics, different vector norms are selected:

### 1. $L_2$ norm (Euclidean unit sphere)
The Euclidean norm projects every non-zero instance onto the boundary of the unit hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$:

$$
\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^D x_j^2}, \qquad x' = \frac{x}{\|x\|_2}
$$

After transformation, $\|x'\|_2 = 1$ for all instances. This is standard in text mining, information retrieval, and
dense deep learning embeddings.

### 2. $L_1$ norm (Manhattan simplex)
The Manhattan norm scales elements so that their absolute values sum to unity:

$$
\|x\|_1 = \sum_{j=1}^D |x_j|, \qquad x' = \frac{x}{\|x\|_1}
$$

For non-negative vectors (such as word counts or topic mixtures), $L_1$ normalization projects observations onto the
standard probability simplex $\Delta^{D-1}$, converting raw counts into relative frequency distributions.

### 3. $L_\infty$ norm (Maximum absolute value)
Scales each row by its maximum absolute component:

$$
\|x\|_\infty = \max_{j} |x_j|, \qquad x' = \frac{x}{\|x\|_\infty}
$$

guaranteeing that all coordinates fall within $[-1, 1]$ with at least one coordinate reaching $\pm 1$.

## Cosine geometry and Euclidean equivalence

The central theoretical advantage of $L_2$ row normalisation is establishing exact mathematical equivalence between
Euclidean distance and cosine similarity.

For any two unit-length vectors $u, v \in \mathbb{R}^D$ where $\|u\|_2 = \|v\|_2 = 1$:

$$
\|u - v\|_2^2 = \|u\|_2^2 + \|v\|_2^2 - 2 \langle u, v \rangle = 1 + 1 - 2 \cos(\theta) = 2(1 - \cos(\theta))
$$

Taking the square root yields:

$$
\|u - v\|_2 = \sqrt{2(1 - \cos(\theta))}
$$

where $\cos(\theta) = \frac{\langle u, v \rangle}{\|u\|_2 \|v\|_2}$ is the cosine similarity.

Because $\sqrt{2(1 - s)}$ is a strictly monotonically decreasing function of similarity $s \in [-1, 1]$:
- Finding the nearest neighbor that minimizes Euclidean distance $\|u - v\|_2$ is **strictly identical** to finding the
  neighbor that maximizes cosine similarity $\cos(\theta)$.
- Standard indexing algorithms and libraries optimized for Euclidean distance (such as FAISS, ScaNN, or KD-Trees) can
  perform exact cosine similarity retrieval simply by $L_2$-normalizing vectors prior to insertion.
- The inner product simplifies to a pure dot product: $\langle u, v \rangle = \cos(\theta)$, enabling fast matrix
  multiplication for batch similarity queries.

## Normalised TF-IDF vectors

In bag-of-words and term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) representations, document vectors have dimensions
corresponding to vocabulary terms. Without row normalisation, long documents with thousands of words naturally exhibit
large component magnitudes, whereas brief one-sentence abstracts have tiny magnitudes.

In un-normalised Euclidean space, a long article and a brief summary discussing the exact same topic would appear
separated by an enormous Euclidean distance simply due to length disparity.

$L_2$ normalisation neutralizes document length bias:
1. Two documents with identical relative word frequencies map to collinear rays from the origin.
2. Normalisation collapses both documents to the exact same point on the unit sphere.
3. Euclidean clustering ($k$-Means) or classification (SVM) then groups documents by topical content rather than
   verbosity.

## Neural embedding vectors

Modern representation learning in deep learning widely enforces unit-norm geometry on learned embeddings:

- **Sentence and text embeddings:** Models like sentence-transformers and retrieval bi-encoders normalize output
  embeddings so that similarity scoring is computed via standard inner products $\langle e_q, e_d \rangle$.
- **Face recognition (ArcFace / CosFace):** Normalizing both feature embeddings and classifier weight vectors to unit
  norm maps the classification problem onto angular margins on the hypersphere, dramatically boosting intra-class
  compactness and inter-class discrepancy.
- **Multimodal contrastive learning (CLIP):** Image and text projection vectors are normalized to unit norm before
  computing the cross-modal cosine similarity matrix scaled by a learnable temperature $\tau$.

## Pitfalls and failure modes

1. **Zero vectors:** If an instance contains all zeros (e.g. an out-of-vocabulary document with zero matched terms),
   $\|x\|_p = 0$. Division by zero causes `NaN` or runtime crashes. Robust implementations (such as scikit-learn's
   `Normalizer`) leave all-zero rows unchanged.
2. **Loss of magnitude information:** In applications where the absolute volume carries critical predictive signal (e.g.
   total customer transaction volume, total sensor energy, or raw financial portfolio size), row normalisation discards
   this scale. If total magnitude is important, retain $\|x\|_p$ as an explicit standalone scalar feature before
   normalising.
3. **Confusing row normalisation with column scaling:** Applying row normalisation when column standardization was
   intended (or vice versa) can severely distort model training. Column scaling standardizes distributions across
   samples; row normalisation alters the internal geometry within an individual record.
