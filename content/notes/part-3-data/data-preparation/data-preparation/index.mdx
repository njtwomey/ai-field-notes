<Definition>
**Data preparation** is the set of transformations that turn raw records into the inputs, targets, weights and splits a
learning algorithm consumes. Each step is a function with constants estimated from data, such as a median, a scale or a
category mean. The steps are fitted on training rows only and applied unchanged to every later row, so together they
form part of the model.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Raw data are incomplete, inconsistent, on mismatched scales and full of strings. Most algorithms want a dense numeric
matrix with no gaps. Between the two sit choices that change accuracy as much as the choice of model: how a missing
value is filled, whether a skewed feature is logged, how a category with ten thousand levels is coded, which rows are
held out. The same choices are a common source of invalid results. A scaler or encoder fitted on all rows, or a split
that ignores how data arrive, makes the validation score overstate real performance <Cite id="kaufman2012" />.

## Main ideas

**Every fitted step belongs inside the training fold.** Imputation, scaling, encoding and
<NoteLink to="feature-selection">feature selection</NoteLink> estimate quantities from data. Estimated on all rows, they
carry information from validation and test rows into training. <NoteLink to="preprocessing-pipelines" /> composes the
steps per column, fits them on training folds only and saves them with the model for serving
<Cite id="pedregosa2011" />.

**Different models need different preparation.** Linear and distance-based models need scaled numeric inputs and
explicit encodings. Tree ensembles need almost none and handle missing values natively. Neural networks need scaled
inputs and learned embeddings for categories. <NoteLink to="preprocessing-by-model-family" /> sets the requirements out
in one table.

**Repair before transforming.** <NoteLink to="data-quality-and-cleaning" /> detects and fixes defects: missing values,
<NoteLink to="outliers-and-gross-errors">gross errors</NoteLink> and outliers, inconsistent formats, duplicates and
violated constraints. It comes first because every later step assumes typed, valid values.

**Transform and encode to suit the model.** <NoteLink to="scaling-and-transforms" /> changes the scale and shape of
numeric features. <NoteLink to="encoding-features-and-targets" /> turns categories, cycles, positions, sets, missingness
and targets into vectors. <NoteLink to="resampling-and-weighting" /> changes how much each row counts.

**Hold out data the way deployment will.** <NoteLink to="splitting-and-leakage" /> matches the split to what will be
new in production and checks that nothing from the held-out rows leaks into training.
