<Definition>
**Data privacy** in machine learning concerns the people a dataset describes: whether they can be identified from it,
whether a trained model can reveal their records, and what rights they hold over the data and its use. The notes here
cover the data side: identifiability, redaction, anonymisation, memorisation and deletion.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Clinical records, transaction logs, chat transcripts and web text all contain personal data. Removing names is not
enough. Combinations of ordinary attributes, such as postcode, birth date and sex, single out most people
<Cite id="sweeney2002k" />, and sparse records such as film ratings can be linked to public profiles
<Cite id="narayanan2008robust" />. Models add a second channel: a <Gloss name="language-model">language model</Gloss>
can reproduce training text verbatim when prompted <Cite id="carlini2021extracting" />. Law adds obligations: a lawful
basis for use, limits on retention and a right to erasure that reaches trained models.

## Main ideas

**Identification is often indirect.** <NoteLink to="personal-data-and-identifiability" /> separates direct identifiers
from quasi-identifiers and explains linkage attacks. Pseudonymised data, with identifiers replaced by codes, remain
personal data. Anonymised data must resist linkage with other sources.

**Redaction reduces risk without removing it.** <NoteLink to="pii-detection-and-redaction" /> finds names, addresses
and identifiers in text and records, then redacts them or replaces them with realistic surrogates. Detector recall is
never complete, so some personal data always remain.

**Anonymisation trades privacy against utility.** <NoteLink to="k-anonymity-and-disclosure-control" /> generalises and
suppresses quasi-identifiers until each record matches at least $k$ others, and adds l-diversity for sensitive values.
In high dimensions these guarantees destroy most of the data's utility.

**Models memorise.** <NoteLink to="training-data-extraction" /> formalises memorisation, describes extraction attacks
and shows that duplicated records are memorised most, so deduplication lowers extraction risk.

**Rights reach the model.** <NoteLink to="data-rights-and-deletion" /> covers lawful basis, licences, retention and
erasure requests. Deleting a record's influence from a trained model requires retraining or machine unlearning.
