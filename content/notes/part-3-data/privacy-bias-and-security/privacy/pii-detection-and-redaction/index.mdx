<Definition>
**PII detection and redaction** is an automated text-processing pipeline $\mathcal{T}: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{X}$ that
scans unstructured text $x = (w_1, \dots, w_T)$ to locate token spans $\mathcal{S} = \{(i, j, \tau)\}$ representing
personally identifiable information (PII) of entity type
$\tau \in \{\text{NAME}, \text{EMAIL}, \text{PHONE}, \text{SSN}, \text{ADDRESS}\}$ and applies a sanitization operator
$\phi(w_{i:j}, \tau)$ <Cite id="carlini2021extracting" />:

$$
x' = \mathcal{T}(x) = \left( \dots, w_{i-1}, \phi(w_{i:j}, \tau), w_{j+1}, \dots \right).
$$

Redaction limits the exposure of personal secrets in training corpora, reducing downstream memorization and unintended
disclosure.
</Definition>

## Detectors

Industrial sanitization pipelines deploy multi-tiered architectures combining deterministic pattern matching with
contextual statistical models:

### 1. Syntactic regex and checksum validators
Rigid, standardized identifiers follow predictable syntaxes:
- **Social Security Numbers (SSNs)**: Formatted as `^\d{3}-\d{2}-\d{4}$` with designated area-group-serial number
  constraints.
- **Credit card numbers**: Verified via the **Luhn modular 10 algorithm**:

  $$
  \sum_{i=1}^k d'_i \equiv 0 \pmod{10}, \quad \text{where } d'_i = \begin{cases} d_i & \text{if position is even} \\ 2d_i - 9 \cdot \mathbb{I}(2d_i > 9) & \text{if position is odd} \end{cases}
  $$

- **Network identifiers**: IPv4 and IPv6 addresses, MAC addresses, and RFC 5322 compliant email regular expressions.
- *Trade-off*: Near $100\%$ precision with sub-microsecond latency, but completely incapable of detecting names,
  occupations, or clinical notes.

### 2. Contextual Named Entity Recognition (NER)
Unstructured person names, organization affiliations, and physical locations lack fixed syntactic structure and require
context-aware sequence taggers <Cite id="lample2016" />:
- Deep transformer token classifiers (e.g. fine-tuned RoBERTa, DeBERTa, or Microsoft Presidio engines) predict BIO
  sequence tags ($B\text{-PER}, I\text{-PER}, B\text{-LOC}, B\text{-ORG}$).
- Disambiguates polysemous words based on grammatical syntax (e.g., distinguishing "May" as a calendar month from
  "Theresa May" as a person, or "Apple" as a fruit from an organization).

### 3. Contextual LLM verifiers
High-ambiguity spans are passed to lightweight language models to arbitrate subtle contextual references (e.g.
determining whether an alphanumeric token is a random password, a git commit hash, or an internal patient identifier).

## Recall limits and the long tail of leakage

The central vulnerability of automated redaction is that **recall is fundamentally bounded**:

$$
\text{Recall} = \frac{\text{True Positives}}{\text{True Positives} + \text{False Negatives}} < 1.0.
$$

In controlled academic evaluations, state-of-the-art ensembles achieve $95\%\text{--}98\%$ recall. However, when applied
to web-scale pretraining corpora (e.g. Common Crawl, RedPajama, or FineWeb spanning trillions of tokens), the arithmetic
of scale exposes dangerous leakage:

- In a corpus of $100$ million documents containing $50$ million PII occurrences, a $98\%$ recall rate leaves
  **$1{,}000{,}000$ sensitive records completely unmasked**.
- As Carlini et al. <Cite id="carlini2021extracting" /> demonstrated, large language models disproportionately memorize
  outlier and high-perplexity strings, frequently memorizing the exact unredacted personal telephone numbers and emails
  that slipped through the pipeline.
- **The long-tail distribution**: Leakage concentrates in the long tail: uncommon international surnames, non-Latin
  scripts, deliberate typos, and creative obfuscations (`j_o_h_n [dot] d_o_e`).

## Redaction versus surrogate replacement

Once a PII span is identified, pipelines apply one of two sanitization strategies:

### Hard token masking
Replaces the sensitive span with a fixed sentinel token:
```text
Raw:        "Contact Dr. Alice Vance at alice.vance@blackmesa.gov regarding the test."
Sanitized:  "Contact [NAME] at [EMAIL] regarding the test."
```
- **Limitation**: Sentinel tokens destroy language modeling perplexity. Because `[NAME]` appears repeatedly across
  millions of contexts, downstream models suffer degraded grammar and frequently learn to output raw placeholder tags
  during production generation.

### Surrogate replacement (Synthetic substitution)
Substitutes detected PII with realistic synthetic entities drawn from demographic dictionaries or generative models:
```text
Raw:        "Contact Dr. Alice Vance at alice.vance@blackmesa.gov regarding the test."
Sanitized:  "Contact Dr. Robert Chen at robert.chen@syntheticmail.org regarding the test."
```
- **Benefits**: Preserves natural language syntax, part-of-speech distributions, and token frequency statistics,
  allowing pretraining to proceed smoothly without distribution distortion.
- **Consistency constraint**: Replacement must maintain document-level coreference consistency: every subsequent mention
  of "Dr. Vance" or "her" must map to "Dr. Chen" and "his" (or consistent gender agreement) throughout the entire text
  passage.

## Pitfalls and limitations

1. **Over-redaction of public domain knowledge**: Naive NER models routinely redact historical figures ("George
   Washington"), authors, public APIs, and scientific references, degrading the factual capability of the trained model.
2. **Contextual re-identification (the mosaic effect)**: Redacting a person's name while leaving highly specific
   biographical context ("The French general who crowned himself Emperor at Notre-Dame in 1804") allows language models
   to immediately reconstruct the redacted entity via parametric association.
3. **Redaction is not privacy certification**: Automated redaction is an empirical heuristic that substantially lowers
   privacy risk, but it does not provide formal mathematical guarantees against membership inference or extraction
   attacks.
