<Definition>
**Dimensionality reduction as preprocessing** transforms an input matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$ into a
lower-dimensional representation $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}$ ($K \ll D$) using an unsupervised mapping
$g: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^K$:

$$
\mathbf{z}_i = g(\mathbf{x}_i), \quad \text{fitted strictly on } \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}} = \{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^N,
$$

prior to training a downstream supervised model $\hat{y} = f(\mathbf{z})$. The mapping optimizes a reconstruction error
or variance preservation objective without knowledge of target labels $y$.
</Definition>

## PCA before a model

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) <Cite id="pearson1901" /> <Cite id="hotelling1933" /> finds an orthogonal coordinate
rotation that concentrates data variance into a minimal set of uncorrelated latent dimensions:

1. **Centering and covariance**: Features are centered around the empirical training mean
   $\boldsymbol{\mu} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{x}_i$:

   $$
   \mathbf{\Sigma} = \frac{1}{N-1} (\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{1}\boldsymbol{\mu}^\top)^\top (\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{1}\boldsymbol{\mu}^\top) = \mathbf{V} \mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{V}^\top,
   $$

   where $\mathbf{\Lambda} = \text{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_D)$ contains sorted eigenvalues
   ($\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_D \ge 0$) and $\mathbf{V} = [\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_D]$
   contains orthonormal eigenvectors.
2. **Linear projection**: Truncating to the top $K$ eigenvectors projects the data onto the $K$-dimensional subspace of
   maximum variance <Cite id="jolliffe2002" />:

   $$
   \mathbf{z}_i = \mathbf{V}_K^\top (\mathbf{x}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^K, \quad \text{where } \mathbf{V}_K = [\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_K] \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times K}.
   $$

### Downstream benefits
- **Elimination of multicollinearity**: Because principal components are mutually orthogonal
  ($\text{Cov}(\mathbf{z}) = \mathbf{\Lambda}_K$), PCA eliminates condition-number inflation in ordinary least squares
  and logistic regression.
- **Acceleration of distance-based estimators**: Algorithms with computational complexity scaling in input dimension
  ($k$-nearest neighbors, kernel SVMs, and tree split histograms) execute substantially faster over $K$ orthogonal
  components than over $D$ redundant features.
- **Noise filtering**: Under an isotropic Gaussian noise model $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{s} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}$,
  trailing components ($\lambda_j \approx \sigma_{\text{noise}}^2$) capture predominantly incoherent measurement noise.

## Non-negative matrix factorisation

When inputs represent non-negative physical quantities—such as word counts in documents, pixel intensities in images, or
spectral absorption energies—PCA yields components with negative loadings that lack physical interpretation.
Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) <Cite id="lee1999" /> constrains the decomposition to non-negative factors:

$$
\mathbf{X} \approx \mathbf{W} \mathbf{H}, \quad \text{subject to } \mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{N \times K}, \quad \mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{K \times D}.
$$

- $\mathbf{W}$ contains the compressed non-negative representations (the downstream features $\mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{W}$).
- $\mathbf{H}$ contains non-negative basis vectors representing dictionary parts.
- Because subtraction is prohibited, factors cannot cancel each other out; the model learns purely additive, parts-based
  representations (e.g., individual facial parts in images or thematic topics in text corpora).

## Random projections and the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma

When input dimensionality is very high ($D > 10{,}000$, such as bag-of-words or genomic markers), computing an exact
singular value decomposition (SVD) for PCA is computationally prohibitive. Random projection compresses features using a
data-independent random matrix $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times K}$:

$$
\mathbf{z}_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{K}} \mathbf{R}^\top \mathbf{x}_i.
$$

By the **Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma** <Cite id="johnson1984" />, for any set of $N$ points in $\mathbb{R}^D$ and any
$\epsilon \in (0, 1)$, if:

$$
K \ge \frac{8 \ln N}{\epsilon^2},
$$

then all pairwise Euclidean distances are preserved within a factor of $1 \pm \epsilon$:

$$
(1 - \epsilon) \|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j\|_2^2 \le \|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{z}_j\|_2^2 \le (1 + \epsilon) \|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j\|_2^2.
$$

- **Computational cost**: Random projection requires zero fitting time and requires no data storage; projection weights
  are generated on the fly via pseudo-random number seeds.
- **Sparse projections**: Entries in $\mathbf{R}$ can be sampled from Achlioptas discrete distributions ($\{-1, 0, +1\}$
  with probabilities $\{1/6, 2/3, 1/6\}$), accelerating multiplication into integer additions.

## Non-linear autoencoders

When data lie on curved, non-linear manifolds (such as a Swiss roll or articulated pose coordinates), linear subspaces
fail to unfold the geometry without high reconstruction error. An **autoencoder** passes inputs through a bottleneck
neural network:

$$
\mathbf{z}_i = f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i), \quad \hat{\mathbf{x}}_i = g_\phi(\mathbf{z}_i), \quad \min_{\theta, \phi} \sum_{i=1}^N \|\mathbf{x}_i - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_i\|_2^2.
$$

While non-linear autoencoders capture non-linear structure, they introduce practical drawbacks: non-convex optimization,
risk of overfitting on small tabular datasets, and latent dimensions that may not preserve metric distances without
contractive or variational regularization.

## Signal in low-variance directions

The fundamental risk of unsupervised dimensionality reduction as a preprocessing step is the **variance-signal
dissociation paradox** <Cite id="jolliffe2002" />:

> Unsupervised variance does not equal supervised predictive power.

PCA identifies axes that maximize input variance $\max_{\mathbf{w}} \text{Var}(\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{X})$, completely
oblivious to the relationship with the target $y$:

- If high variance is driven by uninformative background noise, sensor calibration drift, or dominant batch effects, PCA
  allocates the leading components ($\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_K$) to noise.
- The actual predictive signal for $y$ may reside in subtle, low-variance directions ($\mathbf{v}_{D-1}, \mathbf{v}_D$).
- Truncating features by keeping components that explain 95% of total variance systematically **discards the target
  signal**, causing the downstream model to fail.

### Supervised alternatives
When the objective is supervised prediction, prefer supervised dimensionality reduction techniques:
- **Partial Least Squares (PLS)**: Finds latent orthogonal directions that maximize covariance with target $y$:
  $\max_{\mathbf{w}} \text{Cov}^2(\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{X}, y)$.
- **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)**: Maximizes between-class variance relative to within-class variance:
  $\max_{\mathbf{w}} \frac{\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{S}_B \mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{S}_W \mathbf{w}}$.
- **Supervised PCA**: Performs screening feature selection based on marginal target correlation before computing
  principal components over the retained subset.

## Pipeline hygiene and pitfalls

1. **Data leakage through global fitting**: Never fit PCA, NMF, or an autoencoder on the entire dataset prior to
   splitting. Centering and covariance estimation across the full dataset leaks test distribution statistics into the
   training pipeline. Always fit transformers inside an estimator pipeline on training folds only:

   ```python
   # Correct: Fitted strictly on training fold
   pca = PCA(n_components=10)
   X_train_pca = pca.fit_transform(X_train)
   X_test_pca = pca.transform(X_test)
   ```

2. **Failure to standardize before PCA**: Principal component directions are scale-variant. If feature $x_1$ is measured
   in millimeters ($[0, 10{,}000]$) and $x_2$ in kilograms ($[0, 10]$), $x_1$ will artificially dominate the first
   principal component regardless of physical significance. Standardize all features to zero mean and unit variance
   before computing PCA.
3. **Loss of feature interpretability**: Projecting $D$ named features into linear combinations $\sum_j v_{kj} x_j$
   eliminates direct domain meaning, complicating model explainability (e.g., SHAP values attribute importance to
   abstract linear combinations rather than actionable business variables).
