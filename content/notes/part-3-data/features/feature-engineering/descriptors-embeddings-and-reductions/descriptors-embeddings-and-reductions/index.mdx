<Definition>
A **descriptor**, an **embedding** and a **reduction** are three kinds of fixed map $\phi$ that turn an input $\xvec$
into a feature vector $\zvec = \phi(\xvec) \in \reals^K$ for a downstream model. A descriptor is designed by hand from a
principle of the signal. An embedding is the output of an encoder trained on other data
and frozen. A reduction is fitted to the training inputs without their labels, so that $K$ is smaller than the input
dimension $D$.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Many inputs are too raw or too wide for a small model. An image is a grid of pixels, an audio clip tens of thousands of
samples, a product description a string, a gene-expression row thousands of numbers. A linear model or a
gradient-boosted tree cannot learn from such inputs directly with the data at hand. Each of the three maps puts prior
knowledge in front of the model: knowledge of the signal in a descriptor, knowledge learned from a large corpus in an
embedding, and the structure of the inputs themselves in a reduction. The downstream model then learns from a few
hundred informative columns instead of the raw input.

## Main ideas

**Descriptors encode a principle of the signal.** <NoteLink to="handcrafted-descriptors" /> covers <Gloss name="sift" />
keypoint descriptors, which are invariant to scale and rotation <Cite id="lowe2004" />, histograms of oriented gradients
for shape, <NoteLink to="mel-frequency-cepstral-coefficients" /> for the spectral envelope of speech, and curated
time-series feature sets such as catch22. Descriptors need no training data and are easy to interpret. They remain the
better choice when labelled data or compute are scarce.

**Embeddings transfer a learned representation.** <NoteLink to="pretrained-embeddings-as-features" /> passes a text,
image or audio column through a frozen encoder, such as a sentence encoder or <NoteLink to="clip" />
<Cite id="radford2021" />, and appends the vector to the other columns. The encoder version is part of the feature
definition. A change of encoder changes every value, so the version is pinned and recorded.

**Reductions compress without labels.** <NoteLink to="dimensionality-reduction-as-preprocessing" /> fits PCA
<Cite id="jolliffe2002" />, non-negative matrix factorisation, random projections or an autoencoder on the training
rows and applies the fitted map to every later row. Unsupervised reduction keeps the directions of high variance. The
target signal can sit in a direction of low variance, which the reduction then discards.

**All three are fixed before the model is fitted.** None of the maps sees the target. A descriptor or a frozen encoder
has no parameters fitted to the dataset. A reduction does, so it is fitted inside each training fold, like any other
preprocessing step.
