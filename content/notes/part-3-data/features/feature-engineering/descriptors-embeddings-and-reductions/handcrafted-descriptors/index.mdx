<Definition>
A **hand-built descriptor** (or handcrafted feature) is a deterministic, unlearned transformation
$\phi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^K$ designed from analytical principles—such as differential geometry, psychoacoustics,
or dynamical systems theory—to extract invariant summary statistics from high-dimensional continuous signals (images,
audio, or time series):

$$
\mathbf{z} = \phi(\mathbf{x}), \quad \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C} \text{ or } \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}.
$$

Unlike deep representations learned by gradient descent, hand-built descriptors have fixed parameters, require zero
training data to construct, and provide explicit physical interpretability.
</Definition>

## Image descriptors

Before the dominance of deep convolutional networks, computer vision relied on analytical operators invariant to affine
transformations, illumination shifts, and scale:

### SIFT (Scale-Invariant Feature Transform)
Developed by Lowe <Cite id="lowe2004" />, SIFT extracts local keypoint descriptors invariant to scale and rotation
through a multi-stage pipeline:

1. **Scale-space extrema detection**: Identifies candidate keypoints across octaves of blurred images using Difference
   of Gaussians (DoG):

   $$
   D(x, y, \sigma) = (G(x, y, k\sigma) - G(x, y, \sigma)) * I(x, y).
   $$

2. **Keypoint localisation and orientation**: Rejects low-contrast edge points and assigns a dominant local orientation
   based on gradient directions in a surrounding Gaussian window.
3. **Descriptor representation**: Computes gradient orientation histograms across a $4 \times 4$ spatial grid of
   subregions, yielding a canonical $4 \times 4 \times 8 = 128$-dimensional descriptor vector that is robust to 3D
   viewpoint changes and illumination scaling.

### HOG (Histograms of Oriented Gradients)
Introduced by Dalal and Triggs <Cite id="dalal2005" /> for pedestrian detection, HOG captures local object appearance
and shape by modeling edge directions:

- The image is tiled into small spatial regions called **cells** (e.g., $8 \times 8$ pixels).
- Each cell accumulates a 1D histogram of gradient orientations (typically 9 bins from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$),
  weighted by gradient magnitude.
- Groups of adjacent cells are aggregated into overlapping **blocks** and normalized using $L_2$-norm or $L_1$-sqrt to
  achieve invariance to shadowing and illumination gradients.
- Linear SVM classifiers trained over concatenated HOG descriptors formed the state of the art in object detection for
  over a decade.

## Audio descriptors (MFCCs)

In speech processing and bioacoustics, raw audio waveforms oscillate rapidly at sampling rates of
$16\text{–}48\text{ kHz}$. Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) <Cite id="davis1980" /> condense raw acoustic
pressure waves into spectral energy envelopes aligned with human auditory perception:

1. **Framing and windowing**: Partition the waveform into short overlapping frames ($20\text{–}30\text{ ms}$, where
   speech is quasi-stationary) and apply a Hamming window to prevent spectral leakage.
2. **Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)**: Compute the discrete Fourier transform to calculate the power spectrum
   $|X(t, f)|^2$.
3. **Mel filterbank integration**: Pass spectral energies through a bank of triangular bandpass filters spaced linearly
   below $1{,}000\text{ Hz}$ and logarithmically above $1{,}000\text{ Hz}$ according to the Mel scale:

   $$
   m = 2595 \log_{10}\left(1 + \frac{f}{700}\right).
   $$

   This mimics the cochlea's decreasing frequency resolution at higher pitches.
4. **Logarithmic compression**: Apply $\ln(\cdot)$ to filterbank energies to emulate human logarithmic perception of
   loudness.
5. **Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)**: Compute the DCT-II of log energies:

   $$
   c_n = \sum_{m=1}^M \log(S_m) \cos\left[\frac{\pi n}{M}\left(m - \frac{1}{2}\right)\right].
   $$

   The DCT decorrelates filterbank outputs, concentrating speech formants into the first $12\text{–}20$ cepstral
   coefficients while discarding fine pitch harmonics.

## Time-series feature sets

In industrial IoT, telemetry, and medical sensor analysis (ECG/EEG), machine learning models often classify
variable-length time series. Automated feature extraction libraries systematically generate hundreds of statistical and
dynamical summaries:

- **tsfresh** <Cite id="christ2018" />: Calculates over 700 statistical characteristics per series—including empirical
  distribution moments, Fourier and wavelet coefficients, sample entropy, approximate entropy, autoregressive
  coefficients, and peak counting. It couples extraction with Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) hypothesis
  tests to discard irrelevant features.
- **catch22 (Canonical Time-Series Characteristics)** <Cite id="lubba2019" />: Distills a candidate pool of over 7,000
  algorithmic features into a core subset of 22 computationally efficient, minimally redundant features covering:
  - Distribution properties (median, interquartile range, outlier counts).
  - Autocorrelation and temporal structure (first zero of autocorrelation, automutual information).
  - Non-linear dynamics and periodicity (fluctuation analysis, symbolic dynamics).

## Where handcrafted descriptors remain essential

Despite the power of end-to-end deep learning, hand-built descriptors remain indispensable in specific engineering
regimes:

1. **Small-sample regimes ($N \ll 500$)**: In rare disease diagnostics, structural health monitoring of bridges, or
   high-value machinery failure forecasting, historical failure samples are scarce. Deep networks overfit instantly,
   whereas simple linear models or random forests trained on MFCCs or catch22 descriptors generalize reliably.
2. **Ultra-low-power edge deployment**: Microcontrollers (ARM Cortex-M, sensor ASICs) operate within milliwatt power
   budgets and kilobytes of RAM. Computing 10 statistical descriptors takes microseconds on integer hardware, whereas
   running a deep neural network requires megabytes of memory and specialized tensor accelerators.
3. **Regulatory auditability and certification**: In aviation, medical devices (FDA approvals), and nuclear
   instrumentation, predictions must link to explicit physical phenomena (e.g., "vibration kurtosis exceeded 4.5"
   indicating bearing surface spalling), which black-box neural embeddings cannot provide.

## Pitfalls and limitations

1. **Heuristic hyperparameter coupling**: Handcrafted pipelines depend heavily on unlearned hyperparameters (window
   length, filter count, frame stride, sampling rate). If operating conditions change (e.g., a motor changes operational
   RPM), fixed filter banks fail to capture shifted resonant frequencies.
2. **Representation bottlenecks**: By projecting raw signals into fixed summaries, handcrafted descriptors irrevocably
   discard fine phase relationships and subtle temporal micro-patterns that end-to-end architectures might exploit.
3. **Extraction computational scaling**: Running exhaustive libraries like tsfresh across millions of streaming time
   series introduces severe feature engineering pipeline latency, often requiring distributed map-reduce architectures
   to compute offline.
