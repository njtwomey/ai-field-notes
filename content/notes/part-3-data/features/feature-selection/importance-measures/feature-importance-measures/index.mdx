<Definition>
A **feature importance measure** is a quantitative metric scoring the relative contribution or predictive reliance of
each input feature within a trained machine learning model. Rather than selecting a hard binary subset, importance
metrics provide continuous rankings that guide post-hoc variable screening, interpretability diagnostics, and
dimensionality reduction.
</Definition>

Different importance measures operationalize "importance" through fundamentally distinct mechanisms: accumulated
in-sample split criteria, out-of-sample prediction degradation under marginal shuffling, retrained performance
differences upon feature omission, or cooperative game-theoretic attributions.

## Impurity-based importance (MDI)

Mean Decrease in Impurity (MDI), commonly termed Gini importance in classification trees, aggregates the weighted
reduction in node impurity achieved by every split that utilizes feature $X_j$ across an ensemble of $T$ decision trees:

$$
\text{MDI}(X_j) = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_t : v(\tau) = j} p(\tau) \Delta i(\tau)
$$

where $\mathcal{T}_t$ is the set of internal nodes in tree $t$, $v(\tau)$ is the feature chosen to split node $\tau$,
$p(\tau) = \frac{N_\tau}{N}$ is the proportion of training instances reaching node $\tau$, and
$\Delta i(\tau) = i(\tau) - p_L i(\tau_L) - p_R i(\tau_R)$ is the reduction in impurity (Gini index or variance).

### Advantages and inherent pathologies

- **Speed**: MDI is computed as an instantaneous byproduct of tree induction with zero additional model evaluations.
- **The cardinality trap**: As demonstrated by Carolin Strobl et al. <Cite id="strobl2007" />, MDI exhibits severe
  structural bias toward features with many distinct values—specifically continuous variables and high-cardinality
  discrete categories (such as user IDs or zip codes). Because a feature with $K$ unique categories offers $2^{K-1} - 1$
  potential split partitions, it has vastly more mathematical degrees of freedom to accidentally separate training
  labels in-sample, receiving artificially inflated MDI scores even when it represents pure white noise.

## Permutation feature importance (MDA)

Mean Decrease in Accuracy (MDA), or Permutation Feature Importance <Cite id="breiman2001" />, shifts evaluation from
in-sample split artifacts to out-of-sample predictive degradation on validation or out-of-bag (OOB) data.

### The permutation protocol

1. Given a trained model $f$ and a validation dataset $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$, compute the
   baseline generalization loss:
   $$
   \mathcal{L}_{\text{orig}} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \ell\big(y_i, f(x_i)\big)
   $$
2. For candidate feature $X_j$, construct a corrupted dataset $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}^{(j)}$ by randomly shuffling the
   entries of column $j$, severing its statistical relationship with target $Y$ while preserving its marginal empirical
   distribution.
3. Compute the corrupted validation loss
   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{perm}}^{(j)} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \ell\big(y_i, f(\tilde{x}_i^{(j)})\big)$.
4. The permutation importance is the resulting error increase:
   $$
   \text{MDA}(X_j) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{perm}}^{(j)} - \mathcal{L}_{\text{orig}}
   $$

Because MDA evaluates predictions strictly on unseen hold-out observations, it is immune to the high-cardinality
overfitting bias that plagues MDI. A high-cardinality noise column will yield $\text{MDA} \approx 0$ (or slightly
negative due to random test variation).

### Collinear distortion in marginal permutation

When features are strongly correlated ($X_1 \approx X_2$), independently shuffling $X_1$ destroys the joint covariance
structure $P(X_1, X_2)$, synthesising off-manifold synthetic points that could never occur in reality (e.g., vehicle
weight = $3000\,\text{kg}$ with engine displacement = $0.5\,\text{L}$). If the model behaves erratically in these
unpopulated regions, permutation importance can drastically misestimate true operational relevance.

## Drop-column importance

Drop-column importance measures the performance drop when a feature is omitted and a completely new model is retrained
from scratch on the remaining $p - 1$ features:

$$
\text{DropImp}(X_j) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}\big(f_{\mathcal{X} \setminus \{j\}}\big) - \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}\big(f_{\mathcal{X}}\big)
$$

### The collinear masking effect

While drop-column importance provides the cleanest theoretical measure of whether a feature contains non-redundant
predictive signal, it suffers from severe **collinear masking**:

If features $X_1$ and $X_2$ are near-perfect substitutes ($r = 0.98$) that jointly drive the target:
- Dropping $X_1$ allows the retrained model to rely entirely on $X_2$, resulting in negligible loss increase
  ($\Delta \mathcal{L} \approx 0$).
- Dropping $X_2$ allows the model to rely entirely on $X_1$, again yielding $\Delta \mathcal{L} \approx 0$.

Consequently, both core drivers appear completely disposable according to drop-column metrics, misleading practitioners
into dropping both variables simultaneously and destroying predictive capacity.

## Conditional permutation importance

To resolve the off-manifold artifacts of standard marginal permutation without incurring the collinear masking of
drop-column refitting, Carolin Strobl et al. <Cite id="strobl2008" /> introduced **conditional permutation importance**.

Instead of shuffling $X_j$ uniformly across the entire dataset, conditional permutation partitions the sample space into
homogeneous conditioning strata $\mathcal{B}_k$ based on the values of correlated peer features
$Z \in \text{Corr}(X_j)$. The values of $X_j$ are then permuted **only within each stratum**:

$$
\tilde{x}_{ij} \sim \text{Permute}\big( \{ x_{kj} : k \in \mathcal{B}(x_i) \} \big)
$$

By permuting within local neighborhoods, conditional permutation respects the conditional distribution $P(X_j \mid Z)$,
preventing unrealistic synthetic combinations and accurately isolating the marginal incremental information contributed
by $X_j$ beyond its correlated peers.

## Shapley values and global attribution

Game-theoretic Shapley values <Cite id="lundberg2017" /> provide a mathematically unified attribution framework by
allocating credit for an individual prediction across all candidate coalitions of features:

$$
\phi_j(x) = \sum_{S \subseteq \mathcal{X} \setminus \{X_j\}} \frac{|S|! (p - |S| - 1)!}{p!} \left[ f_x(S \cup \{X_j\}) - f_x(S) \right]
$$

Global feature importance is then derived by computing the mean absolute Shapley attribution across the entire
population:

$$
I_{\text{SHAP}}(X_j) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n |\phi_j(x_i)|
$$

This formulation satisfies efficiency, symmetry, and monotonicity, ensuring that redundant or substitute features share
attribution equitably rather than masking each other out.
