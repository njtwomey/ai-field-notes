<Definition>
**Feature engineering and selection** decide the input vector of a model. Engineering adds features: functions of the
raw fields, of related records or of time that expose a relation directly. Selection removes features: it keeps a
subset that predicts as well or better, with less cost, less noise and less to explain <Cite id="guyon2003" />. Both
are fitted choices and are validated like any other part of the model.
</Definition>

## What it is for

The input representation sets what a model can learn from a fixed amount of data.
Engineering adds the ratios, aggregates and calendar effects that a model would otherwise have to discover. Selection
removes columns that add variance, cost or leakage risk without adding signal, and answers which variables matter. The
two pull in opposite directions. A practical workflow alternates them: build candidates, select among them under
cross-validation, inspect the result and build again <Cite id="kuhn2019" />.

## Main ideas

**Features encode knowledge the model lacks.** <NoteLink to="feature-engineering" /> covers construction of
interactions, ratios and crosses, <NoteLink to="date-and-time-features">date and time features</NoteLink>, aggregates
over related tables, automated search over candidate features, descriptors and embeddings, and
<NoteLink to="feature-stores">feature stores</NoteLink> that serve one definition to training and production.

**Selection has three families.** <NoteLink to="feature-selection" /> sets them out. Filter methods score features one
at a time by a statistic, such as mutual information. Wrapper methods search subsets by validated model performance.
Embedded methods select while fitting, as the lasso does through its $\ell_1$ penalty.

**Importance is not the same as selection.** <NoteLink to="feature-importance-measures" /> scores how much a fitted
model relies on each feature, by impurity decrease, permutation, refitting without the feature or Shapley values.
Impurity importance favours high-cardinality features, and permutation importance misleads when features are correlated.

**Selection must sit inside the validation loop.** Selecting on all rows and then cross-validating reports high
accuracy even on pure noise <Cite id="hastie2009" />. <NoteLink to="selection-inside-cross-validation" /> shows the
effect and the nested protocol that avoids it.

**Selected sets are unstable.** Small changes in the data change which features are selected.
<NoteLink to="stability-of-feature-selection" /> covers stability selection, the Boruta shadow-feature test and
knockoffs, which control the <Gloss name="fdr">false discovery rate</Gloss> among selected features.

## How the notes fit together
