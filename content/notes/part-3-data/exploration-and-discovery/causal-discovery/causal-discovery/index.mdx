<Definition>
**Causal discovery** (or causal structure learning) is the algorithmic recovery of the directed acyclic graph (DAG)
$\mathcal{G} = (\mathbf{V}, \mathbf{E})$ governing a joint observational distribution $P(\mathbf{V})$. It identifies
direct causal arrows, confounding paths, and unshielded colliders from observational data under explicit structural
axioms regarding faithfulness, Markov properties, and error distributions.
</Definition>

Standard predictive models learn correlations $P(Y \mid X)$ that collapse when deployed under external interventions,
policy changes, or distribution shifts. While randomized controlled trials (RCTs) represent the gold standard for causal
inference, experiments are frequently unethical, cost-prohibitive, or physically impossible. Causal discovery algorithms
systematically exploit conditional independencies, penalised graph scores, and non-Gaussian noise asymmetries to
estimate underlying causal architectures directly from observational measurements <Cite id="pearl2009, spirtes2000" />.

## Causal graphs and foundational axioms

A causal Bayesian network represents $D$ random variables $\mathbf{V} = \{V_1, \dots, V_D\}$ as nodes in a DAG
$\mathcal{G}$. The joint distribution factorizes according to the **Causal Markov Condition**:

$$
P(V_1, \dots, V_D) = \prod_{i=1}^D P(V_i \mid \text{Pa}(V_i))
$$

Where $\text{Pa}(V_i)$ denotes the set of direct parents (immediate causes) of $V_i$.

Algorithmic causal discovery relies on three foundational assumptions:
1. **Causal sufficiency**: There are no unobserved common causes (latent confounders) influencing any pair of observed
   variables in $\mathbf{V}$.
2. **Causal Markov assumption**: Each variable $V_i$ is conditionally independent of all its non-descendants given its
   direct causal parents:
   $$
   V_i \perp\!\!\!\perp \text{NonDesc}(V_i) \mid \text{Pa}(V_i)
   $$
3. **Causal faithfulness**: All conditional independencies present in the joint distribution $P(\mathbf{V})$ are
   structural consequences of $d$-separation in $\mathcal{G}$. Faithfulness rules out exact algebraic parameter
   cancellations (such as two opposing causal pathways whose strengths sum exactly to zero) <Cite id="spirtes2000" />.

| Triad Geometry | Graphical Topology | Marginal Relation | Conditional Relation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chain (Mediation)** | $X \to Z \to Y$ | $X \not\perp\!\!\!\perp Y$ (dependent) | $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z$ (blocked given mediator $Z$) |
| **Fork (Confounding)** | $X \leftarrow Z \to Y$ | $X \not\perp\!\!\!\perp Y$ (dependent) | $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z$ (blocked given confounder $Z$) |
| **Collider (v-Structure)** | $X \to Z \leftarrow Y$ | $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y$ (independent) | $X \not\perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z$ (conditioning opens path!) |

Chains and forks share identical conditional independencies ($X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z$ and
$X \not\perp\!\!\!\perp Y$). In stark contrast, the **collider (v-structure)** exhibits the inverted pattern: $X$ and
$Y$ are marginally independent ($X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y$), but conditioning on collider $Z$ induces an artificial
dependence ($X \not\perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z$). This mathematical asymmetry forms the cornerstone of constraint-based
discovery <Cite id="pearl2009" />.

## Constraint-based methods: PC and FCI algorithms

Constraint-based discovery infers causal edges by conducting systematic statistical hypothesis tests of conditional
independence.

### The PC algorithm

The PC algorithm (Peter Spirtes and Clark Glymour <Cite id="spirtes2000" />) operates in four distinct phases:
1. **Skeleton discovery**: Initialize a complete undirected graph where every variable pair is connected. For each pair
   $(X, Y)$, test conditional independence $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid \mathbf{S}$ for conditioning sets $\mathbf{S}$ of
   increasing cardinality ($|\mathbf{S}| = 0, 1, 2, \dots$). If an independence is found, delete the edge $X - Y$ and
   store $\mathbf{S}$ as the separation set $\text{SepSet}(X, Y)$.
2. **Collider orientation**: For every unshielded triple $X - Z - Y$ (where $X$ and $Y$ are not directly adjacent), test
   whether $Z \in \text{SepSet}(X, Y)$. If $Z \notin \text{SepSet}(X, Y)$, orient the triple as a collider:
   $$
   X \to Z \leftarrow Y
   $$
3. **Meek orientation propagation**: Apply Meek's four logical orientation rules to orient remaining undirected edges
   without creating new colliders or directed cycles.

The PC algorithm outputs a Completed Partially Directed Acyclic Graph (CPDAG) representing the **Markov equivalence
class**—the set of all DAGs that share the exact same skeleton and v-structures <Cite id="spirtes2000" />.

### FCI algorithm and hidden confounding

When causal sufficiency does not hold (latent confounders exist), the PC algorithm can output false causal arrows. The
**Fast Causal Inference (FCI)** algorithm <Cite id="spirtes2000" /> relaxes causal sufficiency. FCI outputs a Partial
Ancestral Graph (PAG) that distinguishes genuine direct causal edges ($\to$) from edges contaminated by latent common
causes, marked with bidirected arrows ($\leftrightarrow$).

## Score-based methods and continuous optimization

Score-based methods frame causal discovery as a model selection optimization problem over candidate DAGs.

### Greedy Equivalence Search (GES)

GES evaluates candidate Markov equivalence classes using a penalized likelihood criterion, such as the Bayesian
Information Criterion (BIC) <Cite id="chickering2002" />:

$$
\text{BIC}(\mathcal{G}) = -2 \ln L(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}; \mathcal{G}) + k \ln N
$$

GES begins with the empty graph, greedily adds forward edges that maximize BIC improvement until local optimality, and
then greedily removes backward edges. Under faithfulness and large samples, GES is guaranteed to return the true Markov
equivalence class <Cite id="chickering2002" />.

### Continuous optimization: NOTEARS

Traditional DAG search is computationally NP-hard due to the combinatorial explosion of super-exponential acyclic graph
space. Zheng et al. introduced **NOTEARS**, transforming combinatorial structure learning into a continuous optimization
problem <Cite id="zheng2018" />.

Given observational data matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$, let $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$
be the weighted adjacency matrix. Zheng et al. proved that a graph is strictly acyclic if and only if the matrix
exponential satisfies <Cite id="zheng2018" />:

$$
h(\mathbf{W}) = \text{Tr}\left( e^{\mathbf{W} \odot \mathbf{W}} \right) - D = 0
$$

Where $\odot$ is the Hadamard (element-wise) product. Causal discovery becomes a continuous constrained optimization
problem:

$$
\min_{\mathbf{W}} \frac{1}{2N} \|\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}\|_F^2 + \lambda \|\mathbf{W}\|_1 \quad \text{subject to } h(\mathbf{W}) = 0
$$

Solved via augmented Lagrangian methods, NOTEARS scales causal DAG learning smoothly using standard gradient-based
optimizers <Cite id="zheng2018" />.

## Functional causal models and non-Gaussian noise

Markov equivalence classes leave many causal directions undirected (e.g. $X - Y$). By leveraging non-Gaussian noise
distributions, **functional causal models** break equivalence classes and identify unique DAGs
<Cite id="shimizu2006" />.

### Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Models (LiNGAM)

Consider a linear structural equation model:

$$
X_i = \sum_{j \in \text{Pa}(i)} \beta_{ij} X_j + \varepsilon_i
$$

Where error terms $\varepsilon_i$ are continuous, mutually independent noise variables.
- If all $\varepsilon_i$ are Gaussian, the true causal direction between $X$ and $Y$ is mathematically unidentifiable
  without interventions.
- **The Darmois-Skitovich theorem**: If noise terms $\varepsilon_i$ are **non-Gaussian** (e.g., Laplace, uniform, or
  skewed distributions), the full causal DAG is **uniquely identifiable** <Cite id="shimizu2006" />.

When fitting $Y = \beta X + \varepsilon_Y$, the estimated residual $\hat{\varepsilon}_Y$ is statistically independent of
$X$. However, in the reversed model $X = \tilde{\beta} Y + \tilde{\varepsilon}_X$, the residual $\tilde{\varepsilon}_X$
is *dependent* on $Y$. LiNGAM uses Independent Component Analysis (FastICA) to extract the unique causal permutation
matrix <Cite id="shimizu2006" />.

## Time series and temporal ordering

In longitudinal and streaming datasets, physical time provides an immutable asymmetric constraint: causes must precede
their effects ($t_{\text{cause}} < t_{\text{effect}}$).

### Granger causality

Variable $X$ is said to **Granger-cause** $Y$ if historical values of $X$ provide statistically significant information
for forecasting $Y$ beyond all information contained in past values of $Y$ alone:

$$
P(Y_t \mid Y_{<t}, X_{<t}) \neq P(Y_t \mid Y_{<t})
$$

Evaluated via vector autoregression (VAR) F-tests, Granger causality establishes predictive precedence. However, if an
unobserved latent cause $Z$ drives both $X$ (with a 1-day lag) and $Y$ (with a 2-day lag), Granger causality falsely
concludes that $X \to Y$. Genuine causal claims in time series require combining temporal lags with constraint-based
independence testing.

## Diagnostic checklist

To execute causal discovery on real-world observational datasets:

1. **Verify causal sufficiency assumptions**: Explicitly audit whether unmeasured confounders (e.g., user wealth,
   geographical weather) influence both treatment and outcome; if unmeasured confounding is plausible, use FCI rather
   than standard PC <Cite id="spirtes2000" />.
2. **Test for non-Gaussian noise**: Compute kurtosis and skewness of feature residuals. If distributions deviate
   significantly from normality, deploy LiNGAM to orient undirected Markov equivalence edges uniquely
   <Cite id="shimizu2006" />.
3. **Exploit temporal ordering**: Enforce strict directional constraints ($t_i < t_j$) in the adjacency matrix prior to
   graph optimization to eliminate reversed causal loops.
4. **Solve continuous acyclicity via NOTEARS**: When features exceed 30 dimensions, formulate structure learning as
   continuous augmented Lagrangian optimization <Cite id="zheng2018" />.
5. **Differentiate predictive features from causal targets**: Never assume high regression weights imply intervention
   efficacy without verifying graph $d$-separation <Cite id="pearl2009" />.
