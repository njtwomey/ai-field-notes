<Definition>
Reading raw examples is the qualitative diagnostic practice of directly inspecting individual, unaggregated data
points—drawn via uniform random sampling, feature extrema, and model error rankings. While summary statistics condense
millions of observations into scalar moments, reading raw records reveals data entry bugs, sentinel encodings,
formatting corruptions, and label errors that numerical aggregates conceal.
</Definition>

A prevalent failure mode among novice machine learning engineers is treating datasets as abstract numerical matrices,
immediately invoking `df.describe()` and jumping straight to model training. As Andrej Karpathy emphasized in his
foundational recipe for neural network development, the indispensable first step of any project is to spend hours
manually inspecting hundreds of raw examples <Cite id="karpathy2019" />. Aggregate metrics are mathematical
compressions; like Anscombe's famous quartet <Cite id="anscombe1973" />, identical summary statistics can hide
completely pathological distributions.

## Random samples

The baseline routine begins by drawing small, uniformly distributed random samples (e.g., 50 to 100 rows) across the
training split:

- **Bypassing Ingestion Order:** Never inspect data by running `df.head(10)`. The first few rows of a database export
  reflect chronological sorting, primary key sequences, or batch extraction order. If the table is sorted by date,
  `df.head()` inspects only obsolete historical records from five years ago; if sorted by user ID, it inspects only
  early beta-testers.
- **Mental Sanity Checking:** Reading random rows forces the engineer to evaluate whether feature values conform to
  physical and operational reality. Does a session duration of $14{,}000$ seconds make sense for a mobile micro-app?
  Does an e-commerce order with $0$ total spend represent a promotional gift, a canceled order, or a software bug?
- **Text and Media Artifacts:** In NLP and computer vision, random sampling unmasks raw formatting corruptions:
  unescaped HTML tags (`&amp;`, `<br/>`), character encoding failures (Mojibake, UTF-8 replacement characters `\ufffd`),
  corrupted watermarks, and truncated audio files.

## Extremes of each feature

Summary statistics like means and standard deviations are notoriously vulnerable to extreme values, yet they rarely
reveal *why* an extreme value exists. Systematic inspection requires sorting each feature and examining its top 10 and
bottom 10 records:

1. **Sentinel Values (Magic Numbers):** Legacy enterprise systems and sensor microcontrollers encode missingness or
   sensor failures as arbitrary numerical constants: `-999`, `9999`, `999999`, or `1970-01-01`. In an uncurated column,
   an engineer who sees a maximum temperature of $9999^\circ\text{C}$ will immediately realize it represents a sensor
   disconnect code rather than a volcanic eruption.
2. **Integer Overflow and Underflow:** Unsigned 8-bit integers that wrap from $0$ to $255$, or 32-bit timestamps
   overflowing to negative integers.
3. **Unit Incommensurability:** Inspecting the highest values of a `weight` column might reveal entries clustered around
   $180$ alongside entries clustered around $0.08$. Sorting by extrema immediately reveals that one clinic recorded
   patient weight in pounds while another recorded in metric tons.

## Review sorted by loss

Once an initial baseline model is trained, the most informative raw examples to read are those where the model made its
most egregious errors <Cite id="karpathy2019" />:

$$
\mathcal{S}_{\text{error}} = \operatorname{arg\,top}_K \left\{ \ell\left(f_\theta(x_i), y_i\right) \right\}
$$

Sorting the validation split by descending loss unmasks three distinct failure classes:
- **Mislabeled Ground Truth:** In many real-world benchmarks, the top 1% highest-loss cases are not model failures at
  all; they are blatant human labeling errors. An image clearly showing a golden retriever is labeled "banana"; a
  fraudulent transaction is labeled "legitimate". Identifying mislabeled training points cleans the supervisory signal.
- **Ambiguous and Multi-Object Instances:** An image containing both a dog and a cat where only single-label
  classification is supported, forcing the model to make an arbitrary guess.
- **Critical Feature Blindspots:** Instances where the human reviewer immediately understands the correct label from
  contextual clues that were completely omitted from the model's feature set (e.g., recognizing that an address is a
  commercial warehouse rather than a residence).

## What aggregates cannot show

Summary statistics are lossy projections that hide multi-dimensional structure <Cite id="anscombe1973" />:

- **Bimodal Distributions with Empty Centers:** A feature with a mean of $50$ and standard deviation of $15$ might
  possess a bimodal distribution with dense clusters at $20$ and $80$ and exactly zero mass at $50$. Imputing missing
  values with the column mean ($50$) injects synthetic data into an empty physical state.
- **Categorical Cardinality and String Typos:** Summary frequencies miss lexical variants: `New York`, `new york`, `NY`,
  `N.Y.`, and `New York ` (with trailing whitespace) appear as five separate categories in group-by operations,
  fragmenting statistical power.
- **Contradictory Duplicates:** Aggregates miss identical feature vectors $x_i = x_j$ that carry contradictory labels
  $y_i = 1$ and $y_j = 0$, setting an artificial ceiling on model accuracy.

## Pitfalls

- **Stopping at the First Clean Batch:** Inspecting 10 rows, seeing no obvious bugs, and concluding the entire
  100-million-row dataset is clean. Systematic inspection requires stratified sampling across rare subpopulations and
  edge-case strata.
- **Human Rationalization Bias:** When a human reviewer encounters an anomalous raw record, their cognitive instinct is
  to invent an elaborate justification for why the data point is valid, rather than recognizing it as an upstream ETL
  bug.
- **Visualizing Embeddings Instead of Raw Inputs:** Inspecting 2D t-SNE or UMAP scatter plots of pre-computed embeddings
  rather than reading the original raw text strings or looking at uncompressed raw pixels. Latent embeddings smooth over
  character-level corruptions and tokenization artifacts.
- **Ignoring Low-Confidence Correct Predictions:** Inspecting only misclassifications while ignoring high-confidence
  incorrect predictions or borderline cases where the model succeeded by pure chance.
