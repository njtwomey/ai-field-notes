<Definition>
**Target exploration** is the disciplined, pre-modeling audit of the response variable $y \in \mathcal{Y}$. It evaluates
label balance, distributional geometry (such as skewness and zero-inflation), temporal stationarity, disaggregated slice
rates, and bivariate associations with individual features to establish realistic performance bounds and intercept
target leakage before model fitting.
</Definition>

Before tuning architectures, constructing feature sets, or running automated pipelines, an engineer must rigorously
inspect the target variable. Machine learning loss functions optimize mathematical objectives under strict structural
assumptions about the distribution of $y$. When the target exhibits uninspected pathological properties—such as severe
class imbalance, extreme right-tailed skewness, point-mass zero-inflation, calendar non-stationarity, or post-outcome
leakage—models fail silently or produce deceptively optimistic validation scores that collapse in deployment
<Cite id="provost2001, kaufman2012" />.

## Balance and distributional geometry

The global distribution of $y$ dictates both the choice of loss function and the baseline evaluation metrics.

### Discrete labels and class imbalance

For classification targets $y \in \{1, \dots, C\}$, the primary descriptive metrics are the empirical base rates and
label entropy:

$$
\pi_c = P(y = c) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(y_i = c), \quad H(Y) = -\sum_{c=1}^C \pi_c \log_2 \pi_c
$$

The imbalance ratio is defined as:

$$
\rho = \frac{\max_c \pi_c}{\min_c \pi_c}
$$

When $\rho \gg 10$ (or in extreme fraud/ad-click settings where $\pi_1 \sim 10^{-3}$ or $10^{-4}$), accuracy is entirely
uninformative; predicting the majority class achieves $99.9\%$ accuracy while delivering zero business utility. Target
exploration verifies:
1. **Rare class support**: Whether the rarest class has sufficient raw sample count ($N \pi_{\min} \ge 100$) in holdout
   validation folds to compute statistically meaningful recall or PR-AUC estimates.
2. **Multiclass Zipfian tails**: Whether multiclass labels follow a power-law distribution where dozens of fine-grained
   categories have fewer than 5 positive examples.

### Continuous targets: skewness and zero-inflation

For regression targets $y \in \mathbb{R}$, unconstrained mean squared error ($\text{MSE}$) assumes Gaussian,
homoscedastic residuals. Real-world physical, financial, and operational targets violate this assumption routinely:

- **Heavy right tails**: Quantified by sample skewness $\gamma_1 = \frac{\mathbb{E}[(y - \mu)^3]}{\sigma^3}$ and excess
  kurtosis $\gamma_2 = \frac{\mathbb{E}[(y - \mu)^4]}{\sigma^4} - 3$. When $\gamma_1 > 2$, MSE is dominated by the top
  $0.1\%$ of observations, causing gradients to explode during backpropagation or tree splits to isolate single extreme
  outliers.
- **Power transformations**: Power transforms normalize asymmetric target densities:
  - **Box-Cox transformation** <Cite id="box1964" /> (strictly positive $y > 0$):
    $$
    y^{(\lambda)} = \begin{cases} \frac{y^\lambda - 1}{\lambda} & \text{if } \lambda \neq 0 \\ \ln y & \text{if } \lambda = 0 \end{cases}
    $$
  - **Yeo-Johnson transformation** <Cite id="yeo2000" /> (supports zero and negative values):
    $$
    \psi(\lambda, y) = \begin{cases} \frac{(y + 1)^\lambda - 1}{\lambda} & \text{if } \lambda \neq 0, y \ge 0 \\ \ln(y + 1) & \text{if } \lambda = 0, y \ge 0 \\ -\frac{(-y + 1)^{2 - \lambda} - 1}{2 - \lambda} & \text{if } \lambda \neq 2, y < 0 \\ -\ln(-y + 1) & \text{if } \lambda = 2, y < 0 \end{cases}
    $$
- **Zero-inflation**: Targets like customer lifetime spend, insurance claim amounts, or daily rainfall feature a
  discrete point-mass at zero: $P(y = 0) = p_0 > 0$, combined with a continuous right-skewed distribution for $y > 0$.
  Fitting standard regression to zero-inflated targets yields biased estimates. Proper exploration flags the need for
  two-stage hurdle models (a classifier for $y > 0$ coupled with a conditional regressor) or compound Poisson-gamma
  Tweedie objectives.

$$
P(Y = y) = \begin{cases} 
p_0 & \text{if } y = 0 \quad \text{(discrete point-mass, e.g. no claim / zero spend)} \\ 
(1 - p_0) \cdot f_{\text{tail}}(y) & \text{if } y > 0 \quad \text{(continuous positive severity, e.g. Lognormal or Gamma)} 
\end{cases}
$$

> [!WARNING]
> **Jensen's inequality in back-transformation**: If a target is transformed via $z = \ln(y)$, predicting $\hat{z}$ and naively exponentiating $\hat{y} = \exp(\hat{z})$ underestimates the expected value because $\mathbb{E}[\exp(Z)] > \exp(\mathbb{E}[Z])$. For normally distributed residuals with variance $\sigma^2$, the unbiased mean prediction requires the log-normal correction factor:
> 
> $$
> \hat{y} = \exp\left(\hat{z} + \frac{\hat{\sigma}^2}{2}\right)
> $$

## Rate over time and temporal stability

Logged targets frequently violate the exchangeability / i.i.d. assumption across time. Target exploration plots the
rolling mean $\bar{y}_t = \frac{1}{|W_t|} \sum_{i \in W_t} y_i$ across chronological buckets $t \in [1, T]$ to identify
non-stationarities:

1. **Prior probability shift**: If the overall conversion rate drops from $4.5\%$ in Q1 to $1.8\%$ in Q3, a model
   trained on pooled data will output poorly calibrated probabilities.
2. **Seasonality and cyclic patterns**: Day-of-week dips (e.g. B2B software usage on weekends) and diurnal peaks require
   explicit cyclical feature representations or seasonal de-trending ($y_t = T_t + S_t + I_t$).
3. **Abrupt policy regime shifts**: Sharp discontinuities in rolling rates indicate upstream systemic changes—such as an
   altered fraud detection threshold, updated regulatory definitions, or redesigned UI flows.
4. **Maturity lags and backfill truncation**: In churn, default, or long-sales-cycle modeling, outcomes take 30 to 90
   days to resolve. Recent rows in logged databases may show artificial $0$s simply because the observation window has
   not closed. Exploring target maturity curves is essential to set appropriate historical cutoff dates.

## Rate by group and subpopulation slices

Evaluating the target solely at the global aggregate level conceals severe subpopulation disparities:

$$
P(y = 1 \mid A = a) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(y_i = 1, A_i = a)}{\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(A_i = a)}
$$

Target exploration stratifies the target across critical categorical slices:
- **Device and operating system**: Mobile vs desktop conversion rates.
- **Geographic jurisdictions**: Regional regulatory compliance or default rates.
- **Tenant and organizational cohorts**: High-volume enterprise clients vs self-serve users.

### Simpson's paradox

An unconditional correlation between a slice indicator and the target can reverse when conditioning on an underlying
confounder. For example, a new checkout UI may exhibit a lower overall conversion rate than the old UI
($P(y=1 \mid \text{UI}_{\text{new}}) < P(y=1 \mid \text{UI}_{\text{old}})$) simply because the new UI was deployed
disproportionately to mobile traffic, which has lower baseline conversion across all designs <Cite id="provost2001" />.
Stratified target profiling across confounding dimensions isolates true baseline disparities from compositional shifts.

## Single-feature screening and leakage detection

Before fitting complex multi-variable models, computing univariate predictive power for each candidate feature $X_j$
against $y$ serves two vital diagnostic purposes:
1. Establishing an intuitive benchmark for baseline feature utility.
2. Detecting post-outcome **target leakage** <Cite id="kaufman2012" />.

### Univariate screening metrics

For binary targets, two robust non-parametric screens are Information Value ($\text{IV}$) and single-feature decision
stump ROC-AUC:

$$
\text{WoE}_b = \ln \left( \frac{P(X_j \in B_b \mid y=1)}{P(X_j \in B_b \mid y=0)} \right)
$$

$$
\text{IV}(X_j) = \sum_{b=1}^B \left( P(X_j \in B_b \mid y=1) - P(X_j \in B_b \mid y=0) \right) \cdot \text{WoE}_b
$$

| Information Value ($\text{IV}$) | Univariate AUC | Diagnostic Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $< 0.02$ | $0.50 - 0.52$ | Unpredictive noise / uninformative single feature |
| $0.02 - 0.10$ | $0.53 - 0.59$ | Weak predictive signal |
| $0.10 - 0.30$ | $0.60 - 0.69$ | Moderate, realistic predictive power |
| $0.30 - 0.50$ | $0.70 - 0.82$ | Strong genuine predictor |
| $> 0.50$ | $> 0.90$ | **High probability of target leakage** |

```
Univariate AUC Spectrum:
 [0.50]-------[0.60]-------[0.70]-------[0.85]==============[1.00]
  Noise       Weak       Realistic     Suspicious         LEAKAGE
              Signal      Signal       Review Feature   (Post-Event)
```

In noisy real-world prediction tasks (such as credit default, customer churn, click-through rate, or medical
diagnostics), an individual feature rarely achieves $\text{AUC} > 0.85$ on its own. When a single column exhibits an
$\text{AUC} > 0.95$ or $\text{IV} > 0.50$, it almost invariably records information generated *after* or *concurrent
with* the target event (for example, `ticket_resolution_timestamp`, `refund_status_code`, or `account_closure_reason`)
<Cite id="kaufman2012" />.

## Diagnostic checklist

To execute thorough target exploration before model training, follow this systematic sequence:

1. **Verify target integrity**: Confirm that $y$ contains zero `NaN`, null, or unmapped sentinel values (such as `-999`
   or `999999` mistakenly recorded as numeric quantities).
2. **Quantify base rates and entropy**: Compute class counts, imbalance ratio $\rho$, and entropy $H(Y)$. Ensure every
   evaluation fold retains adequate positive examples.
3. **Inspect regression geometry**: Evaluate skewness $\gamma_1$ and kurtosis $\gamma_2$. If $y \ge 0$ with severe skew,
   test log or Yeo-Johnson transforms <Cite id="yeo2000, box1964" />; check whether zero-inflation demands a two-stage
   hurdle architecture.
4. **Plot chronological rate**: Graph 7-day and 30-day moving averages of the target. Inspect for step-level regime
   shifts, day-of-week oscillations, and backfill truncation at the trailing edge.
5. **Stratify across key sub-cohorts**: Group by primary categorical dimensions and flag slices with extreme target
   scarcity ($N_{s,+} < 10$).
6. **Screen univariate predictors**: Calculate single-feature AUC or mutual information. Flag any feature with
   $\text{AUC} > 0.88$ for immediate data-pipeline audit to prevent silent leakage into production.
