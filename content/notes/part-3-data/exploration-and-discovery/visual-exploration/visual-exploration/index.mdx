<Definition>
**Visual exploration** is the inspection of data through plots and low-dimensional maps
before any model is fixed. It looks for shape, outliers, relations between variables, groups and anomalies. Its results
are hypotheses about the data. They are confirmed by measurements in the original feature space, not by the picture.
</Definition>

## What it is for

Exploratory data analysis, in Tukey's sense, treats looking at data as a step in its own right <Cite id="tukey1977" />.
A histogram shows skew, a second mode or a spike at a <NoteLink to="encoding-missingness">sentinel value</NoteLink>. A
scatter plot shows a curved relation, a variance that grows with the mean or two populations mixed together. For
high-dimensional data, such as embeddings or wide tables, projections and clustering give the same kind of overview.
Each discovery changes a later choice: a transform, a cleaning rule, a slice to evaluate or a model family.

## Main ideas

**Plots of one and two variables come first.** <NoteLink to="exploratory-data-analysis" /> covers histograms, density
estimates and box plots for single variables, scatter plots and conditional plots for pairs, and small multiples and
pair plots for several. It also separates exploration from confirmation: a pattern chosen after looking needs fresh
data to test.

**Projections map many dimensions to two.** <NoteLink to="projection-for-exploration" /> compares PCA, which keeps
global variance and is linear, with t-SNE <Cite id="vandermaaten2008" /> and <Gloss name="umap" />
<Cite id="mcinnes2018" />, which keep local neighbourhoods. Each distorts distances and cluster sizes differently. A
cluster seen in a projection is checked in the original space before it is believed.

**Clusters organise review.** <NoteLink to="clustering-for-exploration" /> partitions data into cohorts so that a
person can review a few examples per cluster instead of thousands at random. It surfaces machine-generated templates,
near-duplicates and rare sub-populations. The cluster-then-sample workflow uses $k$-means for even coverage or HDBSCAN
for dense groups and noise.

**Embeddings make unstructured data browsable.** <NoteLink to="embedding-based-exploration" /> maps images, audio and
text into a vector space with a pretrained encoder. Nearest-neighbour queries find similar items, clusters suggest
themes, and isolated points flag outliers and out-of-distribution inputs.
