<Definition>
**Clustering for exploration** is the unsupervised partitioning of an unlabeled dataset $\mathcal{D}$ into geometric or
semantic clusters $\mathcal{C} = \{C_1, \dots, C_K\}$ to structure human data auditing. By grouping instances into
coherent cohorts, teams inspect cluster medoids and boundaries to uncover rare subpopulations, detect automated
templates and near-duplicates, and map domain diversity far faster than uniform random reading.
</Definition>

When faced with millions of raw text documents, telemetry logs, or customer interactions, practitioners cannot read
every record. The standard fallback—drawing a uniform random sample of $100$ records—fails systematically because
dominant majority patterns drown out critical minority phenomena. Clustering organizes raw data into structurally
homogeneous cohorts, transforming an unmanageable inspection task into a disciplined, stratified audit
<Cite id="campello2013, arthur2007" />.

## Sub-populations and the discovery deficit

The mathematical flaw of uniform random sampling for discovery lies in binomial rarity. If a catastrophic failure mode,
security exploit, or underrepresented customer cohort accounts for a small fraction $p$ of total volume (e.g.,
$p = 0.1\% = 0.001$), the probability of drawing zero occurrences in an audit sample of size $n$ is:

$$
P(\text{miss}) = (1 - p)^n
$$

For a substantial audit sample of $n = 500$ rows:

$$
P(\text{miss}) = (1 - 0.001)^{500} \approx 60.6\%
$$

Even after reading 500 records, the auditor is more likely to miss the rare subpopulation than to find it.

```
 Discovery Failure under Uniform Sampling vs Cluster-Then-Sample:
  Uniform Random Sampling:              Cluster-Then-Sample:
  (Heavily Favors Majority Piles)        (Stratified Subgroup Coverage)
  [ Majority Mode: 95% ] -> Reads 95     [ Cluster 1: Majority A ] -> Reads 5
  [ Majority Mode: 4%  ] -> Reads 5      [ Cluster 2: Majority B ] -> Reads 5
  [ Rare Anomaly:  0.1%]-> Reads 0       [ Cluster 3: Template   ] -> Reads 5
  (Anomaly completely omitted!)          [ Cluster 4: Rare Cohort] -> Reads 5 (Discovered!)
```

By clustering the dataset first, observations belonging to the $0.1\%$ subpopulation coalesce into a distinct, compact
cluster. Sampling a fixed allocation of $m$ records from *each* cluster guarantees that human reviewers examine the rare
cohort regardless of its global prevalence.

## Templates, machine-generated records, and near-duplicates

In operational logs, web scrapes, customer service tickets, and transaction feeds, human behavior is mixed with
programmatic automation. Bots, system alert scripts, and UI form templates generate massive numbers of near-identical
records.

### Diagnosing template clusters

Exploratory clustering detects templated records through intra-cluster dispersion:

$$
\sigma^2(C_k) = \frac{1}{|C_k|} \sum_{\mathbf{x} \in C_k} \|\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k\|^2
$$

- **Natural human clusters**: Exhibit continuous, organic dispersion ($\sigma^2 > 1.0$), reflecting natural lexical or
  behavioral variance.
- **Automated template clusters**: Exhibit near-zero variance ($\sigma^2 \to 0$) and extreme point-density. Every record
  shares identical fixed boilerplate text with minor slot-value variations (e.g.
  `User <ID> reset password at <Timestamp>`).

### Medoids and boundary inspection

To audit an identified cluster $C_k$, practitioners extract two distinct prototype instances:
1. **The cluster medoid**: The observation minimizing total distance to all other members in the cluster:
   $$
   \mathbf{x}_{\text{medoid}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{x} \in C_k} \sum_{\mathbf{x}' \in C_k} d(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')
   $$
   The medoid provides the most representative, clean summary of the cluster's core theme.
2. **Boundary/periphery instances**: Observations located at the outer density threshold or nearest to neighboring
   clusters. Boundary instances expose semantic drift, hybrid categories, and noisy edge cases.

## The cluster-then-sample workflow

A complete exploratory clustering pipeline proceeds through four disciplined phases:

$$
\text{Feature Extraction} \longrightarrow \text{Unsupervised Partition} \longrightarrow \text{Stratified Prototype Sampling} \longrightarrow \text{Qualitative Domain Coding}
$$


1. **Feature representation**: Extract vector representations tailored to the modality:
   - Tabular: Robustly scaled numerical features and target-encoded categoricals.
   - Text: Pre-trained sentence embeddings (e.g., from dense transformer encoders) or TF-IDF.
2. **Clustering partition**: Partition the space using algorithms suited to exploratory goals.
3. **Stratified prototype extraction**: For each of the $K$ clusters, draw:
   - 1 cluster medoid (central exemplar).
   - 3 random intra-cluster instances (measuring internal consistency).
   - 2 boundary instances (identifying contamination).
4. **Qualitative coding**: Domain experts review the extracted prototypes, assigning descriptive category labels (e.g.,
   `"Billing Inquiries"`, `"Password Reset Bot"`, `"API Authentication Crash"`) and flagging anomalous data corruption.

## Algorithmic trade-offs: k-means versus HDBSCAN

The choice of clustering algorithm dictates the exploratory insights revealed:

| Algorithm | Structural Assumptions | Outlier Handling | Exploratory Utility |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$k$-means** <Cite id="lloyd1982, arthur2007" /> | Spherical, convex clusters of equal variance | Forces every outlier into the nearest centroid | Good for broad, uniform partitioning; distorts irregular densities |
| **DBSCAN** <Cite id="ester1996" /> | Density-connected manifolds of fixed density $\varepsilon$ | Flags points below `minPts` as explicit noise | Captures non-linear shapes; struggles with multi-density hierarchies |
| **HDBSCAN** <Cite id="campello2013" /> | Hierarchical density estimates across variable densities | Separates unclustered noise instances ($\text{label} = -1$) | **Premier choice for EDA**: naturally extracts rare dense clusters while isolating noise |

In exploratory auditing, **HDBSCAN** is uniquely powerful because it does not force every observation into a cluster.
Points labeled as noise ($\text{label} = -1$) constitute a high-priority queue of unusual, corrupted, or exploratory
anomaly candidates <Cite id="campello2013" />.

## Diagnostic checklist

To conduct cluster-based exploratory data auditing:

1. **Select dense representations**: Generate semantic embeddings or normalized feature matrices that capture
   domain-relevant similarities.
2. **Prioritize density-based clustering**: Use HDBSCAN <Cite id="campello2013" /> to accommodate variable cluster
   densities and separate genuine outliers into an explicit noise pool.
3. **Audit cluster radii**: Calculate intra-cluster dispersion $\sigma^2(C_k)$; flag ultra-tight clusters
   ($\sigma^2 \approx 0$) as automated bot templates or duplicate dumps.
4. **Sample medoids and boundaries**: For each cluster, inspect the central medoid for semantic labeling and outer
   boundary points for leakage or drift.
5. **Review unclustered noise points**: Audit a sample of noise instances ($\text{label} = -1$) to identify novel
   failure modes and data corruption.
6. **Construct a slice taxonomy**: Convert verified clusters into permanent categorical metadata tags for downstream
   disaggregated evaluation.
