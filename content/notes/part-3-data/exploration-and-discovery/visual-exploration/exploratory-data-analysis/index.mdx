<Definition>
**Exploratory data analysis (EDA)** is the disciplined visual, numerical, and structural investigation of data pioneered
by John Tukey <Cite id="tukey1977" />. It reveals unexpected distributional geometries, detects anomalies and outliers,
evaluates structural modeling assumptions, and generates informed predictive hypotheses prior to formal model selection.
</Definition>

Before committing to specific loss functions, neural architectures, or feature engineering pipelines, an engineer must
visually inspect the data. Tabular aggregates and scalar summary statistics (such as means, standard deviations, and
linear correlations) compress away critical geometric structure. Datasets with identical scalar statistics can possess
radically different underlying data-generating mechanisms, requiring entirely different algorithmic treatments
<Cite id="tukey1977, anscombe1973" />.

## Aims of exploration and Anscombe's quartet

The foundational premise of EDA is that statistical modeling must begin with observation rather than confirmatory
hypothesis testing <Cite id="tukey1977" />. Francis Anscombe formalized this necessity in 1973 by constructing four
synthetic bivariate datasets (Anscombe's quartet) <Cite id="anscombe1973" />.

Every dataset in the quartet shares identical summary statistics to two decimal places:
- Sample size: $N = 11$
- Mean of $X$: $\bar{x} = 9.0$, Mean of $Y$: $\bar{y} = 7.50$
- Variance of $X$: $s_x^2 = 11.0$, Variance of $Y$: $s_y^2 = 4.12$
- Pearson correlation: $r = 0.816$
- OLS regression line: $y = 3.0 + 0.50x$ with coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.67$

As rendered interactively in the visualizer above, each dataset manifests a radically disparate geometry despite
indistinguishable regression summaries:


Without visualization, an automated model selector would apply identical linear models to all four datasets. In reality:
- **Dataset I** represents a genuine noisy linear relationship.
- **Dataset II** exhibits a strict deterministic parabola; a linear model suffers complete structural misspecification.
- **Dataset III** is a near-perfect collinear line ruined by a single vertical measurement error outlier.
- **Dataset IV** contains zero relationship among 10 points clustered at $x=8$; the entire positive regression slope is
  manufactured by a single extreme leverage point at $x=19$.

## Univariate visualization: shape, spread, and modality

Univariate EDA assesses the marginal distribution $P(X_j)$ of each feature.

### Histograms and bandwidth selection

Histograms partition continuous values into discrete bins. Bin width $h$ fundamentally dictates visual interpretation:
- **Freedman-Diaconis rule**: Optimizes bin width to resist heavy tails and outliers:
  $$
  h_{\text{FD}} = 2 \times \frac{\text{IQR}}{N^{1/3}}
  $$
- **Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)**: Produces a smooth continuous density estimate:
  $$
  \hat{f}_h(x) = \frac{1}{Nh} \sum_{i=1}^N K\left(\frac{x - x_i}{h}\right)
  $$
  Using a Gaussian kernel $K(u) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-u^2/2}$, the optimal bandwidth minimizing asymptotic mean
  integrated squared error (AMISE) is given by Silverman's rule of thumb <Cite id="silverman1986" />:
  $$
  h^* = 0.9 \min\left(\hat{\sigma}, \frac{\text{IQR}}{1.34}\right) N^{-1/5}
  $$

> [!WARNING]
> **Bandwidth pitfalls in KDE**: An undersmoothed bandwidth ($h \ll h^*$) hallucinates dozens of spurious multimodal spikes from local sampling variance. Conversely, an oversmoothed bandwidth ($h \gg h^*$) flattens true bimodal mixtures into a deceptively unimodal Gaussian curve <Cite id="silverman1986" />.

### Box plots, violin plots, and letter-value plots

Standard box plots display the five-number summary: median, quartiles ($Q_1, Q_3$), and Tukey fences
($Q_1 - 1.5\text{IQR}, Q_3 + 1.5\text{IQR}$) <Cite id="tukey1977" />. For modern web-scale datasets ($N > 10^5$),
standard box plots designate tens of thousands of points as "outliers" simply because Gaussian tails naturally produce
points beyond $1.5\text{IQR}$. For large sample sizes, **letter-value plots** (boxen plots) compute deeper quantile
depths (eighths, sixteenths, thirty-seconds), providing reliable tail estimates without visual clutter.

## Bivariate visualization: relationships and dependencies

Bivariate EDA investigates joint distributions $P(X_j, X_k)$ and feature-target relationships $P(y \mid X_j)$.

### Mitigating overplotting

When visualizing millions of records, scatter plots suffer from severe overplotting—individual points fuse into solid
black blobs that mask underlying density variations:
1. **Alpha blending**: Reduce point opacity ($\alpha \in [0.01, 0.1]$) so that overlapping points reveal density
   gradients.
2. **Hexagonal binning (hexbin)**: Aggregate points into 2D hexagonal tessellations with logarithmic color scaling
   ($\log_{10}(\text{count})$).
3. **2D Kernel density contours**: Draw iso-density contours indicating bivariate probability mass.

### Heteroscedasticity and non-linear patterns

Bivariate plots uncover structural violations of classical assumptions:
- **Heteroscedastic fan shapes**: The conditional variance $\text{Var}(Y \mid X)$ expands as $X$ increases. Ordinary
  Least Squares (OLS) produces efficient point estimates but severely understated standard errors, demanding weighted
  least squares or variance-stabilizing transforms ($y \to \ln y$).
- **Zero-correlation non-linearities**: Circular or sinusoidal patterns where Pearson's correlation $r \approx 0$, yet
  the two features share an exact deterministic functional dependence ($y = x^2$ or $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$).

## Multivariate visualization

Real-world feature spaces span dozens or hundreds of dimensions ($D \gg 3$).

| Dimension Reduction Paradigm | Mechanism | Preserved Structure | Typical Diagnostic Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Linear (PCA)** | Maximizes global variance | Pairwise Euclidean distances across coordinates | Detecting dominant variance directions and global outliers |
| **Non-Linear (t-SNE / UMAP)** | Optimizes local neighborhood probabilities | Local topological neighborhoods and manifold curvature | Visualizing discrete semantic clusters and subpopulation groupings |

- **Parallel coordinates**: Normalize continuous features to $[0, 1]$ and plot each observation as a connected polygonal
  line spanning parallel vertical axes. Tightly bundled parallel trajectories immediately highlight latent clusters and
  multidimensional outliers.
- **Correlation heatmaps with hierarchical clustering**: Reorder the feature correlation matrix using agglomerative
  hierarchical clustering dendrograms. Highly collinear feature blocks group into distinct visual squares along the
  diagonal.

## Exploration versus confirmation: the forking paths

A critical hazard in data science is treating exploratory discoveries as confirmatory statistical findings
<Cite id="gelman2013forking" />.

When an analyst plots dozens of scatter plots, slices data across various subgroups, and tests various transformations,
they traverse what Andrew Gelman terms the **garden of forking paths** <Cite id="gelman2013forking" />. Finding an
apparent correlation or subgroup pattern after extensive visual search is not independent evidence; it is a hypothesis
generated by the data.

### Preempting HARKing (Hypothesizing After the Results are Known)

1. **Quarantine the evaluation holdout**: Split data into exploratory ($70\%$) and test ($30\%$) partitions *before*
   opening visual exploration tools. All visual inspection, transformation experiments, and feature mining must occur
   strictly on the exploratory split.
2. **Pre-register feature transformations**: When visual exploration suggests a specific non-linear transformation (such
   as a spline knot or log transform), freeze the transformation logic and validate its predictive gain on the untouched
   holdout partition.

## Diagnostic checklist

To execute disciplined exploratory data analysis:

1. **Inspect Anscombe geometries**: Never rely on Pearson correlations or $R^2$ alone; plot bivariate distributions to
   distinguish true linear relationships from parabolas, outliers, or leverage points <Cite id="anscombe1973" />.
2. **Calibrate KDE bandwidths**: Validate density bandwidths against Silverman's rule $h^*$; avoid oversmoothing that
   obscures multimodal mixtures <Cite id="silverman1986" />.
3. **Mitigate scatter plot saturation**: Deploy hexagonal binning or log-density contours on datasets exceeding $10^4$
   points.
4. **Scan for heteroscedasticity**: Inspect residual spreads across feature scales; flag expanding fan patterns
   requiring log transformations or robust regression.
5. **Cluster correlation heatmaps**: Reorder correlation matrices hierarchically to identify collinear predictor blocks.
6. **Enforce holdout quarantine**: Restrict all visual snooping to a development partition to prevent overoptimistic
   validation from data snooping <Cite id="gelman2013forking" />.
