<Definition>
Let observations $x_1, x_2, \dots$ be conditionally independent given a parameter $\thetavec$ with prior $p(\thetavec)$.
**Sequential Bayesian updating** computes the posterior one observation at a time:

$$
p(\thetavec \mid x_{1:t}) = \frac{p(x_t \mid \thetavec)\,p(\thetavec \mid x_{1:t-1})}{p(x_t \mid x_{1:t-1})}, \qquad
p(x_t \mid x_{1:t-1}) = \int p(x_t \mid \thetavec)\,p(\thetavec \mid x_{1:t-1})\,d\thetavec.
$$

After each step the posterior replaces the data: the next update needs only the current posterior and the new
observation. The denominator is the **one-step predictive** probability of $x_t$.
</Definition>

## Why the recursion is exact

By <NoteLink to="bayes-theorem" /> on the whole batch, $p(\thetavec \mid x_{1:t}) \propto p(\thetavec)\prod_{s=1}^{t}p(x_s \mid \thetavec)$,
using <NoteLink to="independence">conditional independence</NoteLink>. Splitting off the last factor, the remaining product is proportional to
$p(\thetavec \mid x_{1:t-1})$, which gives the recursion. Three consequences follow.

- **Order does not matter.** The batch posterior is a product over observations, so any order of updates gives the
  same result.
- **The predictive probabilities multiply to the evidence.** By the <NoteLink to="derivative">chain rule</NoteLink>,
  $\prod_{t=1}^{T}p(x_t \mid x_{1:t-1}) = p(x_{1:T})$, the <NoteLink to="law-of-total-probability">marginal likelihood</NoteLink>. The cumulative <NoteLink to="cross-entropy-and-perplexity">log loss</NoteLink> of the <NoteLink to="online-learning">sequential
  predictions</NoteLink> is $-\ln p(x_{1:T})$, a model score computed online.
- **Regret under log loss.** For a finite set of models, the Bayesian predictive is the aggregating algorithm, and its
  cumulative log loss exceeds the best model's by at most $\ln(1/\pi_i)$, where $\pi_i$ is that model's prior mass
  (see <NoteLink to="aggregating-algorithm-and-mixability" />). For a smooth $d$-parameter family with a prior density
  that is positive near the best parameter, the excess over the best parameter in hindsight grows as
  $\tfrac{d}{2}\ln T + O(1)$ <Cite id="cesabianchi2006" />.

## Conjugate models: streaming by sufficient statistics

When the prior is conjugate to the likelihood, the posterior stays in the prior's family and each update adds the new
observation's <NoteLink to="sufficient-statistic">sufficient statistics</NoteLink> to the hyperparameters (see <NoteLink to="conjugate-priors" />). The state is a
fixed-size vector, whatever the length of the stream.

- **Beta–Bernoulli.** With prior $\Beta(a, b)$ and $x_t \in \set{0, 1}$, the update is $a \leftarrow a + x_t$,
  $b \leftarrow b + 1 - x_t$, and the predictive probability of $x_{t+1} = 1$ is $a/(a + b)$.
- **Gaussian mean with known variance.** With prior $\Gauss(\mu, \tau^2)$ and $x_t \sim \Gauss(\theta, \sigma^2)$,
  precisions add, $1/\tau^2 \leftarrow 1/\tau^2 + 1/\sigma^2$, and the mean becomes the precision-weighted average of
  the old mean and $x_t$.
- **<NoteLink to="linear-regression">Linear regression</NoteLink> with Gaussian noise.** With prior $\thetavec \sim \Gauss(\zeros, \sigma_0^2\I)$ and
  $y_t = \thetavec^\top\xvec_t + \varepsilon_t$, $\varepsilon_t \sim \Gauss(0, \sigma^2)$, the posterior mean is the ridge
  solution with penalty $\sigma^2/\sigma_0^2$. Its online update is <NoteLink to="recursive-least-squares-filter">recursive least squares</NoteLink>, derived in
  <NoteLink to="online-learning-for-linear-models" />, and the posterior covariance is $\sigma^2\Pmat_t$.

**Worked example.** Start from $\Beta(1, 1)$, uniform on $[0, 1]$. The stream $1, 0, 1, 1$ gives $\Beta(2, 1)$,
$\Beta(2, 2)$, $\Beta(3, 2)$, $\Beta(4, 2)$. The predictive probabilities of the four observations were $1/2$, $1/3$,
$1/2$ and $3/5$, whose product $1/20$ is the marginal likelihood
$\int_0^1\theta^3(1 - \theta)\,d\theta = \mathrm{B}(4, 2) = 1/20$. The predictive probability that the fifth observation
is 1 is $4/6 = 0.667$.

## Assumed density filtering

Without conjugacy, the product $p(x_t \mid \thetavec)\,q_{t-1}(\thetavec)$ leaves any tractable family, and exact
posteriors grow in complexity with $t$. **Assumed density filtering** (ADF) keeps an approximation $q_t$ in a chosen
<NoteLink to="exponential-family">exponential family</NoteLink> $\Qcal$. Each step forms the **tilted distribution**
$\hat p_t(\thetavec) \propto p(x_t \mid \thetavec)\,q_{t-1}(\thetavec)$ and projects it back:

$$
q_t = \argmin_{q \in \Qcal}\KL(\hat p_t \,\|\, q).
$$

**Projection is moment matching.** Let $q(\thetavec) = h(\thetavec)\exp(\etavec^\top\svec(\thetavec) - A(\etavec))$, with
sufficient statistics $\svec$ and log-normaliser $A$. Up to terms that do not depend on $\etavec$,
$\KL(\hat p_t \,\|\, q) = -\etavec^\top\expect_{\hat p_t}[\svec] + A(\etavec)$. Its <NoteLink to="gradient">gradient</NoteLink> in $\etavec$ is
$-\expect_{\hat p_t}[\svec] + \nabla A(\etavec)$, and $\nabla A(\etavec) = \expect_q[\svec]$ for an exponential family.
The minimiser therefore sets $\expect_q[\svec] = \expect_{\hat p_t}[\svec]$. For a Gaussian $q$, ADF matches the mean and
covariance of the tilted distribution <Cite id="opper1998" /> <Cite id="minka2001" />.

**Probit regression.** Let $q_{t-1} = \Gauss(\mvec, \Smat)$ and $p(y \mid \thetavec) = \Phi(y\,\thetavec^\top\xvec)$ with
$y \in \set{-1, +1}$ and $\Phi$ the standard normal distribution function. The tilted distribution depends on
$\thetavec$ only through the scalar $\thetavec^\top\xvec$, whose prior is $\Gauss(\mvec^\top\xvec, s^2)$ with
$s^2 = \xvec^\top\Smat\xvec$. Its moments have closed forms. With $z = y\,\mvec^\top\xvec/\sqrt{1 + s^2}$ and
$r = \phi(z)/\Phi(z)$, where $\phi$ is the standard normal density,

$$
\mvec_{\text{new}} = \mvec + \frac{ry}{\sqrt{1 + s^2}}\Smat\xvec, \qquad
\Smat_{\text{new}} = \Smat - \frac{r(z + r)}{1 + s^2}\Smat\xvec\xvec^\top\Smat.
$$

The update is rank one, like recursive least squares, and costs $O(d^2)$.

**Worked example.** Start from $\Gauss(\zeros, \I)$ and observe $\xvec = (1, 2)$, $y = +1$. Then $s^2 = 5$, $z = 0$ and
$r = \phi(0)/\Phi(0) = 0.798$. The update gives $\mvec_{\text{new}} = (0.326, 0.651)$ and
$\Smat_{\text{new}} = \begin{pmatrix}0.894 & -0.212 \\ -0.212 & 0.576\end{pmatrix}$. The exact posterior moments, estimated
by <NoteLink to="importance-sampling">importance sampling</NoteLink> with two million prior draws, agree to within $0.001$ in the mean and covariance.

## Changing parameters

The recursion above assumes $\thetavec$ never changes. With drift, old observations are evidence about a parameter
that no longer holds. Two remedies keep the online form.

- **Add dynamics.** Model $\thetavec_t = \thetavec_{t-1} + \wvec_t$ with noise $\wvec_t$. Each step first predicts,
  which inflates the posterior covariance, then updates. For a linear-Gaussian model this is the
  <NoteLink to="kalman-filter" />; for non-linear models, the <NoteLink to="particle-filter" />.
- **Forget.** Raise the posterior to a power $\lambda \in (0, 1]$ before each update:
  $p(\thetavec \mid x_{1:t}) \propto p(x_t \mid \thetavec)\,p(\thetavec \mid x_{1:t-1})^\lambda$. For a conjugate model this
  multiplies the accumulated pseudo-counts by $\lambda$. For $\lambda < 1$, a Beta posterior holds at most $1/(1 - \lambda)$
  observations' worth of evidence from the data, and the prior's pseudo-counts decay as $\lambda^t$. At $\lambda = 1$
  nothing is forgotten. This is the Bayesian form of the forgetting factor in
  <NoteLink to="concept-drift-adaptation" />.

When the parameter changes abruptly, <NoteLink to="bayesian-online-changepoint-detection" /> keeps a posterior over the
time since the last change and runs a sequential update for each hypothesis.

## Assumptions

- The observations are conditionally independent given $\thetavec$, and the model is fixed. The recursion is exact
  only under both.
- The likelihood can be evaluated for one observation at a time. Models whose likelihood does not factorise over
  observations, such as those with global latent structure, need other online schemes.

## Pitfalls

- **ADF depends on order.** Each projection loses information that later observations cannot restore, so different
  orders give different $q_T$. <NoteLink to="expectation-propagation" /> revisits each factor to remove this dependence,
  at the cost of storing a site per observation.
- **Overconfidence.** A posterior after millions of observations has tiny variance and barely moves. On a drifting
  stream this is the same failure as a <Gloss name="learning-rate">learning rate</Gloss> that decays to zero; add dynamics or forgetting.
- **Numerical drift of the covariance.** The rank-one downdates can lose positive definiteness in floating point, as
  in recursive least squares. Keep a factorised form or symmetrise periodically.
