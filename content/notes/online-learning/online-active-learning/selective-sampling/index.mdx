<Definition>
**Selective sampling** is <NoteLink to="online-learning">online learning</NoteLink> in which labels cost something. In
each round the learner receives $\xvec_t$ and predicts $\hat y_t$. It then decides, possibly at random, whether to
**query** the label $y_t$. If it does not query, it never learns $y_t$, though the prediction still counts towards its
mistakes. The learner trades the number of queries against the number of mistakes (or the regret), on any sequence of
examples <Cite id="cesabianchi2006b" />.
</Definition>

## Notation

Labels are $y_t \in \set{-1, +1}$. The linear learner keeps $\wvec_t$, has margin $\hat p_t = \wvec_t^\top\xvec_t$ and
predicts $\hat y_t = \sgn(\hat p_t)$. $M_t = \indicator\set{\hat y_t \ne y_t}$ marks a mistake and $Z_t \in \set{0, 1}$
a query. $R = \max_t\norm{\xvec_t}$. For a comparator $\uvec$, the <Gloss name="hinge-loss">hinge loss</Gloss> is
$\ell_t(\uvec) = \max(0, 1 - y_t\uvec^\top\xvec_t)$ and $L(\uvec) = \sum_t\ell_t(\uvec)$.

## Which labels are worth paying for

A linear learner's margin $\abs{\hat p_t}$ measures its confidence. An example with a large margin is almost certainly
classified correctly, and even if it is not, the perceptron update it would trigger is the same as for any other
mistake. Examples near the boundary are the ones whose labels change the model most. Selective sampling therefore
queries more often when the margin is small. A deterministic threshold on the margin can be exploited by an
adversary that places every example just outside it. Randomising the query decision makes the analysis work on every
sequence.

## The selective-sampling perceptron

The algorithm has one parameter $b > 0$ <Cite id="cesabianchi2006b" />.

1. Receive $\xvec_t$, compute $\hat p_t = \wvec_t^\top\xvec_t$ and predict $\sgn(\hat p_t)$.
2. Query with probability $b/(b + \abs{\hat p_t})$.
3. If the label was queried and the prediction was wrong, set $\wvec_{t+1} = \wvec_t + y_t\xvec_t$. Otherwise
   $\wvec_{t+1} = \wvec_t$.

A small $b$ queries rarely except at tiny margins. As $b \to \infty$ every label is queried and the algorithm becomes
the <NoteLink to="perceptron" />.

## The mistake bound

**Distance to a scaled comparator.** Let $\alpha > 0$ and track $\Phi_t = \norm{\alpha\uvec - \wvec_t}^2$. In a round
with an update ($M_tZ_t = 1$),

$$
\Phi_t - \Phi_{t+1} = 2\alpha y_t\uvec^\top\xvec_t - 2y_t\hat p_t - \norm{\xvec_t}^2
\ge 2\alpha(1 - \ell_t(\uvec)) + 2\abs{\hat p_t} - R^2.
$$

The inequality uses $y_t\uvec^\top\xvec_t \ge 1 - \ell_t(\uvec)$, and $-y_t\hat p_t = \abs{\hat p_t}$ because the
prediction was wrong. Summing over rounds, $\Phi_1 - \Phi_{T+1} \le \alpha^2\norm{\uvec}^2$ with $\wvec_1 = \zeros$, so

$$
\sum_{t=1}^{T}M_tZ_t\paren{2\alpha - R^2 + 2\abs{\hat p_t} - 2\alpha\ell_t(\uvec)} \le \alpha^2\norm{\uvec}^2.
$$

**Take expectations over the queries.** Given the past, $Z_t$ is independent of everything else in round $t$ and has
mean $b/(b + \abs{\hat p_t})$. Choose $\alpha$ with $2\alpha - R^2 = 2b$. Then

$$
\expect[Z_t \mid \text{past}]\paren{2b + 2\abs{\hat p_t}} = \frac{b}{b + \abs{\hat p_t}}\cdot 2(b + \abs{\hat p_t}) = 2b,
$$

whatever the margin. The query probability was chosen so that this cancellation happens: rounds with large margins are
queried rarely, but when they are, their update makes proportionally more progress. Using
$b/(b + \abs{\hat p_t}) \le 1$ on the loss term,

$$
2b\,\expect\brack{\sum_tM_t} - 2\alpha L(\uvec) \le \alpha^2\norm{\uvec}^2.
$$

With $\alpha = b + R^2/2$, dividing by $2b$,

$$
\expect\brack{\sum_{t=1}^{T}M_t} \le \paren{1 + \frac{R^2}{2b}}L(\uvec) + \frac{(2b + R^2)^2}{8b}\norm{\uvec}^2
$$

for every $\uvec$ <Cite id="cesabianchi2006b" />. The expected number of queries is
$\sum_t\expect[b/(b + \abs{\hat p_t})]$. As the learner improves, most margins grow and the query rate falls.

**The trade-off in $b$.** With $R = 1$, the bound is $6L + 1.8\norm{\uvec}^2$ at $b = 0.1$, $1.5L + 1.1\norm{\uvec}^2$
at $b = 1$, and $1.05L + 5.5\norm{\uvec}^2$ at $b = 10$. A large $b$ brings the coefficient of $L$ close to 1 but
queries nearly every label, and the coefficient of $\norm{\uvec}^2$ grows as $b/2$. A small $b$ queries rarely, and the
coefficient of $L$ grows as $1/(2b)$.

## Worked example

Take $d = 10$, unit-norm features drawn uniformly on the sphere, labels from a fixed unit vector $\uvec$ with 5% of
labels flipped, and $T = 10\,000$ rounds. Averaged over 20 runs:

| Learner | Labels queried | Mistakes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perceptron (every label) | 10 000 | 1751 |
| Selective sampling, $b = 10$ | 9146 | 1697 |
| Selective sampling, $b = 1$ | 5009 | 1400 |
| Selective sampling, $b = 0.1$ | 1087 | 1191 |

Here selective sampling makes fewer mistakes with a tenth of the labels. The flipped labels sit mostly at large
margins, where the perceptron updates on them and the selective sampler rarely asks. The Bayes-optimal classifier
makes about 500 mistakes on this stream.

## Label-efficient prediction with experts

The same idea applies to <NoteLink to="hedge-and-exponential-weights">prediction with expert advice</NoteLink>. With a
budget of about $m$ labels over $T$ rounds, query each round independently with probability $\varepsilon = m/T$, and
feed Hedge the importance-weighted losses $\hat\ell_{t,i} = \ell_{t,i}Z_t/\varepsilon$, which are unbiased. The Hedge
potential argument, with $e^{-x} \le 1 - x + x^2/2$ for $x \ge 0$ in place of
<NoteLink to="hoeffdings-inequality">Hoeffding's lemma</NoteLink>, gives

$$
\expect[R_T] \le \frac{\ln N}{\eta} + \frac{\eta}{2}\sum_t\expect\brack{\sum_ip_{t,i}\hat\ell_{t,i}^2} \le \frac{\ln N}{\eta} + \frac{\eta T}{2\varepsilon},
$$

because $\expect[\hat\ell_{t,i}^2] = \ell_{t,i}^2/\varepsilon \le 1/\varepsilon$. The derivation is that of
<NoteLink to="exp3">EXP3</NoteLink>, with the query decision in place of the arm choice. At
$\eta = \sqrt{2\varepsilon\ln N/T}$ the regret is at most $\sqrt{2T\ln N/\varepsilon} = T\sqrt{2\ln N/m}$. The average
regret per round is of order $\sqrt{\ln N/m}$: it depends on the number of labels, not the number of rounds, and this
rate cannot be improved <Cite id="cesabianchi2005" />. With $T = 10^5$, $N = 10$ and $m = 1000$ the bound is $6786$, an
average of $0.068$ per round, against $0.0034$ with every label.

## Assumptions

- The learner can decide on each label before the next example arrives, and a label, once declined, is gone.
- The bounds hold for any sequence of examples and labels; no distribution is assumed.
- The mistake count includes unqueried rounds, whose mistakes the learner never observes.

## Pitfalls

- **A deterministic margin threshold.** Querying only when $\abs{\hat p_t} < \theta$ saves labels on benign data but has
  no worst-case guarantee, and it can stop querying in a region where the model is confidently wrong.
- **Budgets in labels, not probabilities.** The query rate depends on the data. For a hard budget, cap the queries and
  fall back to a fixed rate, as in <NoteLink to="stream-based-active-learning" />.
- **Sampling bias.** The queried labels are not a random sample, so they cannot be used as an unbiased estimate of
  error without reweighting by $1/\prob(Z_t = 1)$.
