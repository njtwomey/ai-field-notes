<Definition>
In the experts problem with losses $\ell_{t,i} \in [0, 1]$, a **switching comparator** is a sequence of experts
$i_1, \dots, i_T$, and it has $m$ **switches** if $i_{t+1} \ne i_t$ for exactly $m$ values of $t$. The **tracking
regret** against it is $\sum_t\inner{\pvec_t}{\lvec_t} - \sum_t\ell_{t,i_t}$. **Fixed share** updates Hedge's weights
in two steps each round:

$$
v_{t,i} = \frac{p_{t,i}e^{-\eta\ell_{t,i}}}{\sum_jp_{t,j}e^{-\eta\ell_{t,j}}}, \qquad
p_{t+1,i} = \frac{\alpha}{N} + (1 - \alpha)v_{t,i},
$$

with <Gloss name="learning-rate">learning rate</Gloss> $\eta > 0$, share rate $\alpha \in [0, 1)$ and $\pvec_1$ uniform <Cite id="herbster1998" />.
</Definition>

## Notation

$N$ is the number of experts and $T$ the number of rounds. $\pvec_t$ is the learner's distribution over experts and
$\lvec_t = (\ell_{t,1}, \dots, \ell_{t,N})$ the loss vector. The learner's loss is $\inner{\pvec_t}{\lvec_t}$, the expected
loss of an expert drawn from $\pvec_t$. The comparator is any sequence with at most $m$ switches, which the learner
does not know.

## Why Hedge cannot track

<NoteLink to="hedge-and-exponential-weights">Hedge</NoteLink> has weight $p_{t,i} \propto e^{-\eta L_{t-1,i}}$. If expert
$i$ was worse than the leader by $\Delta$ in cumulative loss, its weight is $e^{-\eta\Delta}$ times the leader's.
After expert $i$ becomes the best, it must first make up the whole deficit $\Delta$ before Hedge prefers it. The
deficit grows with the length of the earlier segment, so Hedge's delay after a switch grows with $T$, and its tracking
regret against a sequence with even one switch can be linear in $T$. <NoteLink to="follow-the-regularised-leader">Follow the leader</NoteLink> has the same problem. The
figure below shows both losing about 200 over 1000 rounds to a sequence with three switches.

Fixed share caps the deficit. Every round it hands a fraction $\alpha$ of the mass back uniformly, so no weight falls
below $\alpha/N$. When an expert becomes the best, it starts from at least $\alpha/N$ and needs only about
$\ln(N/\alpha)/\eta$ loss difference to take over, whatever happened before.

## Fixed share is Bayes over switching sequences

Consider exponential weights over the $N^T$ **sequences** of experts, with a prior $\pi$ on sequences. A sequence
$\svec = (i_1, \dots, i_T)$ is treated as a single expert whose loss in round $t$ is $\ell_{t,i_t}$. Choose the Markov prior

$$
\pi(\svec) = \frac1N\prod_{t=2}^{T}\paren{(1 - \alpha)\indicator\set{i_t = i_{t-1}} + \frac{\alpha}{N}}:
$$

start uniformly, and in each round keep the current expert with probability $1 - \alpha$ or redraw it uniformly with
probability $\alpha$. The learner's loss in round $t$ is linear in the sequences' weights, so it depends only on the
marginal weight of each current expert $i_t$. That marginal is computed by the forward recursion of a
<NoteLink to="hidden-markov-model" /> whose states are experts, whose <NoteLink to="markov-chain">transition matrix</NoteLink> is the Markov prior, and whose
emission weight is $e^{-\eta\ell_{t,i}}$. The recursion has two steps: multiply by the emission weight and normalise,
which gives $v_{t,i}$; then apply the transition, $\sum_jv_{t,j}\paren{(1 - \alpha)\indicator\set{i = j} + \alpha/N} = (1 - \alpha)v_{t,i} + \alpha/N$.
This is fixed share. The algorithm costs $O(N)$ per round, although it implicitly weights $N^T$ sequences.

## The regret bound

Hedge with a non-uniform prior satisfies the bound of <NoteLink to="hedge-and-exponential-weights" /> with $\ln N$
replaced by $\ln(1/\pi(\svec))$: the lower bound on the potential becomes $W_T \ge \pi(\svec)e^{-\eta L_T(\svec)}$, where
$L_T(\svec) = \sum_t\ell_{t,i_t}$. The upper bound is unchanged, because it depends only on the learner's
distribution. Applied to the sequences,

$$
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\inner{\pvec_t}{\lvec_t} - L_T(\svec) \le \frac{\ln(1/\pi(\svec))}{\eta} + \frac{\eta T}{8}.
$$

A sequence with $m$ switches has prior at least $\frac1N\paren{\frac{\alpha}{N}}^m(1 - \alpha)^{T - 1 - m}$: each of the
$m$ switches costs $\alpha/N$, and each of the $T - 1 - m$ stays costs at least $1 - \alpha$. So

$$
\ln\frac{1}{\pi(\svec)} \le (m + 1)\ln N + m\ln\frac1\alpha + (T - 1 - m)\ln\frac{1}{1 - \alpha} \eqdef P.
$$

**Tuning.** At $\alpha = m/(T - 1)$, the last term is $(T - 1 - m)\ln\paren{1 + \tfrac{m}{T - 1 - m}} \le m$, so
$P \le (m + 1)\ln N + m\ln\tfrac{e(T - 1)}{m}$. With $\eta = \sqrt{8P/T}$,

$$
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\inner{\pvec_t}{\lvec_t} - L_T(\svec) \le \sqrt{\frac{TP}{2}}
$$

<Cite id="herbster1998" /> <Cite id="cesabianchi2006" />. Each switch costs about $\ln N + \ln(T/m)$ nats of prior: $\ln N$ to
name the new expert and $\ln(T/m)$ to name when the switch happens. With $m = 0$ and $\alpha = 0$ the bound is Hedge's.

## Worked example

The figure's setting has $N = 4$, $T = 1000$ and $m = 3$. With $\alpha = 3/999$,
$P = 4\ln 4 + 3\ln 333 + 996\ln(1/(1 - 3/999)) = 5.55 + 17.42 + 3.00 = 25.97$, the tuned rate is
$\eta = \sqrt{8 \cdot 25.97/1000} = 0.46$ and the bound is $\sqrt{1000 \cdot 25.97/2} = 114$. On the figure's losses,
fixed share with $\eta = 0.3$ has tracking regret about 60, and with $\eta = 1$ about 26. Hedge and follow the leader
have about 210, close to the 207 by which the best single expert trails the switching sequence.

## Variants

- **Fixed share to past posteriors.** Instead of redistributing to the uniform distribution, share mass towards
  weights the algorithm held in the past. When the best expert returns to an earlier one, as in the figure's last
  segment, this recovers faster <Cite id="bousquet2001" />.
- **Adaptive regret.** Fixed share also bounds the regret on every interval $[r, s]$ against the best fixed expert on
  that interval, because a sequence can switch into that expert at $r$ and out at $s$. Hazan and Seshadhri give
  algorithms with small regret on every interval for general <NoteLink to="online-convex-optimisation">online convex optimisation</NoteLink> <Cite id="hazan2009" />.
- **Ensembles for drifting concepts.** Dynamic weighted majority adds and removes experts as the data drift, trained
  online, and weights them with a <NoteLink to="weighted-majority">weighted-majority</NoteLink> rule <Cite id="kolter2007" />; see
  <NoteLink to="concept-drift-adaptation" />.
- **Bandit feedback.** <Gloss name="exp3" />.S applies the same uniform mixing to the bandit algorithm EXP3; see
  <NoteLink to="non-stationary-bandits" />.

## Assumptions

- The tuned $\alpha$ and $\eta$ need $m$ and $T$. With the wrong $m$, the bound degrades gracefully: $P$ grows
  linearly in the true $m$ and logarithmically in the mismatch of $\alpha$.
- Losses lie in $[0, 1]$. The comparator is any switching sequence chosen in hindsight.
- The bound is on the regret, not on detection: fixed share does not report when a switch happened.
  <NoteLink to="bayesian-online-changepoint-detection" /> does, under a probabilistic model.

## Pitfalls

- **Share rate too large.** Every round, fixed share gives away $\alpha$ of the mass. On a long stationary stretch this
  costs about $\alpha T$ in extra loss, visible in the figure when $\alpha$ is pushed towards $0.3$.
- **Share rate too small.** With $\alpha \to 0$, fixed share becomes Hedge and loses the ability to track.
- **Comparing with the wrong baseline.** Low regret against the best fixed expert says nothing when the best expert
  changes; report the tracking regret, or the regret on recent windows, on non-stationary data.
